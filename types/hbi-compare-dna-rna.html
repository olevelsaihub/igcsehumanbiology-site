Knowledge card

Compare DNA with RNA

Written
  • DNA and RNA are both nucleic acids made of nucleotides.
  • DNA is double-stranded; RNA is single-stranded.
  • DNA contains the sugar deoxyribose; RNA contains the sugar ribose.
  • DNA contains the base thymine (T); RNA contains uracil (U) instead. Both contain A, C and G.
  • Two types of RNA in the specification are messenger RNA (mRNA) and transfer RNA (tRNA).
  • RNA is found in the nucleus (where mRNA is made) and in the cytoplasm, at the ribosomes.
  • In double-stranded DNA, %A equals %T and %C equals %G. If these pairs are not equal, the molecule is single-stranded.
  • The job of mRNA is to carry the genetic information from DNA in the nucleus to the ribosomes, where the protein is made.

Before you begin

  • The structure of DNA: double helix, complementary pairs.
  • The parts of a nucleotide.
1

The method

  1. Use the three standard contrasts: number of strands, sugar, and thymine versus uracil.

    Why: Each contrast is one mark and these three are always accepted.

  2. Write each difference with both sides: 'DNA has ..., RNA has ...'.

    Why: A one-sided statement may not show the difference clearly.

  3. For data, first look for U or T, then check whether the paired bases have equal percentages.

    Why: U shows RNA; unequal pair percentages show a single strand. Both are evidence marks.

  4. For 'justify', point to the feature in the diagram and say why it shows DNA (or RNA).

    Why: The mark needs the evidence and the link to the conclusion.

Do not lose marks here

  • ✗
    Writing 'RNA has no sugar' instead of 'RNA has ribose'.
  • ✗
    Saying RNA contains thymine, or DNA contains uracil.
  • ✗
    Writing 'thiamine' for thymine.
  • ✗
    Giving a difference about function only, when the question asks about structure.
  • ✗
    Saying RNA is only in the nucleus.

Common mistakes: Inferred, not measured. Inferred from the official schemes' credit and rejection patterns; not measured student-error statistics.

Explain1Use the data: uracil present, and the paired bases are not equal, so there is only one strand.

Worked explanation

  1. In part (ii) I named RNA as the other nucleic acid, so I compare DNA with RNA.
  2. Difference 1, strands: DNA has two strands; RNA has one.
  3. Difference 2, sugar: DNA has deoxyribose; RNA has ribose.
  4. Difference 3, bases: DNA has thymine; RNA has uracil instead.
  5. I write each difference across one row, so both sides are clear.
Model answer · written by olevels.com, not an official Pearson mark scheme

1. DNA: double-stranded. RNA: single-stranded.

2. DNA: has deoxyribose sugar. RNA: has ribose sugar.

3. DNA: has the base thymine. RNA: has the base uracil.

How the marks are earned

One mark for each row: strands, sugar, and thymine versus uracil.
