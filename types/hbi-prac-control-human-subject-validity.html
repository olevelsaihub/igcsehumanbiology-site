Explain validity problems and fixes in investigations on people

Investigations using volunteers or patients where the student explains why results are not valid, why a rest or fasting step improves validity, or what to change.

Type 7 Investigations HBI-PRAC-CONTROL-HUMAN-SUBJECT-VALIDITY

★★★☆☆ · 5 of 36 eligible examination papers, 2019–2025. Difficulty: Medium.

Students must spot which differences matter and link each to the measured response. Needs knowledge of human variation and of the body system involved. No calculation. Methods on people vary (urine, hearing, statins, caffeine), but the ideas repeat. Links experimental design with physiology such as osmoregulation or heart rate.

Worked explanation

  1. The factor tested is the quantity of salt in the diet. The result measured is the volume of urine.
  2. First problem: each person gets a different food. Foods differ in more than salt, for example water content.
  3. Second problem: there are ten different people. They may be different ages or sexes or have a kidney problem.
  4. For Explain I must link each problem to urine volume.
  5. Two marks, so two problems, each with a link.
Model answer · written by olevels.com, not an official Pearson mark scheme

Each person eats a different food, so things other than salt, such as the water or protein in the food, could change the volume of urine. The ten people may also be different ages or sexes or have health problems, so their kidneys may reabsorb different amounts of water whatever their salt intake.

How the marks are earned

One mark for pointing out that different foods are used (which could change urea or water in the urine). One mark for pointing out that different people may differ in sex, age, health or metabolism (which changes water balance). A third accepted idea is that earlier food and drink were not controlled.
