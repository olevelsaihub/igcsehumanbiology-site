Find a mean, median, mode or mean rate of change

A set of repeat readings or survey values needs an average, or a change over a time interval needs a mean rate per unit time.

Type 7 Maths skills HBI-MATH-MEAN-MEDIAN-MODE-RATE

★★★★☆ · 9 of 36 eligible examination papers, 2019–2025. Difficulty: Medium.

Students must pick the right average and the right data. Mean, median and mode are different ideas that are easily mixed up. Adding, dividing and rounding. The tasks are routine. Rate items also need graph reading.

Knowledge card

Find a mean, median, mode or mean rate of change

Calculation
  • Mean = total of all values / number of values.
  • Median = the middle value when the values are put in order. With an even count, take the mean of the two middle values.
  • Mode = the value that appears most often.
  • Mean rate of change = (end value - start value) / time taken, with units such as 'per hour' or 'per year'.
  • Give the mean to the same number of decimal places as the data, or as the question asks.
  • Spot any anomalous result and leave it out of the mean, unless the question tells you to use all the values.
  • A mean rate over a whole period can also be total amount / total time, e.g. add all the urine volumes for the day and divide by the 4 hours.
  • Mean daily increase = total increase / number of days.

Before you begin

  • Adding decimals accurately.
  • Ordering numbers.
  • Reading start and end values from a graph.
1

The method

  1. List the values you need (one row of a table, or the full list).

    Why: Using the wrong row loses all marks.

  2. Mean: add them up and divide by how many there are.

    Why: The sum or the division is often the first mark.

  3. Median: write them in order first, then pick the middle.

    Why: The ordered list is shown in schemes.

  4. Mode: count how many times each value appears.

    Why: A frequency count avoids mistakes.

  5. Rate: find the change between start and end, then divide by the time, and give the 'per unit time' unit.

    Why: The change and the division are separate marking points.

  6. Round as asked.

    Why: Some schemes reject answers with the wrong number of decimal places.

Do not lose marks here

  • ✗
    Dividing by the wrong count, such as 2 instead of 4.
  • ✗
    Finding the median without ordering the values.
  • ✗
    Giving the highest value as the mode.
  • ✗
    For a rate, giving the total change (520) and forgetting to divide by the time.
  • ✗
    Rounding 172.075 to 172 when one decimal place is asked.

Common mistakes: Inferred, not measured. Inferred from the official schemes' credit and rejection patterns; not measured student-error statistics.

Worked explanation

  1. The age-16 row has four heights: 175.9, 178.5 (males) and 166.3, 167.6 (females).
  2. Total = 166.3 + 167.6 + 175.9 + 178.5 = 688.3 cm (females first, then males; the order does not matter).
  3. There are 4 students, so mean = 688.3 / 4 = 172.075 cm.
  4. To one decimal place: 172.1 cm.
Model answer · written by olevels.com, not an official Pearson mark scheme

Mean = (166.3 + 167.6 + 175.9 + 178.5) / 4 = 688.3 / 4 = 172.1 cm

How the marks are earned

One mark for adding the four heights and dividing by 4. One mark for 172.1 cm. The answer 172 is not accepted, because the question asks for one decimal place.

Calculation check

  • 166.3+167.6+175.9+178.5 = 688.3
  • (166.3+167.6+175.9+178.5) ÷ 4 = 172.075
