Pearson Edexcel · International GCSE Human Biology · 4HB1

Describe a control or explain why a step is in a method

Control tubes, discs or beakers without the active ingredient, and justified processing steps such as blotting, rinsing or using percentage change.

Type 6 Investigations HBI-PRAC-EXPLAIN-CONTROL-OR-METHOD-STEP

★★★★★ · 10 of 36 eligible examination papers, 2019–2025. Difficulty: Medium.

Students must work out what to remove for the control and why a step matters. Needs the idea of a baseline comparison and some knowledge of the practical. No calculation in these parts. Contexts vary widely: enzymes, aspirin, osmosis, Visking tubing. Links experimental design with the biology of each practical.

Knowledge card

Describe a control or explain why a step is in a method

Written

A control experiment is the same set-up without the factor tested, so any change must be caused by that factor. A method step prevents an error.

Worked explanation

  1. The test discs contain antibiotic X or Y. The factor being tested is the antibiotic.
  2. So the control is the same disc of filter paper with no antibiotic, soaked in water instead.
  3. Expected result: bacteria grow up to the disc, with no clear zone.
  4. Purpose: if only X and Y give clear zones, the antibiotics caused them.
  5. Three marks, three ideas: set-up, result, conclusion.
Model answer · written by olevels.com, not an official Pearson mark scheme

Place a filter paper disc soaked in sterile water, with no antibiotic, on the same agar plate. Bacteria should grow right up to this disc, so there is no clear zone around it. This shows that the clear zones around discs X and Y are caused by the antibiotics and not by the filter paper.

How the marks are earned

One mark for a disc of filter paper without antibiotic; one for stating that bacteria grow normally with no clear zone; one for saying this shows whether the antibiotic is responsible for the effect.
