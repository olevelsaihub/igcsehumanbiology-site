Competitive and non-competitive inhibitors, drugs such as aspirin acting as inhibitors, and mutations that change an enzyme's shape.

Explain how inhibitors or a mutation change enzyme activity

Written

A competitive inhibitor blocks the active site. A non-competitive inhibitor or a mutation changes its shape. Either way, fewer enzyme-substrate complexes form.

4

The method

  1. Name the cause

    Competitive, non-competitive or mutation.

  2. Active site

    Blocked, or changed in shape.

  3. No binding

    Substrate cannot bind, so fewer complexes form.

  4. The effect

    Less gas, slower rate, less product or fewer clots.

Do not lose marks here

  • ✗
    Competitive changes shape

    It blocks the active site.

  • ✗
    Substrate changes shape

    The active site is what changes.

  • ✗
    More substrate fixes all

    It does not help a non-competitive inhibitor.

  • ✗
    Stopping at "blocked"

    Say what happens to the product.

Worked explanation

  1. The enzyme makes a gas, and the gas is collected every 10 minutes for one hour. The question asks what a competitive inhibitor added at the start would do to the results.
  2. A competitive inhibitor has a shape like the substrate, so it fits into the active sites of the enzyme and blocks them.
  3. Substrate cannot enter a blocked active site, so fewer enzyme-substrate complexes form.
  4. So the reaction is slower and less gas is collected in each 10-minute period: every bar on the chart would be lower.
  5. There are 2 marks: one for the effect on the results and one for the reason. I write both.
Model answer · written by olevels.com, not an official Pearson mark scheme

Less gas would be produced in each 10-minute period, so every bar would be lower, because the reaction is slower. This is because the competitive inhibitor enters and blocks the active sites of the enzyme, so fewer substrate molecules can bind and fewer enzyme-substrate complexes form.

How the marks are earned

Mark 1: less gas is produced, or the reaction is slower. Mark 2: the reason, that the inhibitor blocks the active sites. An answer that gives the effect with no active-site reason earns only one mark.
