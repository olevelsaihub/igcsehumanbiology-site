Construct or complete a results or frequency table

Raw results, lists or tallies organised into a ruled table with headings and units, or a missing entry filled in.

Type 3 Maths skills HBI-MATH-CONSTRUCT-TABLE

★★★☆☆ · 6 of 36 eligible examination papers, 2019–2025. Difficulty: Easy.

Students must decide which variable goes first and order the data. Needs the idea of independent and dependent variables. No calculation except counting tallies. Common practical skill. Links practical method to data recording.

The method

  1. Rule

    Draw columns and rows.

  2. Head 1

    Independent variable and unit.

  3. Head 2

    Measured result and unit.

  4. Fill

    Put values in order; keep units out.

Do not lose marks here

  • ✗
    Result first

    The measured result is not column 1.

  • ✗
    Jumbled order

    Re-order the values.

  • ✗
    Units everywhere

    Units go in the heading only.

Worked explanation

  1. The student changed the sugar concentration, so it goes in the first column: 'Concentration of sugar solution in au'.
  2. The measured result is the percentage change in mass: 'Percentage change in mass in %'.
  3. Put the concentrations in order: 0.00, 0.25, 0.50, 1.00, 1.50 au.
  4. Match each result: 0.00 is +8, 0.25 is +4, 0.50 is -1, 1.00 is -7, 1.50 is -10.
  5. Rule the table and keep the units in the headings only.
Model answer · written by olevels.com, not an official Pearson mark scheme

A ruled two-column table. Column 1 heading: Concentration of sugar solution in au. Column 2 heading: Percentage change in mass in %. Rows in order of concentration: 0.00 au gives +8; 0.25 au gives +4; 0.50 au gives -1; 1.00 au gives -7; 1.50 au gives -10.

How the marks are earned

One mark for a table with two columns (or rows). One mark for the concentration heading with au. One mark for the percentage change in mass heading. One mark for all the data placed correctly in order from 0.00 to 1.50 au.
