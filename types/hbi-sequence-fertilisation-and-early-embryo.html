Fertilisation joins a sperm and an ovum nucleus to make a diploid zygote. The zygote divides by mitosis into an embryo of identical cells.

The method

  1. Name the event

    Ovulation, fertilisation or mitosis.

  2. Fertilisation

    Sperm and egg nuclei fuse to form a zygote.

  3. Mitosis in order

    DNA copies, chromosomes line up, copies pulled apart, cell splits.

  4. Identical twins

    One zygote or embryo splits into two.

Do not lose marks here

  • ✗
    Zygote called an embryo

    Fertilisation makes a zygote.

  • ✗
    Haploid zygote

    The zygote is diploid.

  • ✗
    Meiosis in the embryo

    The blastocyst forms by mitosis.

  • ✗
    Implantation

    That is not conception.

Worked explanation

  1. Lines 2 to 4 say the zygote becomes a hollow ball of about 100 genetically identical cells.
  2. Identical cells come from mitosis, so I name mitosis first.
  3. Four marks, so I need at least four separate ideas about what happens in each division.
  4. Before dividing, the cell copies its DNA so each chromosome has two copies.
  5. Then the chromosomes line up across the middle of the cell.
  6. Then the two copies of each chromosome are pulled to opposite ends of the cell.
  7. Finally the cell splits into two cells, and this repeats many times.
Model answer · written by olevels.com, not an official Pearson mark scheme

The zygote divides by mitosis. First the DNA in each chromosome is copied. The chromosomes then line up across the middle (equator) of the cell. The copies of each chromosome are pulled apart to opposite ends of the cell. The cytoplasm then splits so one cell becomes two identical cells. These divisions repeat until a hollow ball of about 100 cells, the blastocyst, is formed.

How the marks are earned

One mark each for naming mitosis, DNA copying, chromosomes lining up in the middle, chromosomes moving to opposite poles, and the cell dividing; any four score full marks. Just listing the phase names in the right order earns only one mark.
