Knowledge card

Explain and evaluate uses of embryonic and adult stem cells

Written
  • Stem cells are unspecialised cells that can divide many times and can differentiate into specialised cells.
  • Embryonic stem cells (from an early embryo, e.g. a blastocyst) can become almost any type of body cell.
  • Adult stem cells (e.g. in bone marrow) can only become a few types of cell, and there are few of them.
  • Stem cells could treat diseases by making new cells to replace damaged ones, for example nerve cells in Parkinson's disease.
  • Embryonic stem cells: the embryo is destroyed, which many people think is wrong; the cells may be rejected and may form tumours.
  • Cells from the patient's own body (adult or induced pluripotent stem cells) have the same antigens, so they are less likely to be rejected, and no embryo is used.
  • Risks of stem cell treatments: the cells may form tumours, may be rejected if they come from someone else, may carry infection, and treatment may be offered by unlicensed clinics.
  • Induced pluripotent (iPS) cells are made from a patient's own blood or skin cells, so they are easy to obtain, need no donor and no embryo, and match the patient's tissue type.

Before you begin

  • What a specialised cell is.
  • Mitosis produces genetically identical cells.
  • The immune system rejects cells with foreign antigens.
1

The method

  1. Begin with the key property: stem cells are unspecialised and can differentiate.

    Why: This is the starting mark in most explanations.

  2. For a treatment, name the cell type they would become and say they replace damaged cells in the named organ.

    Why: Each step of the treatment is a mark.

  3. For 'discuss', write a separate list for each source: advantages and disadvantages of embryonic, then of adult stem cells.

    Why: The scheme splits the marks between sources, so both must be covered.

  4. Include at least one ethical point and one medical risk (rejection or tumours).

    Why: Ethics and risks are commonly credited and show a balanced answer.

  5. Avoid vague words like 'good' or 'dangerous' without a reason.

    Why: Only specific advantages and disadvantages earn marks.

Do not lose marks here

  • ✗
    Saying adult stem cells can become any type of cell.
  • ✗
    Writing only about embryonic stem cells in a question that asks about both types.
  • ✗
    Writing 'it is unethical' with no reason, such as the embryo being destroyed.
  • ✗
    Saying stem cells 'cure' a disease without saying they replace damaged cells.
  • ✗
    Saying embryonic cells can become sex cells and body cells when the question is about body cells.

Common mistakes: Inferred, not measured. Inferred from the official schemes' credit and rejection patterns; not measured student-error statistics.

Worked explanation

  1. The question asks for advantages and disadvantages of embryonic and adult stem cells. 6 marks, with space for each type, so I aim for three points for each type.
  2. Embryonic, advantages: they can become almost any type of cell; they can be kept growing in culture for a long time.
  3. Embryonic, disadvantages: the embryo is destroyed, which some people believe is wrong; they may form tumours; they may be rejected.
  4. Adult, advantages: they can be taken from the patient, so they are less likely to be rejected; no embryo is destroyed.
  5. Adult, disadvantages: they can only become a few types of cell; there are only small numbers of them; they cannot be grown in culture for long.
  6. I check I have at least three points for each type.
Model answer · written by olevels.com, not an official Pearson mark scheme

Embryonic stem cells: An advantage is that they can develop into almost every type of body cell. They can also be kept dividing in the lab for a long time, so they are useful for research such as testing drugs or studying Parkinson's disease. A disadvantage is that the embryo is destroyed, and some people think this is wrong because the embryo could become a person. They may also form tumours, and they may be rejected by the patient's immune system.

Adult stem cells: An advantage is that they can be taken from the patient's own body, so they are less likely to be rejected, and no embryo is harmed. A disadvantage is that they can only form a small range of cell types. Also, there are only a few of them in body tissues, and they cannot be grown in the lab for long.

How the marks are earned

Up to three marks for embryonic points and up to three for adult points. Embryonic: forming most cell types, long life in culture, use in drug research, tumour risk, embryo destruction, an ethical objection, rejection. Adult: lower rejection risk, taken from the patient, limited range of cell types, short life in culture, small numbers. The answer gives more than three points for each type.
