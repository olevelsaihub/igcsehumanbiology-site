Explain heart and breathing changes in exercise and how carbon dioxide controls breathing

Written

Exercise raises the need for oxygen and glucose, so the heart rate rises. Rising carbon dioxide is detected and the medulla oblongata raises breathing.

Worked explanation

  1. The graph: pulse rises from about 70 to about 140 beats per minute as exercise time rises from 0 to 100 seconds.
  2. Why? Muscles contract more, so they need more energy.
  3. The heart beats faster, so more blood flows to the muscles each minute.
  4. This blood brings more oxygen and glucose for aerobic respiration.
  5. It also carries away the extra carbon dioxide, and less lactic acid builds up.
Model answer · written by olevels.com, not an official Pearson mark scheme

As the time spent exercising increases, the pulse rate increases, from about 70 to about 140 beats per minute. The heart pumps faster so more blood flows to the muscles. This delivers more oxygen and glucose to the muscle cells, so they can carry out more aerobic respiration to release the energy needed for contraction. The faster blood flow also removes more carbon dioxide, and less lactic acid builds up because less anaerobic respiration is needed.

How the marks are earned

Marks for: pulse rises as exercise time rises; more blood flow to muscles; more oxygen or glucose delivered; more aerobic respiration; more carbon dioxide removed or less lactic acid. Any four give full marks.
