Knowledge card

Describe the products and purposes of mitosis

Written
  • Mitosis makes two daughter cells.
  • The daughter cells are genetically identical to each other and to the parent cell.
  • They are diploid: a human body cell has 46 chromosomes (23 pairs), and each daughter cell also has 46.
  • Mitosis is used for growth, for repair and replacement of damaged cells, and for asexual reproduction (cloning).
  • Meiosis is different: it makes four cells, each haploid (23 chromosomes) and genetically different; it makes gametes.
  • In the cell cycle, before mitosis the cell grows, makes new organelles and proteins, and copies its DNA; after the nucleus divides, the cytoplasm divides (cytokinesis).
  • In the growth phases of the cell cycle (G1 and G2) the cell gets bigger, makes proteins and copies organelles such as mitochondria; in the S phase the DNA is copied. These prepare the cell for mitosis.

Before you begin

  • Chromosomes come in pairs in body cells.
  • DNA is replicated before mitosis.
  • The stages of mitosis.
1

The method

  1. For products, give three facts: two cells, genetically identical, diploid (same chromosome number as the parent).

    Why: Each fact can be a marking point.

  2. For drawings, draw two cells and give each one exactly the same chromosomes as the parent cell.

    Why: The mark checks the number of cells and that each has the same chromosome set.

  3. For uses, name growth or repair in the body's context.

    Why: The scheme accepts growth or repair, or asexual reproduction.

  4. For mitosis versus meiosis, compare the number of cells and the chromosome number in each.

    Why: Matching lines or comparisons test both numbers.

  5. On DNA-mass graphs, a fall to half shows the cell dividing (cytokinesis).

    Why: The drop is where one cell becomes two.

  6. To draw two nuclei after mitosis, draw each nucleus with exactly the same number and shapes of chromosomes as the parent nucleus.

    Why: The marks check that both nuclei match the parent.

Do not lose marks here

  • ✗
    Saying mitosis makes four cells, or that daughter cells have half the chromosomes.
  • ✗
    Saying the daughter cells are 'similar' instead of genetically identical.
  • ✗
    Drawing daughter cells with a different number of chromosomes from the parent.
  • ✗
    Giving 'making gametes' as a use of mitosis in humans.
  • ✗
    Mixing up 'haploid' and 'diploid'.

Common mistakes: Inferred, not measured. Inferred from the official schemes' credit and rejection patterns; not measured student-error statistics.

Worked explanation

  1. The question asks what is produced by mitosis. 2 marks, so I need two features.
  2. Feature 1: two cells, and they are genetically identical to the parent cell.
  3. Feature 2: they are diploid, with the full set of chromosome pairs (46 in humans).

Model answer · written by olevels.com, not an official Pearson mark scheme

Mitosis produces two daughter cells that are genetically identical to each other and to the parent cell. Each daughter cell is diploid, with the same number of chromosomes as the parent (46 in a human body cell).

How the marks are earned

One mark for two genetically identical cells. One mark for diploid (2n).
