Pearson Edexcel · International GCSE Human Biology · 4HB1

Deduce genotypes from a pedigree, family or blood group

Assigning genotypes to people in family trees, including X-linked and Y-linked symbols, possible ABO genotypes for a blood group, and matching children to parents.

Type 14 Reproduction and heredity HBI-DEDUCE-GENOTYPES-FROM-PEDIGREE

★★★★★ · 10 of 36 eligible examination papers, 2019–2025. Difficulty: Difficult.

Works backwards and forwards through generations using one-allele-from-each-parent logic. Needs recessive, dominant, X-linked and Y-linked rules, and ABO matching. Only counting people or writing a simple ratio. Every pedigree is new, but the rules are fixed. Combines pedigree reading with genotype notation and blood groups.

Deduce genotypes from a pedigree, family or blood group

Written

Fill in the certain genotypes first. Anyone linked to an affected person by one allele must be heterozygous. Give a reason for each.

4

The method

  1. Read the key

    Use the symbols the question gives.

  2. Fixed points

    Affected recessive people are aa.

  3. Link by an allele

    Parent or child of an affected person carries it.

  4. Say why

    Write the reason next to each genotype.

Do not lose marks here

  • ✗
    AA for a relative

    If a parent or child is affected, write Aa.

  • ✗
    Only one option

    Group A can be IA IA or IA IO.

  • ✗
    Dropping X and Y

    Sex-linked genotypes need them.

Worked explanation

  1. Sickle cell anaemia is recessive; A is the normal allele and a is the sickle cell allele.
  2. Person 6 is an unshaded square: a male without sickle cell anaemia, so he has at least one A.
  3. His mother, person 2, is shaded: she has sickle cell anaemia, so she is aa.
  4. She can only pass on a, so person 6 got an a from her.
  5. So person 6 has one A and one a.
Model answer · written by olevels.com, not an official Pearson mark scheme

Aa

How the marks are earned

One mark for the heterozygous genotype Aa: he does not have the condition, so he has a dominant A, and his affected mother (aa) must have given him a recessive a. AA or aa would score nothing.
