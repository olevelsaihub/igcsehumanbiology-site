Identify parts of the eye and state their functions

Naming labelled eye structures, choosing structures from descriptions, matching parts to jobs, and describing roles of cornea, lens, retina, fovea, iris and the blind spot.

Knowledge card

Identify parts of the eye and state their functions

Written
  • Cornea: clear front window of the eye. It refracts (bends) light the most.
  • Lens: refracts light to focus it on the retina. Its shape is changed by the ciliary muscles through the suspensory ligaments.
  • Iris: coloured ring of muscle that controls the size of the pupil and so how much light enters. The pupil is the gap in the centre of the iris.
  • Retina: the inner layer at the back with light-sensitive cells (rods and cones). It turns light into electrical impulses.
  • Fovea: the part of the retina with the most cones, where the image is sharpest. Blind spot: the place where the optic nerve leaves the retina. It has no rods or cones, so light that falls there is not detected and that part of the image is not seen.
  • Optic nerve: carries impulses from the retina to the brain.
  • Choroid: dark layer with blood vessels that bring oxygen and nutrients to the retina. Sclera: tough white outer coat. Eyelid: protects the eye from dust.
  • Ciliary muscles (in the ciliary body) change the shape of the lens. The pupil becomes smaller in bright light and larger in dim light, which controls how much light enters.
  • Eye-and-ear tables also need ear parts: the eardrum (tympanum) vibrates when sound waves hit it; the ossicles carry the vibrations across the middle ear; the cochlea turns vibrations into nerve impulses; the auditory nerve carries them to the brain; the semicircular canals help balance.
  • Pigment is found in the eye (retina and choroid) and as haemoglobin in red blood cells, as well as in skin and hair.

Before you begin

  • Light travels in straight lines and is bent (refracted) by curved transparent surfaces.
  • Receptors detect stimuli and start impulses.
  • Sensory neurones carry impulses to the brain.

The method

  1. Follow each label line to its exact end before naming it.

    Why: The answer depends on where the line ends, not on where the letter is printed.

  2. Use the light path from the front: cornea, pupil (in the iris), lens, then retina, then optic nerve at the back.

    Why: Knowing the order helps you place unfamiliar diagrams.

  3. Match descriptions using key words: light-sensitive means retina; controls light entering means iris; carries impulses means optic nerve; refracts means cornea or lens.

    Why: Each correct match is one mark.

  4. For a 'describe the function' answer, give the part's action and its result, for example 'detects light and changes it into impulses'.

    Why: Describe items often give two marks per part: what it does and what follows.

  5. In a mixed eye-and-ear table, first decide if each description is about light or about sound and balance, then choose only from that organ's parts in the box.

    Why: This halves the choices and stops eye words being used for ear descriptions.

Do not lose marks here

  • ✗
    Naming the pupil when the label points to the coloured iris, or the reverse.
  • ✗
    Saying the iris focuses light. The cornea and lens focus light; the iris controls the amount.
  • ✗
    Saying the optic nerve 'sees' the image. It carries impulses; the brain interprets them.
  • ✗
    Mixing up the fovea (most receptors) and the blind spot (no receptors).
  • ✗
    Writing 'retina' for the layer that brings blood; that is the choroid.

Common mistakes: Inferred, not measured. Inferred from the official schemes' credit and rejection patterns; not measured student-error statistics.

Complete3Fill each gap or table cell with one part from the box; one part per gap.
State1Give a single place or part name.
Draw1Draw one line from each structure to one function only.
Name2Write the name of each labelled part.
Describe1Give what the part does and what happens next, for example the optic nerve carries impulses from the retina to the brain.
Explain1Give the meaning and the reason, for example the blind spot has no receptors because the optic nerve leaves there.

Worked explanation

  1. The question asks me to name parts A, B and C on the eye section. It is worth 3 marks, one per part.
  2. A is at the front of the eye, the clear curved layer bulging forward. That is the cornea.
  3. B is the layer lining the back of the eye. That is the retina.
  4. C leaves the back of the eye towards the brain. That is the optic nerve.
Model answer · written by olevels.com, not an official Pearson mark scheme

A: cornea

B: retina

C: optic nerve

How the marks are earned

One mark for each correct name: cornea for A, retina for B, optic nerve for C.
