Explain oral rehydration and treatment of diarrhoeal disease

Oral rehydration therapy for cholera or Ebola, plus antibiotics where the disease is bacterial.

Type 4 Disease HBI-ORAL-REHYDRATION-TREATMENT

★★☆☆☆ · 3 of 36 eligible examination papers, 2019–2025. Difficulty: Medium.

A short cause-and-effect chain from loss to replacement. Water and salt balance is familiar and concrete. No mathematics. The disease context (cholera or Ebola) changes whether antibiotics apply. Links symptoms, osmoregulation ideas and antibiotic action.

Explain oral rehydration and treatment of diarrhoeal disease

Written

Diarrhoea and vomiting remove water and salts, causing dehydration. Oral rehydration therapy replaces what was lost; it does not kill the pathogen.

4

The method

  1. The cause of loss

    Diarrhoea and vomiting.

  2. What is lost

    Water and salts, leading to dehydration.

  3. Describe ORT

    Clean water with salts and sugar, drunk by mouth.

  4. Its action

    It replaces the lost water and salts.

  5. Check the pathogen

    Bacterium: add antibiotics. Virus: no antibiotics.

Do not lose marks here

  • ✗
    Water only

    Always mention salts.

  • ✗
    ORT kills bacteria

    It replaces; antibiotics kill.

  • ✗
    Antibiotics for Ebola

    Ebola is a virus.

  • ✗
    Prevention

    Treatment is asked for, not clean water supply.

Worked explanation

  1. 'Explain how... can be treated', 4 marks: I need four ideas.
  2. Why is treatment needed? Cholera gives severe diarrhoea, so water and salts are lost.
  3. Treatment 1: oral rehydration therapy, a drink of salts and sugar in clean water.
  4. What it does: it replaces the water and salts that were lost.
  5. Treatment 2: cholera is caused by a bacterium, so antibiotics can be used to kill it.
  6. Count the ideas: loss, ORT, replacement, antibiotics, killing bacteria. That is five, enough for four marks.
Model answer · written by olevels.com, not an official Pearson mark scheme

Cholera causes severe diarrhoea, so the person loses a lot of water and salts from the body. The person is given oral rehydration therapy: a drink of clean water with salts and sugar dissolved in it. This replaces the water and salts that have been lost, so the person does not become dehydrated. The person can also be given antibiotics, which kill the cholera bacteria.

How the marks are earned

Marks for: naming oral rehydration therapy; replacing fluid, salts or sugar; the reason (water and salts lost through diarrhoea or vomiting); using antibiotics; antibiotics killing the bacteria. Any four give full marks; the answer has all five.
