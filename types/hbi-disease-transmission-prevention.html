Knowledge card

Describe how a named infection spreads, shows itself and is prevented

Written
  • Every infection follows the same course: infection (the pathogen enters), incubation (it multiplies, no symptoms yet), then symptoms.
  • Symptoms appear late because only a few pathogens enter at first; they need time to reproduce until there are enough to cause harm.
  • Ebola spreads through body fluids (blood, vomit, faeces) of infected people or bodies. Prevent it with protective clothing, masks and goggles, isolating patients, avoiding contact with body fluids, disinfecting surfaces and safe burial.
  • HIV spreads through sexual intercourse, infected blood (shared needles, transfusions) and from mother to baby. Prevent it with condoms, screening donated blood and not sharing needles. There is no cure; antiretroviral drugs slow the virus. Ebola has no antibiotic cure; patients get fluids (rehydration) and care.
  • Cholera spreads through water or food contaminated with faeces from an infected person. Prevent it with clean or boiled water, sewage treatment and good hygiene.
  • Gonorrhoea is caused by a bacterium and spreads by sexual intercourse. Barrier contraception (condoms) prevents it, and antibiotics treat it.
  • A reservoir is an animal that carries a pathogen without becoming ill, for example fruit bats for Ebola.
  • Pinworm eggs pass from the anus to fingers by scratching, then to surfaces and other people's mouths. Hand washing, short nails, not sharing towels and washing bedding break this chain. Other signs are eggs or worms in faeces, weight loss and poor nutrition; sticky tape pressed on the anus picks up eggs that can then be seen.
  • Athlete's foot is caused by a fungus that grows on warm, damp skin, especially between the toes. It spreads by direct skin contact and from damp floors in showers and changing rooms, and from shared towels, socks or shoes. It is treated with antifungal creams, powders or sprays (not antibiotics). Prevent it by washing and drying feet well, changing socks, not sharing towels or shoes, and wearing sandals in shared showers.
  • Diarrhoeal diseases are prevented with clean (boiled or treated) drinking water, sewage treatment, hand washing and cooking food well. Malaria is prevented with mosquito nets, insect repellent, insecticides, draining standing water and anti-malarial drugs.

Before you begin

  • The meaning of pathogen and the main organism groups.
  • Microorganisms reproduce quickly when conditions are right.
  • Blood transfusion and blood groups.

The method

  1. Identify the pathogen and its route into the body (water, food, body fluids, sex, blood, hands to mouth).

    Why: Each prevention mark must block a real route for that disease.

  2. For prevention, give one barrier per route, and make each answer a different idea.

    Why: Repeating the same idea in new words earns only one mark.

  3. For delayed symptoms, explain: few pathogens enter, they must multiply, this takes time (incubation), symptoms appear when numbers are large.

    Why: Each step of the multiplication chain is a separate mark.

  4. In a passage question, use the line numbers given and add your own knowledge.

    Why: Marks often need an idea from the passage turned into biology.

  5. For screening or tick tables, choose only infections that are caused by pathogens and can travel in blood.

    Why: Extra ticks lose marks.

Do not lose marks here

  • ✗
    Giving 'wash hands' and 'good hygiene' as two separate precautions; they are one idea.
  • ✗
    Saying cholera spreads by 'dirty water' without the link to faeces from an infected person when the question asks how it is transmitted.
  • ✗
    Explaining delayed symptoms as 'the virus is spreading around the body' instead of multiplying to large numbers.
  • ✗
    Ticking genetic or deficiency conditions (cystic fibrosis, anaemia, scurvy) as infections to test blood for.
  • ✗
    Suggesting vaccination or antibiotics for every disease, even where they do not apply: antibiotics do not treat Ebola or HIV (viruses) or athlete's foot (a fungus).

Common mistakes: Inferred, not measured. Inferred from the official schemes' credit and rejection patterns; not measured student-error statistics.

Worked explanation

  1. 'State three', 3 marks: three different precautions, one per line.
  2. How does Ebola spread? Through body fluids of infected people and dead bodies.
  3. Barrier 1: protect health workers with a physical barrier, such as protective clothing, gloves, masks and goggles.
  4. Barrier 2: keep sick people apart from healthy people by isolating them.
  5. Barrier 3: stop spread from dead bodies by burying them safely.
  6. Check: the three lines are different ideas, not the same idea repeated.
Model answer · written by olevels.com, not an official Pearson mark scheme

1 Health workers wear protective clothing, gloves, masks and goggles.

2 Isolate (quarantine) infected people in a separate area away from healthy people.

3 Bury people who have died from Ebola safely, without touching the body.

How the marks are earned

One mark per different precaution: protective clothing or masks; keeping infected people isolated; safe burial. Other creditworthy ideas are avoiding contact with body fluids and disinfecting surfaces. Answers about vaccination or antibiotics are not on the list.
