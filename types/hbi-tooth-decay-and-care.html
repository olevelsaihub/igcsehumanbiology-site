Knowledge card

Explain how tooth decay happens and justify ways to prevent it

Written
  • Bacteria in plaque on the teeth feed on sugar left in the mouth.
  • The bacteria respire the sugar and release acid (lactic acid).
  • The acid dissolves the enamel, then the dentine, making a hole (cavity).
  • Molars decay most because their grooves and ridges trap food, they have a large surface, and they are hard to reach with a toothbrush.
  • Prevention: eat less sugar, brush regularly with fluoride toothpaste, floss, visit the dentist, fluoride in water.
  • Fluoride and calcium (with phosphate) make enamel stronger and more resistant to acid. Fluoride may also help to neutralise acid. Alcohol in mouthwash kills bacteria and so reduces plaque.
  • Calcium and phosphate are minerals taken in from a balanced diet. They build hard enamel, which resists decay.
  • Reading data: a region where fewer people visit the dentist is likely to have more decay. A fall in decay over many years may come from better brushing, fluoride toothpaste or water, more dental visits or less sugar in the diet.

Before you begin

  • Tooth structure: enamel, dentine, pulp.
  • Bacteria respire and can produce acid.
  • Acids react with minerals.
1

The method

  1. Start with sugar or food left on the teeth.

    Why: Food staying on teeth is the first link.

  2. Bacteria use the sugar in respiration.

    Why: Bacteria and their respiration are marks.

  3. They make acid.

    Why: Acid production is a mark.

  4. The acid dissolves enamel.

    Why: The damage step is the final mark.

  5. For prevention, give each method with its reason (fluoride strengthens enamel, alcohol kills bacteria).

    Why: Explain questions credit the reason, not just the method.

  6. For a data question, name the region or year first, then give one reason linked to dental care or diet.

    Why: The reason mark is often given only if the region or year is stated.

Do not lose marks here

  • ✗
    Saying sugar itself dissolves the teeth.
  • ✗
    Saying bacteria 'eat the teeth'.
  • ✗
    Saying molars decay because of 'cusps' alone, which some schemes ignore or reject; say ridges, grooves or crevices.
  • ✗
    Giving 'brush teeth' twice in different words for two methods.
  • ✗
    Saying fluoride kills bacteria.

Common mistakes: Inferred, not measured. Inferred from the official schemes' credit and rejection patterns; not measured student-error statistics.

Worked explanation

  1. Four marks, so four linked steps.
  2. Step 1: bacteria on the teeth feed on sugar left from food.
  3. Step 2: they use it in respiration for energy.
  4. Step 3: this produces lactic acid.
  5. Step 4: the acid dissolves the enamel.
Model answer · written by olevels.com, not an official Pearson mark scheme

Bacteria in plaque feed on sugar from food left on the teeth. They use the sugar in respiration to release energy. This produces lactic acid. The acid dissolves the enamel of the tooth, causing decay.

How the marks are earned

One mark each for: bacteria using food or sugar on the teeth, using it for respiration, making lactic acid, and the acid dissolving enamel.
