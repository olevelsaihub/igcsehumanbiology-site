Written

In the glomerulus, high pressure forces small molecules out of the blood by ultrafiltration. In the proximal tubule, all the glucose is reabsorbed.

4

The method

  1. Pressure

    Narrower vessel leaving, so high pressure.

  2. Out and in

    Small molecules forced out; name some.

  3. Stays in blood

    Proteins and blood cells.

  4. Glucose

    All reabsorbed; if too much, some stays in urine.

  5. Protein in urine

    A large molecule, so the glomerulus is damaged.

Do not lose marks here

  • ✗
    Protein filtered

    Proteins are too large to be filtered.

  • ✗
    Glucose lost

    Normally all of it is reabsorbed.

  • ✗
    "Pressure" with no reason

    Say the leaving vessel is narrower.

Worked explanation

  1. Vessel A brings blood into the glomerulus inside the Bowman's capsule.
  2. Vessel B takes blood out. In the diagram B is narrower than A.
  3. Blood enters faster than it can leave, so the blood pressure inside the glomerulus rises.
  4. This high pressure forces small molecules out of the blood into the capsule.
Model answer · written by olevels.com, not an official Pearson mark scheme

Vessel A carries blood into the glomerulus in the Bowman's capsule. Vessel B, which carries blood away, has a narrower lumen than A. This creates a high blood pressure in the glomerulus, and this pressure forces small molecules such as water, glucose and urea out of the blood into the capsule.

How the marks are earned

One mark for A bringing blood to the glomerulus or capsule; one for B being narrower; one for the higher blood pressure; one for this forcing molecules out.
