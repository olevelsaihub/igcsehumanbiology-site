Knowledge card

Explain how biceps, triceps and tendons move the arm

Written
  • Muscles can only pull (contract); they cannot push.
  • The biceps and triceps are an antagonistic pair: when one contracts, the other relaxes.
  • To raise (bend) the forearm, the biceps contracts and the triceps relaxes.
  • To lower (straighten) the forearm, the triceps contracts and the biceps relaxes.
  • A contracting muscle gets shorter and fatter.
  • Tendons attach muscles to bones. They are inelastic, so the whole pull reaches the bone.
  • The biceps pulls on the radius, and the forearm turns at the elbow, which is a hinge joint.
  • Key words for arm passages: antagonistic pair, hinge joint (elbow), biceps contracts to raise, triceps contracts to straighten, tendons attach muscle to bone, cartilage allows smooth movement, the muscle pulls.

Before you begin

  • Bones of the arm: humerus, radius, ulna.
  • Respiration supplies energy for muscle contraction.
  • The elbow is a hinge joint.
1

The method

  1. Decide the movement: raising (bending) or lowering (straightening) the forearm.

    Why: This decides which muscle contracts.

  2. Say which muscle contracts and which relaxes.

    Why: Each is a separate mark.

  3. Use the word 'antagonistic' for the pair.

    Why: The scheme gives a mark for the antagonistic idea.

  4. Say the contracting muscle pulls on the tendon, which does not stretch, so the bone (radius) is pulled.

    Why: The tendon and bone marks need this link.

  5. For shape changes, say shorter and fatter (contracting) or longer and thinner (relaxing).

    Why: One mark per shape change.

Do not lose marks here

  • ✗
    Saying the triceps 'pushes' the arm down.
  • ✗
    Saying both muscles contract at the same time.
  • ✗
    Saying tendons are elastic or join bone to bone.
  • ✗
    Mixing up which muscle contracts to raise the forearm.
  • ✗
    Saying the muscle pulls on the humerus to raise the forearm.

Worked explanation

  1. The diagram shows the biceps on the front of the upper arm and the triceps on the back. The question asks how they raise the forearm.
  2. To raise the forearm the biceps contracts. That is mark 1.
  3. At the same time the triceps relaxes. That is mark 2.
  4. They work against each other: they are an antagonistic pair. That is mark 3.
  5. The biceps pulls on its tendon, which pulls the radius (forearm) upwards. That is mark 4.
Model answer · written by olevels.com, not an official Pearson mark scheme

The biceps contracts and becomes shorter, while the triceps relaxes. The two muscles work as an antagonistic pair, with one contracting while the other relaxes. The biceps pulls on its tendon, which does not stretch, so the radius in the lower arm is pulled upwards and the forearm bends at the elbow.

How the marks are earned

One mark for the biceps contracting, one for the triceps relaxing, one for the antagonistic idea and one for the lower arm (radius) being pulled up.
