Pearson Edexcel · International GCSE Human Biology · 4HB1

Explain an enzyme practical result, including levelling off as substrate runs out

Observations such as falling pH, a positive sugar test, a clearing film or a plateau in product, explained by digestion and by substrate becoming limiting.

Type 10 Nutrition and energy HBI-ENZYME-RESULT-EXPLAIN

★★★★☆ · 7 of 36 eligible examination papers, 2019–2025. Difficulty: Medium.

Two or three linked steps are needed, from the enzyme to the visible result. Uses standard enzyme, substrate and product pairs. No calculation; tables are read only for trends. Each paper uses a different practical (film, Visking tubing, indicator, gas), so the set-up must be understood first. Combines digestion knowledge with reading practical data.

The method

  1. Enzyme

    Name it, even if the question does not.

  2. Substrate

    Say what it digests.

  3. Product

    Name it exactly, such as fatty acids.

  4. Link

    Join the product to the observation.

  5. Levelling off

    Substrate used up, so the rate falls.

Do not lose marks here

  • ✗
    Wrong product

    Lipase makes fatty acids, not glucose.

  • ✗
    Enzyme used up

    It is the substrate that runs out.

  • ✗
    Starch moves out

    Only small sugar passes through tubing.

Worked explanation

  1. The question asks why the pH falls over 25 hours. It is worth 3 marks, so I need three linked ideas.
  2. Which enzyme is in the tube? Lipase. What does it act on? Olive oil, which is a lipid.
  3. What does lipase make? Fatty acids and glycerol.
  4. Why does that change the pH? Fatty acids are acids, so as more are made the mixture becomes more acidic and the pH goes down.
  5. Check: enzyme and substrate (1), product (1), link to pH (1). Three marks covered.
Model answer · written by olevels.com, not an official Pearson mark scheme

Lipase breaks down the olive oil (a lipid) into fatty acids and glycerol. The fatty acids are acidic, so as more of them are made the solution becomes more acidic and its pH falls.

How the marks are earned

One mark for saying lipase digests the oil, one for naming fatty acids as a product, and one for saying that these acids make the pH go down. Glycerol is not needed for a mark, but it does no harm.
