How a spirometer records breathing, describing its use to measure tidal volume, and recognising tidal volume, vital capacity and residual volume or exercise changes on a trace.

Knowledge card

Use a spirometer and identify lung volumes on its trace

Written

A spirometer trace shows tidal volume in small waves, vital capacity in the biggest wave and residual volume below the lowest point.

1

Read the trace

Say it first, then tap the ? to check.

4 readings to find

Name each part of the trace.

  1. Tidal volume
  2. Vital capacity
  3. Residual volume
  4. Inspiratory reserve volume

Tidal volume

Write it as
One normal breath. Read top and bottom, then subtract: 2.4 − 2.0 = 0.4 dm³.

Exam tipNot the one big breath.

Vital capacity

Write it as
The full height of the biggest wave: 3.25 − 1.0 = 2.25 dm³.

Residual volume

Write it as
Air that always stays in the lungs.

Inspiratory reserve volume

Write it as
The extra air breathed in above a normal breath.

What does each part do or show?

  1. All air via the mouthpiece
  2. Absorbs carbon dioxide
  3. Draws the trace
  4. Deeper and faster

All air via the mouthpiece

Write it as
The nose clip makes sure all air goes through the mouthpiece.

Absorbs carbon dioxide

Write it as
Soda lime absorbs the carbon dioxide breathed out.

Draws the trace

Write it as
The chamber rises and falls; the pen draws on the rotating drum.

Deeper and faster

Write it as
Peaks higher, troughs lower, and peaks closer together.

Exam tipSay peaks closer together.

4

The method

  1. Match letters

    Small waves tidal; biggest wave vital capacity.

  2. Read a volume

    Top of arrow minus bottom.

  3. Describe the method

    Mouthpiece, chamber, pen, drum, nose clip, soda lime.

  4. Exercise

    Give depth and how close the peaks are.

Do not lose marks here

  • ✗
    Big breath is tidal

    Tidal is a normal breath.

  • ✗
    Top only

    Subtract the bottom.

  • ✗
    Faster, no peaks

    Say peaks closer together.

  • ✗
    No nose clip

    Add nose clip and soda lime.

Worked explanation

  1. W is a small arrow on one normal breath. A normal breath is tidal volume, so tidal volume is W.
  2. W runs from 2.0 dm3 at the trough to 2.4 dm3 at the peak. 2.4 minus 2.0 is 0.4 dm3.
  3. Z is the long arrow on the one very deep breath in and out. That is vital capacity, so the letter is Z.
  4. Z runs from 1.0 dm3 at the lowest point to 3.25 dm3 at the highest point. 3.25 minus 1.0 is 2.25 dm3.
  5. I check: X and Y are only part of the deep breath, so they are not vital capacity.
Model answer · written by olevels.com, not an official Pearson mark scheme

Tidal volume: letter W, volume 0.4 dm3. Vital capacity: letter Z, volume 2.25 dm3.

How the marks are earned

One mark for each correct letter (W, Z) and one for each correct volume (0.4 and 2.25 dm3). Using X or Y for vital capacity loses the letter mark, because each shows only the extra breath in or out, not the whole range.

Calculation check

  • round(2.4-2.0,2) = 0.4
  • round(3.25-1.0,2) = 2.25
