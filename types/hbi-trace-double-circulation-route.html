Knowledge card

Trace the route of blood through the heart, lungs and body

Written
  • Route: body, vena cava, right atrium, right ventricle, pulmonary artery, lungs, pulmonary vein, left atrium, left ventricle, aorta, artery to an organ.
  • The heart is a double pump: the right side pumps deoxygenated blood to the lungs; the left side pumps oxygenated blood to the body.
  • Blood passes through the heart twice in one complete circuit of the body.
  • Organ vessels: hepatic vein leaves the liver; renal artery enters the kidney and renal vein leaves it; hepatic portal vein runs from the gut to the liver.
  • Arteries carry blood away from the heart and veins carry blood towards the heart.
  • The heart, not the liver, pumps blood. The liver has no muscular chambers; its jobs include detoxification, digestion (bile) and homeostasis.
  • Every organ gets some blood, including the liver, kidneys and lungs, so total blood flow is more than the flow to any short list of organs.
  • The left ventricle has a thick muscular wall and pumps blood to the body. When it contracts, artery pressure reaches the systolic value of about 120 mmHg.

Before you begin

  • Names of the four heart chambers and the main vessels.
  • Oxygenated and deoxygenated blood.
  • Where the liver and kidneys are and what they do.
1

The method

  1. Write down the start and the end point.

    Why: You need to know where to begin and stop the list.

  2. Follow the fixed route, writing every vessel and chamber in order.

    Why: Each structure in the right place earns a mark.

  3. Do not skip the lungs or the heart chambers.

    Why: A missing step breaks the order and loses marks.

  4. Count the boxes or blanks to check you have the right number of items.

    Why: Flow charts tell you how many structures they want.

  5. For a double pump, give both sides with what they pump and where to.

    Why: Schemes credit each side separately, plus the 'twice per circuit' idea.

  6. For a model diagram, name which vessels are arteries (aorta, pulmonary artery) and which are veins (vena cava, pulmonary vein), and say arteries take blood away from the heart while veins bring it back.

    Why: Marks come from naming each vessel and pairing it with its direction of flow.

Do not lose marks here

  • ✗
    Going from the liver straight to the kidney with no trip through the heart and lungs.
  • ✗
    Writing pulmonary vein before pulmonary artery.
  • ✗
    Drawing arrows on only one side of the heart.
  • ✗
    Saying the double pump means 'two hearts' or 'pumps twice per second'.
  • ✗
    Putting 'atrium' where the sentence needs 'ventricle'.

Common mistakes: Inferred, not measured. Inferred from the official schemes' credit and rejection patterns; not measured student-error statistics.

Worked explanation

  1. The start is the liver and the end is the kidney. The first vessel, hepatic vein, is given.
  2. There are five empty boxes, so I need five vessels.
  3. The hepatic vein joins the vena cava, which goes to the right side of the heart.
  4. The right ventricle pumps blood to the lungs in the pulmonary artery.
  5. Blood comes back from the lungs in the pulmonary vein to the left side of the heart.
  6. The left ventricle pumps it into the aorta. The renal artery branches off the aorta to the kidney.
Model answer · written by olevels.com, not an official Pearson mark scheme

hepatic vein, then vena cava, then pulmonary artery, then pulmonary vein, then aorta, then renal artery

How the marks are earned

One mark for each of the five vessels in its right place: first the vena cava, next the pulmonary artery, then the pulmonary vein, then the aorta, and last the renal artery. A vessel in the wrong position does not score.
