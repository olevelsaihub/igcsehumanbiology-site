Describe how to carry out a food test, including DCPIP for vitamin C

Writing the method for Benedict's, iodine, biuret, emulsion or DCPIP tests on foods, urine, plasma or tubing contents, including safe conduct and a DCPIP comparison of two juices.

Type 4 Biological molecules HBI-FOOD-TEST-PROCEDURE

★★★★★ · 13 of 36 eligible examination papers, 2019–2025. Difficulty: Medium.

Ordered recall of steps with a link from colour to conclusion. Four tests with different reagents and colours must be kept apart. No calculation; DCPIP comparison only counts drops. Samples change: plasma, urine, yoghurt, meat, tubing contents. Combines practical method, safety and fair testing.

Benedict's, heat: to brick red

Method
Heat in a water bath; blue turns green, yellow, orange or brick red.

Exam tipNot a naked flame.

Iodine: to blue-black

Method
Add iodine solution; brown turns blue-black.

Biuret: to lilac

Method
Add biuret; blue turns lilac. Do not heat.

Ethanol, then water

Method
Shake with ethanol, then add water; milky emulsion.

The method

  1. Name the test

    Right reagent for the nutrient.

  2. Prepare

    Test tube; grind solids and add water.

  3. Key action

    Heat, shake with ethanol, or drop by drop.

  4. Result

    Positive colour, and what negative looks like.

  5. Safe and fair

    Goggles, water bath, same volumes, repeat.

Do not lose marks here

  • ✗
    Heating biuret

    Do not heat biuret or iodine.

  • ✗
    Wrong colours

    Protein is lilac, not red.

  • ✗
    Water before ethanol

    Ethanol first.

  • ✗
    Volumes differ

    Keep DCPIP volume the same.

Worked explanation

  1. The question asks how to test plasma for protein. The protein test uses biuret solution.
  2. First I need the sample: put some plasma into a clean test tube.
  3. Then add biuret reagent. No heating is needed.
  4. Then give the result: the blue colour turns lilac or purple.
  5. Finally say what this means: protein is present. I can add the negative result too: it stays blue if there is no protein.
Model answer · written by olevels.com, not an official Pearson mark scheme

Put about 2 cm3 of the plasma into a clean test tube. Add an equal volume of biuret reagent and shake gently; do not heat. If the blue colour changes to lilac (purple), this shows that protein is present in the plasma. If the solution stays blue, there is no protein.

How the marks are earned

Mark 1: the plasma sample goes into a test tube. Mark 2: biuret reagent is added. Mark 3: the colour change from blue to lilac or purple. Mark 4: this colour means protein is present (or staying blue means none). The last mark is linked to the colour change mark.
