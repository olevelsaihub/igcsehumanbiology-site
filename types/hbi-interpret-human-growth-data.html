Worked explanation

  1. The question asks how the chart is used, so I describe a process, not one reading.
  2. First the baby must be weighed, not once but regularly, at different ages.
  3. Each mass is plotted on the chart and compared with the percentile lines for girls of the same age.
  4. A healthy baby keeps roughly following one percentile line.
  5. A concern is shown if the baby moves across several lines, gaining or losing too much mass.
Model answer · written by olevels.com, not an official Pearson mark scheme

The baby is weighed at regular intervals, for example every few weeks. Each mass is plotted against age on the chart, so it is compared with other baby girls of the same age. A healthy baby's points should stay along one percentile curve. If the points cross the curves, showing the baby is putting on far too much mass or losing mass, this shows a concern about its growth.

How the marks are earned

One mark for regular weighing; one for following a percentile curve or comparing with others of the same age on the chart; one for spotting too much gain or loss of mass.
