Knowledge card

Identify reproductive organs from their roles or positions

Written
  • The testes make sperm by meiosis; the epididymis stores sperm before it leaves.
  • The sperm duct (vas deferens) carries sperm from the testis to the urethra. The urethra carries both urine and semen out through the penis.
  • The seminal vesicles and the prostate gland add the fluid part of semen, which helps sperm swim.
  • The ovaries make and release ova (eggs). The oviduct (fallopian tube) carries the ovum to the uterus and is where fertilisation happens.
  • The embryo implants in the uterus lining. The muscular uterus wall contracts to push the baby out at birth.
  • The cervix is the ring of muscle at the neck of the uterus; it widens (dilates) during birth. Sperm are deposited in the vagina during intercourse.
  • Do not mix up the ureter (kidney to bladder) with the urethra (bladder to outside).
  • Some tables mix organs and hormones. Progesterone keeps the uterus lining thick during pregnancy, oxytocin makes the uterus muscle contract at birth, and FSH makes follicles mature.
  • Sperm are haploid cells. Even after a vasectomy they are still made in the testes, so an X for 'where haploid cells are made' goes on a testis.

Before you begin

  • Gametes (sperm and ova) are haploid and are made by meiosis.
  • Fertilisation is the joining of a sperm nucleus and an egg nucleus to form a zygote.
  • The urinary system: kidney, ureter, bladder and urethra.
1

The method

  1. Read one job at a time and say to yourself which organ does it.

    Why: Each row is marked on its own, so one careful row at a time protects every mark.

  2. Find that organ in the box or on the diagram. Follow the label line to its end before choosing a letter.

    Why: Label lines often cross; the letter is only right if the line ends on the correct organ.

  3. Copy the word from the box exactly, with correct spelling.

    Why: Examiners need the exact organ name; a misspelt word can look like a different organ (ureter and urethra).

  4. Remember a letter or word can be used more than once, or not at all, if the question says so.

    Why: Testes both make sperm and contain cells dividing by meiosis, so the same answer can earn two marks.

  5. Check every row is filled before moving on.

    Why: A blank row scores zero; a sensible guess may score.

Do not lose marks here

  • ✗
    Writing 'ureter' when the tube that carries urine and sperm is the urethra.
  • ✗
    Saying fertilisation happens in the uterus; it happens in the oviduct.
  • ✗
    Saying sperm are stored in the testis or the prostate; storage is in the epididymis.
  • ✗
    Choosing the cervix or uterus as the place where sperm are deposited instead of the vagina.
  • ✗
    Writing an organ name in a row that asks for a hormone (or the reverse) when the box mixes structures and hormones, for example 'uterus' for 'causes contractions during birth' instead of oxytocin.

Common mistakes: Inferred, not measured. Inferred from the official schemes' credit and rejection patterns; not measured student-error statistics.

Worked explanation

  1. Row 1, 'duct that carries urine and sperm': the shared tube through the penis is the urethra, not the ureter.
  2. Row 2, 'produces sperm': sperm are made by meiosis in the testes.
  3. Row 3, 'where fertilised ovum is implanted': the embryo sinks into the thick lining of the uterus.
  4. Row 4, 'produces the fluid part of semen': the box gives seminal vesicle (the prostate is not in this box), so I choose seminal vesicle.
  5. Row 5, 'where sperm is deposited during intercourse': the penis places sperm in the vagina.
  6. Row 6, 'where fertilisation happens': sperm meet the ovum in the oviduct.
  7. Row 7, 'releases ova': ova are released from the ovary at ovulation.
  8. Check: epididymis and vulva were not needed, which is allowed.
Model answer · written by olevels.com, not an official Pearson mark scheme

duct that carries urine and sperm: urethra

produces sperm: testes

where fertilised ovum is implanted: uterus

produces the fluid part of semen: seminal vesicle

where sperm is deposited during intercourse: vagina

where fertilisation happens: oviduct

releases ova: ovary

How the marks are earned

Seven marks, one per row, each for the single correct organ name. Ureter for row 1 or uterus for row 6 would score nothing.
