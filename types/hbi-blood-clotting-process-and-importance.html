At a wound platelets release an enzyme, prothrombin becomes thrombin, and soluble fibrinogen becomes insoluble fibrin. The fibrin mesh traps cells to form a clot.

Worked explanation

  1. The question asks me to describe blood clotting. It is worth 5 marks, so I need at least five steps in order.
  2. Step 1: a vessel is damaged and the platelets there are damaged too. They release an enzyme.
  3. Step 2: this enzyme, with calcium ions, changes prothrombin into thrombin.
  4. Step 3: thrombin changes soluble fibrinogen into insoluble fibrin.
  5. Step 4: fibrin threads make a mesh.
  6. Step 5: the mesh traps red blood cells and this forms the clot.
  7. I use the word 'release', not 'secrete', for the enzyme.
Model answer · written by olevels.com, not an official Pearson mark scheme

When a blood vessel is cut, platelets collect at the wound and are damaged. The damaged platelets release an enzyme called thrombokinase. With calcium ions (Ca2+) present, this enzyme turns prothrombin into thrombin. Thrombin then changes soluble fibrinogen into insoluble fibrin. The fibrin threads form a mesh across the cut. Red blood cells are trapped in the mesh, and this forms a clot.

How the marks are earned

Marks are given for: an enzyme (thrombokinase or thromboplastin); its release from damaged platelets; prothrombin becoming thrombin; fibrinogen becoming fibrin; a mesh forming; red cells caught in it making the clot; and calcium ions. Any five earn full marks. The word 'secrete' is rejected for the enzyme.
