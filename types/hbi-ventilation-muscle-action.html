Explain how diaphragm and intercostal muscles drive breathing

Written

To breathe in, the intercostal muscles and diaphragm contract. The volume of the thorax rises, the pressure falls, and air is drawn in.

Worked explanation

  1. Six marks, so I need six separate steps.
  2. Muscles first: the intercostal muscles contract and the diaphragm contracts.
  3. Movements: the ribs move up and out; the diaphragm flattens. I avoid writing 'moves down'.
  4. Volume: these movements make the thorax bigger.
  5. Pressure: bigger volume means lower pressure in the lungs than outside.
  6. Air: air is drawn in from outside because of the pressure difference.
Model answer · written by olevels.com, not an official Pearson mark scheme

The intercostal muscles between the ribs contract, which pulls the ribs upwards and outwards. The diaphragm contracts and flattens. Together these increase the volume of the thorax. This lowers the pressure inside the thorax and lungs so it is below the pressure of the air outside. Air is therefore drawn into the lungs from outside, down the pressure difference.

How the marks are earned

One mark each for: the diaphragm flattening; the ribs moving up and out; contraction of the intercostal muscles; a rise in thorax volume; a fall in pressure in the thorax or lungs; and air being drawn or forced in from outside. 'Moves down' for the diaphragm is refused, and a bare 'air flows in' is not enough.
