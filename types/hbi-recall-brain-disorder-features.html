Recalling or extracting from a passage or graph the symptoms, causes, risk factors and evidence for Alzheimer's, Parkinson's, dementia, depression and schizophrenia.

4

The method

  1. Name the condition

    Alzheimer's, Parkinson's and mental illness differ.

  2. Read "other than"

    Do not repeat the excluded symptom.

  3. One idea per mark

    Two wordings of one idea score once.

  4. Use the lines

    Take facts from the passage, in your own words.

Do not lose marks here

  • ✗
    Swapped diseases

    Tremor is Parkinson's, not Alzheimer's.

  • ✗
    Same idea twice

    Confusion and speech problems count as one.

  • ✗
    Copying the passage

    Answer the question, do not copy long sentences.

Written-answer check

After your attempt, see the full worked explanation

Worked explanation

  1. The question asks for two symptoms of Alzheimer's disease, other than memory loss. It is worth 2 marks.
  2. Memory loss is not allowed, so I must not write 'forgetting things' either.
  3. I think of other Alzheimer's symptoms: problems with speaking and finding words, confusion, repeating the same actions, losing thinking ability, changes in social behaviour.
  4. Speech problems, confusion and repeated actions count as one idea on the scheme, so I take only one of them. My second symptom must come from a different idea, such as losing control of muscles you move on purpose.
Model answer · written by olevels.com, not an official Pearson mark scheme

1. Difficulty with language, for example trouble speaking or finding the right words.

2. Gradual loss of control of voluntary muscles, so everyday movements such as walking or dressing become hard.

How the marks are earned

One mark for each different accepted symptom, up to two. Language problems, confusion and repeated actions are grouped as ONE idea, so two of them earn only one mark. Other separate ideas are loss of voluntary muscle control, loss of intellectual ability and worse social behaviour. Memory loss is excluded by the question.
