Explain the liver's roles in deamination and breaking down drugs

Deamination of surplus amino acids to urea, why kidney patients limit protein, the liver's large blood supply, and why liver damage lets alcohol or medicines build up.

Explain the liver's roles in deamination and breaking down drugs

Written

Extra amino acids cannot be stored, so the liver deaminates them and makes urea for the kidneys to excrete. The liver also breaks down drugs and alcohol.

The method

  1. Start with protein

    Digested to amino acids; extra ones cannot be stored.

  2. Process and place

    Deamination in the liver.

  3. Product and route

    Urea, in the blood, to the kidneys, out in urine.

  4. Use the context

    Less protein, less urea; damaged liver, drug builds up.

Worked explanation

  1. Protein in food is digested to amino acids.
  2. Extra amino acids cannot be stored. In the liver they are deaminated.
  3. The amine group becomes urea.
  4. Less protein eaten means less urea made.
  5. A dialysis patient's kidneys do not work, so less urea means less has to be removed by the machine, and urea does not build up to toxic levels.
Model answer · written by olevels.com, not an official Pearson mark scheme

Protein is digested into amino acids. Any extra amino acids are broken down in the liver by deamination, and the nitrogen part is turned into urea. If the person eats less protein, less urea is produced. Their kidneys cannot remove urea, so less urea means there is less waste to be removed by dialysis, and urea does not reach a high, toxic concentration in the blood between treatments.

How the marks are earned

Marks for: protein broken into amino acids; extra amino acids deaminated; urea formed; less protein gives less urea; less urea needs removing; a high level of urea is toxic. Any four.
