Predict how a drug, toxin or disease changes synaptic transmission

Using the synapse model to explain effects of plaques, enzyme inhibitors, curare, alcohol, or too little or too much dopamine or acetylcholine.

Type 17 Coordination HBI-APPLY-SYNAPSE-TO-DRUG-OR-DISEASE

★★★☆☆ · 6 of 36 eligible examination papers, 2019–2025. Difficulty: Difficult.

Several steps of reasoning from an unfamiliar drug to a body effect. Needs enzymes, receptors and synapse steps used together. No arithmetic is needed. Each question uses a new drug, poison or disease. Combines synapse knowledge, enzyme action and disease facts.

Knowledge card

Predict how a drug, toxin or disease changes synaptic transmission

Written

A drug or disease changes one step of the synapse: release, diffusion, binding or breakdown. Say what that does to the impulses.

1

Which step goes wrong?

Decide first, then tap the ? to check.

3 steps to find

Say what changes at the synapse before you tap.

  1. Blocks the receptors
  2. Acetylcholine not broken down
  3. Impulses restart

Blocks the receptors

Write it as
Curare blocks receptors on the post-synaptic membrane, so acetylcholine cannot bind.

Exam tipNot "stops the muscle" alone.

Acetylcholine not broken down

Write it as
More acetylcholine stays in the gap and competes with curare for the receptors.

Exam tipIt does not break down plaques.

Impulses restart

Write it as
Acetylcholine binds, so impulses pass to the muscles again.

3 steps to find

Which step is affected in each?

  1. Plaques and fewer synapses
  2. Less dopamine
  3. Fewer impulses pass

Plaques and fewer synapses

Write it as
Plaques cover receptors and neurones die, so less acetylcholine acts and fewer impulses pass.

Less dopamine

Write it as
Dopamine neurones are destroyed, so fewer impulses reach muscles: shaky, stiff, slow movement.

Fewer impulses pass

Write it as
A depressant: less neurotransmitter is released and bound, so reactions are slower.

Exam tipAlcohol does not speed up the nervous system.

4

The method

  1. Pick the step

    Release, diffusion, binding or breakdown?

  2. More or less

    Does more or less neurotransmitter reach the receptors?

  3. Link to impulses

    More, fewer or no impulses in the next neurone.

  4. End on the body

    Name the effect: memory, tremor, breathing, reactions.

Do not lose marks here

  • ✗
    Symptoms only

    Always mention the synapse or neurotransmitter.

  • ✗
    Wrong direction

    Less transmitter gives fewer impulses, not more.

  • ✗
    Inhibitor "kills"

    It stops the breakdown of acetylcholine.

Written-answer check

After your attempt, see the full worked explanation

Worked explanation

  1. The question says curare blocks receptors on the post-synaptic membrane. It asks why an inhibitor of acetylcholinesterase can reverse this. It is worth 2 marks.
  2. Acetylcholinesterase is the enzyme in the gap that breaks down acetylcholine. An inhibitor stops this enzyme.
  3. So the first idea is: acetylcholine is not broken down, and more of it stays in the gap.
  4. Curare and acetylcholine both try to fit the same receptors. With more acetylcholine around, it can compete with curare and bind to the receptors.
  5. So the second idea is: acetylcholine can now bind to the receptors and start impulses to the muscles again.
Model answer · written by olevels.com, not an official Pearson mark scheme

The inhibitor stops acetylcholinesterase from breaking down acetylcholine, so more acetylcholine builds up in the gap. This extra acetylcholine can compete with curare for the receptor sites on the post-synaptic membrane, so more acetylcholine binds and impulses can pass to the breathing muscles again.

How the marks are earned

One mark for saying the breakdown of acetylcholine (the neurotransmitter) is stopped. One mark for saying acetylcholine can then compete with curare and bind to the receptors.
