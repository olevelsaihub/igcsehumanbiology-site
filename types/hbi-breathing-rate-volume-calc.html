Knowledge card

Calculate breathing rate or air volume from trace or table

Calculation
  • Breathing rate is the number of breaths per minute. Count full breaths (peak to peak) over a known time, then scale to 60 seconds.
  • Volume breathed per minute = tidal volume x number of breaths per minute.
  • 1 dm3 = 1000 cm3. Divide cm3 by 1000 to get dm3.
  • To find a percentage of a value, multiply by the percentage and divide by 100. Do one percentage at a time.
  • On a trace, use the scale bar: work out how many squares equal 1 dm3 or 20 s before reading anything.
  • To find the difference between two levels on a trace, count the squares between them and divide by the number of squares that equal 1 dm3.

Before you begin

  • Tidal volume and breathing rate definitions
  • Percentages
  • Unit conversion between cm3 and dm3

The method

  1. Read the scale first: how many squares are 1 dm3 and how many seconds each square is.

    Why: A wrong scale makes every later number wrong.

  2. Take the right values from the right column or point, for example the row for the stated carbon dioxide percentage.

    Why: The working mark is only given for the correct values.

  3. Write the calculation in steps, one operation per line.

    Why: If the final answer is wrong, you can still get working marks (error carried forward).

  4. Convert units only at the end and check the answer line's unit.

    Why: Units such as dm3 versus cm3 are a common place to lose the final mark.

  5. Check the size: a resting person breathes about 12 to 20 times a minute.

    Why: A silly result shows a slip in reading the trace.

  6. For a volume difference between two points, count the squares between the two levels, then divide by the squares per 1 dm3.

    Why: The scheme gives separate marks for the squares counted, the scale used and the final volume.

Do not lose marks here

  • ✗
    Counting peaks and troughs both as breaths, so the rate is doubled.
  • ✗
    Using the wrong row of the table, for example 3.00% instead of 1.60% carbon dioxide.
  • ✗
    Taking 20% of 20% as 40% or 4% instead of applying each in turn.
  • ✗
    Giving the answer in cm3 when the answer line asks for dm3.
  • ✗
    Forgetting to scale a count over 30 seconds up to one minute.
  • ✗
    Leaving the answer as 19 200 when the answer line asks for dm3; divide cm3 by 1000.

Common mistakes: Inferred, not measured. Inferred from the official schemes' credit and rejection patterns; not measured student-error statistics.

Worked explanation

  1. I find the 1.60% carbon dioxide column: tidal volume 700 cm3, rate 15 breaths per minute.
  2. Volume breathed in per minute = 700 x 15 = 10 500 cm3.
  3. The air is 20% oxygen: 10 500 x 20 / 100 = 2100 cm3 of oxygen breathed in per minute.
  4. The body uses 20% of this oxygen: 2100 x 20 / 100 = 420 cm3.
  5. The answer line is in cm3, so no conversion is needed.
Model answer · written by olevels.com, not an official Pearson mark scheme

Volume of air per minute = 700 x 15 = 10 500 cm3. Oxygen in this air = 10 500 x 20/100 = 2100 cm3. Oxygen used = 2100 x 20/100 = 420 cm3 per minute.

How the marks are earned

One mark for finding 20% of the per-minute air volume, one for finding 20% of that oxygen volume, and one for the final 420 cm3. A wrong per-minute volume can still earn the later marks if the method is right.

Calculation check

  • 700 × 15 = 10500
  • 700 × 15 × 20 ÷ 100 = 2100.0
  • 700 × 15 × 20 ÷ 100 × 20 ÷ 100 = 420.0
