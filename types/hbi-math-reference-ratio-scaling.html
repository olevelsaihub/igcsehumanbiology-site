Scale per-unit or reference quantities and combine them

A content per 100 g, per kg, per unit or per pot is given; amounts for another mass are found, combined, or used to find how much is needed.

Type 18 Maths skills HBI-MATH-REFERENCE-RATIO-SCALING

★★★★☆ · 8 of 36 eligible examination papers, 2019–2025. Difficulty: Difficult.

Several members need a plan: scale two foods, add, then subtract from a target. Proportional reasoning with a reference base. Several steps with decimals, sometimes standard form. Labels, passages and tables differ each time. Combines table reading, scaling, combining and rounding.

5

The method

  1. Reference

    Write the amount and its base.

  2. One unit

    Divide by the base to get 1 g.

  3. Scale

    Multiply by the new mass.

  4. Combine and round

    Add or subtract parts; give the unit.

Do not lose marks here

  • ✗
    Using 6.1 for any mass

    Scale it to the new mass.

  • ✗
    No dividing by 100

    That gives 518.5.

  • ✗
    Mixed units

    Convert mg and g first.

Worked explanation

  1. The protein row gives 6.1 g per 100 g of yoghurt. The 'per 85 g pot' cell is empty, so I must work it out.
  2. Protein in 1 g of yoghurt = 6.1 / 100 = 0.061 g.
  3. Protein in an 85 g pot = 0.061 x 85 = 5.185 g.
  4. Two significant figures: 5.185 rounds to 5.2 g.
  5. Check: 85 g is a bit less than 100 g, so the answer should be a bit less than 6.1 g. 5.2 g fits.
Model answer · written by olevels.com, not an official Pearson mark scheme

6.1 / 100 = 0.061 g per gram. 0.061 x 85 = 5.185 g. Mass of protein = 5.2 g (2 s.f.).

How the marks are earned

One mark for dividing 6.1 by 100. One mark for multiplying by 85. One mark for 5.2 g. If the answer is left as 5.18 the third mark is lost, because the question asks for two significant figures.

Calculation check

  • 6.1 ÷ 100 = 0.061
  • 6.1 ÷ 100 × 85 = 5.185
