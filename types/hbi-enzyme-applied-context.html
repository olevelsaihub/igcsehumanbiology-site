Apply digestive enzyme action to an unfamiliar situation

Novel contexts where digestion, or its absence, changes what is absorbed: oral hormones, PKU, chymosin, lactase loss.

★★☆☆☆ · 4 of 36 eligible examination papers, 2019–2025. Difficulty: Difficult.

Knowledge card

Apply digestive enzyme action to an unfamiliar situation

Written
  • Large molecules (proteins, lipids, starch, disaccharides like lactose) must be digested into small soluble molecules before they can be absorbed.
  • Proteases in the stomach and small intestine break protein into amino acids, which are absorbed into the blood.
  • Lipase breaks lipids down; a lipid-based hormone swallowed as a pill may be digested before it can act.
  • Lactase breaks lactose into glucose and galactose. Without lactase, lactose stays in the gut and is not absorbed.
  • Turning milk protein into a solid (as chymosin does) keeps it in the stomach longer, giving more time for digestion.
  • Less of a substrate in the diet means less of its product is released into the blood.
  • Chymosin is needed only by young children because their diet is mostly or entirely milk. Older people eat solid food, so they no longer need it.
  • A lipid hormone such as natural oestrogen, taken as a pill, is broken down in the small intestine, so it is not absorbed into the blood in its active form.

Before you begin

  • Digestive enzymes, their substrates and products.
  • Absorption of small soluble molecules in the small intestine.
  • Proteins are made of amino acids.
1

The method

  1. Identify the type of molecule (protein, lipid, carbohydrate) in the context.

    Why: This tells you which enzyme acts on it.

  2. Name the enzyme and the product.

    Why: Marks are given for the enzyme and the product.

  3. Say where this happens (stomach, small intestine).

    Why: Location is often a separate mark.

  4. Decide if the molecule is digested or stays whole, and link size to absorption.

    Why: Too large to absorb, or digested and inactive, are the consequence marks.

  5. Finish with the effect on the body named in the passage.

    Why: Ties the chain back to the question.

Do not lose marks here

  • ✗
    Saying low-protein diet means 'less phenylalanine' with no link to protein digestion.
  • ✗
    Saying lactose cannot be absorbed because it is 'not soluble' instead of too large.
  • ✗
    Ignoring the passage and writing unrelated facts.
  • ✗
    Saying the hormone is 'destroyed by acid' when the passage says it is a lipid digested by lipase.
  • ✗
    Stopping at the enzyme without the consequence for absorption.

Common mistakes: Inferred, not measured. Inferred from the official schemes' credit and rejection patterns; not measured student-error statistics.

Worked explanation

  1. People with PKU cannot break down phenylalanine, which is an amino acid.
  2. Where do amino acids come from? Digestion of protein.
  3. Which enzyme? Protease, in the stomach and small intestine.
  4. So less protein eaten means less protein digested by protease, so fewer amino acids, including less phenylalanine, enter the blood.
  5. Three marks: enzyme action, product, location, and less phenylalanine. I will give all four.
Model answer · written by olevels.com, not an official Pearson mark scheme

In a low-protein diet there is less protein for protease enzymes to digest in the stomach and small intestine. So fewer amino acids are produced and absorbed, including less phenylalanine. This stops phenylalanine building up in the blood and brain.

How the marks are earned

Marks are given for: less protein digested by protease, the product being amino acids, the site (stomach or small intestine), and the reduced phenylalanine. Any three of these four give full marks.
