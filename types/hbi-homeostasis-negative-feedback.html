Knowledge card

Explain homeostasis and negative feedback

Written
  • Homeostasis means keeping the internal environment of the body constant, even when the outside conditions change.
  • Examples are body temperature, blood glucose concentration and the water content of the blood.
  • In negative feedback, a change away from the normal level is detected, and a response happens that reverses the change.
  • When the level is back to normal, the response is switched off.
  • Body temperature is kept near 37 °C because enzymes work best there. Too hot denatures enzymes; too cold makes them work slowly.
  • For temperature, the hypothalamus is the detector; for blood glucose, the pancreas detects the change.
  • In temperature control, nerve impulses go from the hypothalamus to effectors such as sweat glands, arterioles in the skin and muscles (shivering).

Before you begin

  • Enzymes have an optimum temperature and can be denatured
  • Insulin and glucagon
  • Thermoregulation responses
1

The method

  1. For a definition, use three ideas: constant or stable, internal environment, despite external changes.

    Why: Each idea is one mark.

  2. For negative feedback, use the example given: the change, the detector, the response, the return to normal.

    Why: Schemes want the example, not a general rule only.

  3. Say the response stops (or reverses) once the level is normal.

    Why: Switching off is a separate mark in glucose questions.

  4. For importance, link temperature to enzymes: optimum, denatured if too hot, slow if too cold.

    Why: All three marks in that question are about enzymes.

  5. In a longer homeostasis definition, also mention that it works by negative feedback.

    Why: A reference to negative feedback can be an extra mark in a 4-mark definition.

Do not lose marks here

  • ✗
    Defining homeostasis as 'keeping the body the same' without 'internal environment'.
  • ✗
    Describing negative feedback as 'when something goes wrong' with no reversal back to normal.
  • ✗
    Saying enzymes 'die' or 'are killed' at high temperature; say denatured.
  • ✗
    Forgetting to say the response stops once the level is normal.
  • ✗
    Mixing up which hormone is released for high or low glucose.

Common mistakes: Inferred, not measured. Inferred from the official schemes' credit and rejection patterns; not measured student-error statistics.

Worked explanation

  1. The diagram: if glucose falls, hormone Y (glucagon) is released; if it rises, hormone Z (insulin) is released.
  2. I pick one direction: blood glucose falls below normal.
  3. Insulin release is reduced and glucagon is released, so the liver converts glycogen to glucose.
  4. Blood glucose rises back to the normal level.
  5. Then glucagon release stops and insulin secretion starts again. The response reversed the change.
Model answer · written by olevels.com, not an official Pearson mark scheme

Negative feedback means a change from the normal level causes a response that brings it back. In the diagram, if the blood glucose level falls too low, less insulin (hormone Z) is released and glucagon (hormone Y) is released. The liver converts glycogen into glucose, so blood glucose increases until it reaches the normal level again. Then glucagon is stopped and insulin secretion starts again.

How the marks are earned

One mark each for: a low blood glucose level as the starting change; reduced insulin release; glucose rising until the normal level is reached; glucagon stopping or insulin starting again. The reverse argument starting from high glucose is also accepted.
