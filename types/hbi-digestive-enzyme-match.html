Knowledge card

Match digestive enzymes to substrate, product and site

Written
  • Amylase (a carbohydrase) digests starch into maltose. It is made in the salivary glands and the pancreas and works in the mouth and small intestine.
  • Maltase digests maltose into glucose in the small intestine.
  • Proteases digest protein into amino acids. The stomach makes pepsin, which works in acid (pH about 2); the pancreas makes trypsin, which works in the small intestine. So proteases are found in the stomach, pancreas and small intestine.
  • Lipase digests lipids into fatty acids and glycerol. It is made in the pancreas and works in the small intestine.
  • Salivary amylase works best at about pH 7. If the pH is too low or too high, digestion slows.
  • An enzyme only digests a substrate whose shape fits its active site.
  • An enzyme that works best at a low pH (about 2 to 4) is most likely to come from the stomach, because the stomach is acidic.
  • In a milk practical with sodium hydrogencarbonate and phenolphthalein, the enzyme is lipase: fatty acids lower the pH and the pink colour disappears.

The method

  1. Decide which food group is involved: starch, protein or lipid.

    Why: Every answer follows from the right group.

  2. Name the enzyme, then the product of that exact step.

    Why: Amylase makes maltose; maltase makes glucose. Skipping a step can lose the mark.

  3. For the site, use where it is made (gland) or where it works (region of gut), whichever is asked.

    Why: 'Produced' and 'found' have different correct answers.

  4. For a 'where' explanation, give the place and its condition (stomach, low pH).

    Why: Two marks: the region and the reason.

Do not lose marks here

  • ✗
    Writing 'lipase turns fat into glucose'.
  • ✗
    Saying bile is an enzyme or the gall bladder makes enzymes.
  • ✗
    Naming 'protein' as an enzyme instead of protease.
  • ✗
    Saying amylase is made in the stomach.
  • ✗
    Giving only one product of lipid digestion when two are asked for.

Common mistakes: Inferred, not measured. Inferred from the official schemes' credit and rejection patterns; not measured student-error statistics.

Worked explanation

  1. The substrates are proteins, so enzyme P must be a protein-digesting enzyme: a protease.
  2. Which protein fits? The active site has a round notch next to a triangle notch.
  3. Protein Q has a round unit joined to a triangle; protein R has a round unit joined to a square. So protein Q fits.
  4. Proteases break protein into amino acids.
  5. Fill the gaps: proteases, protein Q, amino acids.
Model answer · written by olevels.com, not an official Pearson mark scheme

Enzyme P belongs to a group of enzymes called proteases. This enzyme breaks down protein Q in food to amino acids.

How the marks are earned

One mark each for proteases, protein Q and amino acids. Writing 'protein P' for the substrate is refused.
