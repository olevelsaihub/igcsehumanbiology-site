Judge whether data support a claim and spot their limits

Health and investigation data where the student decides if evidence supports a claim, why an estimate is uncertain, which result is anomalous, or why data are unreliable or incomplete.

Knowledge card

Judge whether data support a claim and spot their limits

Written
  • A correlation means two things change together. It does not prove that one causes the other.
  • Other factors, such as age, gender, diet, genes or lifestyle, can also change the result, so they must be controlled or recorded.
  • A small sample, or a sample that does not represent everyone, gives results that may not be reliable.
  • Body measurements such as kidney filtration rate go up and down, so one or two readings make a weak estimate.
  • An anomalous result is one value that does not fit the pattern of the other values.
  • To judge a claim, first say what the data show (with a figure), then say what the data cannot show.
  • A body measurement only shows a problem once it passes the healthy limit. If every reading is already beyond that limit, the data cannot show when the problem began.
  • A prediction made by extending a trend is uncertain when readings go up and down. If the values got better for a while before, they could get better again, so the future value may be different.

Before you begin

  • How to read values from a table and a graph with two y-axes
  • The meaning of reliable, valid, sample and control variable
  • The biology of the context, for example what urea and creatinine are

The method

  1. Read the claim or statement carefully and underline what it says will happen.

    Why: Every mark is about whether the data match this claim, so you must know exactly what is being tested.

  2. Find the data that test the claim and quote one or two real figures with units.

    Why: Supporting marks need evidence from the table or graph, not a general opinion.

  3. Say what the data cannot show: other factors, small or unrepresentative sample, values that vary, missing time points, an anomaly.

    Why: Limitation marks are often half the marks, and examiners want a named problem, not just 'it is not reliable'.

  4. For an anomaly, name the exact value and say it does not follow the pattern of the others.

    Why: One mark is for picking the right value and one is for the reason.

  5. End with a short judgement: the data support, partly support or do not support the claim.

    Why: 'Discuss' asks you to reach a view, and the judgement ties your points together.

  6. For a 'when did it start' or 'how accurate is this estimate' question, compare the readings with the healthy value and look for rises and falls in the pattern.

    Why: Marks come from noticing that the first reading is already abnormal, or that the values fluctuate, so a firm date or prediction is not possible.

Do not lose marks here

  • ✗
    Writing 'the graph shows glyphosate causes kidney disease' because both lines go up; a link is not proof of cause.
  • ✗
    Saying only 'the results are not reliable' without naming why, such as a small number of people tested.
  • ✗
    Describing the trend again when the question asks about the strength of the evidence.
  • ✗
    Naming the anomalous result without saying it does not fit the pattern of the other results.
  • ✗
    In a 'discuss' answer, giving only points for the claim and never reaching a judgement.
  • ✗
    Giving a year when the kidneys stopped working, even though every GFR value in the table is already below the healthy level of 100.

Common mistakes: Inferred, not measured. Inferred from the official schemes' credit and rejection patterns; not measured student-error statistics.

How each command word is answered

Command wordTimes askedWhat to write
Discuss2Give both sides: what in the data agrees with the claim and what limits it, then give a conclusion.
Give2Write one short reason per mark, for example 'sample size too small'. No long explanation is needed.
Describe1Say what extra information is needed, for example data about other lifestyle factors such as diet or exercise.
Explain2Give the point and then the reason, for example name the anomalous value and say why it is anomalous.
State1One short answer only, such as 'too few people were tested'.

What a complete response includes

Marks split between supporting points from the data and limiting points such as sample size, other variables, variability or missing data.

Worked explanation

  1. The claim is: muscle breakdown happens at a constant rate and diet does not change it.
  2. I look at the table. Urea is 14.7 g on the high protein diet but only 2.2 g on the low protein diet. Uric acid and ammonia also change a lot. So these come mostly from protein in the food.
  3. Now creatinine: 0.58 g on the high protein diet and 0.60 g on the low protein diet. The difference is only 0.02 g, so it is almost the same.
  4. Creatinine is the substance that could come from muscle breakdown. It stays almost the same whatever the diet.
  5. So the table supports the statement. I write the comparison with figures and then the conclusion.
Model answer · written by olevels.com, not an official Pearson mark scheme

The masses of urea, uric acid and ammonia are very different for the two diets. For example urea is 14.7 g on the high protein diet but only 2.2 g on the low protein diet, so these depend on what the person eats. Creatinine is different: its mass is almost the same, 0.58 g and 0.60 g, in both diets. Creatinine is likely to be produced when muscle tissue is broken down. Because creatinine output stays the same on both diets, the table supports the statement that muscle breakdown happens at a steady rate that diet does not change.

How the marks are earned

One mark for saying the urea, uric acid and ammonia values change between the diets. One mark for picking out creatinine. One mark for saying its mass is the same or nearly the same, and one for making clear this is true in both diets. Linking creatinine to muscle breakdown is another creditable point, so the answer has more than four points. An answer that talks only about urea scores little, because urea comes from dietary protein.

Calculation check

  • round(0.60-0.58,2) = 0.02
