Knowledge card

Describe transcription and translation in human cells

Written
  • A gene is a length of DNA whose base sequence codes for one protein.
  • Transcription happens in the nucleus: the DNA of the gene unwinds and an mRNA copy is made by complementary base pairing.
  • The mRNA leaves the nucleus and goes to a ribosome in the cytoplasm.
  • Translation happens at the ribosome: each codon (three bases) on the mRNA is read.
  • Each tRNA carries one particular amino acid and has an anticodon that pairs with a complementary codon.
  • Amino acids brought by tRNA are joined by peptide bonds in the order set by the codons, forming a polypeptide that folds into a protein.
  • If a gene is 'switched off', it is not transcribed, so no mRNA and no protein are made.
  • When all the amino acids are joined, the finished polypeptide (protein) is released from the ribosome.
  • On a low-protein diet, fewer amino acids reach the cells, so translation at the ribosomes slows and less protein (or incomplete protein) is made.

Before you begin

  • The difference between DNA and RNA.
  • Complementary base pairing, with uracil replacing thymine in RNA.
  • Ribosomes are in the cytoplasm; DNA stays in the nucleus.
1

The method

  1. Split the answer into two named stages: transcription (in the nucleus) and translation (at the ribosome).

    Why: Each stage name, with its place, earns marks.

  2. In transcription, say mRNA is made as a complementary copy of the DNA.

    Why: The mark is for mRNA being formed from the DNA, not just 'DNA is copied'.

  3. Say mRNA moves out of the nucleus to a ribosome.

    Why: The movement links the two stages and is a specification point.

  4. In translation, use the words codon, tRNA, anticodon, amino acid, peptide bond.

    Why: Each correct term used in context is a separate marking point.

  5. Finish with the product: a polypeptide or protein, named if the question gives one.

    Why: The final step, amino acids joined into a protein, is always a mark.

Do not lose marks here

  • ✗
    Saying the DNA leaves the nucleus and goes to the ribosome.
  • ✗
    Mixing up transcription and translation, or putting translation in the nucleus.
  • ✗
    Saying the ribosome carries the amino acids, instead of tRNA.
  • ✗
    Pairing the anticodon with DNA instead of the mRNA codon.
  • ✗
    Writing that amino acids are 'made' at the ribosome; they are joined there.

Common mistakes: Inferred, not measured. Inferred from the official schemes' credit and rejection patterns; not measured student-error statistics.

Worked explanation

  1. The question asks how the host cell makes viral proteins. The cell uses its normal protein synthesis, so I describe that.
  2. It is worth 5 marks, so I plan at least five points in order.
  3. Stage 1, transcription: in the nucleus, the gene is copied as mRNA by complementary base pairing.
  4. Stage 2: the mRNA leaves the nucleus and attaches to a ribosome.
  5. Stage 3, translation at the ribosome: tRNA molecules bring amino acids.
  6. Each tRNA anticodon pairs with a matching mRNA codon, so amino acids line up in the right order.
  7. The amino acids are joined by peptide bonds to make the viral protein.
Model answer · written by olevels.com, not an official Pearson mark scheme

First, transcription happens in the nucleus. The DNA of the gene unwinds and a molecule of mRNA is built as a complementary copy of the base sequence. The mRNA moves out of the nucleus to a ribosome in the cytoplasm. Then translation takes place at the ribosome. tRNA molecules bring specific amino acids to the ribosome. The anticodon on each tRNA pairs with the complementary codon on the mRNA, so the amino acids are placed in the correct order. The amino acids are joined together by peptide bonds to form a polypeptide, which becomes the new viral protein.

How the marks are earned

Credit is given for: naming transcription; mRNA as its product; naming translation at the ribosome; tRNA being involved; tRNA anticodons pairing with mRNA codons; amino acids being put together into the viral protein. The answer has all six ideas for five marks.
