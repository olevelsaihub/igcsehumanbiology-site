Knowledge card

Read an enzyme optimum from data and judge how reliable it is

Written

The optimum is where the enzyme works fastest: the highest rate, or the shortest time. Gaps between values and no repeats make it uncertain.

3 readings to find

How reliable is the optimum?

  1. Highest rate at 40 °C
  2. Could lie between values
  3. Smaller steps, repeats

Highest rate at 40 °C

Write it as
The optimum is about 40 °C, with the unit.

Could lie between values

Why
Values are 10 °C apart, so the true optimum may be 35 or 45 °C.

Exam tipNot "exactly" 40 °C.

Smaller steps, repeats

Write it as
Test every 2 °C around 40 °C and repeat each test.

4

The method

  1. Check the data

    Rate or product: highest. Time taken: lowest.

  2. Read the value

    Quote the pH or temperature with its unit.

  3. Compare

    Quote data for both enzymes.

  4. Evaluate

    Gaps, repeats and fair tests.

  5. Improve

    Smaller steps around the peak.

Do not lose marks here

  • ✗
    Longest time

    A long time means a slow reaction.

  • ✗
    No unit

    Write °C, or say pH.

  • ✗
    Exactly a tested value

    The optimum may lie between values.

  • ✗
    One enzyme only

    A comparison needs both enzymes.

Worked explanation

  1. The table gives time taken to digest starch at pH 2, 4, 6, 8 and 10.
  2. The data are times, not rates. The enzyme works fastest when the time is shortest.
  3. Times: pH 2 = 60 min, pH 4 = 45 min, pH 6 = 20 min, pH 8 = 35 min, pH 10 = 55 min. The shortest is 20 minutes.
  4. So the optimum pH is 6.
Model answer · written by olevels.com, not an official Pearson mark scheme

pH 6

How the marks are earned

One mark for pH 6, the pH with the shortest digestion time (20 minutes). Choosing pH 2 (the longest time) shows the student has read time as if it were rate.

Calculation check

  • min([60, 45, 20, 35, 55]) = 20
