Use genetic terms: alleles, genotypes, carriers and blood-group genotypes

Defining and using gene, allele, dominant, recessive, homozygous, heterozygous, genotype, phenotype and codominance; listing the genotypes for a blood group; and explaining how carriers pass on a recessive condition.

Type 6 Reproduction and heredity HBI-DEFINE-GENETIC-TERMS

★★★★★ · 10 of 36 eligible examination papers, 2019–2025. Difficulty: Easy.

Mostly recall; carrier and inheritance parts need a short two-step link (masking, one allele from each parent). Several abstract terms plus multiple alleles must be kept distinct. None. The method

  1. Spot the term

    Name the exact idea being tested.

  2. Add the qualifier

    "Only when two copies are present" or "both expressed".

  3. Two marks

    Give the condition and the result.

  4. Blood groups

    List every genotype, using I symbols.

  5. Carriers

    Heterozygous, masked, two recessive alleles needed.

Do not lose marks here

  • ✗
    Allele as chromosome

    An allele is a version of a gene.

  • ✗
    Weaker or overpowers

    Write masked.

  • ✗
    Missing IO

    Group A is IA IA or IA IO.

  • ✗
    Genotype or phenotype

    Pp is the genotype, heterozygous.

Written-answer check

After your attempt, see the full worked explanation

Worked explanation

  1. The term is co-dominant and the context is ABO blood groups: IA and IB are co-dominant.
  2. Two marks, so I need two ideas.
  3. Idea 1, the condition: both different alleles are present in the genotype (IA IB).
  4. Idea 2, the result: both alleles show their effect equally, so the person is group AB.
  5. I must not say one allele hides the other.
Model answer · written by olevels.com, not an official Pearson mark scheme

When a person has two different alleles, both of them are present in the genotype (for example IA and IB). Both alleles are expressed equally in the phenotype, so neither hides the other; IA IB gives blood group AB.

How the marks are earned

One mark for the idea that both different alleles are present together; one mark for both being expressed (equally). Saying only 'both are dominant' would not earn the expression mark.
