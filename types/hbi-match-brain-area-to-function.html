Identify brain regions and match them to their functions

Labelling or locating the cerebral hemispheres, cerebellum, medulla, hypothalamus and pituitary, and pairing each with functions such as balance, memory, breathing or temperature control.

Knowledge card

Identify brain regions and match them to their functions

Written
  • Cerebral hemispheres (cerebrum, cerebral cortex): the large wrinkled top part. They control voluntary actions, conscious thought, intelligence, memory, language and the senses.
  • Cerebellum: the small wrinkled part at the back, under the cerebrum. It coordinates muscle movement and controls balance, posture and muscle tone.
  • Medulla (medulla oblongata): at the base of the brain, joining the spinal cord. It controls heart rate and breathing rate.
  • Hypothalamus: a small area above the pituitary. It monitors body temperature and the water content of the blood (osmoregulation), and controls sweating.
  • Pituitary gland: a small gland hanging below the hypothalamus. It releases hormones such as ADH into the blood.
  • The motor area of the cerebral hemispheres sends impulses to muscles to cause voluntary movements.
  • The pituitary gland is found in the brain, at its base. It belongs to the endocrine (hormone) system.
  • Alcohol can affect many brain areas, for example the cerebrum, cerebellum, hypothalamus or medulla. Any named brain area is a fair answer.

Before you begin

  • The central nervous system is the brain and spinal cord.
  • Homeostasis: keeping body temperature and water content constant.
  • Hormones are chemicals released by glands into the blood.
1

The method

  1. Picture the brain map: cerebrum on top, cerebellum at the back, medulla at the base above the spinal cord, hypothalamus and pituitary in the middle underneath.

    Why: Label marks need the line to touch the right area.

  2. Match each function to its part using key words: thought or voluntary to cerebrum, balance or posture to cerebellum, heart or breathing to medulla, temperature or water to hypothalamus, hormone release to pituitary.

    Why: Each correct pair scores one mark.

  3. When drawing a label line, end it clearly inside the correct area.

    Why: A line that ends between two areas is marked wrong.

  4. In tick tables, tick only the rows that are correct.

    Why: Ticking every box limits the marks you can get.

Do not lose marks here

  • ✗
    Mixing up cerebrum and cerebellum because the names look alike.
  • ✗
    Giving the pituitary for temperature control. The hypothalamus monitors temperature; the pituitary releases hormones.
  • ✗
    Putting the breathing control label on the cerebellum instead of the medulla.
  • ✗
    Label lines that stop outside the brain outline or between two parts.
  • ✗
    Ticking every row in a tick table.

Common mistakes: Inferred, not measured. Inferred from the official schemes' credit and rejection patterns; not measured student-error statistics.

Worked explanation

  1. The table has four functions. I must write one structure from part (a) for each. It is worth 4 marks, one per row.
  2. Muscle tone and posture are part of coordinating movement and balance. That is the cerebellum.
  3. Intelligence and thought happen in the large top part: the cerebral hemisphere.
  4. Monitoring body temperature is a homeostasis job: the hypothalamus.
  5. ADH is a hormone. Hormones from the brain area are released by the pituitary gland.
Model answer · written by olevels.com, not an official Pearson mark scheme

Row 1 (muscle tone and posture): cerebellum

Row 2 (intelligence and thought): cerebral hemisphere

Row 3 (body temperature): hypothalamus

Row 4 (ADH release): pituitary gland

How the marks are earned

One mark for each correct row. The cerebellum goes with posture, the cerebral hemisphere with thought, the hypothalamus with temperature and the pituitary with ADH. Writing hypothalamus for ADH release is wrong, because the pituitary releases it.
