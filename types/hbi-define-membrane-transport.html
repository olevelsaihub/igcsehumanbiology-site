Knowledge card

Define diffusion, osmosis and active transport

Written
  • Diffusion is the net movement of particles from where they are more concentrated to where they are less concentrated. It needs no energy from the cell.
  • Osmosis is the net movement of water only. Water crosses a partially permeable membrane from a higher water potential to a lower water potential.
  • Active transport moves particles from a lower concentration to a higher concentration. This is against the concentration gradient.
  • Active transport needs energy from respiration, in the form of ATP. Carrier proteins in the membrane move the particles.
  • Diffusion and osmosis are passive: the cell spends no ATP on them.
  • A dilute solution has a high water potential. A concentrated solution has a low water potential.
  • 'Partially permeable' and 'selectively permeable' mean the same. The speed of diffusion depends on the surface area to volume ratio of the cell.

Before you begin

  • Particles in liquids and gases move about at random.
  • The cell membrane controls what enters and leaves the cell.
  • Respiration releases energy and makes ATP.
1

The method

  1. Decide which process is named and write its name in your head: diffusion, osmosis or active transport.

    Why: Each process has its own key words, so naming it first stops you mixing definitions.

  2. Write the direction compared with the gradient: down the gradient (high to low) or against it (low to high).

    Why: Direction is the first mark in almost every scheme for this type.

  3. Add the special condition: water and a partially permeable membrane for osmosis; energy as ATP for active transport.

    Why: This second idea is what separates the three processes and earns the second mark.

  4. For a 3-mark active transport answer, add a third idea: ATP from respiration, or carrier proteins in the membrane.

    Why: Extra marks come from detail about the energy source or the membrane protein.

  5. In matching or cloze questions, check every line or gap against these key words, and use one line per process only.

    Why: More than one line from a process loses the mark.

Do not lose marks here

  • ✗
    Saying active transport moves particles 'along' or 'down' the gradient.
  • ✗
    Defining osmosis as the movement of particles or of a solute, instead of water.
  • ✗
    Leaving out the partially permeable membrane in an osmosis definition.
  • ✗
    Writing 'high to low' for active transport, or forgetting the energy (ATP) point.
  • ✗
    Writing 'from a high concentration gradient to a low concentration gradient' instead of comparing two concentrations.

Worked explanation

  1. The command word is 'Give' and the process is active transport. There are 3 marks, so I need three separate ideas.
  2. Idea 1 is direction: active transport moves molecules from a low concentration to a high concentration, so against the gradient.
  3. Idea 2 is energy: the cell must spend energy to do this.
  4. Idea 3 is where the energy comes from: ATP made in respiration.
  5. Idea 4, as a spare, is how it happens: carrier proteins in the membrane carry the molecules across. I will include it in case one idea is not credited.
Model answer · written by olevels.com, not an official Pearson mark scheme

Active transport is the movement of molecules across a cell membrane from a region of low concentration to a region of high concentration, which is against the concentration gradient. It needs energy, which comes from respiration in the form of ATP. Protein carriers in the cell membrane move the molecules across.

How the marks are earned

One mark for moving against the gradient (low to high). One mark for needing energy. One mark for naming ATP as the energy form. A further mark is available for mentioning the membrane proteins. Any three of these four reach full marks.
