Knowledge card

Draw or label the parts of a synovial joint

Written
  • A synovial joint is a freely movable joint between two bones.
  • Cartilage covers the end of each bone in the joint.
  • The synovial membrane lines the joint and makes synovial fluid.
  • Synovial fluid fills the space between the bones.
  • A tough fibrous capsule surrounds the joint, and ligaments join the two bones.
  • In a ball and socket joint (shoulder, hip) the rounded head of one bone sits in a cup-shaped socket of the other.
  • When asked to draw missing cartilage, draw a smooth layer over the end of each bone in the joint: one on the rounded end and one lining the hollow it fits into.

Before you begin

  • The names of the bones at the shoulder and elbow.
  • Functions of cartilage, ligaments and synovial fluid.
1

The method

  1. Draw the two bone ends first, with a small gap between them; label the bones.

    Why: Bone shape and labels give the first mark.

  2. Cover each bone end with a layer of cartilage and label it.

    Why: Cartilage must cover the ends of both bones.

  3. Fill the gap with synovial fluid and label it.

    Why: One mark for the fluid.

  4. Draw a lining (synovial membrane) and an outer capsule or ligament around the joint; label each.

    Why: Each labelled part is a mark.

  5. Use a sharp pencil, clear outlines and straight label lines that do not cross.

    Why: Some schemes give a mark for a neat, clear diagram.

Do not lose marks here

  • ✗
    Drawing cartilage on only one bone end.
  • ✗
    Mixing up synovial fluid (in the space) and synovial membrane (the lining).
  • ✗
    Labelling a tendon where the capsule or ligament should be.
  • ✗
    Drawing without labels, or with label lines that do not touch the part.
  • ✗
    Drawing a hinge-shaped joint when the joint shown is a ball and socket.

Common mistakes: Inferred, not measured. Inferred from the official schemes' credit and rejection patterns; not measured student-error statistics.

Worked explanation

  1. The synovial joint shown is at the top of the humerus, where it meets the shoulder. That is a ball and socket joint.
  2. So I draw the round head of the humerus fitting into a cup (the socket of the scapula), and label both.
  3. I draw a layer of cartilage over the ball and inside the socket, and label it.
  4. I fill the space between them with synovial fluid and label it.
  5. I draw the synovial membrane lining the joint, and a fibrous capsule with ligaments outside it. I label both.
  6. I keep lines clear and label lines straight, for the overall quality mark.
Model answer · written by olevels.com, not an official Pearson mark scheme

A clear labelled drawing showing: the rounded head of the humerus (ball) fitting into the socket of the scapula, labelled as bones; a layer of cartilage covering the ball and lining the socket, labelled 'cartilage'; the space between them filled and labelled 'synovial fluid'; a thin layer lining the joint labelled 'synovial membrane'; and a tough outer layer joining the two bones labelled 'capsule / ligament'. Outlines are smooth and label lines are ruled and touch the parts.

How the marks are earned

One mark each for: the ball and socket bones drawn and labelled; capsule or ligament labelled; cartilage labelled; synovial fluid labelled; synovial membrane labelled; a clear overall diagram.
