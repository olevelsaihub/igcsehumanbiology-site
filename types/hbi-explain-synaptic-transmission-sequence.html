Describe how an impulse crosses a synapse

Naming the synapse, defining a neurotransmitter, and sequencing transmission from an arriving impulse to a new impulse in the next neurone, including through a reflex arc.

Type 9 Coordination HBI-EXPLAIN-SYNAPTIC-TRANSMISSION-SEQUENCE

★★★☆☆ · 6 of 36 eligible examination papers, 2019–2025. Difficulty: Medium.

A long ordered sequence must be kept in the right order. Chemical transmission across a gap is an abstract idea for many students. No arithmetic is needed. Contexts change (dopamine, Parkinson's, a reflex arc) but the steps stay the same. Uses diffusion and membrane receptors from cell biology.

Worked explanation

  1. The question asks how an impulse gets from neurone A, across the gap, to neurone B. It is worth 6 marks, so I need at least six steps.
  2. Step 1: the synaptic knob of neurone A contains vesicles.
  3. Step 2: the vesicles hold a neurotransmitter, for example acetylcholine.
  4. Step 3: when the impulse arrives, the vesicles move to the pre-synaptic membrane and fuse with it.
  5. Step 4: the neurotransmitter is released into the gap (exocytosis).
  6. Step 5: it diffuses across the gap from high to low concentration.
  7. Step 6: it attaches to receptors on the post-synaptic membrane of neurone B.
  8. Step 7: this starts a new impulse in neurone B. I now have seven ideas for six marks, which is safe.
Model answer · written by olevels.com, not an official Pearson mark scheme

When the impulse reaches the end of neurone A, vesicles in the synaptic knob move towards the pre-synaptic membrane. The vesicles contain a neurotransmitter such as acetylcholine. They fuse with the membrane and the neurotransmitter is released into the gap by exocytosis. The neurotransmitter diffuses across the gap from a high concentration to a low concentration. It binds to receptor molecules on the post-synaptic membrane of neurone B. This triggers a new electrical impulse in neurone B.

How the marks are earned

Marks are given for: vesicles; the vesicles holding neurotransmitter (a named one is fine); vesicles moving to or fusing with the pre-synaptic membrane; release (exocytosis); diffusion across the gap; binding to receptors on the post-synaptic membrane; a new impulse. Any six of these seven give full marks. Saying the impulse itself crosses the gap gains nothing.
