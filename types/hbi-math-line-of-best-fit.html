Knowledge card

Draw a line or curve of best fit through plotted points

Written
  • A line of best fit shows the overall trend; it does not have to go through every point.
  • Decide from the pattern whether a straight line or a smooth curve fits better.
  • Draw one single thin line, with points spread evenly on both sides.
  • Ignore an anomalous point that clearly does not fit the pattern.
  • Do not join the points dot to dot, and do not draw beyond the first and last points unless asked.
  • A U-shaped or peaked pattern needs one smooth curve through the lowest or highest region, not two straight lines meeting at a point.

Before you begin

  • Plotting points on a grid.
  • Recognising anomalous results.
  • Straight and curved trends.
1

The method

  1. Look at all the points and decide if the pattern is straight or curved.

    Why: A straight line through curved data, or the reverse, loses the mark.

  2. Spot any anomalous point and leave it out.

    Why: Schemes ignore lines that miss an anomaly.

  3. Draw one smooth line (ruler for straight) with about as many points above as below.

    Why: This is what the mark is for.

  4. Stop the line at the first and last points.

    Why: Keeps the line to the data.

Do not lose marks here

  • ✗
    Joining each point to the next with straight lines.
  • ✗
    Forcing the line through an anomalous point.
  • ✗
    Drawing a straight line through data that clearly curve.
  • ✗
    Drawing a thick or double, sketchy line.

Common mistakes: Inferred, not measured. Inferred from the official schemes' credit and rejection patterns; not measured student-error statistics.

Worked explanation

  1. Look at the plotted pH values: 6.8, 6.4, 6.0, 5.8, 5.6, 5.2, 5.0, 4.8, 4.6, 4.6 from 1 to 10 minutes.
  2. The pH falls steadily, then the last two points are the same, so the line flattens a little at the end.
  3. None of the points is far from the pattern, so there is no anomaly to leave out.
  4. Draw one thin smooth line from the 1-minute point to the 10-minute point, with the points balanced on both sides.
Model answer · written by olevels.com, not an official Pearson mark scheme

One single, thin, smooth line drawn from 1 minute to 10 minutes that follows the falling pH, passing among the points with roughly equal numbers above and below it and flattening slightly at the end. It does not join the points dot to dot.

How the marks are earned

One mark for a single line that fits the trend of the points. A dot-to-dot line, or a line that ignores the overall pattern, does not get the mark.
