Knowledge card

Explain dietary needs for bone and muscle and describe osteoporosis

Written
  • Calcium (with phosphate) is built into bone and makes it hard and strong.
  • Vitamin D helps the small intestine absorb calcium from food.
  • Protein is needed to build muscle and to grow bone.
  • Milk, cheese and yoghurt contain calcium, protein and some vitamin D.
  • In osteoporosis, bones lose mass and become less dense, with larger spaces, so they are brittle and break easily.
  • Osteoporosis can cause fractures, back pain, a stooped posture and loss of height.
  • Causes of osteoporosis include old age, low oestrogen after the menopause, too little calcium or vitamin D in the diet, and too little weight-bearing exercise.

Before you begin

  • The nutrients in a balanced diet.
  • Structure of a long bone: compact and spongy bone.
  • Absorption of digested food in the small intestine.
1

The method

  1. Name each nutrient that matters: calcium, vitamin D, protein (and phosphate).

    Why: Each nutrient named earns a mark.

  2. Give the job of each nutrient straight after naming it.

    Why: The role is a separate mark from the name.

  3. Link vitamin D to calcium absorption from the gut, then calcium to hard, dense bone.

    Why: This chain carries two or three marks in longer answers.

  4. For osteoporosis, give the change to bone (less dense, porous) and then effects (fractures, pain, height loss, stoop).

    Why: Each different effect is one mark.

Do not lose marks here

  • ✗
    Saying vitamin D makes bones directly, without the calcium link.
  • ✗
    Naming calcium but not saying what it does.
  • ✗
    Forgetting protein for muscle.
  • ✗
    Saying osteoporosis makes bones 'softer' with no idea of density or breaking.
  • ✗
    Listing nutrients from a table without saying why they help bone.

Common mistakes: Inferred, not measured. Inferred from the official schemes' credit and rejection patterns; not measured student-error statistics.

Worked explanation

  1. The label shows the yoghurt contains calcium, vitamin D and protein. All three help bones.
  2. Calcium: it is laid down in bone, making it hard and dense.
  3. Vitamin D: it helps the gut absorb calcium.
  4. Protein: it is needed for growth of bone.
  5. There are 5 marks, so I name calcium and vitamin D, give both roles, add the strength effect, and add protein for a spare mark.
Model answer · written by olevels.com, not an official Pearson mark scheme

Yoghurt contains calcium and also vitamin D. Vitamin D helps the small intestine absorb calcium into the blood. The calcium is then laid down in the bones, forming hard compact bone. This makes the bones stronger and denser, so they are less likely to break. Yoghurt also contains protein, which is needed for bones to grow and develop.

How the marks are earned

Marks for: yoghurt contains calcium; it contains vitamin D; vitamin D lets calcium be absorbed from the intestine; calcium is stored in bone or forms compact bone; bones become stronger or denser; protein is needed for bone growth. Any five of the six.
