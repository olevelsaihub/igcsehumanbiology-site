Knowledge card

Describe virus structure and reproduction

Written
  • A virus is not a cell. It is a particle made of genetic material (DNA or RNA) inside a protein coat called a capsid.
  • Some viruses have protein spikes on the outside that attach to host cells.
  • An RNA virus has RNA as its genetic material and no DNA.
  • A virus can only reproduce inside a living host cell; it uses the cell's ribosomes and enzymes.
  • Reproduction steps: the virus attaches to a host cell; it injects its genetic material; the viral genes may join the host DNA and are copied; the cell makes viral proteins; new viruses are assembled; the cell bursts (lysis) and releases them to infect more cells.
  • Antibiotics do not affect viruses.

Before you begin

  • DNA and RNA are nucleic acids that carry genetic information.
  • Ribosomes make proteins in cells.
  • Viruses cause diseases such as Ebola, AIDS and hepatitis.
1

The method

  1. For structure, give the genetic material and the protein coat; add spikes if more marks are available.

    Why: Each part is one mark.

  2. For reproduction, start with attachment to a host cell.

    Why: The scheme gives the first mark for attachment.

  3. Follow the order: inject genetic material, copy it, make viral proteins, assemble new viruses, burst the cell, release.

    Why: Each step is a separate mark and the order shows understanding.

  4. Use the host cell as the factory in every step.

    Why: Saying the virus 'divides' by itself is wrong and earns no credit.

Do not lose marks here

  • ✗
    Calling a virus a cell, or saying it has a nucleus or cytoplasm.
  • ✗
    Saying a virus reproduces by dividing in two like a bacterium.
  • ✗
    Missing out the bursting (lysis) and release steps.
  • ✗
    Saying an RNA virus contains both DNA and RNA.

Common mistakes: Inferred, not measured. Inferred from the official schemes' credit and rejection patterns; not measured student-error statistics.

Worked explanation

  1. 'Describe the reproduction', 6 marks: I need six separate steps in order.
  2. Step 1: the virus attaches to a host cell (a liver cell).
  3. Step 2: it injects its DNA into the cell.
  4. Step 3: the viral DNA joins the host cell's DNA, and is copied when the host DNA is copied.
  5. Step 4: the cell makes viral proteins.
  6. Step 5: new viruses are assembled.
  7. Step 6 and 7: the cell bursts and the new viruses are released to infect more cells.
  8. Count: attach, inject, join, copy, proteins, assemble, burst, release. That is more than six.
Model answer · written by olevels.com, not an official Pearson mark scheme

The hepatitis B virus attaches to the surface of a liver cell, its host cell. It injects its DNA into the cell. The viral DNA joins into the host cell's own DNA. When the host cell copies its DNA, the viral DNA is copied as well. The cell's ribosomes then make viral proteins, such as coat proteins. These parts are put together to make many new viruses. Finally, the host cell bursts open (lysis), and the new viruses are set free to infect other cells.

How the marks are earned

One mark per step: attachment to the host cell; DNA injected; viral DNA joining host DNA; viral DNA copied with host DNA; protein synthesis; new viruses made; lysis of the cell; release of new viruses. Any six give full marks; the answer gives all eight.
