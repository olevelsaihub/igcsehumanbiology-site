Calculate an amount from a percentage, fraction or probability

A total population, volume or mass is given with a percentage, fraction or probability; the matching number, or the new value after a percentage rise or fall, is wanted.

Knowledge card

Calculate an amount from a percentage, fraction or probability

Calculation
  • To find a percentage of a total: amount = total x percentage / 100.
  • A fraction such as 'one in four' means total / 4, which is the same as 25%.
  • A probability is already a decimal: number expected = total x probability.
  • For a value after a rise of p%, multiply by (1 + p/100). For a fall of p%, multiply by (1 - p/100).
  • Keep the same units as the total, for example millions stay in millions.
  • Round only at the end, to the number of significant figures asked for.
  • If a group is split equally (for example equal numbers of males and females tested), halve the total before you take the percentage.
  • If you know the whole population and one group, find the other group by subtraction, e.g. women = 66.5 million - 33 million = 33.5 million.
  • One unit of alcohol is 10 cm3 of pure alcohol. Units = volume of drink x (% alcohol / 100) / 10.
  • A percentage change read from a graph works the same way: new mass = starting mass x (100 + change) / 100.

Before you begin

  • Changing percentages to decimals.
  • Working with millions and standard form.
  • Rounding to significant figures.
1

The method

  1. Find the total and the percentage, fraction or probability. Check lines in a passage if the question gives line numbers.

    Why: The method mark needs the correct values.

  2. Turn the percentage into a decimal by dividing by 100.

    Why: This makes the multiplication clear and is often shown in the scheme.

  3. If the value rises or falls, either use (1 + or - decimal), or find the change and then add or subtract it.

    Why: Many questions need the new total, not just the size of the change.

  4. Multiply the total by the decimal. Chain more steps if needed (for example volume of alcohol, then units).

    Why: Each correct step can earn a mark.

  5. Round to the required figures and give the unit or word (million, cm3, g).

    Why: Some schemes cap marks if the rounding is wrong.

Do not lose marks here

  • ✗
    Dividing the total by the percentage, for example 4600 / 30.
  • ✗
    Finding the size of the fall (1380 cm3) and giving that as the final value instead of 4600 - 1380.
  • ✗
    Forgetting to add the increase back on, giving 26 100 instead of 171 100.
  • ✗
    Using the wrong total, such as the whole population when only males are needed.
  • ✗
    Giving 64.5 when two significant figures (65) are asked for.

Common mistakes: Inferred, not measured. Inferred from the official schemes' credit and rejection patterns; not measured student-error statistics.

Worked explanation

  1. The total is 66.65 million. The percentage is 6.0%.
  2. The answer line says 'million', so I can keep working in millions.
  3. 1% of the population = 66.65 / 100 = 0.6665 million.
  4. 6% = 0.6665 x 6 = 3.999 million.
  5. Round sensibly: 4.0 million.
Model answer · written by olevels.com, not an official Pearson mark scheme

66.65 / 100 = 0.6665 million per 1%. 0.6665 x 6.0 = 3.999 = 4.0 million people.

How the marks are earned

One mark for finding 1% of the population (0.6665, or 0.67). One mark for multiplying by 6 to get 4.0 million (3.999 is also accepted).

Calculation check

  • 66.65 ÷ 100 = 0.6665000000000001
  • 66.65 ÷ 100 × 6.0 = 3.9990000000000006
