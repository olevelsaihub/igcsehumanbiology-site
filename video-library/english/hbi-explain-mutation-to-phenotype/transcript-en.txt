Idea. Your genes are made of DNA. A gene is a short length of DNA that codes for one protein. The code is a long row of bases: the four DNA letters A, T, C and G. Every three bases, called a triplet or codon, code for one amino acid. Amino acids are the small units that join to make a protein. The chain of amino acids folds into a protein with its own shape. The shape lets it do its job. Proteins build your characteristics. Your phenotype is what you are like: all your characteristics.

Method. Step one. A mutation is a change in the base sequence of a gene. Here, one base has changed. Step two. So that triplet changes. It may now code for a different amino acid. Or it may become a stop signal, so the protein is not made at all. Step three. The amino acids are in a different order, so the protein folds into a different shape. It may not work. An enzyme is a protein that speeds up a reaction. Its active site is the part where its substrate, the molecule it acts on, fits. After a mutation, the active site may no longer fit its substrate. Step four. A faulty or missing protein changes a characteristic. That is a change in phenotype. In sickle cell anaemia, one base change gives faulty haemoglobin, the protein that carries oxygen. The red blood cells become curved like a sickle.

Worked example. Here is a real exam question. People with polycystic kidney disease have cysts on their kidneys. Cysts are sacs of fluid. The disease is caused by a gene mutation. Explain how a gene mutation can affect the phenotype. Three marks. Start at the DNA. Link one: the base sequence of the gene changes. Link two: so a different order of amino acids is coded. Link three: the protein has a different shape, so it does not work. Link four: a characteristic changes. Here, cysts form in the kidneys. Here is the full answer: four links for three marks. This answer is by olevels.com, not an official mark scheme.

Common mistake. A common mistake is to jump from the DNA straight to the disease. You lose the middle marks. Always go through the amino acids and the protein. Another mistake is to say every mutation changes the phenotype. Some mutations have no effect. The new triplet may code for the same amino acid, or the protein may still work.

Your turn. Melanin is the dark colour in skin and hair. An enzyme makes it. A mutation in the gene for this enzyme causes albinism: very pale skin and hair. Explain how. Pause here and work it out.

Answer. The mutation changes the base sequence of the enzyme's gene. So a different order of amino acids is coded. The enzyme folds into a different shape. Its active site no longer fits its substrate. So the enzyme cannot make melanin. With no melanin, the skin and hair are very pale. That is the change in phenotype: albinism. So, every time: bases, then amino acids, then protein, then phenotype. Join each link with 'so'.
