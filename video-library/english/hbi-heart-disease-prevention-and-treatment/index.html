Read the narration

Heart disease is more likely with some lifestyles. The risks are too much saturated fat, salt and sugar in the diet, too little exercise, smoking, too much alcohol, obesity and stress. Obesity means being very overweight. So first decide what the question wants. Prevention means stopping the disease before it starts. Treatment means helping a heart that is already diseased.

For prevention, give a clear change with a direction. Eat less saturated fat and cholesterol. Eat less salt. Do more exercise. Stop smoking. Keep a healthy body mass. Cholesterol is a fatty substance in the blood that builds plaques in the arteries. Statins are drugs that lower the cholesterol in the blood. So fewer plaques build up. Some foods contain plant stanol esters, which cut the cholesterol absorbed from the gut. A stent is a tube made of mesh. A thin tube called a catheter carries it into the narrow artery. A balloon inflates and opens the stent. The stent holds the artery open, so more blood and oxygen reach the heart muscle. A bypass uses another blood vessel. It is joined on before and after the blockage. Blood flows round the blockage, to the heart muscle beyond it. A heart transplant has problems. A donor is the person who gives the heart. There are few donors. The tissue must match, or the body's immune system, which fights pathogens, attacks the new heart. This is called rejection. The donor heart may also be diseased, or the wrong size. Some questions give data from a study. A placebo is a tablet with no drug in it. Here, the statin and placebo scores are nearly the same. So the statins probably did not cause the symptoms.

Here is a real exam question. It starts with a passage about statins. Lines two and three say that statins are used when changes in diet and lifestyle have not worked. The question asks for those changes, for two marks. Two marks, so two separate changes. Change one, in the diet: eat less saturated fat, and fewer foods high in cholesterol, such as butter and fatty meat. Change two, in lifestyle: do more exercise. That is the full answer. Each change says what to do less of or more of. This explanation is by olevels.com, not an official mark scheme.

A common mistake is to write 'eat healthily'. That gets no mark. Say exactly what to eat less of, or what to do more of. Another mistake is to say a stent removes the fat. It does not. It holds the artery open, so more blood flows through. And remember, statins lower cholesterol, not blood pressure.

Your turn. Omar is fifty-five. He smokes, eats a lot of fried food and fatty meat, is very overweight, and never exercises. His blood cholesterol is high. Suggest three changes he could make. Then name a drug his doctor could give him, and say how it helps. Pause here and work it out.

Omar should stop smoking. He should eat less saturated fat, so less fried food and fatty meat. He should do more exercise, which also helps him lose mass. His doctor could give him statins. Statins lower his blood cholesterol, so fewer plaques form in his coronary arteries. Every time: decide if it is prevention or treatment. For prevention, give a change with a direction. For treatment, say what it does, then the effect on blood and oxygen.
