Idea. A virus is a tiny particle that can cause disease. It is much smaller than a bacterium. A virus is not a cell. It has no nucleus, no cytoplasm and no cell membrane. Inside is its genetic material, the instructions for making new viruses. This is DNA or RNA. An RNA virus has RNA and no DNA. Around it is a protein coat. Some viruses also have protein spikes, which attach to a host cell. A host cell is the living cell that a virus gets into.

Method. A virus can only reproduce inside a living host cell. Step one: the virus attaches to the host cell. Step two: it injects its genetic material into the cell. Step three: the viral genes may join the host cell's own DNA. When the host cell copies its DNA, the viral genes are copied too. Step four: the cell makes viral proteins. It uses its ribosomes, the tiny parts of a cell that make proteins, and its enzymes. Step five: the proteins and genes are put together into new viruses. This is called assembly. Step six: the cell bursts open. This is called lysis. Step seven: many new viruses are released. Each one can infect another cell.

Worked example. Now a real exam question. Hepatitis B is a virus that infects the liver. The question asks you to describe the reproduction of a hepatitis B virus. It is worth six marks, so give at least six steps, in order. The host is a liver cell. Point one: the virus attaches to the liver cell. Point two: it injects its DNA. Point three: the viral DNA joins the host cell's DNA, and it is copied when the host DNA is copied. Point four: the cell makes viral proteins. Point five: these parts are put together into new viruses. Point six: the cell bursts. Point seven: the new viruses are set free to infect other cells. Here is the full answer. Common mistake. A common mistake is to say a virus divides in two. That is how a bacterium reproduces. A virus cannot do anything on its own. It needs the ribosomes and enzymes of a living host cell. Antibiotics do not affect viruses. Another mistake is to give a virus cell parts, such as a nucleus or cytoplasm. A virus has only genetic material and a protein coat. And an RNA virus has RNA only, never both DNA and RNA. The last mistake is to stop after the new viruses are made. Finish the story: the cell bursts, and the new viruses are released.

Your turn. Flu is caused by an RNA virus. It infects the cells that line the airways. First, state what is meant by an RNA virus. Then describe how new flu viruses are made. Pause here and work it out.

Answer. Part a. An RNA virus has RNA as its genetic material and no DNA. Its RNA is inside a protein coat, with spikes that attach to cells. Part b. The flu virus attaches to an airway cell and injects its RNA. The RNA is copied. The cell makes viral proteins, and new viruses are assembled. The cell bursts, and the viruses are released. So, every time: for structure, give the genetic material and the protein coat. For reproduction, start with attachment to a host cell. Then go in order to the burst and the release.
