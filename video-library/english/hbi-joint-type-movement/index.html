Idea. A joint is a place where two or more bones meet. Joints let the skeleton move. Here are the two main types you need. On the left, a round ball fits into a cup, called a socket. On the right, two bones fit together like a door on its hinge. The type of joint decides how much it can move. A plane means one flat direction of movement, like a door that only swings open and shut.

Method. Step one: find the joint. The elbow and the knee are hinge joints. They bend and straighten in one plane only, backwards and forwards. They cannot rotate. The shoulder and the hip are ball and socket joints. They move in all three planes, and they can rotate, which means turn round in a circle. Two more you should know. In the wrist and ankle, small bones slide over each other: these are gliding joints. Hinge, ball and socket and gliding joints are all synovial joints: joints that move freely, with a fluid between the bones. Between the vertebrae, the small bones of the backbone, cartilage allows only a little movement. Step four: read what is asked. If it asks for a type, write hinge, or ball and socket, not elbow or shoulder. If it asks you to compare, describe both joints.

Worked example. Now a real exam question. The diagram shows two synovial joints. Joint A is at the hip: the round head of the thigh bone sits in a cup. Joint B is the knee. The question asks you to compare their range of movement. Two marks. First, name each type. A ball fits into a socket, so joint A is a ball and socket joint. Joint B bends one way only, so it is a hinge joint. Point one, for joint A. It moves in all three planes, and it can rotate. Point two, for joint B. It moves in one plane only, backwards and forwards. It cannot rotate. Here is the full answer. Notice that it says something about each joint, and uses the words plane and rotate. This answer is by olevels.com, not an official mark scheme.

Common mistake. A common mistake is to write shoulder when the question asks for a type of joint. Another is to say a hinge joint can rotate. It cannot. Also, describe both joints in a comparison. And a joint is where two bones meet, not where a muscle meets a bone.

Your turn. A dancer swings her arm round in a full circle at the shoulder. Then she bends her elbow. Name the type of each joint, and compare how they move. Pause here and work it out.

Answer. The shoulder is a ball and socket joint. It moves in all three planes and can rotate, so she can swing her arm in a circle. The elbow is a hinge joint. It moves in one plane only, so it just bends and straightens. So, every time: find the joint, name its type, then give the planes and rotation for each one.
