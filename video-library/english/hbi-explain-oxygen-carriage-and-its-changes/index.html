Read the narration

Red blood cells carry oxygen. They are full of haemoglobin, a red protein. In the lungs, haemoglobin joins with oxygen to make oxyhaemoglobin. In the lungs, oxygen joins on. Then the blood travels to the body tissues. There, oxyhaemoglobin lets go of its oxygen. The oxygen diffuses into the cells: it spreads from where there is a lot to where there is little. Cells use it for aerobic respiration, which uses oxygen to release energy from glucose.

The structure of a red cell suits its job. Give each feature, then 'so', then the benefit. It has no nucleus, so there is more room for haemoglobin. It is biconcave, which means dented in on both sides. This gives a large surface area for oxygen to diffuse. It is small and flexible, so it can squeeze through narrow capillaries. Its membrane, the outer layer of the cell, is thin, so oxygen has only a short distance to diffuse. Now changes. If less oxygen is carried, less oxygen reaches the respiring cells. So there is less aerobic respiration, and less energy is released. The person feels tired. Breathing rate and heart rate rise. The chain also works the other way. Training at high altitude, high up where the air has less oxygen, makes the body produce more red cells. So more oxygen reaches the muscles, there is more aerobic respiration, and more energy for running. Two special cases. In sickle cell anaemia, red cells become sickle shaped when oxygen is low. They have less surface area, and they can block capillaries, so less oxygen is carried. The septum is the wall between the two sides of the heart. A hole in the septum lets blood with little oxygen, from the right side, mix with blood from the left side. Blood going to the body carries less oxygen, so the person breathes faster.

Here is a real exam question. The photograph shows a group of red blood cells. Part two asks you to explain how the structure of the red cell suits its function, for four marks. Its job is to carry oxygen. Point one: it contains haemoglobin, which joins with oxygen. Point two: it has no nucleus, so there is more room for haemoglobin. Point three: its biconcave shape gives a large surface area for diffusion. Point four: it is small and flexible, so it fits through capillaries. Point five: its thin membrane makes the diffusion distance short. Here is the full answer. This explanation is by olevels.com, not an official mark scheme.

A common mistake is to write: no nucleus, so it carries more oxygen. You must give the middle link. No nucleus means more room for haemoglobin. Another mistake is to say the blood has no oxygen, or that respiration makes energy. Say less oxygen, and say respiration releases energy.

Your turn. Nadia has anaemia. She has fewer red blood cells than normal. Explain why she feels tired and breathes faster, even when she walks slowly. Pause here and work it out.

Fewer red cells means less haemoglobin, so less oxygen is carried. Less oxygen reaches her cells, so there is less aerobic respiration, and less energy is released. She feels tired, and breathes faster to take in more oxygen. Every time: for structure, give a feature, then 'so', then its benefit. For a change, follow the chain: oxygen, respiration, energy, then the result.
