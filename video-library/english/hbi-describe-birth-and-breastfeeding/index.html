Read the narration

At the end of pregnancy, about nine months, the baby lies head down in the uterus, or womb. It floats in fluid inside the amniotic sac. The placenta joins the baby to the uterus wall. The uterus wall is muscle. At the bottom is the cervix, a ring of muscle at the neck of the uterus. It stays closed. Below it is the vagina. Labour is the process of giving birth. It starts when the muscles of the uterus wall contract, which means they get shorter and squeeze. The contractions are strong, and come again and again, at regular times.

Step one: name the hormone. A hormone is a chemical messenger carried in the blood. Oxytocin is released from the pituitary gland, under the brain. It travels in the blood to the uterus, and makes its muscles contract. Step two: give the result, in order. The amniotic sac breaks, and its fluid comes out. Then the cervix widens, or dilates, so the baby's head can pass through. Then the contractions push the baby down and out through the vagina, head first. The umbilical cord, which joined the baby to the placenta, is tied and cut. Last, the placenta comes out. This is called the afterbirth. Step three: oxytocin has more than one job. It makes the uterus contract in labour. It also makes milk flow from the breasts when the baby feeds. And it helps the mother and baby bond, which means form a close, loving link. Step four: breastfeeding has different advantages. Breast milk has antibodies, proteins that help destroy pathogens. They pass from mother to baby, giving passive immunity: protection the baby did not make itself. Milk also has the right nutrients, at the right temperature, and it is free and sterile, which means it has no microbes.

Now a real exam question. Oxytocin and LH are two hormones found in females. Part one asks you to describe the functions of oxytocin. Three marks, so give three separate ideas. Its main job: it makes the muscles of the uterus contract. That is one mark. When? During labour: a second mark. It also makes milk be released when the baby feeds: a third. A common mistake is to say the uterus muscles relax to push the baby out. They contract. Another is to say the cervix contracts. The cervix widens. And progesterone does not rise during birth. Also, do not write that breast milk is healthier, with no reason. And do not repeat one advantage in different words. Each point needs a different idea.

Your turn. A woman's labour is very slow. Doctors give her extra oxytocin through a drip into her blood. Explain how this helps her baby to be born. Then give two advantages of breastfeeding in a place where clean water is hard to find. Pause here and work it out.

The oxytocin travels in her blood to the uterus. It makes the muscles of the uterus wall contract more strongly. So the cervix widens, and the baby is pushed out through the vagina. Part b. Breast milk is sterile, so the baby avoids microbes from dirty water used for bottle milk. It also has antibodies, giving passive immunity, so fewer infections. And it is free.
