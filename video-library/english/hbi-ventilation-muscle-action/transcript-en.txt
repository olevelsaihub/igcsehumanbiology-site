Idea. Breathing in is called inhaling. Here is the key idea. Take the same amount of air. In a small space, the air is squashed, so its pressure is high. In a big space, its pressure is low. Air always moves from higher pressure to lower pressure. So if the chest gets bigger, the pressure inside falls, and air from outside rushes in.

Method. Step one: the intercostal muscles between the ribs contract. They pull the ribs upwards and outwards. Step two: at the same time, the diaphragm contracts. The diaphragm is the sheet of muscle under the lungs. It flattens: the dome becomes flatter. Step three: these two movements make the volume of the thorax bigger. The thorax is the chest: the space around the lungs. Step four: so the pressure inside the lungs falls below the air pressure outside. Step five: air is drawn into the lungs from outside, down the pressure difference. Muscles only contract when nerve impulses reach them. A nerve impulse is an electrical message along a nerve. And breathing out is helped by elastic fibres in the lungs, which recoil: they spring back and push air out.

Common mistake. A common mistake is to write only that the diaphragm moves down. Write that it contracts and flattens. Another mistake is to get the pressure backwards. Air comes in because the pressure inside falls. It does not rise.

Your turn. A poison stops nerve impulses reaching the diaphragm and the intercostal muscles. Explain why a person with this poison cannot breathe in. Pause here and work it out.

Answer. Without nerve impulses, the muscles cannot contract. So the ribs do not move up and out, and the diaphragm does not flatten. The thorax volume does not increase, the pressure does not fall, and no air is drawn in. Every time: muscles contract, ribs go up and out, the diaphragm flattens, volume goes up, pressure goes down, and air is drawn in.
