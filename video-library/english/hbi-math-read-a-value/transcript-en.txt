Idea. A graph links two quantities. This made-up example shows one baby's mass as it grows. The x-axis runs across the bottom: age. The y-axis runs up the side: mass. A when question wants an x value. A how much question wants a y value.

Method. Step one. Work out the scale. Count the small squares between two labels, then divide. Here, 10 is split into 5 small squares. 10 divided by 5 is 2, so one small square is worth 2. Step two. Use a ruler. Draw a line from the value you know to the curve. Then draw across to the other axis. How heavy is the baby at 5 months? Go up from 5, then across. Step three. Read the value to the nearest half of a small square, and add the unit. Here, the line meets the y-axis at about 7.2 kilograms. Some questions show a picture instead. A proportion is the fraction of the whole. To estimate one, measure the part and the whole with a ruler, in millimetres. Then divide the part by the whole. Here, 11 divided by 44 is 0.25: a quarter. The same skill works for a bar chart. Go across from the top of the bar to the y-axis. In this made-up example chart, the bar for running reaches 45. So the reading is 45 kilojoules per minute.

Worked example. Here is a real exam question. A graph shows the blood alcohol concentration of a person after a drink. The legal limit to drive is 80 milligrams per 100 millilitres of blood. How many hours after drinking is it legal to drive? This is the graph. Time in hours is on the x-axis. On the exam grid, each hour has 10 small squares, so one small square is 0.1 hours. The level rises to a peak of about 150, then falls. The limit, 80, is a y value. Draw a ruler line across from 80, to where it meets the falling part of the curve. Then go straight down. It meets the x-axis at 2.4 hours. 0.4 hours is 0.4 times 60, which is 24 minutes. So it is legal to drive 2.4 hours, or 2 hours 24 minutes, after drinking. Common mistake. A common mistake is giving 80 as the answer. 80 is the limit on the y-axis. The question asks how many hours, so the answer is an x value. Another mistake is using the rising part of the curve. It also crosses 80, near the start. But the level is still going up there. Use the falling part.

Your turn. A patient with a fever takes a medicine. The graph shows the patient's body temperature. How many hours after the medicine does the temperature fall to 38 degrees Celsius? Pause here and work it out.

Answer. 38 degrees Celsius is a y value. Go across from 38 to the curve. Then go straight down to the x-axis. The line lands halfway between 4 and 5. So the answer is 4.5 hours, which is 4 hours 30 minutes. So, every time: decide which axis you need. Work out the scale. Draw ruler lines to the curve and across. Read the value, and add the unit.
