Idea. Cells take in substances through their surface. To compare cells, we use cubes. A cube has six faces, and every face is a square. The surface area is the area of all six faces added together. The volume is the space inside. The surface area to volume ratio compares the two. A small cube has a larger ratio than a big cube. So substances diffuse to its centre more quickly. This is why small organisms do not need a transport system.

Method. Here is a cube with sides of three centimetres. Step one: one face is three times three, nine square centimetres. Step two: there are six faces, so the surface area is six times nine, fifty-four square centimetres. Step three: the volume is side times side times side. Three times three times three is twenty-seven cubic centimetres. Step four: divide the surface area by the volume. Fifty-four divided by twenty-seven is two. So the ratio is two to one. Area units are squared. Volume units are cubed. Some questions ask for the area of a circle. Area equals pi times r times r. Pi is about three point one four. The radius, r, is half the diameter. So a diameter of ten millimetres gives a radius of five. The area is seventy-eight point five square millimetres.

Worked example. Now a real exam question. A student puts agar cubes in acid. The acid diffuses in, and the cube turns colourless from the edge. The cube has a side of two millimetres. Calculate its surface area to volume ratio, in the form n to one. Three marks. One face is two times two, four square millimetres. Six faces: six times four is twenty-four square millimetres. The volume is two times two times two: eight cubic millimetres. The ratio is twenty-four to eight. Divide both by eight: three to one. Here is the full working. Common mistake. A common mistake is to use four faces, or to work out side times three instead of side cubed. A cube has six faces, and volume is side times side times side. Another mistake is to write the ratio upside down, as one to three. Also, for a circle, use the radius, not the diameter.

Your turn. A teacher makes a model cell from a cube of jelly with sides of four millimetres. A dye diffuses into it from the outside. Calculate its surface area to volume ratio. Will the dye reach its centre faster or slower than in a two millimetre cube? Pause here and work it out.

Answer. One face is four times four, sixteen square millimetres. Six faces: ninety-six square millimetres. The volume is four times four times four, sixty-four cubic millimetres. The ratio is ninety-six to sixty-four, which is one point five to one. That is smaller than three to one, so the dye takes longer to reach the centre of this bigger cube. So, every time: surface area is six times side times side. Volume is side cubed. The ratio is surface area divided by volume, written as n to one.
