A pie chart shows how a total is shared out. A full circle is 360 degrees, and it stands for 100 percent of the total. Each slice is called a sector. A quarter of the circle is 90 degrees, which is 25 percent. So one percent is 3.6 degrees, because 360 divided by 100 is 3.6.

Here is the method. Step one: find the total the whole circle stands for. Step two: work out degrees per item, which is 360 divided by the total. Step three: divide the angle by that, to get the number. Step four: check the sectors make 360 degrees, or 100 percent. Step five is for drawing. Here is an example with made-up numbers. A pie chart shows 40 students. 360 divided by 40 is 9 degrees per student. A sector of 72 degrees is 72 divided by 9, which is 8 students. As a percentage, 72 divided by 360, times 100, is 20 percent. To turn a percentage into an angle, multiply by 3.6. To turn an angle into a percentage, divide by 3.6. For example, 35 percent times 3.6 is 126 degrees. For a missing sector, take the others away from 100. Here the sectors are 40 and 35 percent, so the missing one is 100 minus 40 minus 35, which is 25 percent. These are made-up values. A cycle diagram is a circle that shows time. If the whole circle is 24 hours, a 90 degree sector is 90 divided by 360, times 24, which is six hours. When you draw a pie chart, use a protractor, a tool for measuring angles. Now a real exam question. Scientists test 30 types of antibiotic. The pie chart shows how many days each takes to halve a resistant strain of bacteria. The 6 days sector is 108 degrees. Calculate the number of types of antibiotic for 6 days. Three marks. The whole circle is 30 antibiotics. So 360 divided by 30 is 12 degrees for each antibiotic. The 6 days sector is 108 degrees. Now divide the angle by 12. 108 divided by 12 is 9. So nine types of antibiotic halve the bacteria in 6 days. 360 divided by 30 is 12. 108 divided by 12 is 9. A quick check: 108 over 360, times 30, is also 9. So the answer is 9. This answer is by olevels.com, not an official mark scheme.
