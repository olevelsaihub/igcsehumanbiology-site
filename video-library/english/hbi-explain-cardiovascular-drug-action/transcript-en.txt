Adrenaline is a hormone that prepares the body for action. It binds to receptors on heart muscle cells. A receptor is a protein on a cell that a chemical fits into. When adrenaline binds, the heart beats faster and more strongly. Nerve endings release a neurotransmitter, a chemical, that does the same. Blood pressure is the force of blood pushing on the vessel walls. Narrower vessels give higher pressure. A hormone called angiotensin II narrows blood vessels. It is made by an enzyme called ACE.

To explain any of these drugs, use four steps. Step one: name the target, a receptor or an enzyme. Step two: say what is now prevented. Step three: give the change in the body. Step four: finish with the effect asked for. For two drugs, describe each, and use whereas. Beta-blockers bind to the receptors on heart muscle and block them. So adrenaline cannot bind. The heart rate and force fall, so blood pressure falls. This helps in angina, chest pain from a poor blood supply to the heart. ACE inhibitors stop the enzyme ACE working. ACE makes angiotensin II, so less of it is made. The blood vessels widen, and blood pressure falls. Losartan works on blood vessels in a different way. Angiotensin II is still made, but Losartan blocks its receptors, so it cannot bind. So an ACE inhibitor means less angiotensin II, whereas Losartan blocks its receptors. Either way, blood pressure falls. In archery, beta-blockers make a person calmer, with less shaking. A steadier aim is an unfair advantage, so they are banned.

Now a real exam question. The diagram shows a junction between a neurone and heart muscle. Use the diagram to explain how beta blockers can reduce the risk of heart disease. Three marks. The nerve ending is at the top, full of neurotransmitter. Step one: the beta-blocker binds to the receptors on the heart muscle. Step two: so the neurotransmitter cannot bind to those receptors. Step three: the heart muscle is stimulated less, so the heart rate falls and blood pressure falls. Here is the full chain. Lower pressure and a slower heart reduce the risk of heart disease. A common mistake is saying beta-blockers stop adrenaline being released. They block the receptors. Another is saying ACE inhibitors block angiotensin receptors. That is Losartan. Do not just write that beta-blockers lower blood pressure. Mention the receptors and the slower heart rate. They never make the heart beat faster.

Your turn. Beta-blockers are banned in archery. A 400 metre runner takes a beta-blocker before a race. Suggest why the beta-blocker would make the runner slower. Three marks. Pause here and work it out.

The beta-blocker blocks the receptors, so the heart rate falls. Less blood is pumped to the leg muscles, so they get less oxygen and less glucose. Aerobic respiration, which releases energy using oxygen, slows. So there is less energy, and the runner is slower. So, for any heart drug: name the target. Say what is prevented.
