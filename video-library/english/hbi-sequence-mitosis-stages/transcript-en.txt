Idea. Mitosis is a type of cell division. It makes two cells that are identical to the parent cell, for growth and repair. Before mitosis starts, each chromosome is copied. So each one has two identical strands, called chromatids, joined in the middle. Mitosis has four stages, always in the same order: prophase, metaphase, anaphase, telophase. Remember the letters PMAT.

Method. Prophase. The chromosomes coil up. They become shorter and thicker, so they become visible under a microscope. The nuclear membrane, the skin around the nucleus, breaks down. Metaphase. The chromosomes line up across the middle of the cell. This middle line is called the equator. Each chromosome is attached to spindle fibres, thin threads that stretch from the two ends of the cell. If you are asked to draw metaphase, draw each chromosome as an X, all in one line across the middle. Then draw spindle fibres from each end of the cell to the middle of each chromosome. Anaphase. The two chromatids of each chromosome separate. The spindle fibres pull them to opposite ends of the cell. These ends are called the poles. Telophase. A new nuclear membrane forms around each group of chromosomes. So there are two nuclei. Then the cytoplasm divides, making two new cells. So, write PMAT first. Then, for each stage, give one chromosome event with a position word. For a set of pictures, find metaphase first, because the chromosomes are in a line.

Worked example. Now a real exam question. The diagram shows a cell early in mitosis. Part b: describe what happens to the chromosomes during the four stages of mitosis. Five marks: one for the order and one for each stage. Point one: the order, prophase, metaphase, anaphase, telophase. Point two: in prophase, chromosomes get shorter and thicker, so they are visible. Point three: in metaphase, they line up on the equator. Point four: in anaphase, the chromatids separate and move to opposite poles. Point five: in telophase, a new nuclear membrane forms around each group of chromosomes. Here is the full answer. This answer is by olevels.com, not an official mark scheme.

Common mistake. A common mistake is to put anaphase before metaphase. Another is to say the chromosomes line up at the sides, or at the poles, in metaphase. They line up in the middle. In anaphase, chromatids separate, not whole chromosomes. And in telophase, a nuclear membrane forms, not a cell membrane.

Your turn. A student draws four cells from a slide, labelled J, K, L and M. Part a: put the four cells in the order of mitosis. Part b: name the stage in cell L, and say what the chromosomes are doing. Pause here and work it out.

Answer. Here is the answer. M is prophase: the chromosomes are thick and visible. K is metaphase: they are in a line. L is anaphase. J is telophase: two new nuclei. So the order is M, K, L, J. Part b: L is anaphase. The chromatids separate and are pulled to opposite poles. So, every time: prophase, visible. Metaphase, middle. Anaphase, poles. Telophase, new nuclei.
