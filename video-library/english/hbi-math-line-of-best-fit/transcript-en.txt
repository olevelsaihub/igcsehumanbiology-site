A line of best fit is one line that shows the trend of the points. The trend is the overall pattern. In these made-up example results, heart rate goes up as running speed goes up. The line does not need to touch every point.

Step one. Look at all the points together. Do they follow a straight line, or do they bend? These points rise, then level off. So they need a curve, not a straight line. Step two. Look for an anomalous point. An anomalous result is one that does not fit the pattern, often because of an error. Leave it out. Do not bend your line to reach it. Step three. Draw one thin line, with a sharp pencil. Try to have about as many points above the line as below it. Step four. Start the line at the first point and stop it at the last point. Do not stretch it beyond your data unless the question asks. Some patterns rise to a peak, then fall, like enzyme activity as temperature goes up. Draw one smooth curve over the top. Do not draw two straight lines meeting in a sharp point.

Here is a real exam question. An enzyme is a protein that speeds up a reaction. A student adds an enzyme to milk and measures the pH every minute for 10 minutes. pH tells you how acidic a solution is. The results are plotted, and you must draw a line of best fit. These are the ten points from the table: 6.8 at 1 minute, falling to 4.6 at 9 and at 10 minutes. Now judge the shape. The pH falls steadily. At the end, the last two points are the same, so the pattern flattens a little. No point is far from the pattern, so there is no anomaly. Draw one thin, smooth line from the 1-minute point to the 10-minute point. Points sit on both sides of it. It is not dot to dot. This answer is by olevels.com, not an official mark scheme.

The most common mistake is joining the points dot to dot. That is not a line of best fit. A line of best fit is one smooth line through the middle of the points. Other mistakes: bending the line to reach an anomalous point, or forcing a straight line through points that clearly curve. A thick or double line also loses the mark.

Your turn. A student tests their reaction time eight times in a row. Reaction time is how long it takes to respond. The times in seconds are 0.40, 0.35, 0.31, 0.45, 0.26, 0.24, 0.23 and 0.22. Plot them, then draw a line of best fit. Pause here and work it out.

Plot the eight points. Most fall, then level off. But try 4, at 0.45 seconds, does not fit the pattern. It is anomalous, so leave it out. The rest of the points curve. So draw one thin, smooth curve from try 1 to try 8, through the middle of the points. It falls, then levels off, and it ignores try 4. So, every time: decide straight or curved. Leave out any anomaly. Draw one thin line with points on both sides. Start and stop with the data.
