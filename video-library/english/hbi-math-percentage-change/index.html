Calculate a percentage change or percentage difference

Read the narration

Idea. A percentage change compares a change with the starting value. A baby's mass goes from 4.0 to 5.0 kilograms. It gained 1.0 kilogram. As a share of the start, that is 1.0 out of 4.0, which is 25 per cent.

Method. Here is the rule. Subtract the original value from the new value. Divide by the original value. Then multiply by 100. The original value is always on the bottom. Step one. Find the two values, with units. Step two. Decide which is the original. It is the starting value, or the one after the words 'compared with'. A percentage difference works the same way: the value after 'compared with' goes on the bottom. Step three. Subtract: new minus original. A positive answer is an increase. A negative answer is a decrease. Step four. Divide the change by the original, and multiply by 100. Step five. Round as the question asks. Keep all the digits until then. Add the per cent sign, and say increase or decrease. A minus sign shows a fall. Both values must be in the same units. 650 thousand is 0.65 million. So 1.4 million minus 0.65 million is 0.75 million. Divide by 0.65 and times 100: 115 per cent. An increase can be over 100 per cent.

Worked example. Here is a real exam question. In 2000, 7.0 million people died from heart disease. A bar chart shows deaths in 2016. Calculate the percentage change from 2000 to 2016. 2000 comes first, so 7.0 million is the original value. Now read the 2016 bar. The heart disease bar ends halfway between 9 and 10. So the new value is 9.5 million. Subtract: 9.5 minus 7.0 is 2.5 million. It is positive, so deaths went up. Divide by the original: 2.5 divided by 7.0, times 100, is 35.7. To a whole number, that is a 36 per cent increase. This working is by olevels.com, not an official mark scheme.

Common mistake. The commonest mistake is dividing by the wrong value. 2.5 divided by 9.5 gives 26 per cent. That is wrong, because 9.5 is the new value, not the original. Another mistake is writing the new value as a percentage of the old. 9.5 out of 7.0 is 136 per cent. That is not the change. Also, do not round too early, and do not lose the minus sign for a fall.

Your turn. These are made-up figures for one country. 1.6 million adults smoked in 2010. In 2020, 1.2 million adults smoked. Calculate the percentage change from 2010 to 2020. Pause here and work it out.

Answer. 2010 comes first, so 1.6 million is the original. The change is 1.2 minus 1.6, which is minus 0.4 million. It is negative, so it is a decrease. Divide by the original: minus 0.4 divided by 1.6, times 100, is minus 25. So the answer is minus 25 per cent: a 25 per cent decrease. So, every time: find both values in the same units. Pick the original. Subtract new minus original. Divide by the original and times 100. Round, and say up or down.
