A gene is a short section of DNA. Its order of bases is a code for one protein. A protein is a chain of amino acids. So the gene tells the cell which amino acids to join, and in what order. Making a protein has two stages. Stage one is transcription, in the nucleus. Stage two is translation, at a ribosome in the cytoplasm. A ribosome is a tiny organelle that joins amino acids. The DNA itself never leaves the nucleus.

Transcription. The DNA of the gene unwinds, and the two strands separate. Free RNA nucleotides pair with the bases on one strand. This builds messenger RNA, or mRNA. It is a complementary copy of the gene. Complementary means each base pairs with only one partner. In RNA, adenine pairs with uracil. The mRNA then leaves the nucleus. It moves into the cytoplasm, to a ribosome. Translation happens at the ribosome. The mRNA is read in groups of three bases. Each group is a codon. A tRNA molecule carries one amino acid. Its anticodon is three bases that pair with a matching codon. The amino acids are joined by peptide bonds, in the order the codons set. The chain grows, then it is released and folds into a protein. Questions may change the story. If a gene is switched off, it is not transcribed. So no mRNA is made, and no protein. If a diet has little protein, fewer amino acids reach the cells. So translation slows, and less protein is made.

Here is a real exam question. A virus uses the host cell's genetic material to make new viral proteins. Describe how the host cell carries out protein synthesis. It is worth five marks. Five marks, so plan six points in order. Point one: transcription happens in the nucleus. Point two: it makes mRNA, a complementary copy of the gene. Point three: the mRNA moves out to a ribosome. Point four: translation happens at the ribosome. Point five: tRNA brings amino acids, and each anticodon pairs with a codon. Point six: peptide bonds join the amino acids into the viral protein. A common mistake is to say the DNA goes to the ribosome. Only the mRNA copy leaves the nucleus. Another is to say the ribosome carries the amino acids. tRNA carries them. Also, the anticodon pairs with a codon on the mRNA, not with the DNA. And amino acids are joined at the ribosome. They are not made there.

Your turn. A pancreas cell makes insulin, a protein. A skin cell has the same insulin gene, but it is switched off. First, explain why the skin cell makes no insulin. Second, name the molecule that carries amino acids to the ribosome, and the bond that joins them. Pause here and work it out.

Part a. The insulin gene is switched off, so it is not transcribed. No insulin mRNA is made. With no mRNA to translate, the ribosomes make no insulin. Part b. Transfer RNA, or tRNA, carries the amino acids to the ribosome. Peptide bonds join them. So, every time: two stages, two places. Transcription in the nucleus makes mRNA. The mRNA goes to a ribosome. In translation, tRNA brings amino acids, anticodons pair with codons, and peptide bonds join them.
