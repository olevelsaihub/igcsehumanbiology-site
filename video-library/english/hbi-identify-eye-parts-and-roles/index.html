Read the narration

This is a section through the eye, seen from above, with the front on the left. Light comes in at the front and travels to the back. First it passes the cornea, then the pupil, then the lens. It reaches the retina at the back. The optic nerve leaves the back of the eye. Every part has a job. To answer, you must know the name, and the job, of each part. We will go from front to back.

The cornea is the clear window at the front. It refracts light the most. Refract means bend. The iris is the coloured ring of muscle. It controls the size of the pupil, the gap in its centre, and so how much light gets in. The lens refracts the light again, to focus it on the retina. The ciliary muscle is a ring of muscle around the lens. It changes the shape of the lens. It pulls through the suspensory ligaments, the threads that hold the lens in place. The retina lines the back. It has light-sensitive cells, rods and cones, which turn light into electrical impulses. The fovea has the most cones, so the image is sharpest there. At the blind spot the optic nerve leaves. There are no rods or cones there. The optic nerve carries impulses from the retina to the brain. The choroid is a dark layer with blood vessels that feed the retina. The sclera is the tough white outer coat. Some tables mix the eye with the ear. First decide: is it about light, or about sound and balance? In the ear, the eardrum vibrates. The ossicles, three small bones, pass the vibrations on. The cochlea turns them into impulses, and the auditory nerve takes them to the brain. The semicircular canals help balance.

Now a real exam question. The diagram is a section through the eye, with the lens missing. The front is on the left. The question asks you to name the parts labelled A, B and C. Three marks. Line A ends on the clear, curved layer that bulges at the front. That is the cornea. Line B ends on the layer lining the back of the eye, where the light lands. That is the retina. Line C goes to the cord leaving the back of the eye, towards the brain. That is the optic nerve. This answer is by olevels.com, not an official mark scheme.

A common mistake is to mix up the iris and the pupil. The iris is the coloured muscle. The pupil is the gap. Also, the iris does not focus light. The cornea and lens do that. Another mistake is to say the optic nerve sees the image. It carries impulses; the brain makes sense of them. And do not swap the fovea, with the most cones, and the blind spot, with none.

Your turn. Name the part of the eye that: (a) controls how much light enters; (b) gives the sharpest image; (c) has no rods or cones; (d) brings blood to the retina. Then name the part of the ear that turns vibrations into impulses. Pause here and work it out.

(a) The iris. (b) The fovea. (c) The blind spot. (d) The choroid. And (e), in the ear: the cochlea. So, every time: follow the label line to its end. Use the path of light from front to back. Then match the key word to the part.
