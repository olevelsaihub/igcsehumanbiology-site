Idea. To see clearly, light must be focused exactly on the retina. The lens helps by refracting the light, which means bending it. The eye changes the shape of the lens to focus on near or distant objects. This is called accommodation. The lens is held in a ring of ciliary muscle by suspensory ligaments, like strings. When the muscle changes, the pull on the lens changes, and so does its shape.

Method. Light from a near object spreads out, so it needs more bending. For a near object, the ciliary muscles contract. The suspensory ligaments go slack, which means loose. The lens becomes thicker and more rounded, so light is refracted more. For a distant object, everything reverses. The ciliary muscles relax. The suspensory ligaments become tight. They pull the lens thinner and flatter, so light is refracted less. Always give the chain in this order: muscles, ligaments, lens shape, then refraction. A thicker lens also has a shorter focal length. That is the distance at which it brings light to a point. Some questions ask you to draw the lens for a near object. Draw it fat and rounded, between the ciliary muscles, with a suspensory ligament joining each end to a muscle. A thicker lens is also narrower from top to bottom. Some questions ask the job of each part in seeing clearly. Follow the light: the cornea refracts it, the lens refracts it more onto the retina, rods and cones turn it into impulses, and the optic nerve carries them to the brain.

Worked example. Now a real exam question. A person is looking at a near object. Then they look at a distant object. The question asks you to describe the changes in the eye. Four marks. Start from the near picture: thick lens, slack ligaments. The change is to a distant object, so the lens must become thinner. The whole chain reverses. Point one, the muscles relax. Point two, the ligaments tighten. Point three: they pull on the lens and stretch it, so it becomes thinner. Point four: light is refracted less. Here is the full answer. Common mistake. A common mistake is to say the ciliary muscles contract for a distant object. They relax. Another is to say the ligaments contract. Ligaments are not muscles. They become tight or slack. Say thicker or thinner, not bigger or smaller. Write light is refracted less, not less light is refracted. And leave out the iris. It controls how much light enters, not the focus.

Your turn. A driver looks at the road far ahead. Then she looks down at the speed display close to her. Describe the changes in her eye. Pause here and work it out.

Answer. This change is from distant to near. Her ciliary muscles contract. Her suspensory ligaments go slack. Her lens becomes thicker and more rounded. So the light is refracted more, and focused on the retina. So, every time: decide near or distant first. Then give muscles, ligaments, lens shape and refraction, in that order.
