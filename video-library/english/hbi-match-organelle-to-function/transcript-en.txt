Each part of a cell has its own job. The nucleus holds the DNA, the genetic information, and controls the cell. The cell membrane controls what goes in and out. The cytoplasm is the jelly where most chemical reactions happen. Mitochondria release energy by aerobic respiration. Respiration is the reaction that releases energy from glucose. Ribosomes make proteins: they join amino acids together. Amino acids are the small units that make up a protein. The endoplasmic reticulum, or ER, is a network of tubes. It folds new proteins and carries them through the cell.

Here is the method. First, see which box is missing: the name, the job, or both. Next, name the part from its place and shape. Then write one job with an action verb: controls, releases, makes, or carries. Some questions give a context. Link the job to it. A protein is made on the ribosomes. It passes into the ER, which folds it into the right shape. The ER carries it to where it is needed, such as the cell membrane. The nucleus has its own membrane, the nuclear membrane. It keeps the DNA inside the nucleus. It also controls what passes in and out. For example, messenger RNA, a copy of a gene, leaves through small holes called pores. Another context is a synapse, the gap between two nerve cells. The end of the nerve cell has many mitochondria. They release energy. The cell uses it to make a chemical called neurotransmitter, and to move small sacs of it, called vesicles, to the membrane.

Here is a real exam question. The diagram shows two cheek cells. Complete the table: give the name and the job of parts X, Y and Z. It is worth six marks. First the names. Follow each line to its tip. X ends on the outer edge of the cell. An animal cell has no wall, so X is the cell membrane. Y ends on the dark oval, so Y is the nucleus. Z ends in the space around the nucleus, so Z is the cytoplasm. Now one job for each. The membrane controls what enters and leaves. The nucleus holds the genetic information and controls the cell. The cytoplasm is where reactions happen. This answer is by olevels.com, not the official mark scheme.

A common mistake is to write that mitochondria make energy. Energy cannot be made. Mitochondria release energy, by respiration. Another mistake is to say the cell membrane protects the cell. Say what it does: it controls which substances enter and leave.

Your turn. A runner's leg muscle cells have many mitochondria. First, explain why. Second, a gland cell makes an enzyme, which is a protein. Name the part that makes the enzyme, and the part that folds and carries it. Pause here and work it out.

Part a. Mitochondria release energy by aerobic respiration. A runner's muscles need a lot of energy to contract. So many mitochondria release more energy. Part b. The ribosomes make the enzyme, by joining amino acids. The endoplasmic reticulum folds it and carries it through the cell. So, every time: name the part from its place. Give one job with a verb.
