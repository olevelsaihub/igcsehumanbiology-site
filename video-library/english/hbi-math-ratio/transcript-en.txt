Idea. A ratio compares two amounts in a fixed order. Here there are 2 red cells and 6 blue cells. Red to blue is 2 to 6. Dividing both by 2 gives 1 to 3. For every red cell, there are 3 blue ones.

Method. Step one. Write the two values in the order the question names them. If it says the healthy person compared with person A, the healthy value goes first. Step two. Check both values have the same units. Then simplify. Simplest form means dividing both sides by the biggest number that goes into both. 250 to 750 becomes 1 to 3. Step three. Make the side the question asks for equal to 1. For 1 to n, divide both sides by the first number. 4 to 10 becomes 1 to 2.5. For n to 1, divide by the second number instead: 0.4 to 1. Some questions ask how many times greater one value is. Divide one by the other. 10 is 2.5 times greater than 4, because 10 divided by 4 is 2.5. Standard form writes a big number as a number times a power of 10. These numbers work the same way. Use your calculator to divide. 4.5 times 10 to the 6, to 5.4 times 10 to the 6, becomes 1 to 1.2.

Worked example. Here is a real exam question. The table shows white blood cells in three people. Calculate the ratio of white blood cells in the healthy person compared with person A, in the form 1 to n. Find the white blood cell row. The healthy person has 7500 per cubic centimetre. Person A has 13 750. The question names the healthy person first, so write 7500 to 13 750. For 1 to n, divide both sides by the first number, 7500. 7500 divided by 7500 is 1. 13 750 divided by 7500 is 1.833. Round sensibly: the ratio is 1 to 1.83. Common mistake. A common mistake is the wrong order: 13 750 to 7500. The question named the healthy person first, so 7500 goes first. Another mistake is stopping too soon. 7500 to 13 750 is a ratio, but not in the form 1 to n. And 1.83 on its own needs the 1 and the colon.

Your turn. At rest, a person breathes 15 times a minute. During exercise, they breathe 36 times a minute. Give the ratio of the resting rate to the exercise rate, in the form 1 to n. Pause here and work it out.

Answer. The question names resting first. So write 15 to 36. Divide both sides by 15. 36 divided by 15 is 2.4. So the ratio is 1 to 2.4. So, every time: write the values in the named order. Check the units match. Divide so the asked side is 1. Write the answer with a colon.
