Explain how insulin and glucagon control blood glucose

Idea. Glucose is the main sugar in your blood. Your cells use it for respiration, to release energy. After a meal, glucose from the food goes into the blood, so blood glucose rises. The pancreas is an organ below the stomach. It detects the blood glucose level. It controls it with two hormones: insulin and glucagon. A hormone is a chemical carried in the blood.

Method. When glucose is too high, the pancreas releases insulin. Insulin makes the liver and the muscles take in glucose. They turn it into glycogen, a store. So blood glucose falls. When glucose falls too low, the pancreas releases glucagon instead. Glucagon makes the liver turn glycogen back into glucose. The glucose goes into the blood, so the level rises. Be careful, these two words look alike. Glucagon is a hormone. Glycogen is a store, in the liver and muscles. Glucagon makes glycogen turn into glucose. Insulin makes glucose turn into glycogen. In diabetes, insulin is not made, or it does not work. Compare the two lines after a meal. With diabetes, glucose stays high. Over many years, this damages blood vessels and neurones in the eye and the ear.

Worked example. Now a real exam question. Explain the role of insulin in regulating the concentration of glucose in the blood. Three marks. When does insulin act? When blood glucose rises above normal, for example after a meal. Where is it from? The pancreas releases insulin into the blood. What does it do? It makes glucose turn into glycogen. Where? In the liver and muscles. So blood glucose falls back to normal. Here is the full answer. Explanation by olevels.com, not an official mark scheme.

Common mistake. A common mistake is to swap glucagon and glycogen. Glucagon is the hormone. Glycogen is what is stored. Another mistake is to say insulin breaks down glucose, or that the liver makes insulin. Insulin comes from the pancreas. It makes cells take in glucose and store it.

Your turn. Maya runs for two hours. Her muscles use a lot of glucose, and her blood glucose starts to fall. Explain how her body brings it back to normal. Pause here and work it out.

Answer. Maya's muscles use glucose for respiration, so blood glucose falls. Her pancreas detects this, and releases glucagon into the blood. Glucagon makes her liver turn glycogen into glucose. The glucose goes into the blood, so her blood glucose rises back to normal. So every time: is glucose too high or too low? The pancreas detects it. If high, insulin. If low, glucagon.
