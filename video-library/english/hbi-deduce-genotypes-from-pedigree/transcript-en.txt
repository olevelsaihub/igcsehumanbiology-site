Idea. A pedigree is a family tree. A square is a male, and a circle is a female. A shaded shape has the condition. A line joins two parents, and a line down leads to their children. Always read the key. An allele is one form of a gene. A genotype is the pair of alleles a person has. This condition is caused by a recessive allele, small a. A recessive allele is hidden when the dominant allele, capital A, is with it. So every shaded person is small a, small a. Person 3 is small a, small a.

Method. Now the parents. Person 3 got one small a from each parent. But both parents are unshaded, so each also has a capital A. So persons 1 and 2 are both capital A, small a: carriers. A carrier has the recessive allele but not the condition. Person 4 is unshaded, so he has at least one capital A. He could be capital A, capital A, or capital A, small a. When two genotypes fit, give both. If the condition is dominant, capital H, flip the rule. A dominant allele shows even with only one copy. Unshaded people have no capital H, so they are small h, small h. A shaded person with an unshaded parent or child is capital H, small h. For a sex-linked condition, write the X and the Y. An affected son is X small h, Y. He got his X from his mother. She is unshaded, so she is a carrier: X capital H, X small h. For a Y-linked trait, only males have it, on the Y. Blood groups have three alleles: I-A, I-B and I-O. I-A and I-B are codominant: both show. I-O is recessive. For group A, the genotype is I-A, I-A, or I-A, I-O. Group B is the same pattern. Group AB is I-A, I-B. Group O is only I-O, I-O. So a group O child means both parents carry I-O.

Worked example. Here is a real exam question. Some people in this family have sickle cell anaemia. It is caused by a recessive allele. Give the genotype of person 6. One mark. Here is the family tree. Squares are males and circles are females. Shaded means sickle cell anaemia. Person 6 is the unshaded square on the left. Person 6 is an unshaded square: a male without the condition. Look at his mother, person 2. She is shaded, so she is small a, small a. She can only pass on small a. Person 6 is unshaded, so he has at least one capital A. He got small a from his mother. So person 6 is capital A, small a. Answer by olevels.com, not an official mark scheme.

Common mistake. A common mistake is to write capital A, capital A for an unshaded person with a shaded parent. The shaded parent gave them a small a, so they must be capital A, small a. Another mistake is to give only one genotype when two fit. For group A, give both: I-A, I-A, and I-A, I-O, unless the family rules one out.

Your turn. A mother has blood group A, and a father has blood group B. Their child has blood group O. Give the genotypes of all three people, and explain. Pause here and work it out.

Answer. Start with the child. Group O has only one genotype: I-O, I-O. So the child got one I-O from each parent. The mother is group A, so she has I-A. She also gave I-O, so she is I-A, I-O. The father is group B, so he has I-B, and he gave I-O. He is I-B, I-O. So, every time: read the key, write the certain genotypes first, then work out the rest from one allele in each parent. Give all that fit.
