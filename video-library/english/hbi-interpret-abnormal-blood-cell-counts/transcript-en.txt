Idea. Blood has three kinds of cell. Red cells carry oxygen. White cells defend the body against pathogens, the microorganisms that cause disease. Platelets are small pieces of cells that help blood clot. A clot is a plug that stops bleeding. There are two main kinds of white cell. Phagocytes engulf pathogens, which means they take them inside, and then digest them. This is phagocytosis. Lymphocytes make antibodies, proteins that stick to pathogens and help destroy them.

Method. Step one: compare each value with the healthy value. In this made-up example, the patient's white cells are more than double the healthy value. The red cells are the same. The platelets are much lower. So the ratio of red cells to white cells has changed too. Step two: give a likely cause. More white cells usually means an infection. Step three: add the job of the cells. More white cells are made so that phagocytes engulf the pathogens and lymphocytes make antibodies. Other causes include inflammation, which is swelling and redness where tissue is hurt, an allergy, or leukaemia, a cancer of white cells. Fewer white cells than healthy means fewer phagocytes and antibodies, so infections are more likely. A low platelet count means the blood clots slowly. After a cut, the person bleeds for longer. The wound stays open for longer, so pathogens have more time to get in.

Worked example. Now a real exam question. The table gives blood components in three people. The question asks you to explain a possible reason for the difference in white blood cells between the healthy person and person A. Two marks. Here is the table. White blood cells: the healthy person has 7500, person A has 13 750, and person B has 7250. Red cells and platelets are also listed. Compare the white cells. Person A has 13 750. The healthy person has 7500. So person A has 6250 more white cells. Point one: person A may have an infection. Point two: more white cells are needed to destroy the pathogen. Phagocytes engulf and digest the pathogens, and lymphocytes make antibodies against them. Here is the full answer. This answer is by olevels.com, not an official mark scheme.

Common mistake. A common mistake is to write only that the person is ill, or fighting infection. These words alone are not enough. Say what the white cells do: engulf pathogens or make antibodies. Another mistake is to say fewer platelets means less oxygen is carried. Red cells carry oxygen. Platelets help blood to clot. Last, always read the right row, and compare with the healthy value. Do not explain the platelet row when the question asks about white cells.

Your turn. Sara has a blood test. These are made-up values. Her white cells are 7100 per centimetre cubed; healthy is 7000. Her platelets are 0.5 times ten to the five; healthy is 2.5 times ten to the five. Describe and explain how her results could affect her health. Pause here and work it out.

Answer. Compare first. Her white cells are about normal. Her platelets are much lower, only one fifth of the healthy value. Platelets help blood clot. With few platelets, her blood clots slowly, so after a cut she bleeds for longer. The wound stays open, so pathogens have more time to get in. So, every time: find which part has changed, and whether it is higher or lower than healthy. Give a likely cause or effect. Then add the job of those cells.
