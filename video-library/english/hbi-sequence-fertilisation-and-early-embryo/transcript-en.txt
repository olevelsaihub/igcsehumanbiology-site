Idea. It starts with ovulation: the release of an egg, or ovum, from an ovary. The egg moves into the oviduct, the tube that leads to the uterus. Sperm and egg are gametes, or sex cells. Chromosomes are threads of DNA in the nucleus. They carry the genes. Each gamete is haploid: its nucleus has one set of chromosomes, 23. Fertilisation is when the sperm nucleus fuses with the egg nucleus. This makes one cell, the zygote. It is diploid: two sets, 46.

Method. Step one: name the event first. Ovulation releases the egg. Fertilisation fuses the gametes. Then mitosis, a kind of cell division, makes the zygote into many cells: the embryo. Step two: describe mitosis in order. First, the DNA in each chromosome is copied. Then the chromosomes line up across the middle of the cell. The two copies of each are pulled to opposite ends. Then the cell splits in two. This repeats again and again: one cell makes two, two make four, and so on. All the cells are genetically identical, with the same genes. By day five, the embryo is a hollow ball of about 100 cells, called a blastocyst. Identical twins form when one zygote or early embryo splits into two. Each part grows into a baby. Both came from the same egg and the same sperm, so they have the same genes. Two eggs fertilised by two sperm give non-identical twins.

Common mistake. A common mistake is to say fertilisation makes an embryo, or that the zygote is haploid. It makes a zygote, and the zygote is diploid. Also, the blastocyst forms by mitosis, not meiosis. Also, identical twins do not come from two eggs. Naming the four phases in order, prophase, metaphase, anaphase and then telophase, may earn one mark. But a list alone is not enough: say what the chromosomes do.

Your turn. In a clinic, a zygote divides until it has eight cells. One cell is removed and tested for a genetic disorder. How many chromosomes are in each of the eight cells? Name the process that made them. Explain why the test result also applies to the other seven cells. Pause here and work it out.

Answer. The zygote is diploid, with 46 chromosomes. Mitosis keeps the number the same, so each of the eight cells has 46. The process is mitosis. For part c: mitosis copies the DNA exactly, so all eight cells are genetically identical. They all came from the same zygote. So the genes in the tested cell are the same as in the other seven. So, every time: name the event, say the nuclei fuse to make a diploid zygote, and describe mitosis step by step. For identical twins, one embryo splits.
