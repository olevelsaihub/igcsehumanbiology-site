Your arm moves because muscles pull on bones. Here is the upper arm. The bone is the humerus. The biceps is the muscle at the front. The triceps is the muscle at the back. The elbow is a hinge joint: it bends in one plane only, like a door. A muscle can only pull. It cannot push. When a muscle contracts, it gets shorter and pulls. So two muscles are needed: one to bend the arm, one to straighten it. They are an antagonistic pair: when one contracts, the other relaxes.

Step one: decide the movement. To raise the forearm, which means bending the arm, the biceps contracts. It gets shorter and fatter. At the same time, the triceps relaxes. Step two: follow the pull. A tendon is a tough cord that joins a muscle to a bone. The biceps pulls on its tendon. The tendon does not stretch, so it pulls the radius, a bone in the forearm. The forearm turns at the elbow and goes up. To lower the forearm, which means straightening the arm, it is the other way round. The triceps contracts and pulls on the ulna, the other forearm bone. The biceps relaxes. Step three: use the key words in your answer. Contracts and relaxes. Antagonistic pair. Tendon. And the bone that is pulled.

They work against each other: as one contracts, the other relaxes. Mark four: the biceps pulls on its tendon. The tendon does not stretch, so it pulls the radius, and the forearm is raised. A common mistake is to say the triceps pushes the arm down. Muscles never push. The triceps contracts and pulls. Also, the two muscles never contract at the same time. Another mistake is to say the tendon is elastic, or that the biceps pulls the humerus. The tendon does not stretch, and the biceps pulls the radius in the forearm.

Your turn. A swimmer straightens her arm at the elbow to push back through the water. Explain how her muscles straighten her arm. Pause here and work it out.

Straightening means the triceps contracts and gets shorter. The biceps relaxes. They are an antagonistic pair. The triceps pulls on its tendon, which pulls the ulna, so the forearm straightens at the elbow. So, every time: decide the movement. Say which muscle contracts and which relaxes. Use the word antagonistic. Then say the tendon pulls the bone.
