A chromosome is a long thread of DNA in the nucleus. It carries genes, the instructions for our features. A human body cell has forty-six chromosomes, in twenty-three pairs. One chromosome of each pair comes from your mother, one from your father. The two in a pair are a homologous pair: same size, same shape, same genes. This cell is diploid. We draw just two pairs, to keep it simple. A gamete is a sex cell: a sperm or an egg. It has only one chromosome from each pair: twenty-three single chromosomes. It is haploid, with half the DNA of a body cell.

Mitosis is cell division for growth and repair. One cell makes two cells. Each is diploid, with forty-six chromosomes, and they are genetically identical. Meiosis makes gametes. One cell makes four cells. Each is haploid, with twenty-three chromosomes. And they are all genetically different, which gives variation. Why halve the number? At fertilisation, a sperm and an egg fuse. Twenty-three plus twenty-three gives forty-six in the zygote, the first cell of the baby. Without meiosis, the number would double every generation. A karyotype is a picture of all the chromosomes in pairs. Pair twenty-three is the sex chromosomes. X X is female, and X Y is male. If there are three copies of chromosome twenty-one, there are forty-seven in all. This is Down's syndrome. Look at the DNA in meiosis. First the DNA is copied, so it doubles. Then it halves, and then it halves again. So each gamete ends with half the starting amount.

Now a real exam question. Meiosis is another type of cell division. Give two differences between the cells produced by meiosis and by mitosis. Two marks. Compare the cells produced. Difference one: chromosome number. Mitosis gives diploid cells with forty-six. Meiosis gives haploid cells with twenty-three. Difference two: how many cells. Mitosis makes two cells. Meiosis makes four. A third difference you could give: mitosis cells are genetically identical, but meiosis cells are all different. Here is the full answer. A common mistake is to say a gamete has twenty-three pairs. It has twenty-three single chromosomes. A body cell has twenty-three pairs. Another mistake is a one-sided difference, like "meiosis gives variation". Say both sides: meiosis cells are different, but mitosis cells are identical.

Your turn. A dog body cell has seventy-eight chromosomes. How many are in a dog sperm cell? How many in a dog zygote? And explain why the sperm must have this number. Pause here and work it out.

A sperm cell is haploid, so it has half: thirty-nine. An egg also has thirty-nine. When they fuse, the zygote has seventy-eight. If the sperm were not halved, the zygote would have one hundred and fifty-six. That number would double every generation. With thirty-nine in each gamete, the number stays the same in every generation. So remember: a body cell is diploid, with forty-six in pairs. A gamete is haploid, with twenty-three. Mitosis gives two identical cells. Meiosis gives four different ones.
