Idea. Many questions ask for a difference. Read two values, then take the smaller one from the larger one. For example, a child is 112 centimetres tall at age 6, and 130 at age 9. The difference is 130 minus 112, which is 18 centimetres.

Method. Step one. Find the value of one small square. If 20 is split into 10 small squares, then one small square is worth 2. Step two. Find the two points the question names. This example graph shows one runner's breathing rate during a race. The maximum is the highest point. The resting rate is the flat part at the start, before the race. Step three. Use a ruler to read each value, and write both down: 38 and 14. Step four. Subtract the smaller from the larger. 38 minus 14 is 24 breaths per minute. For a stacked bar, the size of one part is the top of that part, minus its bottom. In this example bar, the fat part starts at 40 and ends at 55. So there are 55 minus 40, which is 15 grams of fat. Some questions ask for the full range of a curve. This made-up example curve shows the light absorbed by one cone cell. A cone cell is a cell in the eye that detects colour. Wavelength, in nanometres, tells you the colour of the light. The range is the right-hand end minus the left-hand end, where the curve meets zero: 540 minus 380 is 160 nanometres.

Worked example. Here is a real exam question. The graph shows how a person's heart rate changes with exercise. You must find the difference between the maximum heart rate and the resting heart rate. Here is the graph. The y-axis starts at 40 and goes up in 20s. Each 20 is split into 10 small squares, so one small square is 2 beats per minute. The resting rate is the flat line at the start: 72. The maximum is the flat top, from 14 to 20 minutes: 156. Difference: 156 minus 72 is 84 beats per minute. Explanation by olevels.com, not an official mark scheme.

Common mistake. A common mistake is counting each small square as 1. Here each small square is 2 beats per minute. Always work out the scale first. Another mistake is giving one reading, like 156, instead of the difference. The question wants the gap between the two values.

Your turn. The graph shows a woman's body temperature over one day. Find the difference between her highest and lowest temperatures. Pause here and work it out.

Answer. Each gridline is 0.1 degrees Celsius. The highest point is 37.2, at 16 hours. The lowest point is 36.2, at 4 hours. Difference: 37.2 minus 36.2 is 1.0 degrees Celsius. So, every time: find the scale. Find the two points the question names. Read both, then subtract the smaller from the larger, and add the unit.
