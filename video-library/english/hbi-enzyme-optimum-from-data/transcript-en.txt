The optimum is the temperature, or pH, at which an enzyme works fastest. If the graph shows the rate, the optimum is at the highest point. But some data show the time taken. A short time means a fast enzyme. So for time data, the optimum is at the shortest time.

The highest bar is at forty degrees Celsius. But the tests are ten degrees apart. The true optimum could be anywhere between thirty and fifty. To find it more precisely, test smaller steps, every two degrees, near the peak. Repeat each test and find a mean, which is the average. To compare two enzymes, quote data for both. This human gut enzyme has an optimum near thirty-seven degrees. The enzyme from bacteria in hot springs has a much higher optimum, about seventy degrees. Some questions ask about validity and accuracy. Validity: was it a fair test? Only the factor being tested should change. Accuracy: was the end point measured well? Judging by eye is subjective, which means it depends on the person judging. A colorimeter, which measures colour, would be better.

A common mistake is to pick the longest time as the optimum. For time data, the best value is the smallest. And always give the unit. Another mistake is to say the optimum is exactly a tested value, when the steps are ten degrees apart. And do not just say repeat. Say test smaller steps near the peak.

Your turn. A protease makes cloudy milk go clear. The times are: fifteen degrees, fifty minutes. Twenty-five degrees, thirty. Thirty-five degrees, twelve. Forty-five degrees, eighteen. Fifty-five degrees, sixty. Give the optimum, and suggest how to find it more precisely. Pause here and work it out.

These are times, so look for the shortest. Twelve minutes, at thirty-five degrees. The optimum is about thirty-five degrees Celsius. The steps are ten degrees apart, so the true optimum is between twenty-five and forty-five. Test every two degrees in that range. Repeat each test, and find the mean. So, every time: check whether the data show rate or time. Read the best value, with its unit. Then think about gaps, repeats and fair testing.
