Idea. How do we see an object? Light rays from the object enter the eye. They are bent, and they make an image on the retina at the back. In the retina, rods and cones are receptor cells. They turn light into electrical impulses. Sensory neurones, which are nerve cells, in the optic nerve carry these impulses to the brain. The brain makes sense of them, so we see the object the right way up.

Method. To draw the rays, use a ruler. Step one: put arrowheads on the rays, pointing into the eye, to show which way light travels. Step two: where each ray enters the cornea, bend it inwards, towards the middle. The cornea does most of the bending. Step three: at the lens, bend each ray inwards again. Step four: the rays cross after the lens, and end exactly on the retina. Step five: draw the image on the retina, upside down. The top of the object lands at the bottom of the image. If the optic nerve is damaged, for example by the disease glaucoma, fewer impulses, or none, reach the brain. So vision becomes blurred, or the person becomes blind.

Worked example. Now a real exam question. The diagram shows an arrow, a near object, in front of an eye. Two straight rays go from the arrow to the cornea. The question asks you to continue the rays to show the image on the retina. Five marks. Mark one: arrowheads pointing into the eye. Mark two: bend each ray inwards at the cornea. Mark three: bend each ray inwards again at the lens. Mark four: the rays cross over inside the eye, after the lens. Mark five: both rays end on the retina, with a small upside-down arrow drawn between them. This answer is by olevels.com, not an official mark scheme.

Common mistakes: rays that do not bend at the cornea, rays that cross before the lens or meet in front of the retina, and an image drawn the right way up. Another mistake is saying the optic nerve carries light, or the image, to the brain. It carries electrical impulses, from the retina to the brain.

Your turn. In an accident, a woman's optic nerve is cut. Her cornea, lens and retina are still healthy. (a) Name the two places where light is bent, and describe the image on her retina. (b) Explain why she cannot see with this eye, even though an image still forms. Pause here and work it out.

Answer. Light is bent at the cornea, and again at the lens. The image on the retina is upside down and smaller. The rods and cones still make impulses, but her optic nerve is cut, so no impulses reach the brain. The brain cannot interpret them, so she cannot see. So, every time: arrowheads, bend at the cornea, bend at the lens, cross, and an upside-down image on the retina. Then impulses go along the optic nerve to the brain.
