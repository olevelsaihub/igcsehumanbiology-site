In an enzyme practical, you see a result: a colour change, a film going clear, or a pH falling. To explain it, use a chain. Name the enzyme. Say what substrate it digests: the substrate is the substance the enzyme acts on. Name the product. Then link the product to what you saw. Many results also level off. The amount of product rises, then stays the same. This happens when the substrate runs out.

Lipase digests lipids, such as oil, into fatty acids and glycerol. Fatty acids are acids. pH measures how acidic a liquid is: the lower the pH, the more acidic. So as they are made, the pH falls. An indicator in the tube changes colour. Amylase digests starch into maltose, which is a sugar. So the Benedict's test turns from blue to brick red when heated. Saliva contains amylase. Pepsin is a protease. On photographic film, it digests the protein layer into amino acids. These are small and soluble, so they wash away, and the film goes clear. In Visking tubing, starch molecules are too big to pass through. Small sugar molecules can diffuse out. Diffusion is movement from where there is a lot to where there is less. So sugar is found outside the tubing only after amylase has digested the starch. Now the levelling off. At the start there is lots of substrate. An active site is the part of an enzyme that the substrate fits into. Many substrate molecules fit active sites, so the rate is high. Later, most substrate is used up. Fewer fit, so the rate falls, and the amount of product stays the same.

Now a real exam question. A student mixes olive oil, lipase, bile salts and a blue indicator. The indicator turns yellow below pH five. Explain why the pH decreases during the twenty-five hours. Point one: lipase digests the olive oil, which is a lipid. Point two: this makes fatty acids, and glycerol. Point three: fatty acids are acids. As more are made, the mixture gets more acidic, so the pH goes down. That is why the indicator turns yellow. Here is the full answer as a chain. A common mistake is to say the reaction stops because the enzyme is used up. Enzymes are not used up. The substrate is used up. Another mistake is to write only that the enzyme lowers the pH. Say what is made. Lipase makes fatty acids, and the fatty acids lower the pH.

Your turn. Amylase is added to starch at thirty-seven degrees Celsius. The amount of maltose rises fast at first. After ten minutes it stays the same. Explain the shape of the graph. Pause here and work it out.

At the start, amylase digests starch into maltose. There is lots of starch, so many molecules fit active sites, and maltose is made fast. After ten minutes, the starch is used up. The substrate has run out, so no more maltose is made, and the amount stays the same. So, every time: name the enzyme, the substrate and the product. Link the product to the result. For levelling off, say the substrate is used up.
