Idea. Results are reliable if you get similar results when you repeat the investigation. Here, three repeats give 12, 12 and 13 seconds. They are close together, so they are reliable. These three are spread out: 4, 12 and 20 seconds. They are not reliable. One person may be unusual. Their result could be very high or very low. Testing many people is better. Then you calculate a mean: add the results, then divide by how many there are. One unusual person has less effect on the mean.

Method. So, step one. Repeat each test at least three times, with the same conditions. Step two. Calculate a mean for each condition. Here, 12 plus 13 plus 12 is 37. Divide by 3 to get 12.3 seconds. Sometimes one result does not fit the pattern. This is an anomalous result, or an anomaly. Here, 25 seconds is far from the others. Leave it out when you calculate the mean, unless the question says to use every value. Including it pulls the mean up to 15.5. To show a result is true in general, widen the test. Test other ages and both sexes. With bacteria, test other types. Or compare with another group's data. You can also use more people in each group, with smaller gaps between ages. So, four steps. Find what was done only once, or with too few people. Say repeat it, or use more people. Add: calculate a mean, without anomalies. For a general conclusion, test other groups too.

Worked example. Here is a real exam question. The table shows the highest sound frequency heard by people of different ages. A higher frequency means a higher-pitched sound. It is measured in kilohertz. In the method, one person of each age is tested. Describe how the reliability of the results could be improved. It is worth two marks. Find what was done only once. Only one person of each age was tested. That person may hear unusually well or badly. So test several people of each age, and repeat the test for each person. That is mark one. Mark two: calculate a mean maximum frequency for each age. Another accepted pair is to check the results against another group's data. This answer is by olevels.com, not the official mark scheme.

Common mistake. The first mistake: writing only repeat it, in a two-mark question. Say what you do with the repeats. Calculate a mean. The second mistake: use more accurate equipment. That is about accuracy, how close a result is to the true value. Reliability is about getting similar results again. The third mistake: changing a condition and calling it a repeat. A repeat keeps every condition the same. Doing it again at 40 degrees Celsius instead of 30 is a new test, not a repeat. And never put an anomaly into the mean.

Your turn. A ruler drop test measures reaction time. Reaction time is how fast you respond. A partner drops a ruler, and you catch it. The distance it falls is recorded. One student did it once. Part a: describe how to make the results more reliable. Part b: one student's four repeats were 18, 17, 31 and 18 centimetres. Calculate the mean. Pause here and work it out.

Answer. Part a. The test was done once, on one student. So repeat the test at least three times for each student. Test many students. Then calculate a mean distance. Part b. The value 31 does not fit the pattern. It is an anomaly, so leave it out. 18 plus 17 plus 18 is 53. Divide by 3. The mean is 17.7 centimetres. So, every time. Repeat the test with the same conditions. Use more people. Calculate a mean without anomalies. Test other groups for a general conclusion.
