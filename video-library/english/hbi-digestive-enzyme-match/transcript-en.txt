Idea. Digestion breaks large food molecules into small ones. Small molecules can pass into the blood. Enzymes do this job. An enzyme is a protein that speeds up a reaction, and is not used up. The substrate is the molecule an enzyme acts on. Each enzyme has an active site, a dip in its surface. Only a substrate with the right shape fits it. So for every enzyme, learn three things. Its substrate: what it breaks down. Its product: what it makes. And its site: where it is made, or where it works.

Method. Start with carbohydrates. Amylase breaks starch into maltose. It is made in the salivary glands and the pancreas. Then maltase breaks maltose into glucose, in the small intestine. That is two steps, not one. Next, protein. Proteases break protein into amino acids. The stomach makes one called pepsin. The pancreas makes another, trypsin, which works in the small intestine. Last, lipids, which are fats and oils. Lipase breaks lipids into fatty acids and glycerol. That is two products. Lipase is made in the pancreas. Now the map. The mouth gets amylase from the salivary glands. The stomach makes pepsin. The pancreas makes amylase, protease and lipase. They all work in the small intestine, which also makes maltase. pH matters too. pH tells us how acid a liquid is: the lower the number, the more acid. Pepsin works best at about pH two, because the stomach is acid. Salivary amylase works best at about pH seven, which is neutral. So an enzyme that works best in acid probably comes from the stomach.

Worked example. Now a real exam question. The diagram shows enzyme P and two proteins, Q and R. Use the words in the box to complete the passage. Three gaps, three marks. The substrates are both proteins. So enzyme P is a protease. Which protein fits? The active site has a round dip, then a triangle. Protein Q has an oval, then a triangle. Protein R has an oval, then a square. Only Q fits. Now fill the gaps. Enzyme P belongs to the proteases. It breaks down protein Q. The products are amino acids. Common mistake. A common mistake is to say lipase turns fat into glucose. Only carbohydrates give glucose. Lipase gives fatty acids and glycerol. Another mistake is to call bile an enzyme, or to say the gall bladder makes enzymes. Bile is not an enzyme. And amylase is not made in the stomach.
