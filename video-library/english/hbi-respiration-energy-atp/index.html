Explain how respiration releases energy and ATP transfers it

Read the narration

Every living cell needs energy. It gets this energy from respiration. Respiration is a set of chemical reactions in cells that releases energy from glucose. Most of it happens in small parts of the cell called mitochondria. What is the energy for? Muscles use it to contract, so we can move. Cells use it to grow and divide, to make new molecules, and for active transport. Active transport moves substances from where there are few of them to where there are many. Some energy keeps us warm.

Here is how the energy gets from glucose to a muscle. Glucose is broken down in respiration, and energy is released. Say released. Energy is never made or produced. The cell does not use this energy straight away. It uses it to make ATP. ATP is a small molecule that carries energy to where it is needed. The energy joins a phosphate group onto ADP. A phosphate group is a small group of atoms. ADP plus phosphate makes ATP. When a muscle needs energy, ATP is broken down into ADP and phosphate again. This releases the energy. Now name the job. During exercise, the energy is used for muscle contraction. Then the ADP is turned back into ATP, again and again. It is a cycle. You can measure how fast a person respires. This is their metabolic rate. Measure the oxygen they take in, or the carbon dioxide they breathe out. In cold weather, metabolic rate goes up. More respiration releases more heat, to keep the body warm.

Now a real exam question. It says both kinds of respiration make ATP. Describe the role of ATP in energy transfer during exercise. It is worth five marks, so plan five separate points. Follow the cycle. Point one: energy is released from glucose. Point two: this happens in respiration. Point three: the energy is used to add a phosphate group to ADP. Point four: this forms ATP. Point five: during exercise, ATP is broken down. Point six: it splits into ADP and phosphate, and energy is released. Point seven: this energy is used for muscle contraction. Any five of these points would do. Here is the full answer as a chain. This explanation is by olevels.com, not an official mark scheme.

A very common mistake is to say respiration makes energy. Energy cannot be made. Respiration releases the energy that is in glucose. The second mistake is to mix up ADP and ATP. ATP has one more phosphate group. Adding phosphate to ADP makes ATP. Taking it away releases energy and leaves ADP. ATP is not a store of glucose.

Your turn. Cells in the kidney tubules take glucose back into the blood by active transport. This needs energy. Describe the role of ATP in supplying this energy. Pause here and work it out.

First, the kidney cells respire. Respiration releases energy from glucose. This energy adds a phosphate group to ADP, and ATP forms. Then the ATP is broken down into ADP and phosphate. This releases energy, and the energy is used for active transport of glucose. So, every time: respiration releases energy from glucose. The energy turns ADP into ATP. ATP breaks down and releases energy. Then name the job.
