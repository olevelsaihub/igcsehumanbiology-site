Read the narration

Food labels give amounts per one hundred grams. Per means for each. This made-up bread label says eight grams of protein in every one hundred grams of bread. But you never eat exactly one hundred grams. One slice is forty grams. So a slice has less than eight grams of protein. We must scale the amount to the new mass.

Step one: write the amount and its base. Eight grams per one hundred grams. Step two: find the amount in one gram. Divide by one hundred: zero point zero eight grams. Step three: multiply by the mass you have. A forty gram slice: zero point zero eight times forty is three point two grams. Sometimes you need the mass instead. Divide the target by the amount in one gram. For twenty grams of protein: twenty divided by zero point zero eight is two hundred and fifty grams of bread. A label may give a percentage of the daily need. Twenty milligrams is fifty per cent of the need. So the daily need is twenty times one hundred, divided by fifty: forty milligrams. Last, to combine foods, scale each food first, then add the amounts. Keep the units the same: milligrams with milligrams. To find how much more is needed, take what was eaten away from the daily need. Round as the question asks, and round up for whole pots.

Now a real exam question. The table shows the nutrients in yoghurt, per one hundred grams and per eighty-five gram pot. The protein cell for the pot is empty. Calculate the mass of protein in one pot, to two significant figures. Three marks. Read the protein row. Six point one grams per one hundred grams of yoghurt. Divide by one hundred. One gram of yoghurt has zero point zero six one grams of protein. Now multiply by eighty-five. Zero point zero six one times eighty-five is five point one eight five grams. Round to two significant figures: five point two grams. A significant figure is a digit that counts, starting from the first one that is not zero. Check: the pot is less than one hundred grams, so the answer must be less than six point one. It is. Here is the full working. It is by olevels.com, not an official mark scheme.

A common mistake is to multiply by eighty-five and forget to divide by one hundred. That gives five hundred and eighteen grams of protein in a small pot. Always check that your answer makes sense. Another mistake is to ignore the rounding instruction. Five point one eight has three significant figures. The question asked for two.

Your turn. Peanut butter has twenty-five grams of protein per one hundred grams. A sandwich uses thirty grams of peanut butter. Calculate the protein in the sandwich, to two significant figures. Pause here and work it out.

Twenty-five grams per one hundred grams. Divide by one hundred: zero point two five grams in each gram. Multiply by thirty: seven point five grams. Seven point five already has two significant figures. Check: thirty grams is under a third of one hundred grams, so the answer is under a third of twenty-five. It is. So, every time: write the amount and its base. Find the amount in one unit. Multiply or divide for the new amount. Then round, and give the unit.
