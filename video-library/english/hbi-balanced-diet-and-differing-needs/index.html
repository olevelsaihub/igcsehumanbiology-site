Define a balanced diet and explain why needs differ between people

Idea. A balanced diet has two parts. First, it contains all the food components: carbohydrate, protein, lipid, vitamins, minerals, fibre and water. Second, it has them in the right proportions. Proportion means how much of each one, compared with the others. It is not equal amounts. We need lots of carbohydrate, but only a little of each vitamin.

Method. Needs differ between people. Males usually need more energy than females. They have more muscle, they are often more active, and they have a higher metabolic rate. Metabolic rate means how fast the body uses energy. A pregnant woman needs more iron. The fetus is the baby growing inside her. Iron is used to make haemoglobin, the red substance in blood that carries oxygen. She needs it for the extra blood of the mother and the fetus. More haemoglobin carries more oxygen for respiration, which releases energy from glucose. That energy lets the fetus grow. She also needs more calcium. Calcium builds the bones and teeth of the growing fetus. Women need more iron than men. Menstruation is the monthly period, when a woman loses some blood. With the blood, she loses haemoglobin. The iron must be replaced. Age, work and climate matter too. Babies live on milk alone. Growing children need plenty of protein for growth. A person with an active job, or who lives in a cold place, needs more energy from food.

Worked example. Now a real exam question. Describe what is meant by a balanced diet. Two marks, so two ideas. Idea one: what is in it? All the nutrients: carbohydrate, protein, lipid, vitamins, minerals, fibre and water. Idea two: how much? Each one in the correct proportion for the person. A balanced diet contains all the nutrients in the correct proportions. This answer is by olevels.com, not an official mark scheme.

Common mistake. A common mistake is to say a balanced diet means equal amounts of every food. It does not. It means the right amount of each. Another mistake is a reason with no biology. Males do not need more energy because they eat more. And iron does not carry oxygen itself. Iron makes haemoglobin, which carries oxygen.

Your turn. Ali carries bricks all day on a building site, in a cold country. His brother works at a desk in a warm office. Who needs more energy from food? Explain why. Pause here and work it out.

Answer. Ali needs more energy. His muscles contract all day, so they respire more. Respiration uses glucose from food to release that energy. In the cold, his body also respires more to release heat, to keep warm. So he needs more food energy than his brother. So, every time: for a definition, give both halves. For a difference, say who needs more, then give the body reason.
