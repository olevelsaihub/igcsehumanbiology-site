Antibiotics are substances made by microorganisms, mainly fungi such as Penicillium, and some bacteria. An antibiotic kills bacteria, or stops them growing. So antibiotics treat bacterial diseases, such as pneumonia, cholera, gonorrhoea and TB. Antibiotics work on bacteria only. Viruses live inside our own cells, and they do not have the parts that antibiotics attack. So antibiotics do not treat Ebola or flu. They do not treat fungal infections, like athlete's foot, either.

Now resistance. Resistance means the bacteria are not killed by the antibiotic. It starts by chance. A random mutation, a change in the DNA of one bacterium, makes it resistant. The mutation happens before the antibiotic is used. Then the antibiotic is used. It kills the bacteria that are not resistant. The resistant bacterium survives. The survivor reproduces, and passes on the resistance gene. Over many generations, most of the population becomes resistant. Overuse of antibiotics, and patients who do not finish the course, help resistant bacteria to be selected. Resistant infections are hard to treat. MRSA is a strain of Staphylococcus aureus that resists many antibiotics, and it spreads in hospitals.

Here is a real exam question. Explain why it is not possible to treat athlete's foot using antibiotics. Two marks. First, the cause. Athlete's foot is caused by a fungus, not a bacterium. Second, antibiotics only act on bacteria. Link them. Antibiotics have no effect on fungi, so they cannot cure athlete's foot. Answer by olevels.com, not an official mark scheme. So the answer has two linked ideas.

A common mistake is to say bacteria become immune to an antibiotic. Immunity is about antibodies in your body. Bacteria become resistant. Another mistake is to say the antibiotic causes the mutation. The mutation is random, and it happens first. The antibiotic only kills the others.

Your turn. Sara has a throat infection caused by bacteria. She stops taking her antibiotics after two days, because she feels better. Explain how this could lead to resistant bacteria. Then explain why a doctor will not give her antibiotics for flu. Pause here and work it out.

After two days, the antibiotic has killed the bacteria that are easy to kill. The most resistant ones are still alive. If she stops, they survive, reproduce and pass on their resistance. Flu is caused by a virus. Antibiotics only work on bacteria, so they would have no effect, and using them adds to overuse. So, every time: check the pathogen type. Antibiotics act on bacteria only. For resistance: mutation, the others die, survivors reproduce, the population becomes resistant.
