Idea. Sex is decided by one pair of chromosomes. A chromosome is a long thread of DNA in the nucleus. Females have two X chromosomes: X X. Males have one X and one Y: X Y. The Y is much smaller. Haemophilia is a sex-linked condition: the blood does not clot well. An allele is one form of a gene. Sex-linked means the allele is on a sex chromosome, the X. Capital H is the normal allele. Small h causes haemophilia, and it is recessive. The Y has no allele for this gene.

Method. Step one: females. A female has two X chromosomes. Capital H, capital H is normal. Capital H, small h is a carrier: the dominant allele hides the small h, so she has no haemophilia. Small h, small h has haemophilia. Step two: males. A male has only one X. If it carries small h, he has haemophilia. There is no second allele to hide it, because the Y has none. So males are never carriers. Here is a cross. A carrier mother and a normal father. One child in four is a boy with haemophilia. No girl has it, but one in four is a carrier. Compare a Y-linked trait. The allele is on the Y, so only males can have it. A father passes his Y to every son, and never to a daughter.

Worked example. Here is a real exam question. Haemophilia is a sex-linked condition that stops the blood clotting. Explain why males are more likely to have haemophilia than females. Four marks. Point one: say where the allele is. It is on the X chromosome, and it is recessive. Point two: males are X Y. They have only one X, so one small h means haemophilia. Point three: females are X X. If one X has small h, the normal capital H hides it, so she is a carrier. Point four: a female needs two small h alleles to have haemophilia, so fewer females are affected. Common mistake. A common mistake is to say the haemophilia allele is on the Y. It is on the X. The Y has no allele for this gene. Another mistake is to say a male can be a carrier. A male has one X, so he either has the condition or he does not.

Your turn. Red-green colour blindness is also caused by a recessive allele on the X. A woman who is a carrier has children with a colour-blind man. Can a daughter be colour blind? Explain with a genetic diagram. Pause here and work it out.

Answer. The mother is a carrier: X capital B, X small b. The father is colour blind: X small b, Y. A gamete is a sex cell: an egg or a sperm. Her gametes are X capital B or X small b. His are X small b or Y. Fill the square. A daughter gets her father's X, which has small b. So if she also gets small b from her mother, she is colour blind. Yes: one child in four is a colour-blind girl. So, every time: the allele is on the X and recessive. Males have one X, so one allele shows. Females have two, so they are usually carriers.
