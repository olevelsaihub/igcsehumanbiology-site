Your body is built in levels. The smallest level is the cell. A specialised cell has features that suit it to one job. For example, a sperm cell has a tail to swim. Similar cells group together, and work together to do one job. This group is a tissue. Muscle tissue is one example. Then several different tissues work together to do a job. This is an organ. The heart is an organ. Its wall is mostly cardiac muscle tissue, but it has other tissues too.

For a definition, write two linked parts: what it is made of, and that it does one job. A tissue is similar cells, working together for one function. An epithelium is a layer of cells that lines a surface. Squamous epithelium lines the cheek. Its cells are flat and uneven, with no cilia, and the nucleus in the middle. Ciliated epithelium lines the trachea, the tube that carries air to the lungs. Its cells are tall, with cilia on top. Cilia are tiny hairs. The nucleus is near the base. There are three kinds of muscle tissue. Look for three features: stripes, branches, and the number of nuclei. Skeletal muscle moves your bones. It is striped, not branched, with many nuclei. Cardiac muscle is in the heart wall. It is striped and branched, with one nucleus in each cell. Smooth muscle has no stripes. Its cells are tapered, thin at each end, with one nucleus. It works without you thinking. It is in the gut wall, blood vessels, the uterus and the iris.

Here is a real exam question. It shows a human body cell. Part three asks: explain how body cells are organised to form organs. It is worth two marks. Two marks means two steps. Step one: cells of the same type group together and work together. This group is a tissue. Step two: several different tissues work together. They form an organ, such as the heart. A common mistake is to write that a tissue is just a group of cells. That misses two ideas: the cells are similar, and they do one job together. Another mistake is to jump straight from cells to organs. Another mistake: naming a muscle or a bone as a type of tissue. Those are organs. Give the full tissue name, such as cardiac muscle or squamous epithelium.

Your turn. The stomach wall has a lining of epithelium, a layer of connective tissue, and layers of smooth muscle. First, is the stomach a cell, a tissue or an organ? Explain. Second, describe what smooth muscle cells look like. Pause here and work it out.

Part a. The stomach is an organ. It is made of several different tissues: epithelium, connective tissue and smooth muscle. They work together to digest food. Part b. Smooth muscle cells have no stripes. Each cell is tapered, thin at both ends, with one nucleus. So, every time: definitions have two parts, what it is made of and its one job. Cells form tissues, and tissues form organs. Name tissues in full. For muscle, check stripes, branches and nuclei.
