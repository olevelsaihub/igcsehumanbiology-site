Idea. Tooth decay means a hole slowly forms in a tooth. It starts on the outside, in the enamel. Here is the top of a molar, cut in half. Enamel is on the outside, and dentine is under it. Molars decay most. Their grooves trap food. They have a large surface. And they are at the back, so they are hard to reach with a toothbrush.

Method. Here is how decay happens, in four steps. Step one: sugar from food is left on the teeth, in the grooves. Step two: bacteria live on the teeth in a sticky layer called plaque. Bacteria are tiny living cells. They feed on the sugar, and use it in respiration, the process that releases energy from food. Step three: this respiration makes acid, called lactic acid, right next to the tooth. Step four: the acid dissolves the enamel. Then it dissolves the softer dentine. A hole forms, called a cavity. If it reaches the nerve, it hurts. How do we stop it? Eat less sugar, so bacteria have less food. Brush and floss, to remove food and plaque. Visit the dentist, who finds and fills holes early. Fluoride is a mineral. In toothpaste or in water, it makes enamel stronger, so acid dissolves it less. Calcium and phosphate from a balanced diet build hard enamel too. Alcohol in mouthwash kills bacteria, so there is less plaque.

Worked example. Now a real exam question. Explain the role of bacteria in the process of tooth decay. Four marks, so four linked steps. Step one: bacteria on the teeth feed on sugar left from food. Step two: they use the sugar in respiration, to release energy. Step three: respiration produces lactic acid. Step four: the acid dissolves the enamel, and a cavity forms. Common mistake. A common mistake is to say sugar dissolves the teeth, or that bacteria eat the teeth. Neither is true. Bacteria feed on the sugar and make acid. The acid dissolves the enamel. Another mistake is to say fluoride kills bacteria. Fluoride makes enamel stronger, so it resists acid. It is the alcohol in mouthwash that kills bacteria.

Your turn. Town A adds fluoride to its tap water. Town B does not. Children in town A have fewer holes in their teeth. Explain why. Then explain why sipping sweet drinks all day causes more decay. Pause here and work it out.

Answer. Part a: fluoride makes the enamel stronger. So the acid made by bacteria dissolves it less, and fewer holes form. Part b: sipping all day leaves sugar on the teeth all day. So bacteria respire all day and keep making acid. The acid has more time to dissolve the enamel. So, every time: sugar on the teeth, bacteria respire it, acid is made, and the acid dissolves the enamel. For prevention, give each method with its reason.
