Draw or label a human cell as seen under the microscope

Idea. Under a light microscope, a human cell shows three parts. The cell membrane is the thin outer layer. It controls what goes in and out. The cytoplasm is the jelly inside, where reactions happen. The nucleus holds the DNA and controls the cell. Mitochondria and ribosomes are also there. They are organelles: small parts of a cell, each with its own job. They are very small. They are often left off a light microscope drawing. A human cell never has a cell wall, chloroplasts or a large vacuole. Those are plant cell parts.

Method. To draw a cheek cell, use a sharp pencil. Draw one clear outline, with no gaps and no shading. A cheek cell is flat and uneven in shape, so do not draw a neat circle. Put a clear nucleus near the middle. Then add labels. Draw each label line with a ruler, and make it touch the part. Label the outline cell membrane. Label the space around the nucleus cytoplasm. Label the nucleus. Some questions show a different cell. This cell lines the trachea, the tube to the lungs. It is tall and narrow. The tiny hairs on its top surface are cilia. It still has a nucleus and a cell membrane. If the parts have letters, follow each line to its tip. The tip shows the part. Line A crosses the nucleus, but its tip is in the cytoplasm. So A is the cytoplasm. Dark threads inside a nucleus are chromosomes.

Worked example. Here is a real exam question. Draw a labelled diagram of a cheek cell from inside a mouth, as seen under a light microscope. It is worth four marks. Four marks means four features: the shape, and three labels. First the shape. Draw one smooth outline that is a bit uneven, not a circle or a rectangle. Next, draw a clear, rounded nucleus near the middle of the cell. Now three ruled labels. Cell membrane touches the outline. Cytoplasm ends inside the cell, but outside the nucleus. Nucleus touches the nucleus. Last, check: there is no cell wall and no vacuole. That is the full answer. This answer is written by olevels.com. It is not the official mark scheme.

Common mistake. The biggest mistake is drawing a plant cell: a neat rectangle with a cell wall. A human cell has no cell wall. Its outer line is the cell membrane. Another mistake is to draw a good cell with no labels. Then only the shape mark can be earned. Also, never let a label line end in empty space.

Your turn. A student drew a cell from the lining of the stomach. The labels were cell wall, nucleus and vacuole. First, give two mistakes in these labels. Second, name one part in the cell that is too small to show on the drawing. Pause here and work it out.

Answer. Part a. The nucleus label is correct. Cell wall is wrong: a human cell has no cell wall, so that line is the cell membrane. Vacuole is wrong too: a human cell has no large vacuole. That space is the cytoplasm. Part b. Name a small organelle, such as mitochondria, ribosomes or the endoplasmic reticulum. One is enough for the mark. So, every time: one clear uneven outline, a nucleus near the middle, and three ruled labels that touch their parts. Never a cell wall in a human cell.
