An antigen is a chemical, usually a protein, on the surface of a cell or a pathogen. A pathogen is a microorganism that causes disease. Your own cells have antigens too. Your immune system uses antigens to tell your own cells from foreign ones. An antibody is a protein made by lymphocytes, a type of white blood cell. It is not a cell. It is shaped like a Y. The tips of the Y are the binding sites, also called the variable region.

Each antibody's binding site has one particular shape. It is complementary to one antigen: the two shapes fit together, like a key in a lock. So this antibody binds to the triangle antigens. It cannot bind to the square ones. A different antibody has a different binding site, so it fits the square antigens instead. Each antibody binds to one type of antigen only. This is why the reaction is specific. The bound pathogens are then destroyed, for example by phagocytes: white blood cells that engulf pathogens. Sometimes the body treats its own antigens as foreign. This is autoimmune disease, such as coeliac disease. Antibodies attach to its own cells and damage them. Proteins from another species, such as insulin taken from animals, can also act as foreign antigens. Scientists can make lots of one antibody. Inject a mouse with the antigen. Take lymphocytes from its spleen. Fuse them with tumour cells, which keep dividing, to make hybridoma cells. Clone the hybridomas. They make large amounts of one antibody, called a monoclonal antibody.

Here is a real exam question. Lymphocytes produce antibodies in response to specific antigens. Explain why the reaction between an antigen and an antibody is specific. Three marks. Point one: each antibody has a binding site, called the variable region. Point two: this region has its own shape, different in each type of antibody. Point three: the shape is complementary to one antigen. Point four: so the antibody can bind only to that antigen, and to no other. Answer by olevels.com, not an official mark scheme. A common mistake is to say the antibody has the same shape as the antigen. It has a complementary shape: the two fit together. Another mistake is to call antibodies cells that engulf bacteria. Phagocytes engulf. Antibodies are proteins. And do not mix up antibody and antibiotic.

Your turn. A pregnancy test uses antibodies that bind to a hormone called H C G. This hormone is made only in pregnancy. Explain why the antibodies bind to H C G, but not to other hormones in urine. Pause here and work it out.

The antibody has a binding site, the variable region, with its own shape. That shape is complementary to the shape of H C G. Other hormones have different shapes. They do not fit the binding site. So the antibody binds to H C G only. So, every time: binding site, its own shape, complementary to one antigen, so it binds to that antigen only.
