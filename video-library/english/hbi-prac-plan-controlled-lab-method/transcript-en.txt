Idea. To plan a method, write steps that someone else could follow. Change one factor only: the independent variable. Use at least five values, for example 20, 30, 40, 50 and 60 degrees Celsius. Five values show the pattern.

Method. Step one. Set up equal starting materials. Amylase is an enzyme, a protein that speeds up a reaction. It digests starch. Put 5 cubic centimetres of 1 per cent starch solution in one tube. Put 1 cubic centimetre of amylase solution in another. Use the same volumes every time. Step two. Stand both tubes in a water bath at the test temperature for five minutes. A water bath keeps the temperature steady. The solutions must reach that temperature before you mix them. Step three. Add the amylase to the starch, and start a stopwatch at once. Step four. Every 30 seconds, put one drop of the mixture into iodine on a spotting tile. Iodine is blue-black when starch is there. Record the time when it stays orange-brown. All the starch has been digested. Step five. Repeat the test at each temperature. Do each one three times, and work out a mean time. Other plans use the same steps. For osmosis, the movement of water through a partially permeable membrane, from a dilute solution to a more concentrated one, cut potato pieces of the same size and weigh them. Leave each in a different sugar solution for 30 minutes. Blot them dry, and weigh again. Some questions ask what extra information is needed to repeat a method. List what is missing: the volume and concentration of each solution, and how the result was measured.

Worked example. Here is a real exam question. Students investigate how temperature affects enzyme activity. The graph shows activity rising to a peak, then falling. You must design a method that would give these results. It is worth five marks. Choose an enzyme you can test, like amylase. Put 5 cubic centimetres of starch solution in a test tube, and 1 cubic centimetre of amylase in another. Stand both in a water bath at 20 degrees Celsius for five minutes. Add the amylase to the starch, and start a stopwatch. Every 30 seconds, test a drop with iodine. Record the time when the iodine stays orange-brown. Repeat at 30, 40, 50 and 60 degrees Celsius. Use the same volume and concentration of starch and amylase each time. This answer is by olevels.com, not an official mark scheme.

Common mistake. A common mistake is writing add some enzyme. Some is not a measurement. Give the volume. Another mistake is heating the tube with a Bunsen burner. The temperature will not stay steady. Use a water bath, and warm both solutions before mixing.

Your turn. Agar is a jelly. These agar cubes contain an indicator, a dye that changes colour. It is pink. When acid diffuses in, the cube goes colourless. Diffusion is the spreading of particles from where there are many to where there are few. Design a method to find how temperature affects the time for a cube to go colourless. Pause here and work it out.

Answer. Cut cubes of the same size, for example 1 centimetre on each side. Put the same volume and concentration of acid in five beakers. Warm them in water baths to 20, 30, 40, 50 and 60 degrees Celsius. Drop a cube into the acid and start a stopwatch. Record the time when the cube goes colourless. Repeat three times at each temperature, and work out a mean. So, every time: change one factor over five values. Keep the volumes and concentrations the same. Use a water bath. Measure a number, and repeat.
