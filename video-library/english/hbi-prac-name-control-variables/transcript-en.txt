Idea. A variable is any factor that can change, like temperature or volume. An investigation has three kinds. The independent variable is the one you change. The dependent variable is the one you measure. Control variables are the ones you keep the same. Why keep them the same? Suppose a student changes the temperature, but the pH changes too. The results change. Which factor caused it? You cannot tell. So only the independent variable may change. Everything else is controlled.

Method. Step one. Find the two variables that can never be controls. An enzyme is a protein that speeds up a reaction. Amylase is an enzyme that digests starch. A student tests it at different temperatures, and times how long the starch takes to disappear. Temperature is the independent variable. Time is the dependent variable. Step two. List every other quantity in the method. Here, keep the volume and concentration of each solution the same. Concentration means how much is dissolved in the water. Step three. Add factors that the method forgets. Enzymes are affected by pH, so keep the pH the same with a buffer, a solution that holds the pH steady. Temperature is usually a control too. Not here, because temperature is being changed. Step four. Write each control as a factor you could measure, one on each line. Write volume of starch solution, not amount of starch. Never write same conditions, or fair test. They do not name a factor. Some investigations are on people, for example how exercise changes heart rate. Heart rate is the number of heartbeats in one minute. Use people of the same age and sex, or the same person each time. Keep the type and length of exercise the same, and the food and drink before the test.

Worked example. Now a real exam question. A student studies the enzyme phosphatase with an inhibitor, a substance that slows an enzyme down. The method uses six concentrations of the substrate, the substance the enzyme acts on. The question asks for three variables to keep constant. First, find the two variables that cannot be answers. Six concentrations of substrate are made, so substrate concentration is the independent variable. The pink colour is read with a colorimeter, a machine that measures colour. That is the dependent variable. The volumes are already fixed in the method. So think about what else changes how fast an enzyme works. Temperature. pH. And the concentration of the enzyme. The concentration of the inhibitor would also earn a mark. So the answer is three precise factors, one on each line. One: temperature. Two: pH. Three: concentration of the enzyme. This answer is by olevels.com, not an official mark scheme.

Common mistake. A common mistake is giving the independent variable as a control. In this question, the substrate concentration is changed on purpose. So it can never be one of your answers. Another mistake is vague words. Amount is often refused, and same conditions names nothing. Write volume or mass, and name the factor.

Your turn. A student burns a piece of food under a tube of water. She measures how much the water temperature rises. She does this for four different foods. State three variables she should keep the same. Pause here and work it out.

Answer. The independent variable is the type of food. The dependent variable is the rise in temperature of the water. So neither of these can be an answer. Now the controls. Keep the mass of each piece of food the same. Keep the volume of water the same. Keep the distance between the food and the tube the same. So, every time: cross off the independent and dependent variables. List the other quantities in the method. Add temperature or pH. Then name each factor precisely.
