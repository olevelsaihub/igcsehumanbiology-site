Read the narration

Every infection follows the same course. First, infection: a pathogen enters the body. A pathogen is a microorganism that causes disease. At first, only a few enter. Then they multiply. While they multiply, there are no symptoms yet. This time is called incubation. Symptoms appear only when there are enough pathogens to cause harm. That is why symptoms come days or weeks after infection.

Step one: find the route into the body. Cholera comes in water or food with faeces from an infected person. Ebola spreads in body fluids. HIV and gonorrhoea spread by sex, and HIV also in blood. Pinworm eggs go from hands to mouth. Malaria comes from a mosquito bite. Step two: block each route with a barrier. Clean water and sewage treatment stop cholera. Protective clothing and isolation stop Ebola. Condoms and screened blood stop HIV. Hand washing stops pinworm. Nets stop mosquitoes. Donated blood is screened only for pathogens that travel in blood, such as HIV, not for genetic conditions. Some questions want the chain. Pinworm eggs are laid round the anus. Scratching puts them on fingers. Then on surfaces and food, and into someone else's mouth. Hand washing, short nails and clean bedding break the chain. Step four: treatment. Antibiotics kill bacteria, so they treat gonorrhoea. They do not work on viruses or fungi. HIV has no cure, but drugs slow it. Ebola patients get fluids. A reservoir is an animal that carries a pathogen without being ill, like fruit bats for Ebola.

Here is a real exam question. Ebola is a disease that has become an epidemic in some countries. State three precautions that can be taken to prevent the spread of Ebola. Three marks. First, the route. Ebola spreads through body fluids of infected people, and from dead bodies. Barrier one: health workers wear protective clothing, gloves, masks and goggles. Barrier two: isolate infected people, away from healthy people. Barrier three: bury people who die from Ebola safely, without touching the body. Three different ideas. This answer is by olevels.com, not an official mark scheme. So the answer has three lines, each a different barrier.

A common mistake is to give the same idea twice. 'Wash hands' and 'good hygiene' are one idea, so they score one mark. Another mistake is to say symptoms are late because the virus is spreading round the body. Say that it is multiplying, until there are enough to cause harm.

Your turn. Typhoid is caused by a bacterium in food or water with faeces in it. Houseflies can carry it onto food. State three ways to stop typhoid spreading. Then explain why symptoms appear one to two weeks after infection. Pause here and work it out.

Block each route. One: boil or treat drinking water. Two: cover food, and kill or keep away the flies. Three: wash hands after using the toilet, and treat sewage. Only a few bacteria enter at first. During incubation they multiply. Symptoms appear only when there are enough of them to cause harm, and that takes time. So, every time: find the route in, put one barrier on each route, and explain delays by multiplying during incubation.
