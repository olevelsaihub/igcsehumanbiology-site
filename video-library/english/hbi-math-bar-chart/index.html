Read the narration

A bar chart shows data in separate groups, called categories. Here, the categories are the ways pupils travel to school: walk, cycle, bus and car. The height of each bar shows how many pupils. Why a bar chart? The data are discrete. Discrete means separate groups, with nothing in between. Time or temperature are continuous: every value in between is possible. Continuous data go on a line graph.

Step one. Put the categories on the x-axis, and the values on the y-axis. Label the y-axis with the quantity and its unit, like energy in kilojoules per gram. Step two. Choose a scale with equal steps, starting at zero. It must use more than half of the grid. These are made-up example values for four foods. The biggest value is 15, so steps of 2 up to 16 fill the grid well. Step three. Draw each bar to the right height with a ruler. Be accurate to half a small square. Step four. Make every bar the same width, with equal gaps between them. Then write the name of each category under its bar.

Here is a real exam question. The table shows the chance of a baby being born with a chromosome aberration, for mothers of different ages. A chromosome aberration is a change to a whole chromosome. Plot a bar chart of the results. The largest value is 0.0140. The grid is 8 medium squares tall. If each medium square is 0.002, then 0.0140 needs 7 of them. That fills most of the grid, with equal steps from zero. Now draw six bars of equal width, with equal gaps. The first three are 0.0010. Then 0.0015, then 0.0045. The last one, for mothers aged 40 to 44, is 0.0140. Both axes are labelled: age of mother in years, and probability of chromosome aberration. Each bar has its age group. A common mistake is drawing a line graph for categories. The age groups are separate, so the data need bars. Another mistake is giving the reason for a bar chart as it is easier to read. The reason is that the data are in separate categories, so they are discrete.

Your turn. A class records how many pupils have each blood group: O 14, A 10, B 4, and AB 2. Describe how you would plot a bar chart of these results, and say why a bar chart is suitable. Pause here and work it out.

Blood groups go on the x-axis. Number of pupils goes on the y-axis. The biggest value is 14, so use steps of 2 from zero up to 16. Draw four bars of equal width: 14, 10, 4 and 2. Name each bar. A bar chart suits these data because blood groups are separate categories. So, every time: categories across, values up. Label the axes with units. Use an even scale from zero. Draw equal bars to the right heights.
