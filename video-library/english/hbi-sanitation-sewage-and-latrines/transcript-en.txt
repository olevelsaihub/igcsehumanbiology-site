Sewage is the waste water from toilets, sinks and drains. It carries faeces, urine and pathogens. A pathogen is a microorganism that causes disease. In drinking water, they spread diseases such as cholera. So a sewage works cleans it before the water goes back to a river. Decomposers are bacteria and fungi that feed on dead organic matter. Organic matter is material from living things, such as faeces or dead leaves. Decomposers release enzymes, proteins that speed up reactions, which break it down. This returns nutrients, such as nitrates, to the soil for plants.

Follow the sewage in order. Raw sewage first passes through a screen, a grid of metal bars. The screen traps large objects such as rags, plastic and grit. Next, in a settlement tank, solids sink to the bottom. This layer is called sludge. The liquid goes to the activated sludge tank. Here, air is bubbled in. The tank is full of aerobic bacteria and other microorganisms. Respiration releases energy from food. Aerobic respiration uses oxygen. They break down the organic matter, so clean effluent, the treated liquid, flows out to a river. The sludge goes to the anaerobic digester. Anaerobic means without oxygen. Anaerobic bacteria break down the sludge and make biogas. Biogas is mainly methane, a fuel. The treated sludge contains nitrates and other minerals, so it is used as fertiliser. Where there is no sewage works, people use a pit latrine. It is a toilet over a deep hole in the ground. Build it downhill from the well, so rain and waste flow away from drinking water. Dig it in permeable soil, which water soaks through, so urine drains away. A cover over the pit keeps out flies and rats. They can carry pathogens to food. The cover also reduces the smell. A long handle keeps hands away from the waste in the pit.

Now a real exam question. The diagram shows a sewage works. The question asks you to explain why air is added at stage Y. It is worth two marks. Stage Y is the activated sludge tank. Air bubbles up from a pipe at the bottom. Point one: the tank contains bacteria and other microorganisms. They break down the organic matter in the sewage. Point two: these microorganisms need oxygen for aerobic respiration. The air supplies the oxygen. So they can respire, grow and break down the waste quickly. Here is the full answer. A common mistake is to say air is added to kill the bacteria. The air keeps the bacteria alive, so they can respire and break down the sewage. Next, biogas is mainly methane, not carbon dioxide. And the screen removes only large objects. It cannot remove bacteria or dissolved substances. Last, a latrine goes downhill so that waste cannot reach the well, not because it is easier to dig. And decomposers do not make fertiliser. They break down dead matter and release nutrients.

Your turn. A village builds a pit latrine on a hillside. The well is halfway down the hill. Site A is above the well. Site B is below it. Which site should they choose, and why? Then explain why the latrine needs a cover. Pause here and work it out.

Choose site B. Rain and waste flow downhill, away from the well. From site A, waste would flow towards the well, carrying pathogens into the water. The latrine needs a cover. Flies and rats cannot reach the waste, so they cannot carry pathogens to food. The cover also reduces the smell. So, every time: find the stage or the feature. Name who is involved. Give the reason.
