Idea. An enzyme is a protein that speeds up a reaction, and is not used up. A free enzyme floats in the solution, so it mixes into the product. An immobilised enzyme is fixed in place. It is trapped inside small beads of alginate, a jelly made from seaweed. Milk contains lactose, a sugar. Some people cannot digest it. So milk is poured through a column of beads that hold the enzyme lactase. Lactase breaks lactose into glucose and galactose. Lactose-free milk comes out at the bottom.

Method. Making the beads. Step one: mix sodium alginate with distilled water, until it has fully dissolved. Step two: add the enzyme, and mix. Step three: drip the mixture from a syringe into calcium chloride solution. Each drop sets into a small bead, with the enzyme trapped inside. Step four: wash the beads with water. Step five: name the enzyme and its substrate, the substance it works on. Lactase breaks lactose into glucose and galactose. Sucrase breaks sucrose into glucose and fructose. Names ending in o-s-e are sugars; names ending in a-s-e are enzymes. Step six: give different advantages. The enzyme can be reused, so less is bought and it is cheaper. The product has no enzyme in it, so it needs no cleaning. The enzyme is more stable over a wider range of temperature and pH. Small beads give a large surface area, so the reaction is fast.

Worked example. Now a real exam question. Immobilised enzymes are used in industry to make useful molecules. Give two advantages of using immobilised enzymes instead of enzymes that are free in solution. Two marks. Advantage one: the enzyme stays trapped in the beads, so it can be used again and again. Less enzyme is bought, which saves money. Advantage two: the enzyme does not mix into the product. So the product is pure, and no cleaning step is needed. That is a different idea, so it earns the second mark. Here is a full answer. This answer is by olevels.com, not an official mark scheme.

Common mistake. A common mistake is to say the enzyme is used up. Enzymes are never used up. Another is to write it is better, or easier, with no reason. Always say why. Also, do not mix up lactose and lactase. Reusable and can be used again are the same idea: they score only once. And when you describe bead making, do not leave out calcium chloride.

Your turn. A sweet factory pours sucrose solution through a column of sucrase beads, to make a syrup. Name the two products that come out. A worker drips the alginate and enzyme mixture into pure water, and no beads form. Explain why. Give one reason the column saves the factory money. Pause here and work it out.

Answer. Part a: sucrase breaks sucrose into glucose and fructose. Those two come out at the bottom. Part c: the sucrase stays in the beads and is reused, so the factory buys less enzyme. Part b: the beads only form in calcium chloride solution. The calcium chloride makes the alginate set into a jelly. In pure water, the drops do not set, so no beads form and the enzyme is not trapped. So, every time: name the enzyme and substrate, give the bead steps in order, and give different advantages, each with a reason.
