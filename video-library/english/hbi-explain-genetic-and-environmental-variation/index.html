Explain genetic and environmental causes of variation

Read the narration

Variation means the differences between people of the same species. A species is a group of living things that can breed together. Look around a class. Some people are taller. Some have a different blood group or eye colour. Variation has two causes. Genetic variation, such as blood group or eye colour, comes from the alleles you inherit from your parents. A gene is a section of DNA that controls a feature. An allele is one version of a gene. Environmental variation comes from your surroundings and lifestyle, such as diet, exercise, disease or accidents.

Step one: decide the cause. Blood group is set only by your alleles, so it is genetic. A scar from an accident is environmental. Height depends on both: your alleles set a range, and diet affects where you end up. Body mass is also both. Step two: if you must list factors, give different ones, such as genes, sex, age and nutrition. Food, diet and nutrition count as one factor, not three. Step three: in a passage, find the clue. Runs in families, or starts in childhood with no known cause, point to genes. Diet, stress, infection, the menopause, or another illness such as diabetes, point to the environment. The menopause is when a woman's periods stop for good. Step four: explain the clue. If a condition runs in families, a faulty allele is passed on. Faulty means mutated, or changed. Parents pass alleles to their children in gametes. A gamete is a sex cell: a sperm or an egg. Step five is for evolution questions. Lactose is the sugar in milk. Where people drank a lot of milk, those with the allele to digest lactose had an advantage. They survived and had more children. So the allele became more common over many generations.

Now a real exam question. The passage describes hyperhidrosis, which means sweating too much. The question asks you to explain the evidence that one type, primary focal hyperhidrosis, is a genetic condition. Three marks. Find the clue. The passage says it usually begins in childhood and often runs in families. Point one: quote that evidence. Point two: this suggests a faulty, mutated allele of a gene. Point three: the allele is passed from parents to their children. Here is the full answer. This answer is by olevels.com, not an official mark scheme.

A common mistake is to say a condition is genetic because it is common. Being common is not evidence. Another is to write only: it runs in families. You must link it to an allele being passed on. Another mistake is to say people chose to drink milk, so they evolved. People cannot choose their alleles. The allele gave an advantage, so it spread.

Your turn. A report says: colour blindness has affected many members of one family over three generations. Skin cancer is more common in people who spend many hours in strong sunlight. Which condition is genetic, and which is environmental? Explain the evidence for each. Pause here and work it out.

Colour blindness is genetic. The evidence is that it affects one family over three generations. So a faulty allele is passed from parents to their children. Skin cancer here is environmental. The clue is strong sunlight, a factor in the surroundings. It is more common in people who spend many hours in the sun. So, every time: decide if the cause is genetic, environmental, or both. Quote the clue from the passage. Then say what it means: an allele passed on, or a factor in the surroundings.
