Idea. Food travels through one long tube, called the alimentary canal. It runs from the mouth to the anus. In order: mouth, oesophagus, stomach, small intestine, large intestine, and out through the anus. Three more organs join the tube by small tubes called ducts: the liver, the gall bladder and the pancreas. Food never passes through them. They send juices into the gut.

Method. Now the jobs. Digestion means breaking large food molecules into small ones. In the mouth, teeth chew food into small pieces. Saliva is mixed in. Saliva has amylase, an enzyme: a protein that speeds up digestion. It starts to digest starch into maltose. The oesophagus carries food down to the stomach. The stomach makes hydrochloric acid, so its pH is about two. It also makes pepsin, a protease. Protein digestion starts here. The pancreas sends pancreatic juice into the first part of the small intestine, the duodenum. The juice has protease, amylase and lipase. The liver makes bile. The gall bladder stores it, and it also flows into the duodenum. The small intestine finishes digestion. Then it absorbs the small food molecules into the blood, through tiny folds called villi. It is the long, narrow, coiled tube in the middle. The large intestine is wider, and runs round the outside of the coils. It absorbs most of the water that is left. Without it, a person loses too much water and becomes dehydrated: the body has too little water. The faeces leave through the anus.

Worked example. Now a real exam question. The diagram shows the digestive system, with four labels: W, X, Y and Z. Name the four structures. Four marks. Here is the diagram. The letters X and Y are on the upper part of the tube. W and Z are on the lower part. Start at the mouth and follow the tube down. X is the narrow tube going down through the chest: the oesophagus. Y ends on the bag that the oesophagus goes into: the stomach. Z ends on the many tight coils in the middle: the small intestine. W ends on the wider tube round the outside: the large intestine. Common mistake. A common mistake is to call the oesophagus the trachea, or the windpipe. The trachea carries air to the lungs. The oesophagus carries food to the stomach. Another mistake is to swap the two intestines. The small intestine is long and narrow, coiled in the middle. The large intestine is wider, and runs round the outside.

Answer. Part a: from the mouth, the tube goes down the oesophagus. Next it goes into the stomach, and then into the duodenum. Part b: lipids, which are fats and oils. Lipase comes only from the pancreas. Starch and protein are still partly digested, by amylase in saliva and by pepsin in the stomach. So, every time: start at the mouth and follow the tube down.
