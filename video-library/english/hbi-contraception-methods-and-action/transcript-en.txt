Idea. Contraception, or birth control, stops a pregnancy. Look at the female system. Sperm swim up from the vagina, through the uterus, into the oviduct. There they meet an egg from the ovary. So a method can block one of three steps. One: sperm reaching the egg. Two: ovulation, the release of an egg. Three: implantation, when the embryo, the young ball of cells, sinks into the uterus lining.

Method. Barrier methods stop sperm reaching the egg. Examples are the condom, the femidom, which is a female condom, and the diaphragm or cap, which covers the cervix. Condoms also stop the spread of infections like HIV. Hormonal methods use hormones like oestrogen and progesterone. The contraceptive pill stops ovulation, so no egg is released. Implants and injections work in a similar way. An IUD is a small device that a doctor places in the uterus. It is also called a coil. It stops implantation. It can last for years. Natural methods use no device or drug. In the rhythm method, a couple avoid sex on the days of the cycle around ovulation. Natural methods are the least reliable. Sterilisation is surgery. In a woman, the oviducts are cut and tied, so sperm cannot reach the egg. It is very reliable, but usually permanent. In a man, a vasectomy cuts the sperm ducts. No sperm leave the body. But the glands still make semen, so HIV in that fluid can still pass to a partner. Some questions ask you to compare two methods, often with a table of data. Give points for and against each one. Condoms are cheap and stop infections, but they can split. The pill is very reliable, but it must be taken every day, and it can have side effects. Use the figures from the table. Then finish with a judgement: which method is better here, and why.

Worked example. Now a real exam question. Complete the table by giving an example of each method of birth control: barrier, natural and hormonal. Three marks. Three rows, three marks. Barrier: something that blocks sperm. I choose a condom. Natural: no device or drug. I choose the rhythm method. Hormonal: a drug that contains hormones. I choose the contraceptive pill. Common mistake. A common mistake is to put a method in the wrong group, like the pill as a barrier. The pill is hormonal. A barrier physically blocks sperm. Another mistake is to say all methods protect against HIV, or that a vasectomy stops semen. Only barriers protect, and semen is still made.

Your turn. The first couple, couple A, want a method that also protects them from HIV. The second couple, couple B, want a method that works for years, without remembering it every day. Suggest a method for each, and explain how it works. Pause here and work it out.

Answer. Couple A: a condom. It is a barrier, so sperm cannot reach the egg, and body fluids do not pass, which protects from HIV. Couple B: an IUD that stops implantation, and lasts for years. So every time: name the group. Barrier blocks sperm. Hormonal stops ovulation. An IUD stops implantation. And only barriers protect against infections.
