Explain the effects and dangers of alcohol and other drugs

Read the narration

Idea. A drug is a substance that changes how the body works. Some drugs are legal, like alcohol, caffeine and paracetamol. Some are illegal, like heroin, cocaine and cannabis. Many drugs act on the nervous system. A depressant, like alcohol, slows it down, so reaction time gets longer. A stimulant, like caffeine, speeds it up, so reaction time gets shorter.

Method. Step one: name the drug's type. Step two: say what it does. Step three: link that to the danger in the question. For alcohol and driving: a depressant, slower reactions, so accidents are more likely. This graph shows blood alcohol after a drink. It rises steeply at first, because alcohol is absorbed quickly from the stomach into the blood. Then it falls slowly, because the liver breaks the alcohol down slowly. Heavy drinking for many years damages the liver. It becomes scarred, called cirrhosis. Then it cannot remove toxins or make bile, and liver cancer can follow. Reaction time can be measured with a falling ruler. One person drops it without warning, and the other catches it. The shorter the distance it falls, the faster the reaction. Other drugs have serious dangers. Cocaine can cause addiction, heart problems, stroke, anxiety and death from overdose. Addiction means the person cannot stop taking the drug. An overdose is too much of the drug at once. Heroin is very addictive, and an overdose can stop breathing. Cannabis can cause anxiety and mental illness. Painkillers, like paracetamol, reduce the pain signals reaching the brain. A tablet that dissolves faster works sooner.

Worked example. Now a real exam question. The graph shows blood alcohol after drinking. The legal limit for driving is less than eighty milligrams per hundred millilitres. The question asks why it is against the law to drive with a high level of alcohol in the blood. Four marks. Idea one: alcohol is a depressant. Idea two: it slows the nervous system, so the driver's reaction time is longer. Idea three: the driver is less alert, and cannot respond quickly to danger, for example by braking in time. Idea four: so the driver is much more likely to cause an accident, and hurt people. This answer is by olevels.com, not an official mark scheme.

Common mistake. A common mistake is to call alcohol a stimulant, or to say it speeds up reactions. It is a depressant. Another is to write only that alcohol makes you drunk. Explain the slower reactions instead. Also: the steep rise just after drinking is because alcohol is absorbed from the stomach. And do not give the same danger twice in different words, like heart attack and heart problems.

Your turn. A student does the ruler-drop test. The ruler falls eighteen centimetres before she catches it. After two cups of strong coffee, it falls only twelve centimetres. Explain this change. Pause here and work it out.

Answer. Coffee contains caffeine, which is a stimulant. It speeds up the nervous system, so her reaction time is shorter. She catches the ruler sooner, so it falls a shorter distance. So, every time: name the drug type, say what it does to the nervous system or an organ, then link it to the danger.
