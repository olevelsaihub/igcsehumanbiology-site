Idea. When you describe data, start with the trend. A trend is the overall direction of the data. It can increase. It can decrease. Or it can rise and then fall. Always say which way it goes. Then link both variables in one sentence. A variable is a thing that is changed or measured. For example: as light intensity increases, the diameter of the pupil decreases.

Method. Here is a made-up example. A student runs for ten minutes and measures her pulse rate. Pulse rate is the number of heartbeats each minute. Step one: read the axes. Step two: give the overall trend. As time goes on, her pulse rate increases. Step three: find where the shape changes. After six minutes, the pulse stops rising. It levels off. Step four: quote data with units. It rises from seventy to one hundred and forty beats per minute, then stays the same. Step five: compare groups directly. A trained athlete does the same run. Both pulse rates rise, then level off. But the athlete's pulse is lower all the time. It also rises less: by forty-five, not by seventy. Step six: a conclusion must come from the data. Write that the data suggest something, and give the evidence. Never write that the data prove it. Only give a reason if the question says explain.

Worked example. Now a real exam question. The table shows the time an enzyme takes to digest starch at different pH values. The student plots a graph. Then the question asks: describe the trend shown by the graph. It is worth two marks. Here is the table. At pH 2, the enzyme takes 60 minutes. At pH 4, 45 minutes. At pH 6, 20 minutes. At pH 8, 35 minutes. At pH 10, 55 minutes. Plot the five points. The pH goes along the bottom. The time goes up the side. A short time means the enzyme works fast. From pH 2 to pH 6, the time falls, from sixty minutes to twenty minutes. The lowest point is twenty minutes, at pH 6. This is the turning point, where the trend changes. Name it in your answer. After pH 6, the time rises again as the pH increases. It reaches fifty-five minutes at pH 10. So the answer has two parts: falls, then rises, with pH 6 between them. Here is the full answer. Common mistake. A common mistake is to write only that it changes. Say which way it goes. Then say where the shape changes, and give a number there. Another mistake is to describe two groups one at a time. Compare them in the same sentence. Say which group is higher, or which one changes more.

Your turn. A nurse measures a woman's blood glucose after a sugary drink. The table shows her results. Describe the trend in her blood glucose. Pause here and work it out.

Answer. Plot the points first. At first, her blood glucose rises. It goes up from ninety to a peak of one hundred and sixty milligrams per hundred cubic centimetres, at sixty minutes. Then the shape changes. After sixty minutes, her blood glucose falls. It is back to ninety by one hundred and twenty minutes. So, every time: read the axes. Give the trend with both variables. Find where the shape changes. Quote data with units. Compare groups directly.
