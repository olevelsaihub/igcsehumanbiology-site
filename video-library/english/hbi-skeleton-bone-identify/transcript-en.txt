Your skeleton is the frame of bones inside your body. In the exam you often see a drawing of some bones, and you must name them. Position is your best clue. Look at the arm. The upper arm has one bone. The forearm, between the elbow and the wrist, has two bones side by side. Then come the wrist, the hand and the thumb. The leg is built in the same way. The thigh has one long bone. The lower leg has two bones. And a small, flat bone sits at the front of the knee. This is the kneecap.

Step one: find a landmark, a part you know, like the elbow or the thumb. The upper arm bone is the humerus. In the forearm, use the thumb. The radius is on the thumb side. The ulna is on the little-finger side. The finger bones are the phalanges. In the leg, the thigh bone is the femur. Do not swap it with the humerus: the femur is in the leg, the humerus is in the arm. In the lower leg, the large shin bone is the tibia. The thin bone beside it is the fibula. The kneecap is the patella. Now the trunk. The backbone is the vertebral column: a stack of small bones. The clavicle is the collarbone, at the front. The scapula is the shoulder blade, at the back. Some questions show a long bone cut open. Hard, dense compact bone forms the outside of the shaft. Spongy bone, full of small spaces, fills the ends. Marrow, a soft tissue, fills the middle. Cartilage, a smooth, firm tissue, covers the ends.

Now a real exam question. The diagram is a model of the bones in the arm. Each bone is a black block. Letter A is the thumb. The question asks you to name the bones labelled 1, 2, 3 and 4. It is worth four marks. Here is the same model. Bone 1 is the single block at the top: the upper arm. So bone 1 is the humerus. Bones 2 and 3 are the pair in the forearm. The thumb, A, is on the left. Bone 2 is on the thumb side, so bone 2 is the radius. Bone 3 is the ulna. Label 4 points to the fingers, so bone 4 is the phalanges. Four names, four marks. The most common mistake is swapping the radius and the ulna. Always find the thumb first. Another is calling the upper arm bone the femur. The femur is in the thigh. Spell each name carefully. A made-up word such as tibula mixes tibia and fibula, and gets no mark. And in a bone section, compact bone is the dense outside. Spongy bone is the part with spaces.

Your turn. This drawing shows a leg from the side. The person is sitting, so the thigh is flat and the lower leg hangs down. Name bones P, Q, R and S. Pause here and work it out.

P is the single bone from the hip to the knee: the femur. Q is the small bone at the front of the knee: the patella. R is the large bone below the knee: the tibia. S is the thin bone beside it: the fibula. So, every time: find a landmark. Count the bones: one, then two. Use the thumb for the forearm. Then spell each name carefully.
