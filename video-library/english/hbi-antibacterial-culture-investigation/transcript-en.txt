Idea. Bacteria are tiny living cells. In a lab, they are grown on nutrient agar, a jelly with food in it. It sits in a Petri dish, a shallow round dish with a lid. The bacteria reproduce until they form a cloudy layer all over the agar. An antibiotic is a drug that kills bacteria, or stops them reproducing. In a disc test, a paper disc is soaked in antibiotic and put on the agar. The antibiotic diffuses, or spreads out, into the agar. Where it kills the bacteria, a clear zone forms.

Method. Step one: compare the clear zones. Disc A has the largest zone, so antibiotic A is the most effective. Disc B has a small zone. Disc C has no zone at all. The bacteria are resistant to C, which means C does not affect them. This is only fair if the discs, the concentrations and the time are the same. Some questions show a growth graph instead. This made-up example shows the number of bacteria over time. With no antibiotic, the number rises. If the line stays level, the antibiotic is bacteriostatic: the bacteria stop reproducing, but stay alive. If the line falls, it is bactericidal: the bacteria are killed. Step three: use the control. A control shows what happens without the antibiotic, for example a disc in water only. A dish with no bacteria added can still grow a few, from the air, or from a sample being tested, such as milk. Also check how many bacteria were added at the start. Aseptic technique means working so that no unwanted microbes get in, for example a sterile loop and a flame. Tape the lid, but do not seal it all round. Incubate at twenty-five degrees Celsius or below, so that bacteria which cause human disease do not grow.

Worked example. Now a real exam question. The method tests antibiotic X against antibiotic Y. The diagram shows the dish after three days. The question asks you to explain which antibiotic is more effective. Three marks. X is more effective than Y. But the choice alone earns no mark. Point one: the clear zone around X is larger than the clear zone around Y. Point two: the antibiotic diffused out of the paper disc into the agar. Point three: in the clear zone, the bacteria have been killed. So X killed bacteria over a larger area. Here is the full answer. This answer is by olevels.com, not an official mark scheme.

Common mistake. A common mistake is to choose the smaller clear zone, or to say bacteria grow best in the clear zone. The clear zone is where bacteria have been killed. Another mistake is to say a level line on a graph means the bacteria are killed. Level means they stopped reproducing. Only a falling line shows killing. Last, do not just repeat numbers from a table. Explain the difference, for example more bacteria at the start, or bacteria already in the milk.

Your turn. A doctor tests three antibiotics against bacteria from a patient's throat. These are made-up results. There are three discs. The clear zones are: P, 22 millimetres. Q, 0 millimetres. R, 9 millimetres. Which antibiotic should the doctor give? Explain. What does the result for Q show? Pause here and work it out.

Answer. Choose P. Its clear zone is the largest, 22 millimetres. P diffused out of the disc and killed bacteria over the largest area, so it is the most effective. Q has no clear zone. So the bacteria are resistant to Q. It would not help this patient. So, every time: make your choice. Quote the evidence, such as the larger clear zone or a falling line. Then explain it: the antibiotic diffused out and killed bacteria, or stopped them reproducing.
