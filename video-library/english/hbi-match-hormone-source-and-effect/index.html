Read the narration

A gland is an organ that makes and releases a substance. Some glands release hormones straight into the blood. A hormone is a chemical message. These glands make up the endocrine, or hormonal, system. The body has two ways to send messages. Nerves send electrical impulses along neurones, the nerve cells: very fast, and short-lived. Hormones are chemicals carried in the blood. They are slower, because they only move as fast as the blood flows, but their effects often last longer.

Learn each gland, its hormone and its effect. The pituitary gland, at the base of the brain, releases ADH. ADH controls the water content of the blood. The thyroid gland, in the neck, releases thyroxine. Thyroxine controls the metabolic rate: how fast the body's chemical reactions go. The pancreas makes insulin and glucagon. Insulin lowers blood glucose. Glucagon raises it. These are hormones, not digestive enzymes. The adrenal glands, on top of the kidneys, release adrenaline. It prepares the body for fight or flight: to fight, or to run away. Heart rate and breathing rate go up, the pupils widen, and more glucose goes into the blood. More blood goes to the muscles, and less to the gut. To compare nerves and hormones in a table, compare the same feature in both columns. Route: neurones, or blood. Signal: electrical, or chemical. Speed: very fast, or slower. In a table with a box of words, use each word only once. Cross it off when you use it. Some words in the box are not hormones at all, and some are not used.

Now a real exam question. There is a box of ten words: hormones, organs and parts of the brain and ear. The table lists seven body processes. You write the right word for each one. Seven marks. Water levels in the blood: ADH. Conscious thought: the cerebrum. Transport of oxygen: haemoglobin, in red blood cells. Urea formation: the liver. Metabolic rate: thyroxine. Sensing the position of the body: the semicircular canals in the ear. Less blood going to the gut: adrenaline. Three words are left over: cerebellum, cochlea and stomach. That is fine. Seven rows, seven marks. This answer is by olevels.com, not an official mark scheme.

A common mistake is to say the liver makes insulin. The pancreas makes insulin. Another is to mix up thyroxine, for metabolic rate, with adrenaline, for fight or flight. When asked for the type of signal in the hormonal system, write chemical, not hormone. And hormones are slow because they travel in the blood, not because they are big.

Your turn. A runner has just finished a long race on a hot day. She has lost a lot of water in sweat, and her blood glucose is low. Name the hormone that controls the water in her blood, and its gland. Name the hormone that raises her blood glucose, and its gland. Then give one way a nerve message is different from a hormone message. Pause here and work it out.

(a) The hormone is ADH, from the pituitary gland. (b) The hormone is glucagon, from the pancreas. Insulin would lower it. (c) Any one: a nerve message is electrical, not chemical; it travels along neurones, not in the blood; and it is faster. So, every time: name the gland, its hormone, and its effect.
