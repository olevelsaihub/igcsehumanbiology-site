Idea. The pupil is the black hole in the middle of the eye. Light enters through it. The iris is the coloured ring around it. The iris has muscles that change the size of the pupil. The iris has two sets of muscles. Circular muscles sit in rings around the pupil. Radial muscles run outwards, like the spokes of a wheel. When one set contracts, the other relaxes.

Method. The method: decide if the light got brighter or dimmer. Say the retina detects it. Give both muscle actions. Give the pupil change. Then give the purpose. In dim light, the radial muscles contract and the circular muscles relax. The radial muscles shorten and pull the iris outwards. The pupil gets wider; this is called dilates. More light enters, so we can still see clearly. In bright light, it reverses. The circular muscles contract and the radial muscles relax. The circular rings tighten, like pulling a drawstring. The pupil gets narrower; this is called constricts. Less light enters, which protects the retina. Step two: receptors in the retina, the light-sensitive layer at the back, detect the change. Impulses travel to the brain and back to the iris muscles. This is a reflex: automatic and fast.

Worked example. Now a real exam question. The diagram shows the iris in light condition A and light condition B. Describe what happens in the eye when the light changes from A to B. Five marks. Compare condition A with condition B. Look at the open centre, the pupil, in each drawing. From A to B, the pupil gets larger, so the light got dimmer. Idea two: receptors in the retina detect the dimmer light. Ideas three and four: both muscles. The radial muscles in the iris contract. The circular muscles relax. Ideas five and six: the pupil widens, so more light enters. This keeps the amount of light reaching the retina about the same, so we can still see clearly. Here is the full answer. Common mistake. A common mistake is to say the pupil contracts. The pupil is a hole. It cannot contract. The muscles of the iris contract. Another mistake is mixing up the muscles, or giving only one. Remember: radial muscles pull outwards, so they widen the pupil in dim light. Always give both muscle actions. Last, do not confuse this with focusing. Focusing uses the ciliary muscles to change the shape of the lens. The pupil response is about the amount of light.

Your turn. Amir's pupil changes from seven millimetres wide to two millimetres. These are made-up values. Part a: describe what happens in his eye. Part b: suggest why this response must happen quickly. Pause here and work it out.

Answer. Part a. In these made-up values, the pupil got smaller, from seven millimetres to two millimetres. So the light got brighter. Receptors in the retina detect the brighter light. The circular muscles of the iris contract, and the radial muscles relax. The pupil becomes narrower, so less light enters the eye. Part b. Too much light can damage the retina. So the response must happen quickly, as a reflex, to protect it.
