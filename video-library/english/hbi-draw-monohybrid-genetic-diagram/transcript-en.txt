Idea. For most genes, you have two copies: one from your mother and one from your father. The different forms of a gene are called alleles. So a child gets one allele from each parent. Sickle cell anaemia is caused by a recessive allele, small a. The normal allele, capital A, is dominant. Capital A, capital A: no condition. Capital A, small a: a carrier, with no condition. Small a, small a: has the condition.

Method. A genetic diagram has four labelled rows: parents, gametes, offspring genotypes and offspring phenotypes. First, write each parent's genotype. A genotype is the two alleles a person has. Then the gametes. A gamete is a sex cell, a sperm or an egg. It carries only one allele, so a parent who is capital A, small a makes gametes capital A and small a. Next, draw a Punnett square. One parent's gametes go along the top, and the other parent's go down the side. Each box gets one allele from the top and one from the side. Last, write a phenotype for every genotype. Then, if the question asks, give the chance. Some conditions are sex-linked, like haemophilia. The allele is on the X chromosome: X capital H, or X small h. The Y has no allele. A carrier mother and a normal father: one child in four is a son with haemophilia. A son has only one X, so one small h is enough.

Worked example. Here is a real exam question. Sickle cell anaemia is caused by a recessive allele. People 7 and 8 have a child. Draw a genetic diagram for this child. Look at the family tree. Person 7 does not have sickle cell anaemia, so she has a capital A. Her mother, person 2, has it, so she passed on a small a. Person 7 is capital A, small a. Person 8 is the same: his mother, person 4, has it. Now draw the diagram. Start with a key: capital A is the normal allele, small a is the sickle cell allele. Then the parents: capital A, small a, crossed with capital A, small a. Each parent makes two kinds of gamete: capital A or small a. Draw a circle round each one. Put the gametes on the Punnett square. Then fill the four boxes: capital A capital A, capital A small a, capital A small a, and small a small a. Now the phenotypes. Capital A capital A has no sickle cell anaemia. Capital A small a also has no sickle cell anaemia, but is a carrier. Small a small a has sickle cell anaemia. One box in four is small a small a: a one in four chance, or twenty-five per cent. It was not asked here, but it often is next. Explanation by olevels.com, not an official mark scheme.

Common mistake. A common mistake is writing a gamete as capital A small a. A gamete has only one allele, so write capital A and small a separately. Another mistake: no labels on the square, or no phenotypes. Label every row, and give a phenotype for every genotype.

Your turn. A carrier man has a child with a woman who has cystic fibrosis. The allele is recessive. What is the chance the child has cystic fibrosis? Pause here and work it out.

Answer. The man is a carrier: capital F, small f. The woman has cystic fibrosis: small f, small f. His gametes are capital F or small f. All her gametes are small f. Fill the Punnett square. Two boxes are capital F small f: carriers. Two boxes are small f small f: cystic fibrosis. So the chance is two in four, or one in two: fifty per cent. Every time: parents, gametes, a labelled Punnett square, then phenotypes and the chance. Label every row.
