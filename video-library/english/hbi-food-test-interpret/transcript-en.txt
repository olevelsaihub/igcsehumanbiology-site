Each food test detects one nutrient. Benedict's solution tests for reducing sugar. A reducing sugar is a simple sugar, such as glucose. This test must be heated in a water bath. Iodine tests for starch. Biuret tests for protein. Ethanol and then water test for lipid. DCPIP tests for vitamin C. Learn the positive colour, and the negative colour, for every test.

Here is a sample, X. Step one: for each test, write the nutrient it detects. Benedict's for sugar. Iodine for starch. Biuret for protein. Step two: turn each colour into present or absent. Brick red: sugar is present. Yellow-brown: no starch. Blue: no protein. Step three: combine the readings to answer the question. Sample X contains reducing sugar, but no starch and no protein. If a question gives a colour, use it to name the reagent: blue-black means iodine. Tests are also used with Visking tubing. Visking tubing is a bag with tiny holes. Starch molecules are too large to pass through. Glucose is small enough to pass out. So the water outside gives a positive Benedict's test, but a negative iodine test.

Now a real exam question. Three food tests are done on one food solution. Iodine gave blue-black. Biuret gave purple. Benedict's stayed blue. Describe what the tests show. Three marks. Take them one at a time. Iodine turned blue-black. Iodine tests for starch, so starch is present. Biuret turned purple. Biuret tests for protein, so protein is present. Benedict's stayed blue. Blue is the negative result. So there is no reducing sugar. This is the mark most students miss. Here is the full answer. It is by olevels.com, not an official mark scheme.

A common mistake is to say blue after Benedict's means sugar. Blue is the colour it starts with. So blue means no sugar. Another mistake is to list only the nutrients that are present. A negative result is a result too. Write it.

Your turn. Three powders, P, Q and R, are tested with iodine and biuret. P gives yellow-brown and lilac. Q gives blue-black and blue. R gives blue-black and lilac. Which powders contain protein but no starch? Pause here and work it out.

Turn each colour into present or absent. P: no starch, protein. Q: starch, no protein. R: starch and protein. Now combine the two conditions. Only P has protein but no starch. R has protein, but it also has starch. So, every time: say what each test detects. Turn every colour into present or absent. Then combine the readings to answer the question.
