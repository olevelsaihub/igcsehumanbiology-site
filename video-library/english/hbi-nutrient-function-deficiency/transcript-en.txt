A nutrient is a substance in food that the body needs. Each nutrient has a job. If the diet lacks one nutrient for a long time, the body cannot do that job. This causes a deficiency disease. A deficiency disease is not caused by a pathogen, a microorganism that causes disease. So it cannot spread from person to person. A better diet can prevent it, or treat it.

Learn each nutrient in three parts: its job, where it comes from, and what happens without it. Protein is for growth and repair. It comes from meat, fish, eggs and beans. Lack of protein causes kwashiorkor: a swollen belly and poor growth. Vitamin C is needed to make connective tissue. This tissue holds cells together, in the gums, skin and blood vessels. Lack of vitamin C causes scurvy. The gums bleed, and cuts heal slowly. Citrus fruits and vegetables are good sources. Iron is needed to make haemoglobin, the red substance in red blood cells that carries oxygen. Lack of iron causes anaemia. The person is tired and pale. Red meat and green vegetables are good sources. Vitamin A is needed to see in dim light. Lack of it causes night blindness, and then full blindness. Carrots and liver are good sources. Calcium makes bones and teeth strong. Milk and cheese are good sources. Without enough calcium, bones and teeth are weak. Vitamin D helps the body take in calcium. So the method is: name the nutrient, give its job, then the disease or symptom.

Now a real exam question. Humans need a balanced diet. Explain why vitamin C is needed in a balanced diet. Two marks: give the job, and what goes wrong without it. First, the job. Vitamin C is needed to make connective tissue. Connective tissue holds cells and tissues together, for example in the gums. Second, what goes wrong. Without vitamin C, a person gets scurvy. The connective tissue is weak, so the gums bleed. Here is the full answer. This explanation is by olevels.com, not an official mark scheme.

A common mistake is to mix up the diseases. Scurvy is lack of vitamin C. Anaemia is lack of iron. Kwashiorkor is lack of protein. Another mistake is a vague job. Do not write that vitamin C stops colds, or that protein gives energy. Give the real job.

Your turn. In a time of food shortage, a young child has a swollen belly and grows very slowly. Name the disease and the missing nutrient. Explain the poor growth, and suggest one way to reduce this disease. Pause here and work it out.

The disease is kwashiorkor. The missing nutrient is protein. Protein is needed for growth and repair of tissues. Without enough protein, the child cannot grow new tissue. More protein in the diet, or food aid, would reduce the disease. So, every time: name the nutrient, give its job in the body, then name the disease or a symptom.
