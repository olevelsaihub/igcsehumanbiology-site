Read the narration

Protein in food is digested. This means it is broken down into small pieces, called amino acids. They are absorbed into the blood. Many amino acids are used to build new proteins in the body. But extra amino acids cannot be stored. The body must deal with them.

This happens in the liver. Each amino acid has an amine group, which contains nitrogen. Deamination removes the amine group. It becomes ammonia, which is turned into urea. The rest of the amino acid can be used in respiration, to release energy. Urea is a waste. The liver puts it into the blood. The blood carries it to the kidneys, which remove it in the urine. Too much urea in the blood is toxic, which means poisonous. The liver has a second job, called detoxification. It breaks down alcohol, drugs and other toxins. A healthy liver makes alcohol in the blood fall fast. A damaged liver is slow, so alcohol stays high. The liver has a very large blood supply. Hepatic means of the liver. Blood comes in through the hepatic artery, and from the gut through the hepatic portal vein. It leaves in the hepatic vein. It needs this because it has many jobs.

Now a real exam question. The diagram shows a kidney dialysis machine. It cleans the blood of a person whose kidneys do not work. Explain why these people are often told to eat less protein. Four marks. Start with protein. It is digested into amino acids. Extra amino acids are deaminated in the liver, and the amine group becomes urea. So if the person eats less protein, there are fewer extra amino acids. That means less urea is made. Their kidneys cannot remove urea, so it builds up between dialysis treatments. With more protein, it can reach a toxic level. With less protein, there is less urea for the machine to remove. Here is the full answer. Explanation by olevels.com, not an official mark scheme.

A common mistake is to say urea is made in the kidneys. It is made in the liver. The kidneys only remove it. Another mistake is to say the liver filters alcohol. The liver breaks alcohol down. The kidneys are the filters.

Your turn. Mr Khan's liver is badly damaged. His doctor tells him not to drink alcohol, and to take a smaller dose of a painkiller than usual. Explain why. Pause here and work it out.

The liver breaks down alcohol and drugs. But his liver is damaged, so it cannot break them down well. So alcohol and the painkiller build up in his blood. A healthy liver keeps the painkiller low. But with his damaged liver and the same dose, it stays high for longer and can reach a harmful level. It can cause more damage to the liver. A smaller dose keeps the level safe. So remember: protein becomes amino acids, which the liver deaminates. The amine group becomes urea, which goes to the kidneys. And the liver also breaks down alcohol and drugs.
