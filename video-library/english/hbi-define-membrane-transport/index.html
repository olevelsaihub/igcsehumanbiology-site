Define diffusion, osmosis and active transport

Read the narration

Particles in a liquid or a gas move about all the time, in random directions. Concentration means how many particles are in a space. Diffusion is the net movement of particles from a high concentration to a low concentration. Net means overall. The difference in concentration between two places is the concentration gradient. In diffusion, particles move down the gradient, like a ball rolling downhill. The cell spends no energy on it.

There are three ways to cross a membrane. A membrane is the thin layer around a cell. The first is diffusion: high to low concentration. It is passive, which means the cell spends no energy. The second is osmosis. Osmosis moves water only. Water crosses a partially permeable membrane. Partially permeable means it lets small molecules like water through, but not large ones. For osmosis, use water potential. A dilute solution has a high water potential. A concentrated solution has a low water potential. Water moves from high to low water potential. The third is active transport. It moves particles from a low to a high concentration. That is against the gradient, like pushing a ball uphill. So it needs energy, as ATP. ATP is a molecule that carries energy. It is made in respiration, the reaction that releases energy from glucose. Carrier proteins in the membrane carry the particles across. So the method is: name the process. Give the direction compared with the gradient. Then add its special idea: no energy for diffusion, water and the membrane for osmosis, energy as ATP for active transport.

Now a real exam question. It says molecules move in and out of cells in three ways. Part b asks: give a definition of active transport. It is worth three marks, so plan three ideas. Idea one is the direction. Outside the cell the concentration is low. Inside it is high. Active transport moves molecules from low to high: against the concentration gradient. Idea two: this needs energy. Idea three: the energy is in the form of ATP, made by respiration. A spare idea, in case one is not given credit: carrier proteins in the membrane carry the molecules across. Here is the full answer. It is by olevels.com, not an official mark scheme.

A common mistake is to say active transport moves particles down the gradient. It moves them up, against it. Another mistake is to say osmosis moves particles or solutes. Osmosis moves only water, across a partially permeable membrane.

Your turn. A red blood cell is put into pure water. The cell swells. Name the process that makes it swell, and define it. Pause here and work it out.

The process is osmosis. Pure water has the highest water potential. The inside of the red blood cell has a lower water potential. So water moves into the cell, from high to low water potential, across the partially permeable cell membrane. The water goes in, and the cell swells. So, every time: name the process. Give the direction compared with the gradient. Then add its special idea: water and the membrane, or energy as ATP.
