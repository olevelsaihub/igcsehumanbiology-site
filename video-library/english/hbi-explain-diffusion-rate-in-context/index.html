Diffusion moves a substance from where it is more concentrated to where it is less concentrated. Your cells respire. Respiration is the reaction that releases energy from glucose, and it makes carbon dioxide. So there is more carbon dioxide in the cell than in the blood. It diffuses out, into the blood. Bacteria are tiny. Oxygen diffuses into them from all sides, quickly enough for the whole cell. So they need no transport system. The rate of diffusion means how much crosses each second.

Step one: name the process, diffusion. Step two: say where the substance is high and where it is low. Here, glucose is high in the small intestine and low in the blood. So it moves from the intestine into the blood. Step three: name the factor that changes the rate. The concentration gradient is the difference in concentration between two places. A steeper gradient gives faster diffusion. With more particles, more of them hit the membrane and cross it each second. A larger surface area also gives faster diffusion. There is more membrane, so more particles can cross at the same time. That is why the gut wall is folded. A longer dialysis tube works the same way. A shorter distance gives faster diffusion, because the particles have less far to travel. A larger surface area to volume ratio means the centre is reached sooner. And warmer particles move faster. Step four: link the factor to the rate.

Now a real exam question. Glucose moves from the small intestine into the blood by diffusion. Explain why the glucose concentration in the intestine affects the rate. Three marks. Point one. More glucose in the intestine makes a bigger difference between the intestine and the blood. The concentration gradient is steeper. Point two. With more glucose molecules, more of them hit the wall each second. The wall is lined by epithelium, a layer of cells. Point three. So more glucose molecules cross into the blood each second. The rate of diffusion is faster. Here is the full answer. It is by olevels.com, not an official mark scheme.

A common mistake is to write only that it diffuses. Say from where to where, and that it moves from high to low concentration. Another mistake is to write faster with no reason. And glucose moving is diffusion, not osmosis. Osmosis is only for water.

Your turn. Oxygen diffuses from the air in the alveoli into the blood. In emphysema, the walls of the alveoli break down, so the lungs have a smaller surface area. Explain how this changes the rate of oxygen diffusion. Pause here and work it out.

Healthy lungs have many small alveoli, with a large surface area. In emphysema, the walls break down. There are fewer, larger air spaces, with a smaller surface area. With less surface, fewer oxygen molecules can cross into the blood at the same time. So oxygen diffuses into the blood more slowly. So, every time: name diffusion. Say from high to low, and from where to where. Name the factor. Give the reason. Then give the effect on the rate.
