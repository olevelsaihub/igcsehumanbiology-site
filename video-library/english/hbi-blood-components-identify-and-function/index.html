Idea. Blood has four components. Three of them float in a liquid: red blood cells, white blood cells and platelets. The liquid is pale yellow, and it is called plasma. To identify each one, look at three clues: its size, its shape, and whether it has a nucleus. The nucleus is the part of a cell that holds its DNA. It looks like a dark shape.

Method. Red blood cells are the small round ones, and there are lots of them. Each is a biconcave disc: thinner in the middle, so the centre looks pale. They have no nucleus. They are full of haemoglobin, which carries oxygen. White blood cells are bigger, and they have a nucleus. A phagocyte has a nucleus with lobes, like bumps. It engulfs pathogens. Engulf means it takes them in and digests them. A pathogen is a microorganism that causes disease. A lymphocyte is the other white cell. Its nucleus is large and round, and it fills most of the cell. Lymphocytes make antibodies: proteins that stick to pathogens and help to destroy them. Platelets are the tiny bits. They are fragments of cells, not whole cells, and they have no nucleus. They help the blood to clot at a cut. Plasma is the liquid between the cells. It is mostly water. It carries dissolved glucose, carbon dioxide, urea, hormones and clotting proteins round the body. Urea is a waste made in the liver. Hormones are chemical messengers. For a tick table, work one row at a time. Has a nucleus? Only the white cells. Contains haemoglobin? Only the red cells. A whole cell? Red and white cells, but not platelets.

Worked example. Now a real exam question. The diagram shows a sample of human blood, with labels X, Y and Z. Name components X, Y and Z. Three marks. X points to the empty space between the cells. Nothing is drawn there, so it is the liquid: plasma. Y points to a small cell with a pale dip in the middle and no nucleus: a red blood cell. Z points to a larger cell with a dark, lobed nucleus. A lobed nucleus means a phagocyte, a kind of white blood cell. This answer is by olevels.com, not an official mark scheme.

Common mistake. A common mistake is to call platelets cells. Platelets are fragments of cells. They have no nucleus. Another mistake is to give one white cell both jobs. Phagocytes engulf pathogens. Lymphocytes make antibodies.

Your turn. A patient's blood has very few platelets. Suggest what problem this causes. Then name the white cell with a large round nucleus, and give its job. Pause here and work it out.

Answer. Part a: platelets help the blood to clot. With very few, a cut keeps bleeding for a long time. Part b: the white cell with a large round nucleus is a lymphocyte. It makes antibodies. So, every time: look at size, shape and nucleus. Then use the exact name, and give its job.
