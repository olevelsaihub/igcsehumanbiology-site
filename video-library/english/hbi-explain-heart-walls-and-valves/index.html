Idea. The heart is a pump made of muscle. It has four chambers. A chamber is a space that fills with blood. The two atria are at the top. The two ventricles are below them. We look at the heart from the front, so its right side is on the left of the picture. The walls of the chambers are muscle. When muscle contracts, it squeezes the blood. This raises the pressure. Pressure is the push of the blood on the vessel walls. More muscle gives a stronger squeeze, so a higher pressure.

Method. Step one: the left ventricle has the thickest wall. It pumps blood into the aorta. The aorta is the main artery. It carries blood to the organs of the whole body. That is a long way, so it needs a high pressure. Step two: the right ventricle has a thinner wall. It pumps blood only to the lungs, through the pulmonary artery. The lungs are close to the heart. They are also delicate, so a lower pressure is better. Step three: the atria have the thinnest walls of all. They only push blood a short way, down into the ventricles below them. Step four: between each atrium and its ventricle there is a valve. Valves are flaps. When the ventricle contracts, the blood pushes the flaps shut. So blood cannot flow backwards into the atrium. Valves keep blood moving one way. Some questions ask you to suggest. If a person has high blood pressure for years, the left ventricle must contract more strongly to push blood out. A muscle that works harder grows bigger, so its wall grows thicker.

Worked example. Now a real exam question. The diagram shows the heart from the front. Chambers X and Y have walls that are different in thickness. Explain this difference. It is worth five marks. Seen from the front, X is the right ventricle and Y is the left ventricle. Point one: Y has the thicker wall. Point two: Y pumps blood into the aorta. Point three: the aorta takes blood to all the organs of the whole body. Point four: this needs a high pressure. Point five: a thicker wall has more muscle, so it contracts more strongly. Point six: X only pumps blood to the lungs, so it needs a lower pressure. Six ideas for five marks is safe. Here is the full answer. This answer is by olevels.com, not an official mark scheme.

Common mistake. A common mistake: the left ventricle does not hold more blood. Both ventricles pump the same volume. The left one is thicker because it pumps blood further, at a higher pressure. Do not give the right ventricle the thinnest wall. The atria are thinner. And the left ventricle pumps to the whole body, not to the lungs. Valves never pump blood. The muscle pumps. Valves only stop the blood flowing backwards.

Your turn. These are example values, not real data. Three heart walls are 2, 5 and 15 millimetres thick. Part a: match each value to the left atrium, the right ventricle and the left ventricle. Part b: in one person, the valve between the left atrium and the left ventricle does not close properly. Suggest the effect on blood flow when the left ventricle contracts. Pause here and work it out.

Answer. Here is the answer. The left atrium is 2 millimetres, the thinnest. The right ventricle is 5. The left ventricle is 15, the thickest. In part b, some blood flows back into the left atrium. So less blood goes out through the aorta to the body. So, every time: say which chamber is thicker. Link it to more muscle. Say where it pumps. Give the pressure needed. And for valves, say they stop backflow.
