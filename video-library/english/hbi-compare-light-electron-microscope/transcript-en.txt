Cells are far too small to see with your eyes. A microscope makes them look bigger. Magnification means how many times bigger the image is than the real object. Here, the image is four hundred times bigger than the real cell. Resolution is different. Resolution is the ability to show two points that are close together as two separate points. With low resolution, they blur into one. High resolution shows more detail.

Now the parts of a light microscope. You look through the eyepiece lens. The objective lens, just above the slide, magnifies the specimen. The specimen is the thing you are looking at. The slide sits on the stage, and clips hold it still. The light source lights the slide from below. To focus, turn the coarse adjustment first. It moves the stage a long way. Then turn the fine adjustment. It moves the stage a little, to make the image sharp. An electron microscope uses a beam of electrons, not light. Electrons are tiny particles, much smaller than atoms. It has a much higher magnification. It also has a much higher resolution, so it shows far more detail. So what can you see? A light microscope shows the nucleus, the cytoplasm and the cell membrane. An electron microscope also shows tiny organelles: ribosomes, the endoplasmic reticulum, and the inside of mitochondria. An organelle is a part of a cell with its own job. Ribosomes make proteins. The endoplasmic reticulum, or ER, is a network of tubes. When a question asks why an electron microscope is better, give two separate ideas. Idea one: higher magnification. Idea two: higher resolution, so more detail and more structures can be seen.

Here is a real exam question. It says an electron microscope can be used to view structures in specialised cells. Explain why it is more useful than a light microscope. It is worth two marks. Two marks means two ideas. Idea one: an electron microscope gives a much higher magnification. So small structures look much bigger. Idea two: it has a higher resolution. Things that are close together are seen as separate. So we see much more detail, and more small structures, such as ribosomes. Here is the full answer. This answer is written by olevels.com. A common mistake is to write that the electron microscope is more powerful, or makes things bigger. That is too vague. Name the two ideas: magnification and resolution. Another mistake is to think magnification alone gives more detail. If you magnify a blur, you just get a bigger blur. Only higher resolution splits it into two clear points.

Your turn. A scientist wants to study the ribosomes in a liver cell. First, which microscope should she use, and why? Second, what is the job of the fine adjustment on a light microscope? Pause here and work it out.

Part a. She should use an electron microscope. Ribosomes are too small to see clearly with light. It has a higher magnification, and a higher resolution, so the ribosomes are seen as separate dots. Part b. The fine adjustment moves the stage up or down a very small amount. This makes the image sharp. The coarse adjustment is the big knob for large moves. So, every time: decide what the question is about. For the electron microscope, give magnification and resolution as two ideas. For a part of a light microscope, give its job: magnifies, holds, or focuses.
