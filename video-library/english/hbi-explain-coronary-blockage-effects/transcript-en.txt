Idea. The heart is made of muscle, and muscle needs its own blood supply. This comes from the coronary arteries. They branch off the aorta and run over the outside of the heart. If a coronary artery is blocked, the heart muscle beyond the blockage gets too little blood. That part of the muscle can die.

Method. How does an artery get blocked? Fatty deposits, called plaques, build up in the artery wall. They are made of cholesterol, a fatty substance in the blood. Eating a lot of saturated fat, the fat in meat and butter, and being very overweight raise blood cholesterol. They make the space for blood narrow. A blood clot can form on a plaque and block it completely. Now follow the chain. Link one: less blood flows to the heart muscle. Link two: so less oxygen and glucose reach the muscle cells. Link three: with less oxygen, there is less aerobic respiration. Aerobic respiration uses oxygen to release energy from glucose. Link four: so less energy is released for the muscle to contract. Link five: the heart pumps less blood, and the muscle cells can die. This is a heart attack. The same chain works for any tissue, such as the legs. Less blood there also means fewer white blood cells arrive. White blood cells fight pathogens, the microbes that cause disease.

Worked example. Here is a real exam question. The diagram shows a long artery that carries blood to the heart muscle. A fatty deposit sits on its lower wall. Explain how this can cause heart disease, for four marks. Link one: the deposit narrows the artery, so less blood can flow along it. Link two: less blood reaches the heart muscle tissue. Link three: the muscle cells get less oxygen and glucose. Link four: so they carry out less aerobic respiration, and release less energy. Link five: the heart muscle cells can die, which causes a heart attack. That is five links for four marks. This answer is by olevels.com, not an official mark scheme.

Common mistake. A common mistake is to say blood cannot get into the heart. The chambers are still full of blood. The problem is blood reaching the heart muscle itself. Another mistake is to write: the heart gets no energy. Give the links: less oxygen, so less aerobic respiration, so less energy released. If you are asked to circle the damaged muscle, look at the direction of blood flow. The damage is after the blockage, further along the artery. The muscle before the blockage still gets blood.

Your turn. In a person with diabetes, an artery that carries blood to the foot becomes narrow. Explain why tissue in the foot may die, and why the foot may become infected. Pause here and work it out.

Answer. The narrow artery means less blood flows to the foot. So less oxygen and glucose reach the cells. There is less aerobic respiration and less energy, so the cells die. Fewer white blood cells arrive too, so infection is more likely. So every time: start with the blockage, then less blood, less oxygen and glucose, less aerobic respiration, less energy, and the cells die.
