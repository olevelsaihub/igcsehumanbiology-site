Idea. Some questions give you an equation. Speed equals distance divided by time. This triangle helps. Distance is on top. Speed and time are at the bottom. Cover the value you want. Cover time, and you see distance over speed. So time equals distance divided by speed. Cover distance, and you see speed times time. This is called rearranging.

Method. Step one: write the equation, and rearrange it for the value you want. Step two: make the units match. For example, five minutes is three hundred seconds, not five hundred. Step three: put each number in its place, and calculate. Step four: give the answer in the unit the question asks for. To change seconds to minutes, divide by sixty. Then round as asked. Other equations work the same way. Cardiac output is the volume of blood the heart pumps each minute. It equals heart rate times stroke volume. Stroke volume is the blood pumped in one beat. One cubic decimetre is a thousand cubic centimetres. For heating water, use the temperature rise, not the final temperature. Some equations have a square root. A square root is the number that, times itself, gives the value. For reaction time, divide the distance by five first, then take the square root. Point one five metres gives zero point one seven seconds.

Worked example. Now a real exam question. A sperm cell swims at zero point zero seven zero millimetres per second. It travels one hundred and eighty millimetres to the egg. Use the equation to find the time, in minutes. Three marks. Here is the picture. The distance is one hundred and eighty millimetres. The speed is zero point zero seven zero millimetres per second. We want time, so time equals distance divided by speed. Both values use millimetres, so the units match, and the time comes out in seconds. One hundred and eighty divided by zero point zero seven zero is two thousand five hundred and seventy-one seconds. The question asks for minutes. Divide by sixty: forty-two point nine. Common mistake. A common mistake is to multiply distance by speed when you want the time. Always write the rearranged equation first. Another mistake is to stop at seconds. The question asked for minutes. Read the answer line for the unit.

Your turn. A woman's heart rate is seventy beats per minute. Her stroke volume is zero point zero seven five cubic decimetres. Calculate her cardiac output, in cubic centimetres per minute. Pause here and work it out.

Answer. First, match the units. The answer must be in cubic centimetres. So change zero point zero seven five cubic decimetres: times a thousand gives seventy-five cubic centimetres. Now write the equation and substitute. Cardiac output equals seventy times seventy-five. That is five thousand two hundred and fifty cubic centimetres per minute. So, every time: write the equation and rearrange it. Make the units match. Substitute and calculate. Then give the unit asked, and round.
