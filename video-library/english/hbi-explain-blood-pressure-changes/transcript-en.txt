Blood pressure is the push of the blood on the walls of the vessels. We measure it in millimetres of mercury, written m m H g. The ventricles create this pressure when they contract. In an artery, the pressure goes up and down with each beat. The highest pressure is the systolic pressure, when the ventricles contract. The lowest is the diastolic pressure, when the ventricles relax. For example, a typical reading is about one hundred and twenty over eighty. These are example numbers, not real data from one person.

Now a whole journey. Region one is the left ventricle. Its pressure swings from about one hundred and twenty right down to nearly zero. Only a heart chamber falls almost to zero. In the arteries the pressure stays high, and it still rises and falls with each beat. This is the pulse. Further from the heart, in the arterioles, the small arteries, the swings get smaller. In the capillaries the pressure falls smoothly, with no pulse. Capillary walls have no muscle or elastic tissue. The pressure falls because they are far from the heart, they are narrow, and fluid leaks out. In the veins the pressure is lowest. Blood always flows from higher pressure to lower pressure. That is why it moves from arteries, through capillaries, into veins. So in a graph question, match each region to a vessel. Swings to nearly zero mean the ventricle. High swings mean arteries. A smooth fall means capillaries. The lowest pressure means veins.

Here is a real exam question. The graph shows blood pressure in five regions of the circulation. Region one goes up to about one hundred and twenty, then down almost to zero, again and again. The question asks: explain why blood pressure varies in region one. It is worth five marks, so plan six ideas. Idea one: it falls almost to zero, so region one is the heart, the left ventricle. Idea two: the ventricle muscle contracts. Idea three: this forces blood out into the aorta, so the pressure is high. Idea four: then the ventricle relaxes. Idea five: so the pressure falls to nearly zero. Idea six: this happens with every heartbeat, so it rises and falls again and again. Here is the full answer. A common mistake is to say the atria make the high pressure. It is the ventricles. Their thick muscle forces blood into the arteries. Another mistake is to mix up the words. Systolic is the highest, when the ventricles contract. Diastolic is the lowest, when they relax.

One hundred and twenty-five is the systolic pressure, the highest. It happens when the ventricles contract and force blood into the arteries. Eighty is the diastolic pressure, when they relax. Blood in the artery is at high pressure. Blood in the vein is at low pressure. Blood always flows from high to low pressure, so it flows from the artery, through the machine, into the vein.
