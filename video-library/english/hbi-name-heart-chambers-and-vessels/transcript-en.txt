Idea. The heart is a pump made of muscle. It has four chambers. A chamber is a space that fills with blood. The two atria are at the top: the right atrium and the left atrium. The two ventricles are below them. Diagrams show the heart from the front, as if the person is facing you. So the heart's right side is on your left, and its left side is on your right.

Method. Veins bring blood into the atria. The vena cava brings blood from the body into the right atrium. The pulmonary vein brings blood from the lungs into the left atrium. Arteries take blood out of the ventricles. The pulmonary artery leaves the right ventricle and goes to the lungs. The aorta leaves the left ventricle and goes to the body. The right side pumps deoxygenated blood, which has little oxygen. The left side pumps oxygenated blood, rich in oxygen. So the pulmonary artery is the only artery with deoxygenated blood, and the pulmonary vein is the only vein with oxygenated blood. Now the whole circulation. The aorta takes blood from the heart to the body. The vena cava brings it back. The pulmonary artery takes blood to the lungs, and the pulmonary vein brings it back. Blood in the aorta is at high pressure, because the left ventricle has a thick wall. So, the method. First, find left and right. Second, follow the vessel to the chamber it joins. Third, use the rule: arteries leave ventricles, veins enter atria. Last, think where the blood has just been.

Worked example. Now a real exam question. The diagram shows a heart in simple form. The chambers are P, Q, R and S, and two vessels at the top are M and N. Which is the name of vessel M? Aorta, pulmonary artery, pulmonary vein, or vena cava. First, the sides. The word right is under Q and P, and left is under R and S. Q and R are at the top, so they are atria. P and S are below, so they are ventricles. Now follow M. It runs down the middle and opens into P, the right ventricle. So M takes blood out of the right ventricle. It is an artery going to the lungs. So M is the pulmonary artery, answer B. N leaves S, the left ventricle, so N would be the aorta. Here is the full answer. Common mistake. A common mistake is to read left as your own left. The heart faces you, so its left side is on your right. Always look for the words on the diagram. Another mistake is to name a vessel by where it goes. Use the chamber it joins. The pulmonary vein carries blood into the left atrium, so it is a vein. And the vena cava joins the right atrium, not the right ventricle.

Your turn. A heart diagram is seen from the front. Vessel W brings blood into the top chamber on the right of the page. Name the chamber, name vessel W, and say if its blood is oxygenated. Pause here and work it out.

Answer. The right of the page is the heart's left side. A top chamber is an atrium. So the chamber is the left atrium. A vessel bringing blood into the left atrium is a vein: the pulmonary vein. The pulmonary vein brings blood from the lungs. So the blood in W is oxygenated.
