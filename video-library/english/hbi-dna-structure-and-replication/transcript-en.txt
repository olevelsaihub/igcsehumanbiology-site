DNA is made of two strands. Each strand is a long chain of nucleotides. A nucleotide is one small unit: a sugar, a phosphate and a base. The two strands coil round each other, like a twisted ladder. This shape is called a double helix. Untwist the ladder. Each rung is a pair of bases, one from each strand. The bases always pair the same way. Adenine pairs with thymine. Cytosine pairs with guanine. These are complementary base pairs. Weak bonds called hydrogen bonds join each pair of bases. They hold the two strands together.

Because adenine always pairs with thymine, DNA has the same amount of each. The same is true for cytosine and guanine. Here, adenine is thirty per cent. So thymine is thirty per cent too. That leaves forty per cent, shared equally. So cytosine and guanine are twenty per cent each. Before a cell divides, it copies its DNA. This is called replication. First, the double helix unwinds. The hydrogen bonds break, and the two strands separate. Free nucleotides in the nucleus pair with the bases on each old strand. They follow the same rule: adenine with thymine, cytosine with guanine. Then an enzyme, DNA polymerase, joins them into a new strand. An enzyme is a protein that speeds up a reaction. The result is two identical DNA molecules. Each one has one old strand and one new strand. So the mass of DNA in the cell doubles, ready for mitosis. Mitosis is cell division that makes two identical cells.

Here is a real exam question. A gene is a short section of DNA. Describe the structure of DNA. It is worth four marks. Four marks, so plan five separate facts. Fact one: DNA has two strands, coiled into a double helix. Fact two: each strand is made of nucleotides. Fact three: there are four bases. Fact four: the bases pair in a complementary way, adenine with thymine and cytosine with guanine. Fact five: hydrogen bonds join the base pairs. A common mistake is pairing the wrong bases, such as adenine with guanine. Another is writing 'bonds', or 'peptide bonds'. Name them: hydrogen bonds. And spell thymine with a y. In replication, students often say DNA polymerase unzips the DNA. Its job is to join the new nucleotides into a strand. Also say that free nucleotides pair with each old strand.

Your turn. A sample of DNA has eighteen per cent guanine. First, what percentage is cytosine, and what percentage is adenine? Second, describe how this DNA is copied before the cell divides. Pause here and work it out.

Part a. Cytosine pairs with guanine, so cytosine is also eighteen per cent. Together they make thirty-six. That leaves sixty-four per cent for adenine and thymine, so adenine is thirty-two per cent. Part b. The helix unwinds, and the two strands separate. Free nucleotides pair with the bases on each old strand. DNA polymerase joins them. This makes two identical molecules. So, every time: for structure, give separate facts: double helix, nucleotides, four bases, pairs, hydrogen bonds. For data, use A equals T, C equals G. For replication, give the steps in order.
