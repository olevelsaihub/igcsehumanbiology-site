When you cut yourself, a blood vessel is damaged. Blood leaks out through the hole. Blood clotting makes a plug, called a clot, that blocks the hole. Why does clotting matter? Two separate reasons. One: less blood is lost. Two: it stops pathogens getting in. A pathogen is a microbe that causes disease. So the clot prevents infection, and the wound can heal.

Here are the steps. Platelets are tiny pieces of cells in the blood. They collect at the cut and are damaged. The damaged platelets release an enzyme. An enzyme is a protein that speeds up a reaction. With calcium ions, this enzyme changes a protein called prothrombin into thrombin. Thrombin is also an enzyme. It changes fibrinogen into fibrin. Fibrinogen is soluble: it is dissolved in the plasma. Fibrin is insoluble: it forms solid threads. The fibrin threads make a mesh, like a net, across the cut. The mesh traps red blood cells and platelets. This is the clot. Here is the clot in the cut. Sometimes we must stop clotting. Drugs called anticoagulants do this. A dialysis machine cleans the blood when the kidneys fail. Anticoagulants are added as blood goes into it, so clots do not block the machine.

Here is a real exam question. The diagram shows parts of the blood. Part C is the platelets. Then the question asks you to describe the process of blood clotting, for five marks. Point one: the platelets at the cut are damaged and release an enzyme. Point two: with calcium ions, the enzyme turns prothrombin into thrombin. Point three: thrombin turns soluble fibrinogen into insoluble fibrin. Point four: the fibrin threads form a mesh. Point five: the mesh traps red blood cells, and this forms the clot. A common mistake is to write the change the wrong way round. Fibrinogen is the starting protein. It is changed into fibrin. Another mistake is to say white blood cells make the clot. They do not. Platelets start it, and fibrin threads trap red blood cells.

Your turn. A person takes a drug that stops thrombin being made. Explain why a small cut on their hand keeps bleeding, and why this could be dangerous. Pause here and work it out.

Without thrombin, fibrinogen is not changed into fibrin. So no fibrin mesh forms, no red cells are trapped, and there is no clot. The cut keeps bleeding. This is dangerous for two reasons. The person could lose a lot of blood. And pathogens could enter through the open cut, causing an infection. Every time: platelets release an enzyme, prothrombin becomes thrombin, fibrinogen becomes fibrin, the mesh traps red cells. Then give two reasons it matters.
