Idea. On a cold day, it may be five degrees Celsius outside. On a hot day, thirty-five. But inside your body, it stays near thirty-seven. Homeostasis means keeping the internal environment constant, even when the outside changes. The internal environment is the conditions inside the body. Constant does not mean it never moves. Body temperature goes up and down a little, but it is kept close to thirty-seven degrees. Blood glucose and the water in the blood are kept steady in the same way.

Method. How? By negative feedback. Start at the normal level. Then something changes it. A detector notices the change. A response happens that reverses the change. When the level is normal again, the response is switched off. This loop is negative feedback. Example one: body temperature. Normal is thirty-seven degrees. If the body is too hot, the hypothalamus detects it. The hypothalamus is a part of the brain. The response is sweating and wider arterioles in the skin. Arterioles are small arteries. If too cold, the response is shivering. Example two: blood glucose. If glucose is too high, the pancreas detects it. It releases a hormone called insulin. A hormone is a chemical carried in the blood. Insulin makes the liver store glucose as glycogen, a stored form of glucose, so glucose falls back to normal. Why keep the body at thirty-seven degrees? Because enzymes work best there. Enzymes are proteins that speed up reactions in cells. Too cold, and enzymes work slowly. Too hot, and enzymes are denatured: their shape changes and they stop working.

Worked example. Now a real exam question. The question says: using the example shown in the diagram, explain what is meant by negative feedback. It is worth four marks. Here is the diagram. It shows how blood glucose is controlled. If glucose falls, hormone Y is released. If it rises, hormone Z is released. Pick one direction: blood glucose falls below normal. Less insulin is released. The pancreas releases glucagon, hormone Y. Glucagon makes the liver turn glycogen back into glucose. So blood glucose rises until it reaches the normal level. Last point. Once the level is normal, the response is switched off. Glucagon release stops, and insulin release starts again. Here is the full answer. Explanation by olevels.com, not an official mark scheme.

Common mistake. A common mistake is to say homeostasis means keeping the body the same. Say which part: the internal environment. And say it stays constant even when outside conditions change. Another mistake is to say enzymes die when the body is too hot. Enzymes are not alive. They are denatured.

Your turn. Ali swims in a very cold lake. His body temperature starts to fall. Using this example, explain what is meant by negative feedback. Pause here and work it out.

Answer. Ali's temperature falls below normal. His hypothalamus detects the change. In response, he shivers, and the arterioles in his skin get narrower, so less heat is lost. Shivering releases heat, so his temperature rises. When it reaches thirty-seven degrees again, the response stops. Cold water made it fall, and the body brought it back. So every time: a change from the normal level, a detector, a response that reverses the change, and the response stops once the level is normal.
