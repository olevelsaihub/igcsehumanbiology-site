Accurate results are close to the true value. A score from 0 to 5, judged by eye, is subjective. Subjective means it depends on the person. Two people could watch the same bubbles and give different scores. A number from an instrument is better. An instrument is a measuring tool. A gas syringe measures the volume of gas. As gas fills it, the plunger moves out, and you read the volume from the scale. A colorimeter does the same job for colour.

Step one. Find the weak measurement: a score, a colour judged by eye, or a count of bubbles. Bubbles are a problem because they are different sizes. Ten small bubbles hold less gas than ten big ones. Step two. Replace it with a number. Say what to measure, and what to measure it with. Measure the volume of oxygen, in a gas syringe, in a fixed time, such as two minutes. Step three. Reliable means you get similar results each time you repeat the test. But they do not fix a poor measurement. Sometimes one result is odd. Distilled water has no protein. If it turns purple with biuret, the test for protein, the tube was probably dirty. The fix is to wash the equipment.

A common mistake is writing use better equipment. That earns nothing. Name the quantity, and name the apparatus. Another mistake is writing only repeat it, when the question asks about accuracy. Repeats help reliability. For accuracy, measure with an instrument, or add more values near the peak.

Your turn. Pepsin is an enzyme that digests protein. A student tests it at 10, 30 and 50 degrees Celsius. Cloudy egg white goes clear as the protein is digested. She judges by eye when it is clear. Describe two ways to make her results more accurate. Pause here and work it out.

First, the gaps. 10, 30 and 50 degrees Celsius are 20 degrees apart. So test more temperatures with smaller gaps near 30, such as 20, 25, 35 and 40 degrees Celsius. The rates on this graph are only example values. Second, the measurement. Clear by eye is a judgement. Measure the cloudiness with a colorimeter at fixed times instead, and time it with a stopwatch. So, every time: find the weak measurement. Replace it with a number from an instrument. For an optimum, add values with smaller gaps. If reliable is asked, add repeats and a mean.
