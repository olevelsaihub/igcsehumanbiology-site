Idea. Accurate results are close to the true value. A score from 0 to 5, judged by eye, is subjective. Subjective means it depends on the person. Two people could watch the same bubbles and give different scores. A number from an instrument is better. An instrument is a measuring tool. A gas syringe measures the volume of gas. As gas fills it, the plunger moves out, and you read the volume from the scale. A colorimeter does the same job for colour.

Method. Step one. Find the weak measurement: a score, a colour judged by eye, or a count of bubbles. Bubbles are a problem because they are different sizes. Ten small bubbles hold less gas than ten big ones. Step two. Replace it with a number. Say what to measure, and what to measure it with. Measure the volume of oxygen, in a gas syringe, in a fixed time, such as two minutes. Step three. To find an optimum, the value that gives the fastest rate, look at the gaps. This graph shows made-up example results. The tests are 20 degrees Celsius apart. The real peak could lie between them. So add more values, with smaller gaps, near the highest result. Step four. If the question also says reliable, repeat each test at least three times, and calculate a mean. Reliable means you get similar results each time you repeat the test. But they do not fix a poor measurement. Sometimes one result is odd. Distilled water has no protein. If it turns purple with biuret, the test for protein, the tube was probably dirty. The fix is to wash the equipment.

Worked example. Here is a real exam question. An enzyme is a protein that speeds up a reaction. The enzyme catalase breaks down hydrogen peroxide into water and oxygen. The student scores the oxygen bubbles on a scale of 0 to 5. The question asks how to make the conclusions more accurate and reliable. The weakness: a score from 0 to 5 is a judgement made by eye. So point one: measure the volume of oxygen given off in a fixed time, for example two minutes. Point two names the apparatus: a gas syringe joined to a sealed flask. Point three: the test was done only once. So repeat each condition three times, and calculate a mean. Here is the full answer, in three points: what to measure, what to measure it with, and repeats. It is by olevels.com, not an official mark scheme.

Common mistake. A common mistake is writing use better equipment. That earns nothing. Name the quantity, and name the apparatus. Another mistake is writing only repeat it, when the question asks about accuracy. Repeats help reliability. For accuracy, measure with an instrument, or add more values near the peak.

Your turn. Pepsin is an enzyme that digests protein. A student tests it at 10, 30 and 50 degrees Celsius. Cloudy egg white goes clear as the protein is digested. She judges by eye when it is clear. Describe two ways to make her results more accurate. Pause here and work it out.

Answer. First, the gaps. 10, 30 and 50 degrees Celsius are 20 degrees apart. So test more temperatures with smaller gaps near 30, such as 20, 25, 35 and 40 degrees Celsius. The rates on this graph are only example values. Second, the measurement. Clear by eye is a judgement. Measure the cloudiness with a colorimeter at fixed times instead, and time it with a stopwatch. So, every time: find the weak measurement. Replace it with a number from an instrument. For an optimum, add values with smaller gaps. If reliable is asked, add repeats and a mean.
