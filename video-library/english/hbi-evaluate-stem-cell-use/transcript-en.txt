Most body cells are specialised. That means each type has one job, like a nerve cell or a red blood cell. Stem cells are unspecialised. They can divide many times. They can also differentiate, which means become specialised cells. This makes stem cells useful in medicine. In Parkinson's disease, some neurones in the brain die. Stem cells could be made into new neurones, to replace the damaged ones.

There are two main sources. Embryonic stem cells come from an early embryo, a tiny ball of cells. They can become almost any type of cell. Adult stem cells, for example in bone marrow, can become only a few types, and there are only a few of them. For discuss questions, make a list for each type. Embryonic stem cells can become almost any cell, and grow in the lab for a long time. But the embryo is destroyed, which many people think is wrong. The cells may form tumours, which are lumps of cells that keep dividing. They may also be rejected: the immune system attacks cells that are not your own. Adult stem cells can be taken from the patient's own body, so they are less likely to be rejected, and no embryo is used. But they can form only a few cell types, there are few of them, and they do not grow for long in the lab. A newer source is iPS cells. They are made from a patient's own skin or blood cells, and reprogrammed to act like embryonic cells, so they can become almost any cell. No embryo and no donor are needed, and they match the patient. So, start with the key property. For a treatment, name the new cell and say it replaces damaged cells. For discuss, give advantages and disadvantages of each type. Include an ethical point and a medical risk. And give reasons.

Now a real exam question. Discuss the advantages and disadvantages of embryonic and adult stem cells in medical research. Six marks, with space for each type. Aim for three points for each type. Embryonic, advantages: they can become almost every cell type, and can be kept dividing for drug research. Disadvantages: the embryo is destroyed, and some say it could become a person. Also tumours and rejection. Adult, advantages: they come from the patient's own body, so less rejection, and no embryo is harmed. Disadvantages: only a few cell types, only small numbers, and they do not grow for long in the lab. Here is the full answer. This answer is by olevels.com, not an official mark scheme.

A common mistake is saying adult stem cells can become any type of cell. They cannot. Another is writing only about embryonic cells when the question asks about both. Do not just write that it is unethical. Give the reason: the embryo is destroyed. And do not say stem cells cure a disease. Say they replace damaged cells.

Your turn. A woman has type 1 diabetes. The cells in her pancreas that make insulin have been destroyed. Doctors could make iPS cells from her skin cells. Part a: explain how stem cells could treat her. Part b: give two advantages of iPS cells over embryonic stem cells. Part c: give one risk. Pause here and work it out.

Here is the answer. Part a: the iPS cells from her skin cells differentiate into insulin-making cells, which replace the destroyed ones. Part b: no embryo is destroyed, and the cells have her own antigens, the markers her immune system checks, so they are not rejected. Part c: the cells may form a tumour. So, every time: say stem cells are unspecialised and can differentiate. Say they replace damaged cells. For discuss, cover both types and both sides, with an ethical point and a risk.
