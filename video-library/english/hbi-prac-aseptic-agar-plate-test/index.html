Use aseptic technique to test antibacterial agents on agar

Read the narration

An antibacterial agent kills bacteria, or stops them growing. To test one, grow bacteria on agar, a jelly with food, in a Petri dish. Around each disc of agent, a clear zone forms where no bacteria grow. A bigger clear zone means a more effective agent. All of this must use aseptic technique. Aseptic technique keeps unwanted microorganisms out of the culture, and keeps the culture in, away from people. So work near a Bunsen flame, and lift the lid only a little.

Step one: sterilise. Sterile means with no living microorganisms. Heat the inoculating loop in a Bunsen flame until it is red hot, then let it cool. Use sterile agar and sterile dishes, and flame the neck of the culture bottle. Step two: after adding the bacteria and the agents, tape the lid on, so bacteria cannot reach people. Incubate at twenty-five degrees Celsius, not thirty-seven. At body temperature, harmful bacteria would grow well. Keep the test fair. Step three: measure. Measure the diameter of each clear zone, or count the colonies. A colony is a spot grown from one bacterium. Fewer colonies means a more effective agent. Repeat, and compare the means. The same method tests a cleaner on a surface. Wash the surface with the cleaner and wait. Wipe it with a sterile swab, and spread the swab on agar. Then incubate, count the colonies, and repeat with the second cleaner.

Here is a real exam question. The concrete slab of a pit latrine is washed with an antibacterial solution. Design an investigation to find out which of two solutions is more effective. Seven marks, in full sentences. Control the variables: the same volume and concentration of each solution. Wash one area of the slab with solution A, and leave it for thirty minutes. Rub a sterile swab over a fixed area. Spread it on sterile nutrient agar, and tape the lid on. Incubate at twenty-five degrees Celsius for three days. Repeat with solution B on a similar area. Count the colonies: fewer colonies means a more effective solution. Repeat the whole test and compare the means. Answer by olevels.com, not an official mark scheme. So a full design covers four things: a fair test, aseptic sampling, incubation, and a measured comparison with repeats.

A common mistake is to write 'keep everything clean'. That scores nothing. Name each step, like flaming the loop, and give its reason. Another mistake is to incubate at thirty-seven degrees Celsius. That is body temperature, so harmful bacteria grow well. Use twenty-five degrees Celsius or below.

Your turn. A hospital lab grows a sample of Staphylococcus aureus bacteria. They want to know if it is MRSA, which resists the antibiotic methicillin. Describe how they could test it on agar, and what result would show MRSA. Pause here and work it out.

Spread the bacteria on sterile agar, using a flamed and cooled loop. Lift the lid only a little. Add a disc soaked in methicillin. Tape the lid, and incubate at twenty-five degrees Celsius. Then look for a clear zone. Ordinary Staphylococcus aureus is killed near the disc, so there is a big clear zone. MRSA still grows next to the methicillin, so there is little or no clear zone. So, every time: sterilise, transfer quickly, tape the lid, incubate at twenty-five degrees Celsius, then measure clear zones or count colonies, and repeat.
