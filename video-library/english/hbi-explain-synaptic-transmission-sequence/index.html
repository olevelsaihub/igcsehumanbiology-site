Describe how an impulse crosses a synapse

Idea. Two neurones do not quite touch. The junction between them is called a synapse, and there is a tiny gap, the synaptic cleft. An electrical impulse cannot jump across this gap. So a chemical carries the signal across. This chemical is a neurotransmitter, for example acetylcholine. It is stored in small sacs called vesicles, inside the synaptic knob at the end of the first neurone.

Method. Step one: an impulse arrives at the synaptic knob. The vesicles move to the membrane at the end of the knob, and fuse with it. Step two: the vesicles release the neurotransmitter into the gap. This release is called exocytosis. Step three: the neurotransmitter diffuses across the gap, from a high concentration to a low concentration. Diffuse means spread out by random movement. Step four: it binds to receptor molecules on the membrane of the next neurone, the post-synaptic membrane. Step five: this starts a new electrical impulse in the next neurone. Count your ideas against the marks. A six-mark question needs at least six separate steps. For two marks, give the two core ideas: a chemical diffuses across the gap, and it binds to receptors.

Worked example. Now a real exam question. The diagram shows the gap between neurone A, at the top, and neurone B, below. The question asks how a nerve impulse travels from one side of the gap to the other. Six marks. One: the knob of neurone A has vesicles. Two: they contain a neurotransmitter, such as acetylcholine. Three: when the impulse arrives, they move to the membrane and fuse with it. Four: the neurotransmitter is released. Five: it diffuses across the gap. Six: it binds to receptors on the membrane of neurone B. Seven: a new impulse starts in neurone B. Seven ideas for six marks is safe. Here it is as a full answer. This answer is by olevels.com, not an official mark scheme.

Common mistake. The biggest mistake is writing that the electrical impulse crosses the gap. It does not. The chemical crosses, and a new impulse starts afterwards. Also: say diffuses, not travels or pumped. Do not leave out the receptors. And a neurotransmitter is not a hormone or an enzyme.

Your turn. In a reflex arc, an impulse reaches the end of a sensory neurone. It must pass to the relay neurone. Describe how the signal crosses the synapse. Five marks. Pause here and work it out.

Answer. The impulse reaches the knob of the sensory neurone. Vesicles move to the membrane, fuse, and release neurotransmitter. It diffuses across the gap and binds to receptors on the relay neurone. A new impulse starts in the relay neurone. So, every time: impulse arrives, vesicles fuse and release, the chemical diffuses, binds to receptors, and a new impulse starts. One step for each mark.
