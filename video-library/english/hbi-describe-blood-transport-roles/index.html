Read the narration

Blood moves round the body inside tubes called blood vessels. Blood is a liquid with cells floating in it. The pale yellow liquid is called plasma. Most of the cells are red blood cells. Blood is like a delivery van. It picks things up in one place and drops them off in another. So a good answer always says three things: the carrier, the substance, and the route, from where to where.

Carrier one: red blood cells. They contain haemoglobin, a red protein. In the lungs, haemoglobin joins with oxygen. The red cells carry the oxygen to the body cells, and let it go there. Carrier two: plasma. Many substances are dissolved in it. Dissolved means mixed into the water so you cannot see them. Plasma carries digested food, glucose and amino acids, from the small intestine to the body cells. It carries carbon dioxide from the cells back to the lungs. Plasma also carries urea, a waste made in the liver, to the kidneys. It carries hormones from glands to their target organs. A hormone is a chemical messenger made by a gland. And it carries heat from busy muscles and the liver to the rest of the body. How does blood reach every cell? The heart pumps it into arteries. Arteries lead to capillaries, tiny vessels close to every cell. Substances pass between the capillaries and the cells. Veins bring the blood back to the heart. Some questions ask why a tablet takes time to work. Give the steps in order. It must be absorbed into the blood. Absorbed means it passes through the gut wall into the blood. Then the blood carries it to its target, such as the brain or the nerves.

Here is a real exam question. It asks you to describe the role of the blood transport system in delivering substances to body cells. It is worth five marks, so plan at least five separate points. Start with the red blood cells. Point one: they contain haemoglobin. Point two: in the lungs, haemoglobin joins with oxygen, and the cells carry it to the body cells. Point three: plasma, the liquid part, carries substances dissolved in it. Point four: name them, such as glucose, hormones, carbon dioxide and heat. Point five: the heart pumps the blood through arteries into capillaries, close to every body cell. Point six is a spare: carbon dioxide is carried away from the cells to the lungs. A common mistake is to say red blood cells carry food, or carry blood. Red cells carry oxygen. Food, carbon dioxide, urea and hormones travel in the plasma. The second mistake is a substance with no route. Do not stop at 'plasma carries urea'. Finish it: from the liver to the kidneys.

Your turn. Bilal plays football. His leg muscles work hard and make a lot of carbon dioxide and heat. Describe how his blood carries each one away. Name the carrier, and say where each one goes. Pause here and work it out.

Both pass from his leg muscles into the blood, and both are carried in the plasma. Carbon dioxide goes in the veins back to the heart, and then to the lungs, where it is breathed out. Heat is carried to the skin and the rest of the body, and is lost from the skin. So, every time: name the carrier, red cells or plasma. Name the substance. Then give the route, from where to where, inside the blood vessels.
