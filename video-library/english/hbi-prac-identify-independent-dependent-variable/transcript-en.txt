A variable is a factor that can change. A student puts the same enzyme into three tubes. An enzyme is a protein that speeds up a reaction. She puts each tube at a different temperature: 20, 40 and 60 degrees Celsius. She chose these on purpose. The factor you change on purpose is the independent variable. Then she measures the time each tube takes to digest its food. She does not choose these times. She reads them. What you measure, to see the effect, is the dependent variable. It depends on the independent variable. Everything else stays the same: the same enzyme, the same volume, the same pH. The pH tells you how acidic or alkaline a liquid is. Factors kept the same are control variables. They make the test fair.

Results tables help you. The independent variable is usually in the first column. The other columns hold the readings, so they show the dependent variable. The independent variable can be a type, not a number. Bacteria grow on jelly in this dish. Each paper disc holds a different antibiotic. An antibiotic is a drug that kills bacteria. A clear zone forms where bacteria die. The independent variable is the type of antibiotic. The dependent variable is the diameter of the clear zone. Time can also be the independent variable. Here one tube gives off oxygen gas. The student reads the volume every two minutes. She chose those times, so time is the independent variable. The volume of oxygen is the dependent variable. So, four steps. Find the one factor that differs on purpose. Find the reading recorded each time. Check the table columns. Then write each variable with its exact name.

Here is a real exam question. A student adds an enzyme that digests lipids to a lipid solution. Lipids are fats and oils. The pH is read every minute for ten minutes, at 37 and at 80 degrees Celsius. Give the independent and the dependent variable. It is worth two marks. Here are both tables together. Compare them. What differs between them? Only the temperature: 37 or 80 degrees. The student chose these. So temperature is the independent variable. What is recorded in every box? The pH of the solution. So pH is the dependent variable. Time is also accepted as independent, because the student chose when to read. This answer is by olevels.com, not the official mark scheme.

The most common mistake is to swap them. The pH did not get chosen. It was measured, so it cannot be independent. Another mistake is naming a control variable. Here every tube is at 37 degrees. Temperature does not change, so it is a control. The volume of enzyme changes on purpose, so that is the independent variable. The last mistake is a vague name. Writing just antibiotic, or just bacteria, scores nothing. Say exactly what changes, or exactly what is measured.

Your turn. Four students each exercise for a different time: one, two, three or four minutes. They all step on the same box. Straight after, each student's pulse rate is counted, in beats per minute. Give the independent variable, the dependent variable, and one control variable. Pause here and work it out.

Put it in a table. The times were chosen, so they go in the first column. The independent variable is the time spent exercising. The pulse rate is counted, so the dependent variable is the pulse rate. Part c. A control is kept the same for everyone. The height of the box is one. The type of exercise is another. So, every time. Changed on purpose: independent. Measured: dependent. Kept the same: control. Write each one with its exact name.
