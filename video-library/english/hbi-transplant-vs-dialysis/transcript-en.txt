Idea. Your kidneys filter your blood. They remove urea, a toxic waste, and extra water, to make urine. If the kidneys fail, urea builds up in the blood, and the person can die. There are two treatments: dialysis and a kidney transplant. In dialysis, the blood flows out through a tube into a machine. A partially permeable membrane, a thin sheet with tiny holes, separates the blood from dialysis fluid. Urea passes out of the blood into the fluid. Then the cleaned blood goes back. In a transplant, surgeons put a healthy kidney from a donor into the patient. A donor is the person who gives the organ. The failed kidneys usually stay in place.

Method. Step one: check what is asked. One treatment or both? Advantages, disadvantages, or both? Step two: make a quick grid with four boxes. A transplant is a long-term solution. After one operation, the patient can live a normal life, with no dialysis visits and fewer limits on diet. Over many years, it costs less. But there are too few donors. The immune system, the body's defence, may attack the new kidney: this is rejection. So the patient takes immunosuppressant drugs for life. These weaken the immune system, so infections are more likely. Surgery also risks infection. Dialysis is available to all patients, and it needs no immunosuppressant drugs. But it is only temporary. The patient spends long sessions in hospital several times a week, keeps a strict diet, and over many years it costs more. Kidneys are donated more often than hearts or livers. People have two kidneys and can live with one. So a living person, often a relative, can donate one. And one dead donor can give kidneys to two patients.

Worked example. Now a real exam question. Kidney disease can be treated by dialysis or by a transplant. The question asks you to discuss the advantages and disadvantages of each. Five marks. Five marks, both treatments, both sides. So fill all four boxes, and write at least six points. Give one more point than the marks, in case one is weak. Common mistake. A common mistake is to write that a transplant is better, with no reasons. Another is giving only advantages in a discuss question. Give both sides. Also, dialysis does not cure kidney failure. Rejection is a transplant problem, not a dialysis one. And if you write expensive, say which treatment, and why.

Your turn. Ali is 25, and his kidneys have failed. He wants to travel for work. His brother offers him one of his kidneys. Give two reasons why a transplant may suit Ali better than dialysis. Then give one risk of the transplant. Pause here and work it out.

Answer. A transplant gives Ali a normal life, with no dialysis sessions several times a week, so he can travel. And his brother is a living donor, so he need not wait for one. The risk: his body may reject the kidney, and he needs immunosuppressant drugs for life, so infections are more likely. So, every time: check what is asked, make the four-box grid, and write each point as a full idea linked to its treatment.
