Idea. A neurone is a nerve cell. It carries electrical signals called nerve impulses. Here is a motor neurone. At one end is the cell body. Inside it is the nucleus. Short branches, called dendrites, come out of the cell body. From the cell body, one long fibre runs away: the axon. It carries impulses away from the cell body. A fatty layer, the myelin sheath, wraps around the axon. At the far end, the axon splits into small branches, the axon terminals, ending in synaptic knobs: small swellings that pass the impulse on to the next cell.

Method. What does the myelin sheath do? It insulates the axon, like the plastic round a wire. This makes the impulses travel faster. Sensory and relay neurones have the same parts, in different places. In a sensory neurone the cell body sits part-way along the fibre. But for a drawing, the exam usually wants a motor neurone. To draw a neurone, keep it simple. Step one: a round cell body at one end, a nucleus inside it, and several short dendrites around it. Step two: one long axon. Make it many times longer than the cell body is wide. End it with a few small branches. Step three: draw the myelin sheath as blocks along the axon. Step four: add each label with a ruled line that touches the exact part.

Worked example. Now a real exam question. It asks you to draw a diagram of a motor neurone, and to label the nucleus, the axon and the myelin sheath. Four marks. Mark one is for the shape. A round cell body at one end with dendrites. Then a long axon ending in small branches. Now the three labels, each with a ruled line. The nucleus line touches the dot in the cell body. The axon line touches the long fibre. Draw the myelin sheath as blocks along the axon, and label one block. Four marks. This answer is by olevels.com, not an official mark scheme.

Common mistakes: a nucleus drawn on the axon, an axon no longer than the cell body, and label lines that stop short of the part. When you compare two neurones, name the feature. Count the branches, look at their length, and count the synaptic knobs. Do not just write smaller.

Your turn. Neurone X is a motor neurone. Neurone Y is a sensory neurone. Give two differences in structure between X and Y. Name the feature each time. Pause here and work it out.

Answer. One: in X, the cell body is at one end of the neurone. In Y, the cell body is part-way along the fibre, on a short side branch. Two: X has dendrites around its cell body. Y has no dendrites on its cell body. Each difference names a feature and compares both neurones. So, every time: cell body at one end with a nucleus and dendrites. A long axon with myelin blocks and small branches. Ruled labels that touch the parts.
