Water comes into your body when you drink and eat. Water leaves in sweat, in your breath, and in urine. Osmoregulation means keeping the water in the blood steady. Part of the brain, the hypothalamus, detects the water in the blood. The pituitary gland, just below it, releases a hormone called ADH into the blood. A hormone is a chemical carried in the blood. ADH travels to the kidneys.

ADH acts on the collecting ducts. It makes their walls more permeable, which means they let more water through. So more water goes back into the blood. So when the blood has too little water, more ADH is released. More water is taken back, and a small volume of concentrated urine is made. Concentrated urine has little water and lots of urea, so it is dark. So if one urine sample is darker than another, that person had less water in the blood, and more ADH. When the blood has too much water, for example after drinking a lot, less ADH is released. Less water is taken back, so a large volume of dilute urine is made. Alcohol lowers the amount of ADH that is released. So less water is taken back, and more dilute urine is made.
