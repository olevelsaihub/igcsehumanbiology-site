You touch a hot plate, and your hand pulls away before you even think. This is a reflex action: a fast, automatic response to a stimulus. A stimulus is a change that the body detects, like heat. Most reflexes protect the body. A neurone is a nerve cell. A nerve impulse is an electrical signal that travels along it. A receptor is a cell that detects a stimulus. The impulse follows a fixed route, called a reflex arc. Receptor, sensory neurone, relay neurone, motor neurone, effector. Learn this order.

The receptor turns the stimulus into electrical impulses. The sensory neurone carries them from the receptor to the central nervous system, or CNS. The CNS is the brain and the spinal cord. The relay neurone is short, and it is inside the spinal cord. It links the sensory neurone to the motor neurone. The gap between two neurones is a synapse. The motor neurone carries impulses away from the CNS to the effector. The effector is a muscle that contracts, or a gland that releases a substance. Here the arm muscle contracts and pulls the hand away. How do you tell them apart on a drawing? Start at the receptor and follow the arrows. A sensory neurone has its cell body part-way along, outside the spinal cord. A motor neurone has its cell body inside the spinal cord, and a long axon. To define a reflex for three marks, give three separate ideas: it is fast, it is automatic, and it protects the body.

Now a real exam question. Diagram 1 shows a reflex arc. The spinal cord is on the left. Neurone C comes in at the top, with an arrow pointing into the cord. Neurone A goes out at the bottom. Name neurones A, B and C. Three marks. Here is a drawing laid out the same way, with the cord on the left. C carries impulses into the spinal cord from the receptor end. So C is the sensory neurone. B is the short neurone inside the spinal cord, joining the other two. So B is the relay neurone. A leaves the spinal cord and goes to the effector muscle. So A is the motor neurone. Check the order: receptor, C, B, A, muscle. It makes sense. This answer is by olevels.com, not an official mark scheme.

A common mistake is getting the order wrong, for example calling the neurone that touches the muscle sensory. Always start from the receptor. Another mistake: saying a motor neurone carries messages to the brain. It carries impulses away from the CNS, to a muscle or a gland.

Your turn. A barefoot boy steps on a sharp pin, and his leg lifts at once. Name the receptor and the effector. Name the three neurones in order. Then say what the motor neurone does. Pause here and work it out.

The receptor is in the skin of his foot. The effector is a muscle in his leg. The impulse goes along the sensory neurone, then the relay neurone, then the motor neurone. The motor neurone carries impulses from the spinal cord to the leg muscle, which contracts. So, every time: find the receptor end, then name sensory, relay, motor in that order. For a job, say impulses, and from where to where.
