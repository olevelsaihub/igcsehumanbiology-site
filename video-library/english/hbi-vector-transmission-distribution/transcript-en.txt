Idea. A pathogen is a microorganism that causes disease. A host is the person it lives in. A vector is an organism that carries a pathogen from one host to another, without causing the disease itself. Think of a new host. Two vectors to know. The female Anopheles mosquito carries the malaria parasite, a single-celled organism. The housefly carries bacteria, such as the ones that cause typhoid.

Method. Steps one to four: name the vector and the pathogen. Then give the pick-up step, where the vector gets the pathogen. Then the carry step. Then the deliver step, how it reaches the next person. For the housefly: Pick-up: it feeds on faeces and rotting waste. Carry: bacteria stick to its legs and mouthparts. Deliver: it lands on food and leaves bacteria there. A person who eats the food takes them in. Breaking the chain prevents disease. For malaria, sleep under mosquito nets, use insect repellent and insecticide, and drain standing water, where mosquitoes lay their eggs. For typhoid, cover food, kill flies, and have good sanitation: safe removal of sewage. Why do disease levels differ between places? Name a specific factor and say which way it acts. Warm, wet places suit mosquitoes, so more malaria. Crowded places with open waste have more flies, and more people to infect.

Worked example. Now a real exam question. P. vivax is one of several parasites that cause malaria. Explain how P. vivax is passed from one person to another. Three marks. First, name the vector. P. vivax is carried by a female mosquito, so the mosquito is the vector. It carries the parasite; it does not cause the disease. Pick-up step: the mosquito bites a person who already has malaria, and sucks up blood containing the parasites. Carry step: the parasites stay in the mosquito. Deliver step: when it bites a second person, it passes the parasites into that person's blood. Now they are infected. Here is the full answer. Common mistake. A common mistake is to write: the mosquito causes malaria. It does not. A parasite causes malaria; the mosquito only carries it. Also, do not start with the mosquito already infected. Another mistake: the housefly does not bite people to pass on typhoid. It lands on food, leaves bacteria there, and a person eats the food. For differences between places, a vague reason scores nothing. Name the factor and the direction. Fit the data: for a city and the countryside, think more people and more waste, not just hotter.

Your turn. Dengue fever is caused by a virus, spread by Aedes mosquitoes. Part a: explain how the virus passes from one person to another. Part b: in a made-up report, a crowded city with many open water tanks has more cases than a dry, quiet town. Suggest two reasons. Pause here and work it out.

Answer. Part a. The Aedes mosquito is the vector. It bites a person who has dengue and sucks up blood with the virus. The virus is carried in the mosquito. When it bites another person, the virus passes into their blood. Part b. Open water tanks give mosquitoes places to lay eggs, so there are more vectors. In a crowded city, more people live close together, so each mosquito can bite and infect more people. So, every time: name the vector and the pathogen. Give pick up, carry and deliver, in order. For places, name a factor and say which way it acts.
