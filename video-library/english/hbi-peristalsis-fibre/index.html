Explain how food moves by peristalsis and why fibre helps

Read the narration

When you swallow, your tongue shapes the chewed food into a soft ball. This ball is called a bolus. The bolus goes into the oesophagus. The oesophagus is the muscular tube from the mouth to the stomach. The wall of the gut has two layers of muscle. Circular muscles go round the tube, like rings. Longitudinal muscles run along its length. A muscle can contract, which means it gets shorter, or it can relax.

Step one. The circular muscles just behind the bolus contract. The ring gets narrower and squeezes the bolus forward. The circular muscles just in front of the bolus relax, so the tube there stays wide open. This happens again and again along the tube, as a wave. This wave of muscle contraction is called peristalsis. Peristalsis moves food along the whole alimentary canal. That is the whole gut, from the mouth to the anus. Now compare two diets. Fibre is the part of plant food, such as cell walls, that we cannot digest. Digest means break down into small molecules, using enzymes. Enzymes are proteins that speed up reactions. Fibre is not digested, so it cannot pass into the blood. It stays in the gut. With little fibre, the waste is small and hard. With lots of fibre, it is bulky and soft. Bulk gives the muscles something to push against. It stretches the gut wall, and this stimulates the muscles, which means it sets them off. They contract, so the food keeps moving. Constipation means the waste, called faeces, moves too slowly and is hard to pass. Fibre prevents constipation. It also lowers the risk of cancer of the bowel, the large intestine. So the method is: name the process. Say what moves. Say what the muscles do. For fibre, say it is not digested and adds bulk. Then link the bulk to the muscles.

Now a real exam question. Explain how food is moved along the alimentary canal, and how fibre helps. Five marks, so plan at least five ideas. Point one: name the process, peristalsis. Point two: the food is a bolus. Point three: circular muscles in the gut wall contract behind the bolus and relax in front, so a wave pushes it along. Point four: fibre cannot be digested. Point five: so it adds bulk. Point six: the bulk stimulates the muscles to contract. Point seven: so food keeps moving and there is no constipation. Seven ideas for five marks is safe. Here is the full answer. This answer is by olevels.com, not an official mark scheme.

A common mistake is to say that food falls down by gravity, or that the food squeezes itself. Food is pushed by the muscles in the gut wall. Another mistake is the wrong side. If the muscles in front of the bolus contract, they block it. The muscles behind the bolus contract, and they push it on. Last, fibre is not digested and gives no energy. Do not just write that fibre helps digestion. Say it adds bulk, and the bulk stimulates peristalsis.

Your turn. Sara eats white bread and meat, and almost no fruit or vegetables. She has constipation. Her doctor tells her to eat beans, brown rice and vegetables, which are rich in fibre. Explain how this advice helps food move through her large intestine. Four marks. Pause here and work it out.

Here is the answer. One: fibre is not digested. Two: so it adds bulk to the waste in her large intestine. Three: the bulk stimulates the circular muscles to contract behind the waste. Four: peristalsis moves it on, so there is less constipation. So, every time: name peristalsis and the bolus. Muscles contract behind and relax in front. For fibre: not digested, adds bulk, and the bulk stimulates the muscles.
