Osmosis is the movement of water across a partially permeable membrane. Water potential tells us which way it goes. A dilute solution has lots of free water, so a high water potential. A concentrated solution has a low water potential. Water moves from high to low water potential. Adding solute, such as glucose or salt, lowers the water potential of that side. A solute is a substance dissolved in water.

Step one: name osmosis. Step two: compare the water potential on each side. In pure water, water enters a red blood cell, so it swells and may burst. If the water potentials are equal, nothing changes. In a concentrated solution, water leaves, and the cell shrinks. Step four: give the effect on the body. In diabetes, blood glucose is high. So the blood has a lower water potential than the cells. Water leaves the cells into the blood, and the extra water leaves as urine. After dehydration, the plasma also has a lower water potential. Red blood cells lose water and shrink, so they carry less oxygen. In lactose intolerance, lactose is not digested. It stays in the gut and lowers the water potential there. Water moves out of the body cells into the gut, causing diarrhoea. Diarrhoea means watery faeces. Some questions use data. Here is a made-up example. Pieces of tissue go into different sugar solutions, and the percentage change in mass is plotted. Where the line crosses zero, there is no net water movement. That is the concentration of the cell contents.

Now a real exam question. Body cells are surrounded by tissue fluid, the liquid that bathes the cells. In cellular oedema, too much water enters body cells. Explain how water can enter body cells from the tissue fluid. Three marks. Point one: name the process. Water moves into cells by osmosis. Point two: compare the water potentials. For water to go in, the cytoplasm, the jelly inside the cell, must have the lower water potential. So the tissue fluid has the higher water potential. Point three: the direction. Water moves from the higher water potential in the tissue fluid, to the lower water potential in the cell, across the partially permeable cell membrane. Here is the full answer. A common mistake is to say water moves from low to high water potential. It always moves from high to low. Another mistake is to say red blood cells burst in a concentrated solution. They lose water, so they shrink. They can burst only in a dilute solution, such as pure water.

One point five, minus six. Which solution gives no net water movement? Explain the result at one point five per cent. Pause here and work it out.

Plot the points. The change is zero at zero point nine per cent. There, the solution and the liver cells have the same water potential, so there is no net water movement. At one point five per cent, the solution has a lower water potential than the liver cells. So water leaves the cells by osmosis, across their partially permeable membranes. The liver loses mass. So, every time: name osmosis. Compare the water potentials. Water moves from high to low, across a partially permeable membrane. Then give the effect on the body.
