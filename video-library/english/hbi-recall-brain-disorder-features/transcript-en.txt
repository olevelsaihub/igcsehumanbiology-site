Idea. Dementia is a group of brain diseases that slowly damage memory and thinking. Alzheimer's disease is the most common kind. In Alzheimer's, clumps of a protein called beta-amyloid build up. These clumps are called plaques. They form in the gaps between neurones, the nerve cells, and later inside them. Beta-amyloid forms because another protein, called APP, is not broken down properly. The first symptom of Alzheimer's is usually short-term memory loss. A symptom is a sign of a disease. Short-term memory holds recent events, like what you did today. Long-term memory holds events from years ago, which have been processed more.

Method. Step one: find the condition the question names. Each one has its own symptoms. Alzheimer's: memory loss, confusion, problems with speech and language, repeating actions, losing thinking ability, and changes in social behaviour. Later, people also lose control of voluntary muscles, the muscles you move on purpose. Parkinson's disease: a tremor, which means shaking, stiff muscles, slow movement, and poor balance and posture. Speech and handwriting change. It can also bring sleep problems, anxiety and depression. Two mental illnesses. Schizophrenia: hallucinations, which means seeing or hearing things that are not there, and confused thinking. Depression: low mood and a lack of energy. Risk factors make illness more likely: low income, low self-esteem, violence or abuse, and drug or alcohol misuse. Step two: if the question says other than memory loss, do not give it. Step three: give one different idea for each mark. Speech problems, confusion and repeated actions count as one group, so give only one of them. Then take a different idea from another group. Step four: for a passage, use the lines given, in your own words. For suggest why, give a sensible reason. Richer countries have more dementia. People live longer, because fewer die young of infectious diseases. Diets also have more saturated fat and processed food, and people may drink more alcohol.

Common mistake. A common mistake is to swap conditions. Tremor is a symptom of Parkinson's, not Alzheimer's. Memory loss belongs to Alzheimer's, not to depression or schizophrenia. Another mistake: two answers that are the same idea. Forgets things and cannot remember score only one mark. Choose two different ideas.

Your turn. Mr Khan is 72. His hands shake when he is resting, and his handwriting has become very small. He finds it hard to start walking. His daughter says he sleeps badly and seems anxious. Part a: which disease does he most likely have? Part b: give two other symptoms of this disease that are not described here. Pause here and work it out.

Answer. Here is the answer. Part a: Parkinson's disease. Part b: shaking, handwriting, slow movement, sleep and anxiety are already in the passage, so cross them out.
