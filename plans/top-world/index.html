A-Grade Plan · 120 types

Cells, DNA and cell division: Compare light and electron microscopes and use their parts · Draw or label a human cell as seen under the microscope · Name the parts of a nucleotide and the DNA backbone · Define specialised cells, tissues and organs and identify human tissues · Match human cell structures to their functions · Prepare cheek-cell or blood slides for a light microscope · Compare DNA with RNA · Describe DNA's double helix, base pairing and replication · Describe the products and purposes of mitosis · Describe transcription and translation in human cells · Apply the triplet code to bases, codons and amino acids · Use image size, actual size and magnification

Core investigation skills: Identify the independent or dependent variable · State or explain safety precautions for a practical · Improve reliability with repeats, means and larger samples · State the variables to control in an investigation · Describe a control or explain why a step is in a method · Identify a measurement weakness and improve accuracy · Plan or specify a single-factor laboratory method

Core maths skills for biology data: Construct or complete a results or frequency table · Read or estimate a value from a graph, chart, table or image · Read two values from a graph, chart or table and find the difference · Express a part as a percentage of a total · Find a mean, median, mode or mean rate of change · Calculate and express a ratio in a required form · Draw a bar chart and justify its use · Plot a line graph from a table of results · Draw a line or curve of best fit through plotted points · Calculate a percentage change or percentage difference · Calculate an amount from a percentage, fraction or probability · Describe a trend or compare data sets and conclude · Judge whether data support a claim and spot their limits · Scale per-unit or reference quantities and combine them · Substitute into a given equation, rearranging and converting units

Food molecules, enzymes and moving substances: Define diffusion, osmosis and active transport · Recall the elements and building blocks of food molecules · Describe how to carry out a food test, including DCPIP for vitamin C · Name the food-test reagent or read food-test results · Calculate area, volume and surface area to volume ratio · Explain diffusion in a context and factors affecting its rate · Explain how inhibitors or a mutation change enzyme activity · Explain how temperature or pH changes enzyme activity · Read an enzyme optimum from data and judge how reliable it is · Explain water movement by osmosis in body contexts

Respiration: Write or complete the respiration equations · Explain how respiration releases energy and ATP transfers it · Set up and explain the indicator practical comparing carbon dioxide in breath

Diet, digestion and teeth: Match digestive enzymes to substrate, product and site · Identify alimentary canal organs and describe what each does · Link a nutrient to its function, source and deficiency disease · Name tooth types and tissues, state their jobs and compare tooth sets · Define a balanced diet and explain why needs differ between people · Explain how tooth decay happens and justify ways to prevent it · Explain an enzyme practical result, including levelling off as substrate runs out · Explain how villi aid absorption and the effect of damage · Explain where bile comes from and how it aids fat digestion · Measure food energy by burning and explain why the value is too low

Blood, heart and circulation: Identify blood components and match them to their features and jobs · Describe phagocytosis, antibody production and barriers to pathogens · Name heart chambers and major vessels from a diagram · Describe what red cells and plasma carry, and from where to where · Trace the route of blood through the heart, lungs and body · Describe how blood clots and explain why clotting matters · Explain how a blocked coronary artery damages the heart · Describe how heart disease is prevented and treated · Identify arteries, veins and capillaries and relate their structure to function · Explain red-cell adaptations and the effects of more or less oxygen carriage · Explain systolic, diastolic and changing pressure around the circulation · Use ABO antigens and antibodies to choose donors and explain clumping · Explain how tissue fluid forms, what it does and why it builds up

Breathing and gas exchange: Name thorax and airway structures and the route of air · Explain how diaphragm and intercostal muscles drive breathing · Explain how smoking damages health, including in pregnancy · Explain heart and breathing changes in exercise and how carbon dioxide controls breathing · Explain how alveoli are adapted for gas exchange and effects of damage

Bones, muscles and joints: Identify bones and bone tissues on a diagram · Name joint types and compare their movement · State the function of a skeletal or joint part and explain effects of damage · Explain how biceps, triceps and tendons move the arm

Nerves, senses, hormones and drugs: Identify brain regions and match them to their functions · Identify parts of the eye and state their functions · Match hormones to glands and effects, and contrast them with nerves · Draw, label and compare the structure of a neurone · Define a reflex and name the neurones on the receptor-to-effector path · Describe how an impulse crosses a synapse · Explain how the eye focuses on near and distant objects · Explain eye defects and how they are corrected or treated · Explain the effects and dangers of alcohol and other drugs · Show how an image forms on the retina and reaches the brain · Plan an investigation measuring a response or treatment in people · Explain validity problems and fixes in investigations on people · Predict how a drug, toxin or disease changes synaptic transmission

Homeostasis: skin, kidney and liver: Identify urinary system and nephron structures · Explain homeostasis and negative feedback · Explain how insulin and glucagon control blood glucose · Explain how skin blood flow and sweating control body temperature · Explain the liver's roles in deamination and breaking down drugs · Explain ultrafiltration and selective reabsorption in the nephron · Explain how ADH changes urine volume and concentration · Explain diffusion across a partially permeable membrane in dialysis and Visking tubing

Reproduction and development: State the role of a named reproductive hormone · Identify reproductive organs from their roles or positions · Compare haploid and diploid cells and explain why meiosis matters · Name contraceptive methods, explain how they work and compare them

Inheritance and genetic diagrams: Use genetic terms: alleles, genotypes, carriers and blood-group genotypes · Define a gene mutation and name its causes · Explain how a mutation changes a protein and phenotype · Draw a genetic diagram for a monohybrid or sex-linked cross · Explain sex-linked inheritance and why males are affected more · Deduce genotypes from a pedigree, family or blood group · Build or read a pedigree to decide how a condition is inherited · Work out the probability of a child's genotype or phenotype from a cross

Pathogens, immunity and public health: Define pathogen, classify diseases and name the organism type causing each · Identify bacterial cell structures and compare with human cells · Describe how a named infection spreads, shows itself and is prevented · Explain antigen-antibody specificity and how monoclonal antibodies are made · Explain how vaccination or infection gives active immunity · Classify immunity as natural or artificial and active or passive · State what antibiotics treat and explain how resistance develops · Use aseptic technique to test antibacterial agents on agar

Grade 8 Plan · 26 types

More nerves, senses, hormones and drugs: State causes and symptoms of dementia, Parkinson's and mental illness · Identify ear parts and use the sound path to explain hearing and hearing loss · Show and explain why impulses travel one way across a synapse

More cells, DNA and cell division: Locate DNA and chromosomes in a human cell · Sequence and describe the stages of mitosis · Describe genetic modification and gene therapy for human health · Explain and evaluate uses of embryonic and adult stem cells

More maths skills for biology data: Convert between pie-chart sectors, percentages and counts · Scale a rate per 100 000 people to a whole population · Estimate an unmeasured value by interpolating or extrapolating data

More diet, digestion and teeth: Explain how food moves by peristalsis and why fibre helps · Explain causes of obesity, its health risks and dietary advice · Calculate BMI and place it in a weight category · Apply digestive enzyme action to an unfamiliar situation

More blood, heart and circulation: Explain chamber wall thickness and heart valves by their function · Explain how beta-blockers and ACE inhibitors lower heart rate or pressure

More breathing and gas exchange: Explain how airway features keep air clean and flowing · Use a spirometer and identify lung volumes on its trace · Calculate breathing rate or air volume from trace or table

More bones, muscles and joints: Draw or label the parts of a synovial joint · Explain dietary needs for bone and muscle and describe osteoporosis

More homeostasis: skin, kidney and liver: Identify skin structures and state what each does · Evaluate kidney transplants and dialysis as treatments

More reproduction and development: Describe labour and the advantages of breastfeeding · Describe fertilisation and early embryo development, including twins

More pathogens, immunity and public health: Explain bacterial growth conditions and how food preservation slows it

Grade 9 Plan · 16 types

Further data and practical skills: Convert units and write numbers in standard form or significant figures

Further cells, reproduction and disease: Describe the steps of IVF treatment · Make alginate beads and give uses and advantages of immobilised enzymes · Relate sperm structures to their roles in fertilisation · State the symptoms and effects of inherited conditions · Explain oral rehydration and treatment of diarrhoeal disease · Explain sewage treatment, pit latrine design and decomposer roles · Describe virus structure and reproduction · Explain genetic and environmental causes of variation · Interpret human growth data, proportions and percentile charts · Explain how the placenta, cord and hormones support a fetus · Interpret antibacterial culture investigations

Further body systems: Name receptors and the senses or stimuli they detect · Compare blood entering and leaving the kidney · Interpret a raised or lowered blood cell count · Explain stereoscopic vision and how it helps judge distance
