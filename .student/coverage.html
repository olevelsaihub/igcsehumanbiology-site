Full difficulty rubric

Expert judgement of the canonical method demonstrated by the source-checked example, using the five factors below. Type-level teaching demand, not a claim that all practice members have identical difficulty. No marks, frequency or grade prediction enters the score.

Each factor is 0–3; score is the sum (0–15). Fixed transparent authoring bands, not percentile bands.

Reasoning

  1. Recall or direct identification
  2. One inference or straightforward procedure
  3. Several dependent decisions or a justified comparison
  4. A sustained chain with intermediate conclusions or competing models

Conceptual demand

  1. Direct observation or factual recognition
  2. Apply one standard subject idea
  3. Connect ideas or explain a causal mechanism
  4. Resolve a subtle limitation, apparent contradiction or competing explanation

Mathematical work

  1. No numerical operation
  2. One routine operation or simple scale reading
  3. Several operations, rearrangement, unit conversions or graph calculation
  4. Demanding algebra, trigonometry or a novel numerical relation combined with other mathematics

Unfamiliarity

  1. Standard familiar presentation
  2. A familiar method in a concrete context
  3. Interpret an unusual setup, representation or constraint
  4. Substantial transfer to an unfamiliar model with assumptions to resolve

Linked skills

  1. Self-contained response
  2. One supporting skill
  3. Several supporting skills or an earlier calculated result
  4. A sustained integration across distinct methods or earlier results

Easy: 0–3 · Medium: 4–6 · Difficult: 7–9 · Very Difficult: 10–15

What these pages claim

  • Stars describe the past, not the future. They summarise how often a type appeared in the 36 eligible 4HB1 papers from 2019–2025 (June 2021 assessment material is kept for practice but not counted). They are not predictions.
