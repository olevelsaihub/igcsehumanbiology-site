Idea. A joint is where two bones meet. A synovial joint is a joint that moves freely. The shoulder and the hip are ball and socket joints: a round head of bone sits in a cup. The elbow and the knee are hinge joints. Inside, every synovial joint has the same parts. You must be able to draw them. Step one: draw the two bone ends, with a small gap between them. Label each bone.

Method. Step two: cover the end of each bone with cartilage. Cartilage is a smooth, tough tissue. It stops the bones rubbing together and wearing away. Draw it on both bone ends, not just one. Step three: draw a tough capsule round the joint. Inside it, draw the synovial membrane, a thin layer that lines the joint. It makes synovial fluid. The fluid fills the space between the bones. It lubricates the joint, which means it makes it slippery, so it moves smoothly. Step four: label the capsule and ligament on the outside. Ligaments are tough bands that join one bone to the other bone. They hold the joint together. Step five: one mark is for the whole drawing. Use clear, smooth outlines. Rule each label line with a ruler. Make it touch the part, and never let two lines cross.

Worked example. Now a real exam question. The diagram shows an arm. The synovial joint is at the top of the humerus, the upper arm bone, where it meets the shoulder. The question asks you to draw a labelled diagram of its inside. Six marks. The shoulder is a ball and socket joint. So first, draw the round head of the humerus fitting into a cup, the socket of the scapula, or shoulder blade. Label both bones. Next, cartilage over the ball and lining the socket: label it. Then synovial fluid filling the space between them: label it. Then the synovial membrane lining the joint, and the capsule and ligament outside, joining the two bones. Keep the outlines smooth and the label lines ruled. Here is what the full answer shows. This answer is by olevels.com, not an official mark scheme.

Common mistake. A common mistake is to draw cartilage on only one bone. Another is to mix up the fluid and the membrane. The fluid is in the space. The membrane is the lining that makes it. Also, do not label a tendon here. A tendon joins muscle to bone. A ligament joins bone to bone. And never leave a part without a label.

Your turn. The knee is a synovial joint between the femur, the thigh bone, and the tibia, the shin bone. It is a hinge joint. Draw and label its inside. Then state what the synovial fluid does. Pause here and work it out.

Answer. Draw the femur above and the tibia below, meeting like a hinge. Add cartilage on both bone ends, synovial fluid in the space, the membrane lining the joint, and the capsule and ligament outside. For part b: the fluid lubricates the joint, so the bones move smoothly. So, every time: check the joint type, draw two bone ends, then cartilage, fluid, membrane and capsule, each with a ruled label line.
