Read the narration

Here is the female system, from the front. The two ovaries make and release eggs, called ova. Each oviduct carries an egg to the uterus. The uterus is where a baby grows. Below it is the cervix, a ring of muscle. Then the vagina. Sperm meet the egg in the oviduct. This is fertilisation. The fertilised egg divides to form an embryo. The embryo then sinks into the thick uterus lining: this is implantation. At birth, the cervix widens, and the muscle of the uterus pushes the baby out.

Now the male system, from the side. The testes make sperm. The epididymis stores the sperm. The sperm duct carries sperm towards the urethra. The urethra runs from the bladder through the penis. Two glands add fluid: the seminal vesicles and the prostate gland. This fluid helps the sperm swim. Sperm plus this fluid is called semen. The method for a matching question. Read one job at a time. Say to yourself which organ does it. Find that word in the box, or follow the label line to its end. Then copy the word exactly, and check every row. Two words cause trouble. The ureter carries urine from a kidney to the bladder. The urethra carries urine from the bladder to the outside. Only the urethra is part of the reproductive system.

Now a real exam question. The box gives nine parts of the reproductive systems. Complete the table: give the part that matches each description. Seven marks. Male rows first. Row one: the duct that carries urine and sperm is the urethra. Row two: sperm are made in the testes. Row four: the fluid part of semen comes from the seminal vesicle. Female rows. Row three: a fertilised ovum implants in the uterus. Row five: sperm are deposited in the vagina. Row six: fertilisation happens in the oviduct. Row seven: the ovary releases ova. Here are all seven answers. Explanation by olevels.com, not an official mark scheme.

A common mistake is to write ureter for the duct that carries urine and sperm. The ureter only carries urine to the bladder. The answer is urethra. Another mistake is to say fertilisation happens in the uterus. It happens in the oviduct. The uterus is where the embryo implants.

Your turn. Name four parts. First, the part that stores sperm. Second, the tube that carries sperm from a testis towards the urethra. Third, a ring of muscle that widens during birth. Fourth, the organ whose muscle pushes the baby out. Pause here and work it out.

Part a: sperm are stored in the epididymis. Part b: the sperm duct carries them towards the urethra. Part c: the ring of muscle that widens is the cervix. Part d: the muscular wall of the uterus contracts to push the baby out. So remember the jobs. The ovary releases eggs. The oviduct is where fertilisation happens. The uterus is for implantation. The testes make sperm, and the sperm duct carries them to the urethra.
