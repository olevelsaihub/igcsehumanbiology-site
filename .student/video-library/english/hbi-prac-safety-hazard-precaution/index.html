Every practical has hazards. A hazard is something that could cause harm. This flame is a hazard: it can burn you. The risk is the chance that harm happens. A precaution is an action that lowers the risk. For example, tie long hair back. Here is a common practical: burning food to heat water. Find the hazards. The hot water may spit out of the tube. The flame can burn skin and hair. The mounted needle is sharp. Now match a precaution to each hazard. Wear safety goggles, to protect the eyes from spitting water. Hold the tube with a test tube holder, so hands are not burnt. Keep your face an arm's length away. Never taste the food: some people have allergies.

Other practicals have other hazards. Very hot or ice-cold water can hurt skin, so check it with a thermometer first. A spirometer mouthpiece may be shared. A spirometer measures the air you breathe. Sterilise the mouthpiece: kill the microbes on it. This stops infection. In any breath test, breathe out gently and never suck, so no liquid gets into your mouth. Biuret solution is an irritant. An irritant makes skin or eyes sore. Wear goggles and wash your hands. Broken glass can cut, so clear it up with a dustpan and brush, never with your hands. Cells and bacteria need care too. Bacteria are tiny living cells, and some cause disease. Keep the dish sealed with tape. Wear gloves and a mask. Wash your hands. Sterilise the equipment. Each one lowers the risk of infection. So, four steps. Read the method and find the real hazards. Give a specific action for each. In an Explain question, add what it protects against. For two precautions, choose two different hazards.

Here is a real exam question. A student burns popcorn on a mounted needle, under a test tube of water. Explain one safety precaution the student should take. It is worth two marks. Which hazard comes from this method? The water gets hot, and hot water may spit out of the tube. So the precaution is: wear safety goggles. The reason: hot water may spit into the eyes. That is two marks. Another correct pair: hold the tube with a test tube holder, so your hands are not burnt. Give one pair only, as the question asks. This answer is by olevels.com, not the official mark scheme.

The first mistake is being vague. Be careful, or wear safety equipment, scores nothing. Name the action or the item. The second mistake is giving a fair test point. Using the same volume of water makes results fair. It does not keep anyone safe. The third mistake is in Explain questions. Students give the precaution but no reason. The reason is the second mark. Always add what it protects against.

Your turn. Limewater is a clear liquid that turns cloudy with carbon dioxide. A student breathes out through a straw into a tube of limewater. Other students then use the same straw. Explain two safety precautions for two different hazards. Pause here and work it out.

First, find the hazards. The straw is shared, so microbes can pass from one mouth to the next. And if the student sucks instead of blowing, limewater could go into the mouth. Precaution one: use your own new straw, or sterilise it, so infection cannot spread. Precaution two: breathe out gently and never suck, so no limewater gets into the mouth. Goggles, in case limewater splashes into the eyes, also score. So, every time. Find the real hazard. Name a specific action. Say what it prevents. Use a different hazard for each precaution.
