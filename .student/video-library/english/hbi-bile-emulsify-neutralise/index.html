Explain where bile comes from and how it aids fat digestion

Bile is a green liquid made in the liver. It is stored in the gall bladder. After a meal, it flows down the bile duct into the duodenum, the first part of the small intestine. Bile has two jobs in digestion. It emulsifies fats: it breaks them into small droplets. And it neutralises stomach acid. Bile is not an enzyme. Enzymes are proteins that speed up digestion. Bile does not break any chemical bonds.

Job one. Fat in food forms large drops. Bile breaks them into many small droplets. This is called emulsifying the fat. Why does this help? Many small droplets have a much larger total surface area than one big drop. So lipase, the enzyme that digests fat, can reach more fat at once, and digests it faster. Job two. Food leaves the stomach with acid, at about pH two. Bile is alkaline: the opposite of acid, with a pH above seven. So it neutralises this acid. This gives the right pH for the enzymes in the small intestine. What if there is no bile, for example when the liver is damaged? Fat is digested more slowly, and less fat is absorbed. More fat leaves in the faeces. The small intestine stays acidic, so its enzymes work less well. In a lipase practical, a tube with bile salts changes colour sooner than a tube without. Compare the two times, then explain: the bile salts emulsified the fat.

Now a real exam question. The diagram shows the liver, stomach and small intestine. Structure A is the small sac under the liver: the gall bladder. Explain the function of the fluid stored in A in digestion. Point one: bile emulsifies fat. It breaks large drops into small droplets. Point two: this gives a larger surface area. Point three: so lipase can digest the fat faster. Point four: bile is alkaline, so it neutralises the acid from the stomach. Four ideas for three marks is a safe answer. A common mistake is to say the gall bladder makes bile, or that bile is an enzyme that digests fat. The liver makes bile. Bile helps lipase, but digests nothing itself. Another mistake is to say bile makes each fat droplet bigger in area. Each droplet is smaller. It is the total surface area of all the droplets that is larger.

Your turn. A baby is born with a blocked bile duct. Bile cannot reach the duodenum. Explain why the baby digests fat poorly, and why its faeces contain a lot of fat. Pause here and work it out.

Without bile, the fat is not emulsified. It stays in large drops, with a small surface area. So lipase digests the fat slowly. Also, the stomach acid is not neutralised, so the enzymes work less well. Less fat is digested and absorbed, so more fat passes out in the faeces. So, every time: bile is made in the liver and stored in the gall bladder. It emulsifies fat, giving a larger surface area for lipase. And it neutralises stomach acid.
