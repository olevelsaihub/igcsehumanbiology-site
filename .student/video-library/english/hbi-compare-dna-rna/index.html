Read the narration

DNA and RNA are both nucleic acids: long molecules that carry the genetic code. Both are made of nucleotides. The first difference is easy to see. DNA has two strands, like a ladder. RNA has only one strand. The second difference is the sugar. The sugar in DNA is deoxyribose. The sugar in RNA is ribose. The third difference is one base. Both have adenine, cytosine and guanine. DNA also has thymine, T. RNA has uracil, U, instead.

So learn three contrasts: strands, sugar, and thymine or uracil. Write each one with both sides. DNA has two strands, RNA has one. There are two kinds of RNA to know. Messenger RNA, or mRNA, is made in the nucleus. It carries the genetic code out to a ribosome in the cytoplasm. A ribosome is where proteins are made. Transfer RNA, or tRNA, brings amino acids to the ribosome. Amino acids are the units that join to make a protein. So RNA is in the nucleus and in the cytoplasm. Some questions give base percentages. First look for U or T. Then check the pairs. In two strands, adenine equals thymine, and cytosine equals guanine. Sample X has thymine, and the pairs are equal, so it is DNA. Sample Y has uracil, and the pairs are not equal. So it is RNA, with one strand. A justify question shows a diagram. Point to a feature, and say why it shows DNA. This one has two strands, and only DNA has two strands. It also has thymine, which only DNA has.

Here is a real exam question. Part two asks you to name one other nucleic acid. The answer is RNA. Part three asks for three differences between DNA and RNA, in a table. It is worth three marks. Fill the table one row at a time. Row one, strands: DNA is double-stranded, and RNA is single-stranded. Row two, sugar: DNA has deoxyribose, and RNA has ribose. Row three, bases: DNA has thymine, and RNA has uracil instead. This answer is by olevels.com, not the official mark scheme.

A common mistake is to write that RNA has no sugar. It does: RNA has ribose. Another is to swap the bases. DNA has thymine. RNA has uracil. Another mistake is to say RNA is only in the nucleus. mRNA is made there, but it moves out to the ribosomes in the cytoplasm. Also, if the question asks about structure, do not give a job.

Your turn. A scientist tests a nucleic acid, sample P. It has adenine twenty-four per cent, uracil thirty, cytosine twenty-one, and guanine twenty-five. There is no thymine. First, is it DNA or RNA? Give two pieces of evidence. Second, name one place in a cell where it is found. Pause here and work it out.

Part a. It is RNA. Evidence one: it contains uracil, and only RNA has uracil. Evidence two: the pairs are not equal. Adenine is twenty-four, but uracil is thirty. So it has only one strand. Part b. RNA is found in the nucleus, where mRNA is made. It is also found in the cytoplasm, at the ribosomes. Either place gets the mark. So, every time: compare strands, sugar and bases, with both sides. For data, look for uracil, then check if the pairs are equal.
