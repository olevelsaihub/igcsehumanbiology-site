Your two kidneys clean your blood and make urine. Each kidney is joined to the bladder by a tube called a ureter. The bladder stores the urine. Then urine leaves the body through one tube, the urethra. Blood comes from the large blood vessels in the middle. The renal artery brings blood to each kidney. Renal means of the kidney. The renal vein takes the cleaned blood away.

Each kidney has about a million tiny tubes called nephrons. Here is one. Blood arrives in a small vessel, the afferent arteriole, and forms a knot of capillaries. This knot is the glomerulus. It sits inside a cup called the Bowman's capsule. Follow the tube in order. First, the proximal convoluted tubule. Proximal means near, and convoluted means twisted. Next, the long U shape: the loop of Henle. Then the distal convoluted tubule. Distal means far. Last, many nephrons empty into one straight tube, the collecting duct. It carries urine down to the ureter. Some questions ask for jobs. The glomerulus filters the blood. The proximal tubule takes all the glucose back into the blood. The collecting duct takes water back, controlled by a hormone called ADH.

Now a real exam question. The diagram shows a kidney tubule with four letters. Name structures L, M, N and O. Four marks. Our drawing is simpler than the exam picture, but it has the same parts. On the exam page, M points to the knot of capillaries inside the cup. So M is the glomerulus. L points to the cup wall around the knot. So L is the Bowman's capsule. N points to the straight tube on the right, which has branches joining it. That is the collecting duct. O points to the long loop going down. That is the loop of Henle. Here are the four answers. The most common mistake is to swap ureter and urethra. The ureter goes from a kidney to the bladder. The urethra goes from the bladder to the outside. Another mistake is to call the glomerulus the Bowman's capsule. The glomerulus is the knot of blood vessels. The capsule is the cup around it.

Your turn. Name four structures. First, the tube from a kidney to the bladder. Second, the vessel that brings blood to a kidney. Third, the part of a nephron that takes glucose back. Fourth, the part where ADH controls water. Pause here and work it out.

Part a: the tube from kidney to bladder is the ureter. Part b: blood comes to the kidney in the renal artery. Part c: glucose is taken back in the proximal convoluted tubule. Part d: ADH acts on the collecting duct. So every time, follow the path in order. Kidney, ureter, bladder, urethra. Renal artery in, renal vein out. Then glomerulus, capsule, proximal tubule, loop, distal tubule and collecting duct.
