Read the narration

The heart is a pump made of muscle. We draw it from the front, so its right side is on the left of the page. It has four chambers. A chamber is a space that fills with blood. The two atria are at the top. The two ventricles are below them. Veins carry blood towards the heart. Arteries carry blood away from the heart. The vena cava and the pulmonary vein are veins. The pulmonary artery and the aorta are arteries.

Follow the blood. From the body it enters the vena cava, then the right atrium, then the right ventricle. The right ventricle pumps it up the pulmonary artery to the lungs. In the lungs the blood picks up oxygen. It comes back in the pulmonary vein to the left atrium, then the left ventricle. The left ventricle pumps it into the aorta, and on to the body. So the heart is a double pump. The right side pumps blood to the lungs. The left side pumps blood to the body. In one full trip round the body, blood passes through the heart twice. Some organs have their own vessels. The renal artery takes blood into the kidney, and the renal vein takes it out. The hepatic vein takes blood out of the liver. The hepatic portal vein brings blood from the gut to the liver. For a flow chart question, write the start and the end. Count the dotted answer lines. Then write every vessel in order. Never skip the heart or the lungs.

Here is a real exam question. A red blood cell travels from the liver to the kidney. Name the vessels in order. The first one, the hepatic vein, is given. The chart starts at the liver, and the hepatic vein is given. Then come five dotted answer lines, with the kidney at the bottom. So we need five vessels. The hepatic vein joins vessel one, the vena cava. It goes into the right side of the heart. The right ventricle pumps the blood into vessel two, the pulmonary artery, to the lungs. From the lungs, vessel three is the pulmonary vein, to the left side of the heart. The left ventricle pumps the blood into vessel four, the aorta. Vessel five, the renal artery, branches off the aorta to the kidney. Here is the full answer. This explanation is by olevels.com, not an official mark scheme.

A common mistake is to go straight from the liver to the kidney. Blood cannot take a short cut. It must go through the heart, the lungs, and the heart again. The second mistake is to swap the pulmonary vessels. The artery goes away from the heart, to the lungs, first. The vein comes back from the lungs, second.

Your turn. A red blood cell leaves a kidney and travels to the lungs. Name, in order, the three blood vessels it passes through. Pause here and work it out.

It leaves the kidney in the renal vein. The renal vein joins the vena cava, which takes it to the right side of the heart. The right ventricle pumps it into the pulmonary artery, to the lungs. Every time: start and end, count the answer lines, and follow the fixed route. Body, right side, lungs, left side, body. Arteries go away from the heart. Veins come back.
