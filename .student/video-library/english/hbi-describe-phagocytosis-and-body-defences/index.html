Idea. Pathogens are microorganisms that cause disease, such as bacteria and viruses. The body defends itself in two lines. The first line is barriers that keep pathogens out. If pathogens get past them, white blood cells are the second line. Physical barriers stop pathogens getting in. The skin is a tough outer layer. In the airways, sticky mucus traps pathogens, and tiny hairs called cilia sweep the mucus up to the throat.

Method. Chemical barriers kill pathogens or slow their growth. Acid in the stomach kills pathogens in food. Tears contain enzymes that kill bacteria. Enzymes are proteins that speed up reactions, such as breaking things down. Now the second line. A phagocyte is a white blood cell that engulfs pathogens. It flows around the pathogen. This process is called phagocytosis. The pathogen is now enclosed in a vacuole inside the cell. A vacuole is a small sac surrounded by membrane. Lysosomes are small sacs of digestive enzymes. A lysosome joins with the vacuole. Its enzymes digest the pathogen into small pieces. Finally, the left-over pieces are removed from the cell. Phagocytes attack any pathogen, not one particular type, so we call them non-specific. Lymphocytes are a second kind of white blood cell. They do not engulf. They make antibodies, which stick to pathogens. Some make antitoxins, which neutralise toxins: the poisons that pathogens release. Some people catch infections more easily. Elderly people often have a weaker immune system: the body's defence against pathogens. And crowded indoor spaces with poor air flow let pathogens spread from person to person.

Worked example. Now a real exam question. The diagram shows a white blood cell engulfing a bacterium, and three stages that follow. A lysosome is labelled: it contains digestive enzymes. Describe what is happening at stages 1, 2 and 3. Three marks. Stage 1 is the arrow from the lysosome to the vacuole holding the bacterium. So at stage 1, the lysosome joins with the vacuole. Stage 2 shows the bacterium in small pieces. The enzymes from the lysosome are digesting it. Stage 3 shows a sac of small pieces leaving the cell. The remains of the bacterium are removed from the white blood cell. Here is the full answer, one sentence per stage. This answer is by olevels.com, not an official mark scheme.

Common mistake. A common mistake is to say the phagocyte eats the bacterium. That earns nothing. Say it engulfs the bacterium, and enzymes from lysosomes digest it. Another mistake is to mix up the jobs. Antibodies do not digest, and lymphocytes do not engulf. Phagocytes engulf, enzymes digest, and lymphocytes make antibodies.

Your turn. A girl cuts her finger, and bacteria get into the cut. Name the barrier that has been broken. Then describe how a phagocyte destroys the bacteria. Pause here and work it out.

Answer. Part a: the skin. The cut breaks it, so the bacteria can get in. Part b: the phagocyte engulfs the bacteria into a vacuole. Lysosomes join the vacuole. Their enzymes digest the bacteria. Then the remains are removed from the cell.
