A partially permeable membrane is a thin layer with tiny holes. Small molecules, like water, glucose, urea and salts, can pass through. Large molecules, like protein and starch, cannot. Molecules move by diffusion. Diffusion is the movement of molecules from a high concentration to a low concentration. We say they move down a concentration gradient.

In dialysis, blood flows on one side of the membrane, and dialysis fluid on the other. The blood has lots of urea. The fresh fluid has no urea. So urea diffuses out of the blood into the fluid. The fluid has glucose and salts at the same concentration as normal blood. There is no gradient, so there is no overall movement. Glucose stays in the blood, for respiration. Water does not move by osmosis either. Osmosis is the movement of water across a membrane like this. The blood and the fluid flow in opposite directions. Fresh fluid keeps coming in, and used fluid goes out. So the gradient for urea is kept along the whole membrane. In the lab, Visking tubing is a model of a partially permeable membrane. Fill it with starch and glucose, and put it in water. Later, test the water. Glucose passes out, but starch stays inside.

Now a real exam question. The diagram shows a dialysis machine. Blood from the arm passes the dialysis membrane, and fresh fluid flows past it. The machine filters waste from the blood. The question asks: explain how diffusion removes waste substances from the blood inside the machine. Four marks. First, compare the two sides. The blood has a high concentration of urea. The fresh dialysis fluid has no urea, so it has a lower concentration. The membrane is partially permeable. Small urea molecules can pass through it. So urea diffuses from the blood, across the membrane, into the fluid, down the concentration gradient. Here is the full answer. Explanation by olevels.com, not an official mark scheme.

A common mistake is to say urea moves by osmosis. Osmosis is only for water. Urea moves by diffusion. Another mistake is to say glucose is in the fluid to feed the blood. It is there so glucose does not diffuse out of the blood.

Your turn. In a dialysis machine, the fluid is not replaced. After a while, it has almost as much urea as the blood. Explain why very little urea is now removed. Pause here and work it out.

Compare the sides. The blood has a high urea concentration. The old fluid now has almost as much. So the concentration gradient is very small. Very little urea diffuses across the membrane. Most of the urea stays in the blood. So every time: which molecules are small enough to pass? Compare the concentration on each side. Molecules diffuse from high to low. With no gradient, there is no overall movement.
