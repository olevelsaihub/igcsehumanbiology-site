Read the narration

Gas exchange happens in the alveoli, the tiny air sacs at the ends of the airways. Each alveolus has a capillary wrapped around it. Air is inside the alveolus. Blood flows past, in the capillary. Gases move by diffusion. Diffusion is the spreading of particles from where there are many to where there are few. Oxygen diffuses from the air into the blood. Carbon dioxide diffuses from the blood into the air.

Feature one: there are millions of alveoli. Together they give a very large surface area, which means a lot of outside area, so a lot of gas can diffuse at once. Feature two: the alveolus wall and the capillary wall are each one cell thick, so the diffusion distance is short. Feature three: the inside is moist, so gases dissolve before they diffuse. Feature four: a dense network of capillaries takes oxygen away quickly. Breathing keeps bringing fresh air. Together they keep a steep concentration gradient. That means a big difference: much more oxygen in the air than in the blood. So air in the alveoli is different from outside air. It has less oxygen, more carbon dioxide, and more water vapour. It is also warmer. In emphysema, the walls between alveoli break down. Many small alveoli join into a few big spaces. So there is less surface area, and less oxygen gets into the blood. Big spaces have a smaller surface area for their volume.

Here is a real exam question. The diagram shows part of a lung from a healthy person, and part of a lung from a person with emphysema. In the emphysema lung, the walls between the air sacs have broken down, leaving large dark holes. The question asks: explain the effect that emphysema has on the health of a person. Four marks, so plan five or six links. Link one: the alveolus walls are damaged and break down. Link two: so there is less surface area. Link three: so less oxygen diffuses into the blood, and less carbon dioxide is removed. Link four: the cells get less oxygen, so there is less aerobic respiration. Link five: less energy is released. Link six: the person is tired, short of breath, and cannot exercise much. Here is the full answer. This explanation is by olevels.com, not an official mark scheme.

A common mistake is to write 'thin cell walls'. Animal cells have no cell wall. Write 'thin walls', or 'walls one cell thick'. Another mistake is to say emphysema gives a bigger surface area, because the spaces are bigger. It is the opposite. Fewer, bigger spaces have less surface area.

Your turn. A lung disease makes the walls of the alveoli thicker. Explain why a person with this disease gets tired quickly. Pause here and work it out.

Thicker walls mean a longer diffusion distance. So oxygen diffuses into the blood more slowly, and less oxygen reaches the body cells. There is less aerobic respiration, and less energy is released. So the person gets tired quickly. Every time: for features, give large surface area, thin walls, a moist lining and a good blood supply. For damage, say which feature is lost, then follow the chain to energy and tiredness.
