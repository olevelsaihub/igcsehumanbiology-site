Idea. Cigarette smoke contains many harmful chemicals. Three matter most. Tar causes lung cancer. Carbon monoxide means less oxygen is carried. Nicotine is addictive, which means the body comes to need it. It also narrows blood vessels.

Method. Step one: match the chemical to the damage. Tar is carcinogenic, which means it causes cancer. Tar also damages the cilia. Cilia are tiny hairs on the airway lining. They sweep mucus, a sticky liquid that traps dirt and bacteria, up out of the lungs. Step two: carbon monoxide. It joins with haemoglobin in red blood cells to make carboxyhaemoglobin. That haemoglobin can no longer carry oxygen. So the blood carries less oxygen. Step three: follow the chain in pregnancy. Less oxygen in the mother's blood means less oxygen crosses the placenta to the fetus. The placenta is the organ that passes oxygen from mother to baby. The fetus is the baby growing in the uterus. Fetal cells respire less, release less energy, and grow more slowly. The baby has a lower birth mass. Nicotine narrows blood vessels. This raises blood pressure and the risk of heart disease. Smoking also causes lung cancer, bronchitis, which means inflamed bronchi, and emphysema, where the alveolus walls break down. Some data show people who never smoked getting lung cancer. Give other causes: passive smoking, which means breathing in other people's smoke, air pollution, or their genes.

Worked example. Here is a real exam question. It starts with a passage. Near the end, it says that carbon monoxide can enter the blood and combine with haemoglobin. It is found in cigarette smoke. The question: explain why pregnant women are advised not to smoke cigarettes. Four marks, so four links in the chain. Link one: carbon monoxide combines with haemoglobin to form carboxyhaemoglobin, so the mother's blood carries less oxygen. Link two: so less oxygen reaches the fetus through the placenta. Link three: the fetal cells carry out less respiration, so less energy is released. Link four: so the fetus grows more slowly, and the baby has a low birth mass. Here is the full answer. Common mistake. A common mistake is to say nicotine causes cancer. It is tar that causes cancer. Nicotine is the addictive chemical. Another mistake is to say the fetus cannot breathe. A fetus does not breathe air. It gets its oxygen from the mother's blood, through the placenta.

Your turn. Long-term smokers often cough a lot, and they get more chest infections. Explain why. Pause here and work it out.

Answer. Tar damages the cilia. So the cilia cannot sweep mucus up out of the lungs. The mucus builds up, so the smoker coughs to clear it. Bacteria trapped in the mucus stay in the lungs, so infections are more likely. Every time: name the right chemical. Say what it does. Then follow the chain all the way to the effect on health.
