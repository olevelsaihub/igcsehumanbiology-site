Explain validity problems and fixes in investigations on people

An investigation tests one factor. A factor is anything that can change, like the salt in a meal. You change that one factor and measure a result. A result is valid when it shows the effect of that one factor only. If anything else changes too, the result is not valid. Investigations on people are hard, because people are all different. They differ in age, sex, body mass, fitness, health and genes. So two people can respond in different ways to the very same test.

Here is the method. Say a student tests if exercise changes heart rate. First, find the one factor being tested, exercise, and the result being measured, heart rate. Next, list everything else that differs between the people or the tests. Maybe they used different people. Maybe some ate food or drank something first. Maybe there was no rest between tests. Then link each one to the result. A fitter person has a lower heart rate. After a big meal, heart rate rises. With no rest, the heart rate is still high from the last test. The last step is the fix. Use the same person for every test. Give them the same food and drink. Let them rest until the heart rate is normal. Never just write make it fair. Why rest? Look at the graph. After exercise, the heart rate stays high for a few minutes. Rest lets it fall back to normal. So each test starts from the same point.

Now a real exam question. It tests if salt in the diet changes urine. Ten people each eat a different food, with a different amount of salt. Then the volume of urine is measured. Explain why the results are unlikely to be valid. It is worth two marks. Step one: the factor is salt, and the result is the volume of urine. There are ten people, and each one eats a different food. Problem one: each person eats a different food. Foods differ in more than salt. One food may hold more water, and more water in the body gives more urine. Problem two: there are ten different people. They may differ in age or sex, or one may have a kidney problem. So their kidneys may reabsorb different amounts of water. Reabsorb means take back into the blood. Here is the full answer: two problems, each with its link. Explanation by olevels.com, not an official mark scheme.

A very common mistake is to write, it is not a fair test. That names nothing. You must name the factor that was not kept the same, and say how it changes the result. Another mistake is to write, they did not repeat it. Repeats make results more reliable. That is a different idea. For validity, name something that was not controlled.

Your turn. Sara wants to know if caffeine changes reaction time. She tests her grandfather in the evening, with no caffeine. Then she tests her brother in the morning, after two cups of coffee. Explain why her results are not valid, and state one change. Pause here and work it out.

The factor is caffeine, and the result is reaction time. But Sara used two different people. Her grandfather is much older, and older people often react more slowly. She also tested at different times of day. People are often tired in the evening. One fix: test the same person, at the same time of day, once without caffeine and once with it. Also make sure they have no other caffeine first, like tea or cola. So every time: name the factor and the result. Find what else differs. Link each one to the result. Then give a precise fix.
