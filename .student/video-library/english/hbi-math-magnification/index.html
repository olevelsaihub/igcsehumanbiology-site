Read the narration

The image is the picture you see, in a drawing or a photo. The actual size is the real size of the object. Magnification tells you how many times bigger the image is. Magnification equals image size divided by actual size. Sometimes you must measure the image yourself. Use a ruler, in millimetres. Measure between the exact points the question names. Here the image is 80 millimetres long.

A triangle helps you rearrange it. Image is on top. Magnification and actual size are below. Cover the one you want. Actual size is image divided by magnification. Image size is actual size times magnification. Before you divide, both sizes must be in the same unit. One centimetre is ten millimetres. One millimetre is one thousand micrometres. A micrometre is a tiny unit for cells. To go back from micrometres to millimetres, divide by one thousand. Some questions give the units in metres. One millimetre is ten to the minus three metres. One micrometre is ten to the minus six metres. So one millimetre is still one thousand micrometres. A light microscope has two lenses. To find the total magnification, multiply them. An eyepiece of ten times, with an objective of forty times, gives four hundred times. Then follow four steps. Write the equation. Put in the numbers. Calculate. Give the unit asked for. Magnification has no unit: write a times sign in front.

Here is a real exam question. The diagram shows a sperm cell. Its actual length is 0.0060 millimetres. The image of the sperm cell is 84 millimetres long. Calculate the magnification. It is worth two marks. First, write the values. Image size, 84 millimetres. Actual size, 0.0060 millimetres. Both are in millimetres, so no change is needed. Magnification equals image divided by actual. So 84 divided by 0.0060. That is 14,000. Write it as times 14,000. Check: the image is much bigger than the cell, so a big number makes sense. This answer is by olevels.com, not the official mark scheme.

A common mistake is to divide the wrong way: actual divided by image. That gives a tiny number, smaller than one. Always put the image size on top. Other mistakes: dividing with one size in millimetres and the other in micrometres. Or adding the lens powers instead of multiplying. Or giving millimetres when the question asks for micrometres.

Your turn. A photo shows a red blood cell at a magnification of times 3,000. The image of the cell is 24 millimetres wide. First, calculate its actual width in micrometres. Second, an eyepiece is times 5 and the objective is times 10. What is the total magnification? Pause here and work it out.

Part a. Cover A on the triangle. Actual equals image divided by magnification. 24 divided by 3,000 is 0.008 millimetres. The question asks for micrometres. One millimetre is 1,000 micrometres, so multiply by 1,000. The answer is 8 micrometres. Part b. Multiply the lenses: 5 times 10 is times 50. So, every time: same units first. Choose the form of the equation. Show the numbers and the answer. Give the unit asked for, or a times sign.
