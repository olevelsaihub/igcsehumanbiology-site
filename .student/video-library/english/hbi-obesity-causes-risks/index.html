Think of energy balance. Energy in comes from food. Energy out is the energy the body uses, for example when you move. Energy is measured in kilojoules. When more energy is taken in than is used, for a long time, the extra energy is stored as fat. Obesity means having so much body fat that it harms health.

Step one: give both causes. On the energy in side: eating too much, especially fat and sugar. On the energy out side: too little exercise. Each side is a separate point. Step two: name the risks. Obesity raises the risk of type 2 diabetes, where blood glucose stays too high. It also raises the risk of heart disease, stroke, high blood pressure, and arthritis. A stroke is when a blood vessel in the brain is blocked or bursts. Arthritis means painful, damaged joints. Why heart disease? Cholesterol is a fatty substance in the blood. Too much of it builds up as fatty deposits in artery walls. The artery narrows. This raises the risk of a heart attack and a stroke. A food label question asks which food is best for losing mass. Choose the food with no energy from fat. The amount of vitamins does not change body mass. So: give the causes on both sides. Say the extra is stored as fat. Name each disease, and skip any the question excludes. For advice, give specific changes: eat less fat, less sugar, and drink less alcohol.

Now a real exam question. It starts with a passage about obesity and diabetes. Part a one: describe the causes of obesity. Three marks, so three ideas. Point one, the energy in side: eating too much food, especially food high in fat and sugar. Point two, the energy out side: doing too little exercise. Point three: more energy is taken in than is used, so the extra is stored as fat. Here is the full answer. This answer is by olevels.com, not an official mark scheme.

A common mistake is vague advice, like eat a healthy diet. Say eat less fat and less sugar. And obesity is not caused only by genes. Explain the energy balance. If the question says other than diabetes, do not give diabetes. And do not give the same risk twice in different words. Heart problems and heart disease are one idea.

Your turn. Tom drives to work and sits at a desk all day. He drinks three cans of sugary cola every day. Tom is obese. Part a: suggest two reasons why Tom became obese. Part b: give two health risks for Tom, other than heart disease. Part c: give two specific changes Tom should make to his diet. Pause here and work it out.

Here is the answer. Part a: the cola is full of sugar, so his energy in is too high. He sits all day, so he uses little energy. Part b: for example, type 2 diabetes and stroke. Part c: drink water instead of cola, to eat less sugar, and eat less fat. So, every time: give both sides of the balance. Say the extra is stored as fat. Name each disease. Give specific changes.
