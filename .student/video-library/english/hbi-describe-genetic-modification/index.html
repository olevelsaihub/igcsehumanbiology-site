Genetic modification means moving a useful gene from one organism into another. A gene is a short length of DNA that codes for one protein. The new organism then makes that protein. For example, bacteria can make human insulin, the hormone that lowers blood glucose. You need three tools. Restriction enzymes cut DNA at particular places. DNA ligase is an enzyme that joins pieces of DNA. And a vector carries the gene into the new cell. For bacteria, the vector is a plasmid, a small ring of DNA.

Steps one and two. Find the insulin gene in human DNA. Cut it out using a restriction enzyme. Step three. Take a plasmid out of a bacterium. Cut it open with the same restriction enzyme, so the ends match. Steps four and five. Put the gene into the plasmid. DNA ligase joins them. The plasmid now holds recombinant DNA, which means DNA from two different organisms joined together. Step six. Put the plasmid back into a bacterium. The bacteria reproduce in a fermenter, a large tank with the best conditions for growth. They all make human insulin, which is then purified, or separated out. The same method works in other organisms. Yeast, a fungus, can be given the insulin gene too. Golden Rice has genes added so that the rice makes beta-carotene. The body turns beta-carotene into vitamin A. Gene therapy treats a disease in a person. In cystic fibrosis, a faulty allele means a protein called CFTR does not work. An allele is a version of a gene. A harmless virus carries a normal allele into the cells lining the airways. These cells then make working CFTR protein. Only body cells change, not sex cells.

Now a real exam question. Describe how bacteria can be genetically modified to produce human insulin. Six marks, so plan at least six steps. Step one: find the human insulin gene. Step two: cut it out with a restriction enzyme. Step three: take a plasmid from a bacterium, and cut it open with the same enzyme. Step four: put the gene into the plasmid. Step five: ligase joins them, making recombinant DNA. Step six: put the plasmid back into a bacterium. It reproduces, and the bacteria make human insulin. Here is the full answer. A common mistake: the insulin itself is not put into the bacteria. The gene is. And do not swap the enzymes. Restriction enzymes cut. Ligase joins. For gene therapy, the vector is a virus, not a plasmid. Use the word allele. And only body cells change, so children can still inherit the faulty allele.

Your turn. Hepatitis B is a disease caused by a virus. A vaccine can be made from one of the virus's proteins. Scientists want a plant to make this protein. Describe how the gene for the protein could be put into the plant's cells. Four marks. Pause here and work it out.

Here is the answer. Find the gene in the virus DNA. Cut it out with a restriction enzyme. Cut a plasmid with the same enzyme, and put in the gene. Ligase joins them. Then put the plasmid into the plant cells. They make the protein. So, every time: cut out the gene with a restriction enzyme. Cut open a plasmid with the same enzyme. Insert, and join with ligase. Put it into the host cell. For gene therapy, a virus carries a normal allele.
