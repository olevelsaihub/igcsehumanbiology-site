Read the narration

Standard form writes a number as A times ten to the power n. A must be at least 1 and less than 10. The power n tells you how many places the decimal point moves. For example, 45 000 is 4.5 times ten to the power 4. For a number smaller than 1, the power is negative. 0.0083: move the point right, to just after the 8. That is three places, so it is 8.3 times ten to the power minus 3.

Step one: read exactly which form or unit the question asks for. Step two, for standard form: the decimal point goes just after the first digit that is not zero. In 45 000, that is after the 4. Step three: count how many places the point moves. From the end of 45 000 back to after the 4 is four places, so the power is 4. Moving left gives a positive power. Moving right gives a negative power. Step four: significant figures, or s.f., are counted from the first digit that is not zero. For 3 319 266 to three s.f., keep 3, 3, 1. The next digit is 9, so round the 1 up to 2. Then fill with zeros: 3 320 000. Step five, for units: multiply or divide by the conversion factor. One millimetre is 1000 micrometres, and a micrometre is one thousandth of a millimetre. One minute is 60 seconds. Going from a big unit to a small unit, multiply.

Now a real exam question. In the rest of the United Kingdom, 600 239 people have diabetes. Give this number in standard form. Two marks. The first non-zero digit is 6, so the point goes after the 6. That gives 6.00239, which is between 1 and 10. Now count. From the end of 600 239 back to after the 6 is five places, to the left. So the power is plus 5. Here is the full answer. This answer is by olevels.com, not an official mark scheme. Check it: 6 times 100 000 is about 600 000, so the size is right.

A common mistake is to write 60.0239 times ten to the power 4. The first part, 60.0239, is bigger than 10, so it is not standard form. Another is to count wrong and get a power of 6. Also, rounding 3 319 266 to three significant figures is not 332: you must fill with zeros. And to change millimetres to micrometres, multiply by 1000; do not divide.

Your turn. These are made-up example values. A sample has 52 400 bacteria. Write 52 400 in standard form. Then give 52 400 to two significant figures. A cell is 0.025 millimetres long: convert this to micrometres. Pause here and work it out.

Part a: the point goes after the 5, giving 5.24. It moves four places to the left, so the answer is 5.24 times ten to the power 4. Part b: keep 5 and 2. The next digit is 4, which is less than 5, so do not round up: 52 000. Part c: millimetres to micrometres, multiply by 1000. 0.025 times 1000 is 25 micrometres. So, every time: read the form asked for, place the point after the first non-zero digit, count the places for the power, fill with zeros when rounding, and use the right factor for units.
