Read the narration

Per cent means out of 100. To find a part as a percentage of a total, divide the part by the whole, then multiply by 100. Here, 5 circles out of 20 are coloured. 5 divided by 20, times 100, is 25 per cent. Percentages make a fair comparison when groups are different sizes. Class A has 6 left-handed pupils out of 20. Class B has 9 out of 40. B has more, but A has the bigger share: 30 per cent against 22.5 per cent. In a compare question, give both the numbers and the percentages.

Step one. Find the part and the whole. This example table shows babies' birth mass. Its numbers are made up, not real data. The question asks: what percentage of babies born to non-smoking mothers had a low birth mass? The part is 20. Step two. The whole is the total for that group only: all babies born to non-smoking mothers. That is 210, not the grand total of 310. If no total is given, add up that row: 20 plus 190 is 210. Step three. Write part divided by whole, times 100. 20 divided by 210 is 0.0952. Times 100 is 9.52, so about 9.5 per cent. Step four. Check. A part can never be more than 100 per cent of its own whole. Then round sensibly, often to one decimal place.

Here is a real exam question. In one year in the United Kingdom there were 4733 organ donations. 3233 of them were kidney donations. Calculate the percentage that were kidney donations. The whole is all organ donations: 4733. The part is the kidney donations: 3233. So the kidney part of the bar is most of it. Percentage equals 3233 divided by 4733, times 100. 3233 divided by 4733 is 0.683. Times 100 is 68.3 per cent. Check: 68.3 per cent is less than 100, and kidneys are most of the donations, about two thirds. So it makes sense. Explanation by olevels.com, not an official mark scheme.

A common mistake is dividing the whole by the part: 4733 divided by 3233. That gives 146 per cent, which is impossible. The part always goes on top. Another mistake is forgetting to multiply by 100, and writing 0.68 as the answer. That is a fraction, not a percentage.

Your turn. In one month, 2400 people gave blood at a hospital. 864 of them had blood group O. Calculate the percentage of donors with blood group O. Pause here and work it out.

The whole is all the donors: 2400. The part is the group O donors: 864. 864 divided by 2400 is 0.36. Times 100 is 36 per cent. Check: it is less than 100, and about a third of the bar. So 36 per cent. So, every time: find the part. Find the whole for that group. Divide the part by the whole, times 100. Then check it is below 100.
