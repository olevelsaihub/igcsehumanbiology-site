There are three main types of blood vessel. Arteries have thick walls and a narrow lumen. The lumen is the space inside, where the blood flows. Veins have thin walls and a wide lumen. Capillaries are tiny. Each structure matches a job. Arteries carry blood away from the heart at high pressure. Veins bring blood back at low pressure. Capillaries exchange substances with the body cells.

An artery wall is thick, with muscle and elastic tissue, so it can withstand high pressure. Elastic tissue can stretch and spring back. The wall stretches and springs back with each beat: this is your pulse. The narrow lumen keeps the pressure high. Small arteries are called arterioles. Their muscle can contract to narrow the lumen, and this raises blood pressure. A vein carries blood at low pressure, so its wall is thin, and its lumen is wide. Veins have valves. The flaps point the way the blood flows. If blood starts to flow back, the flaps shut. A capillary wall is one cell thick, with small pores, or holes, so the distance for diffusion is short. Diffusion means particles spreading from where there are many to where there are few. The lumen is so narrow that red cells pass one at a time. Oxygen and glucose go to the cells. Carbon dioxide comes back. To name a vessel in a picture, use a feature you can see: a valve means a vein. To explain, write the feature, then 'so', then the job. For example: the artery wall is thicker, so it can withstand high pressure. Use comparison words like thicker, wider and narrower.

Here is a real exam question. The diagram shows the circulatory system. Vessel X leaves the heart for the body: it is the aorta. Vessel Y brings blood back to the heart: it is the vena cava. Part four asks you to explain the differences in the structures of X and Y, for three marks. The page also shows a long section through X, with thick dark walls and a narrow lumen. Difference one: X has a thicker wall, with more muscle and elastic tissue, so it can withstand the high pressure from the left ventricle. Difference two: X has a narrower lumen, which keeps the pressure high. Difference three: Y has a wider lumen, because its blood is at low pressure. Difference four: Y has valves, which stop blood flowing backwards. Here is the full answer. This explanation is by olevels.com, not an official mark scheme.

A common mistake is to give a feature with no reason. 'Arteries have thick walls' is only half the point. Add why: to withstand high pressure. Another mistake is to say valves pump blood. Valves do not pump. They stop blood flowing backwards. The heart does the pumping.

Your turn. When an artery is cut, blood spurts out in time with the heartbeat. When a vein is cut, blood flows out slowly and steadily. Use the structure and job of each vessel to explain the difference. Pause here and work it out.

An artery carries blood straight from the heart, at high pressure. The pressure rises with each beat, and the elastic wall stretches and springs back. So the blood spurts out in time with the pulse. A vein carries blood back to the heart at low pressure, with no pulse. So the blood flows out slowly and steadily. Every time: look at the wall, the lumen and the valves.
