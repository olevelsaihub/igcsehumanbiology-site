Read the narration

Water comes into your body when you drink and eat. Water leaves in sweat, in your breath, and in urine. Osmoregulation means keeping the water in the blood steady. Part of the brain, the hypothalamus, detects the water in the blood. The pituitary gland, just below it, releases a hormone called ADH into the blood. A hormone is a chemical carried in the blood. ADH travels to the kidneys.

ADH acts on the collecting ducts. It makes their walls more permeable, which means they let more water through. So more water goes back into the blood. So when the blood has too little water, more ADH is released. More water is taken back, and a small volume of concentrated urine is made. Concentrated urine has little water and lots of urea, so it is dark. So if one urine sample is darker than another, that person had less water in the blood, and more ADH. When the blood has too much water, for example after drinking a lot, less ADH is released. Less water is taken back, so a large volume of dilute urine is made. Alcohol lowers the amount of ADH that is released. So less water is taken back, and more dilute urine is made.

Now a real exam question. The diagram shows a kidney tubule. Explain why a person makes less urine on a hot day than on a cold day. Five marks. On a hot day, the person sweats more, so they lose water. The blood has less water, so its water potential is lower. Water potential is a measure of how much water is in a liquid. The hypothalamus detects this. The pituitary gland releases more ADH into the blood. It travels to the kidney. ADH makes the collecting ducts more permeable. More water is taken back into the blood. So less urine is made, and it is more concentrated. Here is the full answer. Explanation by olevels.com, not an official mark scheme.

A common mistake is to say the hypothalamus releases ADH. The hypothalamus detects. The pituitary gland releases ADH. Another mistake is to get the direction backwards. More ADH means more water is taken back, so less urine, not more.

Your turn. Omar eats a very salty meal. The salt lowers the water potential of his blood. Explain how this changes his urine. Pause here and work it out.

Salt in the blood is like having too little water. His hypothalamus detects the lower water potential. His pituitary gland releases more ADH, which travels to the kidneys. The collecting ducts become more permeable. More water goes back into the blood. So he makes a small volume of concentrated urine. So every time: too little or too much water? The hypothalamus detects. The pituitary releases more or less ADH. The collecting ducts change, and so does the urine.
