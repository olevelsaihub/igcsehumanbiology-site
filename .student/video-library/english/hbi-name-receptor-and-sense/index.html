Idea. A stimulus is a change in the surroundings, like heat, light or sound. A receptor is a cell, or a group of cells, that detects a stimulus. It then starts a nerve impulse, an electrical signal that travels along nerve cells to the brain or spinal cord. We have five senses: sight, hearing, touch, taste and smell. Each sense organ has receptors for one kind of stimulus. Taste and smell receptors detect chemicals. If a question asks for other senses, do not repeat the one it already gave.

Method. Step one: identify the stimulus. Step two: name the receptor in one or two words. In the skin there are three kinds. A temperature receptor detects hot and cold. A pressure receptor detects touch and pressing. A pain receptor detects damage. In the eye, the light receptors are in the retina, the layer at the back. There are two kinds. Rods work in dim light. Cones detect colour. Why are there three types of cone? Light has a wavelength, measured in nanometres, and different wavelengths are different colours. This made-up example graph shows that each cone absorbs a different range: blue, green or red. Together they cover a wide range, and the brain compares them to tell colours apart. Receptors get used to a steady stimulus. Put one hand in hot water and one in ice cold water. Then put both in water at room temperature. The hand from the hot water feels cold. The hand from the cold water feels hot.

Worked example. Now a real exam question. This method puts one hand in hot water and one in ice cold water, then both in water at room temperature. The question asks you to name the structure in the skin that detects temperature. One mark. The water at room temperature felt hot to one hand and ice cold to the other. The stimulus is temperature. Now ask: what detects it? The structure that detects a stimulus is a receptor. So the answer is: temperature receptors in the skin. Here is the answer. Common mistake. A common mistake is to name the whole organ, such as skin or eye. The question wants the structure that does the detecting: the receptor. Another mistake is to call a receptor a sensory neurone. The receptor detects the stimulus. The sensory neurone only carries the impulse onwards. Last, for three cones, do not just say more is better. Say each type absorbs a different range of wavelengths, so together they detect more colours and tell them apart.

Your turn. A cook touches a hot pan by mistake. Part a: name the stimulus, and the receptors in her skin that detect it. Part b: some people have only two working types of cone, blue and green. Suggest why they find it hard to tell red and green apart. Pause here and work it out.

Answer. Part a. The stimulus is heat, a high temperature. It is detected by temperature receptors in the skin. Pain receptors are also stimulated. Part b. With only two types of cone, the range of wavelengths detected is smaller. Without the red cone, red and green light both stimulate mainly the green cones. So the brain cannot tell them apart. So, every time: identify the stimulus. Name the receptor in one or two words. Do not give the whole organ, and do not repeat a sense the question already gave.
