Idea. A joint is where two bones meet. Each part of a joint has a job. Here is a synovial joint, like the knee: a joint that moves freely, with fluid inside. Two bones meet, and several parts help them move. In the exam you are given a part and asked for its job, which we call its function. Give a short, exact phrase. If there are two marks for one part, give two different ideas.

Method. Ligaments join bone to bone. They hold the joint together. Cartilage covers the ends of the bones. It reduces friction, the rubbing between two surfaces, and absorbs shock. The synovial membrane makes synovial fluid. The fluid lubricates the joint, like oil, so it moves easily. Tendons join muscle to bone. A tendon does not stretch. So when the muscle contracts, which means gets shorter, its whole pull passes to the bone, and the bone moves. Bone tissue has jobs too. Compact bone gives strength. Spongy bone holds red marrow, where red blood cells are made. The skull, backbone and ribs support the body and protect the brain, the spinal cord, the heart and the lungs. Damage questions: say what the part normally does, then what goes wrong. If the cartilage wears away, the bone ends rub together. There is more friction, so the joint is painful and stiff. Scurvy, a disease caused by too little vitamin C, damages collagen, a protein in cartilage. The result is the same: painful, stiff joints.

Worked example. Now a real exam question. The diagram shows a knee joint, with a muscle, a tendon and cartilage labelled. The question asks for the functions of tendons and cartilage. Four marks, so two ideas for each. Tendons first. Idea one: a tendon joins a muscle to a bone. Idea two: it does not stretch, so it passes the pull of the muscle to the bone. Now cartilage. Idea three: it reduces friction between the bone ends and absorbs shock. Idea four: so the joint moves smoothly. Here is the full answer: two ideas for tendons, and two for cartilage. Common mistake. A common mistake is mixing up ligaments and tendons. Ligaments join bone to bone. Tendons join muscle to bone. And tendons are not elastic. They do not stretch. Another mistake is to write that cartilage protects the bone, with no more detail. Say it reduces friction or absorbs shock. And synovial fluid lubricates. It does not hold the bones together.

Your turn. A woman has a joint disease. The synovial membrane in her knee makes much less synovial fluid than normal. Explain why her knee becomes painful and stiff. Then give the function of the ligaments in her knee. Pause here and work it out.

Answer. Synovial fluid normally lubricates the joint. With too little fluid, there is more friction between the ends of the bones. So the knee is painful and stiff, and it does not move smoothly. Her ligaments hold the bones of the knee together: they join bone to bone. So, every time: find the part, give its job in a short phrase, and give two ideas for two marks. For damage, give the lost job, then the symptom.
