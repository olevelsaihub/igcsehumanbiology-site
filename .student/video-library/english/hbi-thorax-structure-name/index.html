Read the narration

The thorax is the chest. Air comes in through the nose or mouth, then down a wide tube in the neck, the trachea. The trachea splits into two tubes, one to each lung. Around the lungs is a cage of ribs. Between the ribs are the intercostal muscles. Under the lungs is a sheet of muscle, the diaphragm. It separates the thorax from the abdomen.

Name the airways by how they branch. The trachea is one wide tube, with rings of cartilage, a firm, bendy tissue, that keep it open. It splits into two bronchi. One of them is called a bronchus. Inside each lung, the bronchi branch many times into small tubes, the bronchioles. At their ends are tiny air sacs, the alveoli. Gas exchange happens in the alveoli: oxygen goes into the blood, and carbon dioxide comes out. Now the moving parts. The curved bones are the ribs. The tissue between them is intercostal muscle. The dome under the lungs is the diaphragm. At the front, the ribs join the sternum, the breastbone. So use the branching to name a tube. One tube: trachea. Two tubes: bronchi. Many small tubes: bronchioles. Round sacs at the ends: alveoli. Disease names also follow the parts. Bronchitis means the bronchi are inflamed: red, swollen and sore. Emphysema damages the walls of the alveoli. Some questions use a model of the rib cage. The front bar is the sternum, and the back bar is the backbone. The bars that join them are ribs. Strips or bands that cross between the ribs act as intercostal muscles. Things not shown include the lungs, the diaphragm and the trachea.

Here is a real exam question. The diagram shows the human lungs, with the diaphragm labelled. Add three labels: trachea, bronchus and alveoli. Three marks. The wide ringed tube at the top is the trachea. Draw the line so it touches the tube, above the split. One of the two short tubes going into a lung is a bronchus. The alveoli are the small round sacs at the very ends of the branches. Touch a sac, not a branch. A branch is a bronchiole. This answer is by olevels.com, not an official mark scheme.

A common mistake is to point to the branching tubes and call them alveoli, or call them bronchi. Small branching tubes are bronchioles. Only the sacs are alveoli. Another mistake is to put the oesophagus in the air route. The oesophagus carries food to the stomach. Air goes down the trachea.

Your turn. A bell-jar model of the chest has a glass jar, a Y-shaped tube, two balloons on the ends of the tube, and a rubber sheet across the open bottom of the jar. Name the part of the body that each one stands for. Pause here and work it out.

The glass jar is the wall of the thorax, the rib cage. The stem of the Y-shaped tube is the trachea, and its two branches are the bronchi. The balloons are the lungs. The rubber sheet is the diaphragm. Every time: follow each label line to its end. Use the branching to name a tube. Use position for the ribs, the intercostal muscles and the diaphragm.
