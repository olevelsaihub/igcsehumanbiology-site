Read the narration

Teeth break food into small pieces, so it is easier to swallow and to digest, or break down. There are four types of teeth. Incisors are at the front. Next come the canines. At the back are the premolars and molars. Each shape does a job. Incisors are flat like a chisel, so they cut and bite. Canines are pointed, so they tear and grip. Premolars and molars are broad with bumps called cusps, so they grind and crush.

Now we cut a molar in half, from top to bottom. The crown is the part above the gum. The roots sit inside the jaw bone. Work from the outside in. The outer layer of the crown is enamel. Enamel is the hardest substance in the body. It protects the tooth when we bite. Under the enamel is dentine. Dentine is softer than enamel, and it makes up most of the tooth, down into the roots. In the centre is the pulp cavity. It holds blood vessels, which bring food and oxygen to the tooth, and a nerve. The nerve is a sensory neurone: a nerve cell that carries messages to the brain. It detects pain, heat and cold. Young children have twenty milk teeth, with no premolars. Adults have up to thirty-two permanent teeth, and they are larger. The last molars, called wisdom teeth, often come late, in the late teens or after.

Now a real exam question. The diagram shows a tooth cut in half, in the gum and jaw. Identify the parts labelled A, B, C and D. Four marks. Work from the outside in. A points to the thin white outer layer of the crown: enamel. B points to the shaded grey layer under it: dentine. C points to the space in the centre, with small branching vessels: the pulp cavity. D is a line running down out of the root: a nerve. This answer is by olevels.com, not an official mark scheme.

A common mistake is to swap enamel and dentine, or to call the outer layer bone. Enamel is outside. Dentine is under it. Neither one is bone. Another mistake is to mix up the jobs. Canines do not grind, and molars do not cut. And the pulp is not the root: it is the soft centre.

Your turn. A six-year-old bites into an apple with her front teeth. Name these teeth and give their job. Then give two differences between her teeth and her father's teeth. Pause here and work it out.

Part a: the front teeth are incisors. They are flat like a chisel, and they cut and bite the apple. Part b: she has twenty milk teeth; her father has up to thirty-two. She has no premolars; he has premolars. His teeth are also larger. So, every time: for a section, work from the outside in. For a type, look at the shape. For a job, use one action word. For a difference, give both sides.
