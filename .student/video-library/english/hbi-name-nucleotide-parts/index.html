DNA is a very long molecule. It is made of many small repeating units, joined in a chain. Each unit is called a nucleotide. RNA, a single-stranded molecule that helps make proteins, is made of nucleotides too. Each nucleotide carries one base. A base is a small part that works like a letter of a code. DNA has four bases: adenine, thymine, cytosine and guanine. We write them as A, T, C and G.

Now look at one nucleotide. It has three parts. Find the sugar first. It is the five-sided shape in the middle, joined to both other parts. In DNA, the sugar is called deoxyribose. On the outer side of the sugar is the phosphate group. It is often drawn as a small circle. On the other side is the base. So the order is phosphate, sugar, base. In a DNA molecule, sugar and phosphate join in turn to make a long backbone. The bases point inwards, like the rungs of a ladder. A rung is one step of the ladder. Spell the names with care. In DNA the sugar is deoxyribose. Ribose is the sugar in RNA. And write phosphate, not phosphorus. If a question says describe, do not just list the parts. Say what is joined to what. The phosphate is joined to the sugar. The base is joined to the sugar too.

Here is a real exam question. DNA is formed from nucleotides. The diagram shows a single nucleotide. Name the parts labelled R, S and T. It is worth three marks. Start with S. It is the five-sided shape in the middle, joined to both other parts. So S is the sugar. In DNA, it is deoxyribose. R is the small circle on the outer side of the sugar. So R is the phosphate. T is on the other side of the sugar. In DNA it would pair with a base on the other strand. So T is a base. You could also name one, such as adenine. Here is the full answer. A common mistake is to call the repeating unit an amino acid, or a gene. Amino acids build proteins. The unit of DNA is a nucleotide. Another mistake is to say the bases make the backbone. They do not. The backbone is only sugar and phosphate. The bases are the rungs in the middle.

Your turn. This nucleotide comes from RNA, not DNA. Part J is a small circle on the outside. Part K is the five-sided shape in the middle. Part L is a rectangle on the other side. Name J, K and L. Then say which part is different from a DNA nucleotide. Pause here and work it out.

Part a. Use the positions. J, the circle on the outside, is the phosphate. K, in the middle, is the sugar. This is RNA, so it is ribose. L is the base. Part b. The sugar is different. A DNA nucleotide has deoxyribose. An RNA nucleotide has ribose. So, every time: find the sugar in the middle first. The phosphate is on the outer side. The base is on the other side. Use the exact names: phosphate and deoxyribose.
