Knowledge card

Name heart chambers and major vessels from a diagram

Written
  • The heart has four chambers: right atrium, right ventricle, left atrium and left ventricle. Atria are on top, ventricles below.
  • In a diagram seen from the front, the heart's left side is on the viewer's right.
  • Veins bring blood into the atria: the vena cava into the right atrium, the pulmonary vein into the left atrium.
  • Arteries take blood out of the ventricles: the pulmonary artery from the right ventricle, the aorta from the left ventricle.
  • The pulmonary artery is the only artery carrying deoxygenated blood; the pulmonary vein is the only vein carrying oxygenated blood.
  • Blood in the aorta is oxygenated and at high pressure. Blood in the left side of the heart has come from the lungs.
  • On a whole-circulation diagram, the vessel taking blood from the heart to the body is the aorta, and the vessel bringing blood back from the body is the vena cava.

Before you begin

  • Arteries carry blood away from the heart; veins carry it back.
  • Gas exchange in the lungs makes blood oxygenated.
  • Body cells use oxygen in respiration.
1

The method

  1. Find the words 'left' and 'right' on the diagram, or assume the left side is on your right.

    Why: Most errors in this type come from left-right mix-ups.

  2. Find the labelled structure and see which chamber it connects to.

    Why: The connection tells you the vessel name.

  3. Use the rule: arteries leave ventricles, veins enter atria.

    Why: This rule gives the vessel name from the chamber.

  4. For properties, think about where the blood has just been: lungs (oxygenated) or body (deoxygenated); left ventricle (high pressure).

    Why: Property marks come from the route of the blood.

Do not lose marks here

  • ✗
    Reading 'left' as the viewer's left and naming the wrong side.
  • ✗
    Calling the pulmonary vein the pulmonary artery because it 'goes to the lungs'.
  • ✗
    Saying the vena cava joins the right ventricle.
  • ✗
    Writing 'artery' or 'ventricle' without saying which one.
  • ✗
    Ticking both oxygenated and deoxygenated for the aorta.

Worked explanation

  1. The diagram marks 'right' under chambers Q and P and 'left' under R and S.
  2. Q and R are the top chambers, so they are atria. P and S are the bottom chambers, so they are ventricles.
  3. Vessel M is the tube on the right side. It goes down to the valves at the top of the right ventricle P.
  4. A vessel leaving the right ventricle carries blood to the lungs. That is the pulmonary artery.
  5. So the answer is B, pulmonary artery. The aorta would leave the left ventricle (vessel N).
Model answer · written by olevels.com, not an official Pearson mark scheme

B (pulmonary artery)

How the marks are earned

One mark for B. A is wrong because the aorta leaves the left ventricle. C is wrong because the pulmonary vein enters the left atrium. D is wrong because the vena cava enters the right atrium.
