Explain how skin blood flow and sweating control body temperature

Written

The hypothalamus detects blood temperature. When hot, skin arterioles widen and sweat evaporates; when cold, they narrow and muscles shiver.

4

The method

  1. Hot or cold?

    Decide which direction the body must change.

  2. Detect

    The hypothalamus detects blood temperature.

  3. Arterioles

    They dilate (hot) or constrict (cold).

  4. Heat and sweat

    Say heat lost by radiation; sweat evaporates.

  5. Context

    Exercise: more respiration, more heat, more sweat.

Do not lose marks here

  • ✗
    Capillaries move

    Blood flow changes; the capillaries stay put.

  • ✗
    Sweat "cools"

    Evaporation takes heat from the body.

  • ✗
    Dilation and constriction

    Dilation when hot, constriction when cold.

Worked explanation

  1. The diagram shows skin on a cold day: little blood in the surface capillary, most going through the shunt vessel.
  2. On a hot day the body must lose heat.
  3. Vasodilation happens: the vessels supplying the surface capillaries widen, so more blood flows near the skin surface.
  4. The shunt vessel constricts, so more blood is diverted into the capillaries.
  5. More heat is lost from this blood through the skin surface by radiation.
Model answer · written by olevels.com, not an official Pearson mark scheme

On a hot day vasodilation happens: the arterioles supplying the skin capillaries widen, so much more blood flows through the capillaries near the skin surface. The shunt vessel constricts and becomes narrower, which diverts more blood through the surface capillaries instead of under them. More heat is then lost from the blood through the surface of the skin by radiation, which cools the body.

How the marks are earned

Marks for: vasodilation (vessels to the capillaries widen); more blood flow near the surface; the shunt vessel narrowing; diverting more blood to the capillaries; more heat lost by radiation. Any four. Naming arteries or veins as the vessels that change is ignored.
