Describe how a named infection spreads, shows itself and is prevented

Transmission routes, reservoirs, incubation, symptoms and detection, and prevention measures for Ebola, HIV, cholera, gonorrhoea, pinworm and diarrhoea, including blood-borne risks.

Type 7 Disease HBI-DISEASE-TRANSMISSION-PREVENTION

★★★★☆ · 8 of 36 eligible examination papers, 2019–2025. Difficulty: Medium.

Mostly route-to-barrier links; the incubation and pinworm items need a short chain. Ideas are concrete: routes, barriers and multiplication. No mathematics. Many members are passage-based with new contexts (pinworms, blood donation, reservoirs). Draws on organism types, blood transfusion and hygiene.

Water with infected faeces

Water or food contaminated with faeces from an infected person.
Prevent

Exam tipNot just "dirty water".

Body fluids

Write it as
Blood, vomit and faeces of infected people or bodies.
Prevent
Wear protective clothing; isolate patients; bury safely.

Sex, blood, mother to baby

Write it as
Sexual intercourse, infected blood, and mother to baby.
Prevent
Use condoms, screen blood, do not share needles.

Skin contact, damp floors

Write it as
A fungus spreads by skin contact and shared towels or damp floors.
Prevent
Wash and dry feet; do not share towels.

Exam tipAntifungal, not antibiotic.

4

The method

  1. Pathogen and route

    Water, food, body fluids, sex, blood or hands to mouth.

  2. One barrier per route

    Each prevention must be a different idea.

  3. Late symptoms

    Few enter, they multiply, symptoms show when numbers are large.

  4. Passages

    Use the line numbers, then add your own knowledge.

Do not lose marks here

  • ✗
    Two hygiene answers

    "Wash hands" and "good hygiene" are one idea.

  • ✗
    "Dirty water"

    Link cholera to faeces from an infected person.

  • ✗
    Antibiotics for all

    They do not treat viruses or fungi.

After your attempt, see the full worked explanation

Worked explanation

  1. 'State three', 3 marks: three different precautions, one per line.
  2. How does Ebola spread? Through body fluids of infected people and dead bodies.
  3. Barrier 1: protect health workers with a physical barrier, such as protective clothing, gloves, masks and goggles.
  4. Barrier 2: keep sick people apart from healthy people by isolating them.
  5. Barrier 3: stop spread from dead bodies by burying them safely.
  6. Check: the three lines are different ideas, not the same idea repeated.
Model answer · written by olevels.com, not an official Pearson mark scheme

1 Health workers wear protective clothing, gloves, masks and goggles.

2 Isolate (quarantine) infected people in a separate area away from healthy people.

3 Bury people who have died from Ebola safely, without touching the body.

How the marks are earned

One mark per different precaution: protective clothing or masks; keeping infected people isolated; safe burial. Other creditworthy ideas are avoiding contact with body fluids and disinfecting surfaces. Answers about vaccination or antibiotics are not on the list.
