Knowledge card

Explain diffusion in a context and factors affecting its rate

Written
  • Diffusion moves a substance from where it is more concentrated to where it is less concentrated, until the two sides are equal.
  • Carbon dioxide diffuses out of respiring cells into the blood because cells make it, so its concentration is higher in the cell.
  • Oxygen and nutrients diffuse into bacteria from their surroundings; bacteria are small and need no transport system.
  • A steeper concentration gradient gives faster diffusion, because more particles hit and cross the membrane each second.
  • A larger surface area gives faster diffusion, because there is more membrane for particles to cross at the same time.
  • A shorter diffusion distance, or a larger surface area to volume ratio, means the centre of a cell or cube is reached sooner.
  • Temperature also matters: warmer particles have more kinetic energy and move faster.
  • Free radicals can upset the movement of substances into and out of cells. Antioxidants such as vitamin C change how free radicals behave, so substances can again move in and out of cells normally.

Before you begin

  • Particles move randomly and spread out.
  • Surface area to volume ratio and how to work it out for a cube.
  • Cells respire, using oxygen and making carbon dioxide.
1

The method

  1. Name the process: diffusion.

    Why: Most schemes give a mark just for naming diffusion.

  2. Find where the substance is more concentrated and where it is less concentrated, and say it moves from high to low.

    Why: The direction down the gradient is the second key mark.

  3. If the question is about rate, name the factor that changes: concentration gradient, surface area, distance or SA:V ratio.

    Why: The scheme credits the factor by name.

  4. Explain the mechanism: more particles in contact with the membrane, more area to cross, or a shorter distance to travel.

    Why: The 'why' mark needs a reason, not just 'faster'.

  5. Finish with the effect on rate or time: faster diffusion, so less time taken (or the reverse).

    Why: Linking back to the measured result earns the last mark.

Do not lose marks here

  • ✗
    Writing only 'it diffuses' without saying from where to where.
  • ✗
    Saying 'faster' with no reason, such as more particles crossing or a shorter distance.
  • ✗
    Saying a larger SA:V ratio makes the cube take longer to change colour.
  • ✗
    Calling the movement of gases or glucose osmosis.
  • ✗
    Saying a long dialysis tube gives 'more time' only, without mentioning more surface area.

Common mistakes: Inferred, not measured. Inferred from the official schemes' credit and rejection patterns; not measured student-error statistics.

Worked explanation

  1. Glucose moves from the small intestine into the blood by diffusion. The question asks why the glucose concentration in the intestine changes the rate.
  2. Step 1: more glucose in the intestine means a bigger difference between the intestine and the blood. That is a steeper concentration gradient.
  3. Step 2: why does that matter? With more glucose molecules present, more of them touch the epithelium of the intestine wall each second.
  4. Step 3: so more molecules cross into the blood each second. The rate of diffusion is faster.
  5. I can also give the reverse: a low concentration gives a shallow gradient and slower diffusion.
Model answer · written by olevels.com, not an official Pearson mark scheme

When the concentration of glucose in the small intestine is higher, the concentration gradient between the intestine and the blood is steeper. More glucose molecules are in contact with (collide with) the epithelium of the small intestine wall at any time, so more of them cross into the blood each second. This means glucose diffuses into the blood faster. A lower glucose concentration gives a smaller gradient and slower diffusion.

How the marks are earned

Mark 1: a higher glucose concentration in the intestine gives a larger concentration gradient. Mark 2: more glucose molecules meet or hit the intestine wall (epithelium). Mark 3: so the rate of diffusion is faster. The reverse argument (lower concentration, slower rate) is also accepted.
