Scale per-unit or reference quantities and combine them

A content per 100 g, per kg, per unit or per pot is given; amounts for another mass are found, combined, or used to find how much is needed.

Knowledge card

Scale per-unit or reference quantities and combine them

Calculation
  • Food labels give amounts per 100 g. Amount in another mass = (amount per 100 g / 100) x new mass.
  • First find the amount for one unit (1 g, 1 kg, one slice), then multiply by how many units you have.
  • To find how much you need: needed amount = target / amount per unit.
  • If a portion gives a percentage of the daily need, the daily need = portion amount x 100 / percentage.
  • When counting whole items (pots, slices), think whether to round up.
  • Keep units the same (mg with mg, g with g) before combining.
  • For a rate per 100 g of organ, change kg to g and count the 100 g portions: 0.3 kg = 300 g = 3 portions, so 1200 / 3 = 400.
  • Energy in a food = (mass of protein x energy per gram of protein) + (mass of lipid x energy per gram of lipid).
  • Extra amount needed = recommended daily amount - amount already eaten.

Before you begin

  • Reading nutrition tables.
  • Multiplying and dividing decimals.
  • Rounding to significant figures and using standard form.
1

The method

  1. Write the reference amount and its base (for example 6.1 g per 100 g).

    Why: Reading the right row and column earns the first method mark.

  2. Find the amount for one unit by dividing by the base.

    Why: The per-unit value is a marking point in many schemes.

  3. Multiply by the new quantity, or divide the target by the per-unit value.

    Why: This is the scaling step.

  4. If there are several foods or parts, scale each one and then add or subtract.

    Why: Each scaled amount can earn its own mark.

  5. Round as asked, and add the unit.

    Why: Some schemes cap marks if the rounding instruction is ignored.

Do not lose marks here

  • ✗
    Using the per-100 g value as the answer for a different mass.
  • ✗
    Multiplying by 85 but forgetting to divide by 100, giving 518.5 g.
  • ✗
    Giving 5.18 g when two significant figures are asked for.
  • ✗
    Mixing mg and g, or adding food amounts that are per different masses.
  • ✗
    Dividing the wrong way round, for example 470 / 11 800.

Worked explanation

  1. The protein row gives 6.1 g per 100 g of yoghurt. The 'per 85 g pot' cell is empty, so I must work it out.
  2. Protein in 1 g of yoghurt = 6.1 / 100 = 0.061 g.
  3. Protein in an 85 g pot = 0.061 x 85 = 5.185 g.
  4. Two significant figures: 5.185 rounds to 5.2 g.
  5. Check: 85 g is a bit less than 100 g, so the answer should be a bit less than 6.1 g. 5.2 g fits.
Model answer · written by olevels.com, not an official Pearson mark scheme

6.1 / 100 = 0.061 g per gram. 0.061 x 85 = 5.185 g. Mass of protein = 5.2 g (2 s.f.).

How the marks are earned

One mark for dividing 6.1 by 100. One mark for multiplying by 85. One mark for 5.2 g. If the answer is left as 5.18 the third mark is lost, because the question asks for two significant figures.

Calculation check

  • 6.1 ÷ 100 = 0.061
  • 6.1 ÷ 100 × 85 = 5.185
