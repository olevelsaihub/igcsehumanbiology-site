Explain red-cell adaptations and the effects of more or less oxygen carriage

How red-cell structure suits oxygen transport, and what follows when haemoglobin, red-cell number or oxygenation changes.

Type 12 Internal transport HBI-EXPLAIN-OXYGEN-CARRIAGE-AND-ITS-CHANGES

★★★☆☆ · 5 of 36 eligible examination papers, 2019–2025. Difficulty: Medium.

Several linked steps from a change to its effect on the whole body. Needs the link between oxygen, aerobic respiration and energy, which is standard content. No calculation; reading a data table at most. Contexts such as sickle cells, haemolytic anaemia, altitude training and a hole in the septum are new to students. Links blood, respiration, breathing and heart structure.

Explain red-cell adaptations and the effects of more or less oxygen carriage

Written

A red cell is built to carry oxygen: haemoglobin, no nucleus, a biconcave shape. Less oxygen carried means less aerobic respiration and less energy released.

The method

  1. Feature, so

    Write each feature then its benefit.

  2. Start with the change

    Less haemoglobin, fewer cells, mixed blood.

  3. Chain

    Oxygen carried, oxygen to cells, respiration, energy.

  4. End

    Tiredness, faster breathing or better performance.

Do not lose marks here

  • ✗
    No nucleus alone

    Add more space for haemoglobin.

  • ✗
    Respiration "makes" energy

    It releases energy.

  • ✗
    Stopping at "less oxygen"

    Reach respiration and energy.

  • ✗
    Hole: "no oxygen"

    Less oxygen, from mixing.

Worked explanation

  1. The question asks how the structure of a red cell suits its job. Its job is to carry oxygen. There are 4 marks.
  2. Feature 1: it contains haemoglobin. Benefit: haemoglobin joins with oxygen.
  3. Feature 2: it has no nucleus. Benefit: there is more room for haemoglobin, so more oxygen can be carried.
  4. Feature 3: it is biconcave. Benefit: a large surface area for oxygen to diffuse.
  5. Feature 4: it is small and flexible. Benefit: it can pass through narrow capillaries.
  6. Feature 5: thin membrane. Benefit: oxygen has only a short way to diffuse. I give five pairs to be safe.
Model answer · written by olevels.com, not an official Pearson mark scheme

The red cell contains haemoglobin, which combines with oxygen so it can be carried. It has no nucleus, so there is more space for haemoglobin. Its biconcave shape gives a large surface area for oxygen to diffuse in and out. It is small and flexible, so it can squeeze through narrow capillaries. Its membrane is thin, so the diffusion distance for oxygen is short.

How the marks are earned

Each feature joined to its benefit earns a mark: haemoglobin joining with oxygen; no nucleus giving room for more haemoglobin; biconcave shape raising surface area; small or flexible size for moving through capillaries; thin membrane shortening diffusion. Any four give full marks. A feature with no benefit is weaker.
