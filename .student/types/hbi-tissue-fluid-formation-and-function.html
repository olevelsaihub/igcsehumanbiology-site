Explain how tissue fluid forms, what it does and why it builds up

Pressure at the capillary bed pushing fluid out and drawing it back, its composition compared with blood, exchange with cells, lymph drainage and swelling.

Type 16 Internal transport HBI-TISSUE-FLUID-FORMATION-AND-FUNCTION

★★☆☆☆ · 4 of 36 eligible examination papers, 2019–2025. Difficulty: Difficult.

Needs a balance of two pressures and several steps. Hydrostatic pressure against protein pressure is abstract. One member subtracts pressures in kPa and interprets the sign. Contexts such as lymphoedema and leaky capillary syndrome are new. Links capillaries, blood pressure, diffusion and the lymphatic system.

At the arteriole end of a capillary, high pressure forces water and small molecules out through gaps, making tissue fluid. Cells and large proteins stay in.

4

The method

  1. Where

    The arteriole end, where pressure is high.

  2. The wall

    Capillary walls have pores.

  3. Leaves and stays

    Small molecules out; cells and large proteins stay.

  4. Pressure data

    Blood pressure minus protein pressure shows net flow out or in.

  5. Fate

    Exchange with cells, return at the venule end, extra drains as lymph.

Do not lose marks here

  • ✗
    Cells leave

    Red cells and large proteins stay in the blood.

  • ✗
    Pushed out at the venule end

    Fluid is forced out at the arteriole end.

  • ✗
    Subtracting backwards

    Check which pressure is bigger to find the direction.

Worked explanation

  1. The question asks how tissue fluid is formed. It has 4 marks, so I need four ideas.
  2. Where: at the arteriole end of the capillary.
  3. Why: the blood pressure (hydrostatic pressure) there is high.
  4. How: capillary walls have small gaps or pores.
  5. What: water and small molecules such as glucose and oxygen are forced out through the pores. Large proteins and cells stay behind.
  6. I write these ideas in order in full sentences.
Model answer · written by olevels.com, not an official Pearson mark scheme

At the arteriole end of the capillary, the blood is at high (hydrostatic) pressure because it has just come from the heart through the arteries. The capillary walls have small gaps or pores. The high pressure squeezes water and small molecules, such as glucose, amino acids and ions, out through these gaps into the spaces between the cells. Red blood cells and large plasma proteins are too big to get through, so they stay in the blood. The liquid that has left is tissue fluid.

How the marks are earned

Marks go for: pores or gaps in the capillary wall; water or small molecules being pushed out; this being caused by high pressure in the capillary; and this happening at the arteriole end. 'Plasma' on its own is ignored as the substance leaving.
