Knowledge card

Name thorax and airway structures and the route of air

Written
  • Air goes in through the nose or mouth, then the trachea, then two bronchi (one to each lung), then many bronchioles, then the alveoli.
  • The trachea is the wide tube in the neck with rings of cartilage. It splits into two bronchi.
  • Bronchioles are the small branching tubes inside the lungs. Alveoli are the tiny air sacs at the ends of the bronchioles.
  • The ribs and the sternum (breastbone) form a cage around the lungs. The intercostal muscles lie between the ribs.
  • The diaphragm is the sheet of muscle under the lungs. It separates the thorax from the abdomen.
  • Pleural membranes line the lungs and the inside of the chest wall.
  • Bronchitis is inflammation of the bronchi. Emphysema damages the walls of the alveoli.
  • In a bell-jar or rib-cage model, the front bone is the sternum, the curved bones are the ribs and the tissue between the ribs is intercostal muscle. Parts not shown in such a model include the lungs, the diaphragm and the airways (nose, trachea, bronchi).

Before you begin

  • The difference between the breathing system and the digestive system
  • Singular and plural forms: bronchus and bronchi, alveolus and alveoli
1

The method

  1. Follow each label line to its exact end point.

    Why: A line inside the lung may point to an airway, not to the lung itself.

  2. Decide the level of branching: one tube (trachea), two tubes (bronchi), many small tubes (bronchioles), sacs (alveoli).

    Why: Most wrong answers name the right kind of structure at the wrong level.

  3. For the moving parts, use position: bones in front are sternum, curved bones are ribs, tissue between ribs is intercostal muscle, the dome below is the diaphragm.

    Why: Model questions use string, balloons and plastic sheets for these parts.

  4. Give one name per letter or label. Do not add a second choice.

    Why: Some schemes mark only the first answer, and a second wrong name can cancel a right one.

  5. For an order question, write the structures in the order air meets them.

    Why: Marks are given only for structures in the correct order.

  6. For 'structures not shown in the diagram', give three different structures that are missing from the model, such as lungs, diaphragm and trachea.

    Why: Only the first three answers are marked, and a structure already drawn in the model earns nothing.

Do not lose marks here

  • ✗
    Labelling the bronchiole tree as 'bronchus' or the alveoli as 'bronchioles'.
  • ✗
    Writing 'trachea, bronchioles, bronchi' in the wrong order.
  • ✗
    Calling the oesophagus the trachea, or including it in the air route.
  • ✗
    Drawing a label line that stops in empty space between two structures.
  • ✗
    Naming the lungs or diaphragm when the question asks for structures not shown in the diagram.
  • ✗
    Writing more answers than the lines allow, or naming a part already shown in the model, when asked for structures that are not shown.

Common mistakes: Inferred, not measured. Inferred from the official schemes' credit and rejection patterns; not measured student-error statistics.

Worked explanation

  1. I look at the diagram. There is one wide tube with rings at the top. That is the trachea.
  2. The trachea splits into two short tubes, one going into each lung. Each one is a bronchus.
  3. Inside each lung the tubes branch many times and end in small round bunches. These round sacs are the alveoli.
  4. I draw three label lines, each touching its structure, and write the names.
Model answer · written by olevels.com, not an official Pearson mark scheme

Trachea: label line to the wide ringed tube at the top, above where it divides. Bronchus: label line to one of the two short tubes that go from the trachea into a lung. Alveoli: label line to one of the small round air sacs at the ends of the branches inside a lung.

How the marks are earned

One mark for each label line that touches the right structure: the trachea, one bronchus, and the alveoli. A line that points to a bronchiole branch instead of the sacs is not credited as alveoli.
