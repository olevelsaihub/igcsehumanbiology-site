Explain sewage treatment, pit latrine design and decomposer roles

Stages of a sewage works, aerobic and anaerobic microbes, biogas and sludge, decomposers recycling nutrients, and how pit latrines are sited, built and covered.

Type 5 Disease HBI-SANITATION-SEWAGE-AND-LATRINES

★★☆☆☆ · 3 of 36 eligible examination papers, 2019–2025. Difficulty: Medium.

Each mark links a feature to a reason in one or two steps. Aerobic versus anaerobic microbes and simple design reasons. No mathematics. Diagrams of sewage works and latrines may be unfamiliar in layout. Links respiration, disease transmission by flies and nutrient cycling.

Knowledge card

Explain sewage treatment, pit latrine design and decomposer roles

Written

Sewage treatment uses screening, settlement and bacteria to make waste safe. A latrine is built downhill of wells so waste cannot reach drinking water.

1

Stage by stage

Say what happens first, then tap the ? to check.

4 stages to find

What happens at each stage?

  1. Large objects removed
  2. Solids sink as sludge
  3. Air bubbled in
  4. Biogas (methane) made

Large objects removed

Write it as
Rags, plastic and grit are taken out.

Exam tipIt removes large objects, not bacteria.

Solids sink as sludge

Write it as
Solids sink to form sludge.

Air bubbled in

Write it as
Air gives oxygen so bacteria respire aerobically and break down waste.

Exam tipAir is not added to kill bacteria.

Biogas (methane) made

Write it as
Anaerobic bacteria break down sludge and make methane to burn as fuel.

3 stages to find

Why is each feature used?

  1. Waste cannot reach the well
  2. Urine soaks away
  3. Keeps out flies and rats

Waste cannot reach the well

Write it as
Rainwater and waste flow away from drinking water.

Exam tipNot "easier to dig".

Urine soaks away

Write it as
Liquid drains through the soil.

Keeps out flies and rats

Write it as
Flies and rats cannot carry pathogens; less smell.

4

The method

  1. Find the stage

    Say what happens there.

  2. Name the agents

    Bacteria, flies, rats or rainwater.

  3. Give the reason

    Oxygen for respiration, or stopping contamination.

  4. Finish with the benefit

    Less disease, nutrients returned, or fuel made.

Do not lose marks here

  • ✗
    Screening molecules

    It removes large objects only.

  • ✗
    Air kills bacteria

    Air lets bacteria respire.

  • ✗
    Main gas is CO2

    Biogas is mainly methane.

  • ✗
    Decomposers make fertiliser

    They break down dead matter.

Written-answer check

Worked explanation

  1. 'Explain why air is added at stage Y', 2 marks: who uses the air and what for.
  2. Stage Y is the activated sludge tank. It contains bacteria and other microorganisms that break down the sewage.
  3. Air supplies oxygen. These microorganisms need oxygen for aerobic respiration.
  4. Check: I have said the microorganisms are present and why they need oxygen.
Model answer · written by olevels.com, not an official Pearson mark scheme

The activated sludge tank contains bacteria and other microorganisms that break down the organic matter in the sewage. They need oxygen for aerobic respiration, so air is bubbled in to supply oxygen. This lets them respire, grow and break down the waste quickly.

How the marks are earned

One mark for saying bacteria or other microorganisms are in the tank. One mark for linking the air to oxygen needed for aerobic respiration.
