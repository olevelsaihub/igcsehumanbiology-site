Explain sewage treatment, pit latrine design and decomposer roles

Knowledge card

Explain sewage treatment, pit latrine design and decomposer roles

Written
  • Screening removes large objects (rags, plastic, grit) from raw sewage.
  • Settlement tanks let solids sink to form sludge.
  • In the activated sludge tank, air is bubbled in so aerobic bacteria and other microorganisms can respire aerobically and break down organic matter.
  • In the anaerobic digester, anaerobic bacteria break down sludge without oxygen and produce biogas, which is mainly methane and can be burned as fuel.
  • Treated sludge contains nitrogen compounds (nitrates) and other minerals, so it can be used as fertiliser for plant growth.
  • Decomposers (non-pathogenic bacteria and fungi) release enzymes that break down dead organic matter and return nutrients to the soil.
  • A pit latrine is built downhill from wells so that rainwater and waste flow away from drinking water, in permeable soil so urine soaks away, and with a cover to keep out flies and rats and reduce smell. A long handle keeps hands away from the pit opening.

Before you begin

  • Aerobic respiration needs oxygen; anaerobic respiration does not.
  • Flies and rats can carry pathogens to food and people.
  • Plants need nitrates to make proteins and grow.
1

The method

  1. Find the stage or design feature in the diagram and say what happens there.

    Why: The first mark is usually for the process or feature.

  2. Name the organisms or agents involved: bacteria, flies, rats, rainwater.

    Why: Marks often require the microorganisms or carriers to be named.

  3. Give the reason: oxygen for aerobic respiration, stopping contamination of water, stopping flies carrying pathogens.

    Why: The reason is the second, linked mark.

  4. Finish with the benefit to health or the environment: less disease, nutrients returned, fuel produced.

    Why: Longer questions credit the final consequence.

Do not lose marks here

  • ✗
    Saying screening removes 'molecules' or 'bacteria'; it removes large objects only.
  • ✗
    Saying air is added 'to kill bacteria'; it is added so the bacteria can respire.
  • ✗
    Naming carbon dioxide as the main gas in biogas instead of methane.
  • ✗
    Saying a latrine is placed downhill 'so it is easier to dig', instead of stopping waste reaching the well.
  • ✗
    Saying decomposers 'produce fertiliser' instead of breaking down dead matter and releasing nutrients.

Worked explanation

  1. 'Explain why air is added at stage Y', 2 marks: who uses the air and what for.
  2. Stage Y is the activated sludge tank. It contains bacteria and other microorganisms that break down the sewage.
  3. Air supplies oxygen. These microorganisms need oxygen for aerobic respiration.
  4. Check: I have said the microorganisms are present and why they need oxygen.
Model answer · written by olevels.com, not an official Pearson mark scheme

The activated sludge tank contains bacteria and other microorganisms that break down the organic matter in the sewage. They need oxygen for aerobic respiration, so air is bubbled in to supply oxygen. This lets them respire, grow and break down the waste quickly.

How the marks are earned

One mark for saying bacteria or other microorganisms are in the tank. One mark for linking the air to oxygen needed for aerobic respiration.
