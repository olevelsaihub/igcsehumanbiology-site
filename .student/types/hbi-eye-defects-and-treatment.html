Pearson Edexcel · International GCSE Human Biology · 4HB1

Explain eye defects and how they are corrected or treated

Short sight, long sight, astigmatism, cataracts and corneal disease: where the image falls, cause, effect on vision, lenses, surgery and transplant risks.

Type 10 Coordination HBI-EYE-DEFECTS-AND-TREATMENT

★★★★☆ · 7 of 36 eligible examination papers, 2019–2025. Difficulty: Medium.

Cause, effect and treatment must be linked correctly. Short and long sight are easy to confuse and need ray ideas. No arithmetic is needed. Several different defects and treatments appear. Links eye structure, refraction and transplant ideas.

Explain eye defects and how they are corrected or treated

Written

Short sight: distant objects blurred, rays meet in front of the retina, concave lens. Long sight: near objects blurred, rays meet behind, convex lens.

Distant, in front, concave

Distant objects are blurred; rays meet in front of the retina; a concave (diverging) lens corrects it.

Exam tipMyopia means short sight.

Near, behind, convex

Near objects are blurred; rays meet behind the retina; a convex (converging) lens corrects it.

Exam tipNot "cannot see".

Special lenses

The cornea or lens is unevenly curved; glasses correct it.

Replace the lens

Write it as
A cloudy lens lets less light through; surgery replaces it.

Exam tipNot a growth on the eye.

Corneal transplant

Write it as
A donor cornea restores the shape; risks are infection and rejection.

4

The method

  1. Name the defect

    Say where the light focuses.

  2. Give the cause

    Eyeball or lens too long, short, strong or weak.

  3. Effect on vision

    Which objects look blurred.

  4. Correction and how

    Lens type, then what it does to the rays.

Do not lose marks here

  • ✗
    Mixed sights

    Short-sighted people see near objects well.

  • ✗
    Lens, no action

    Say converges or diverges.

  • ✗
    Cataract a growth

    It is a cloudy lens.

  • ✗
    Myopia for long

    Myopia is short sight.

Worked explanation

  1. The question asks for a way to treat long sight. It is worth 2 marks.
  2. In long sight, light from near objects focuses behind the retina. The eye does not bend the light enough.
  3. So the treatment must add extra bending. A convex (converging) lens does this. That is mark 1.
  4. Mark 2 is how it works: the lens bends the rays inwards before they enter the eye, so they meet on the retina.
Model answer · written by olevels.com, not an official Pearson mark scheme

Wear glasses or contact lenses with a convex (converging) lens. The convex lens bends the light rays inwards before they reach the eye, so the rays converge and are focused on the retina instead of behind it.

How the marks are earned

One mark for a convex or converging lens. One mark for saying this lens converges, refracts or bends the light rays. A concave lens is wrong for long sight.
