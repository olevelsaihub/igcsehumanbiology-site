Knowledge card

Define pathogen, classify diseases and name the organism type causing each

Written
  • A pathogen is a microorganism (or other organism) that causes disease.
  • A communicable (infectious) disease is caused by a pathogen and can pass from one person to another. A non-communicable disease or condition is not caused by a pathogen and cannot be caught, for example diabetes or heart disease.
  • Bacteria cause cholera, typhoid, tuberculosis (TB) and gonorrhoea.
  • Viruses cause Ebola, AIDS (the virus is HIV), polio (poliomyelitis), influenza and hepatitis B.
  • A protozoan (Plasmodium) causes malaria; it is carried by the mosquito, which is the vector, not the cause.
  • A fungus causes athlete's foot.
  • Common transmission routes: malaria by mosquito bites; polio by water or droplets; typhoid by food or water, often carried by houseflies.

Before you begin

  • The main groups of microorganisms: bacteria, viruses, fungi and protoctists (protozoa).
  • Insulin controls blood glucose; diabetes happens when insulin is not made or not used.
  • Living things versus non-living particles (viruses are not cells).
1

The method

  1. Read whether the question wants a general term (pathogen) or a type of organism (bacterium, virus, protozoan, fungus).

    Why: Giving 'bacteria' when 'pathogen' is asked, or the reverse, scores nothing.

  2. For a definition, give both parts: an organism or microorganism, and that it causes disease.

    Why: The mark needs the idea of causing disease, not just 'a germ'.

  3. For a named disease, recall the organism group and write one group only per box.

    Why: Two answers in one box are rejected.

  4. For 'condition versus disease', say a disease is caused by a pathogen and can spread between people, then say the condition has a non-pathogen cause.

    Why: Each idea is a separate mark; you need two.

  5. Do not name the vector as the cause.

    Why: A mosquito carries malaria; the protozoan causes it.

Do not lose marks here

  • ✗
    Choosing 'bacteria' as the general name for disease-causing organisms, although many bacteria are harmless.
  • ✗
    Writing 'mosquito' as the cause of malaria or Ebola.
  • ✗
    Naming AIDS as the microorganism instead of HIV, or calling HIV a bacterium.
  • ✗
    Defining non-communicable as 'not serious' or 'not deadly' instead of 'not caught from another person'.
  • ✗
    Writing two organism types in one table box.

Common mistakes: Inferred, not measured. Inferred from the official schemes' credit and rejection patterns; not measured student-error statistics.

Worked explanation

  1. Command word 'Suggest', 2 marks: I need two separate ideas.
  2. What makes something a disease in this course? It is caused by a pathogen.
  3. Second feature of a disease: it can be passed from one person to another.
  4. Now diabetes: the passage says it comes from not making enough insulin or not reacting to it. That is not a pathogen.
  5. So diabetes cannot be caught. Write the contrast in clear sentences.
Model answer · written by olevels.com, not an official Pearson mark scheme

A disease is caused by a pathogen, such as a bacterium or a virus. Diseases can be passed from one person to another. Diabetes is not caused by a pathogen; it happens because the body does not make enough insulin or does not respond to it. So diabetes cannot be caught from another person, and it is called a condition.

How the marks are earned

One mark each for any two of: diseases come from pathogens such as bacteria or viruses; diseases can spread from person to person; diabetes comes from a lack of insulin (or is not caused by a pathogen). The answer gives all three.
