Explain bacterial growth conditions and how food preservation slows it

Growth-curve phases, temperature groups of bacteria, warmth and moisture, and how chilling, freezing, heating or packaging stop microbes spoiling food.

Type 3 Disease HBI-MICROBE-GROWTH-AND-FOOD-PRESERVATION

★★☆☆☆ · 4 of 36 eligible examination papers, 2019–2025. Difficulty: Medium.

Short links from a condition to growth; one step of inference from a graph. Needs the growth-curve phases and enzyme temperature effects, both standard ideas. No calculation; graph reading is only qualitative. Contexts such as glue ear or temperature groups of bacteria may be new. Joins enzyme knowledge with food hygiene and graph reading.

Knowledge card

Explain bacterial growth conditions and how food preservation slows it

Written

Bacteria grow in four phases: lag, exponential, stationary, death. Cold slows them; high temperature kills them.

  2. Numbers double and double

Not yet dividing

Write it as
Bacteria adjust and make enzymes; few are dividing.

Exam tipNot the stationary phase.

Numbers double and double

Write it as
Numbers double again and again, so the population rises very fast.

Dividing equals dying

Write it as
Food runs short and waste builds up; deaths equal new cells.

Dying beats dividing

Write it as
More cells die than are made, so numbers fall.

Explain bacterial growth conditions and how food preservation slows it · June 2024 · Paper 1 · Question 2 · part (b) · 6 marks

(b) Bacteria can multiply rapidly in food that is not preserved. Use words from the box to complete the passage about the growth of bacteria. exponential more than stationary equal to lag dying death less than live growth The first stage in the growth of bacterial colonies is known as the ________ phase. During this phase, bacteria are not dividing. The second stage of bacterial growth is known as the ________ phase. During this phase the bacteria are multiplying rapidly. In the third stage, nutrients are running out and the number of dying cells is ________ the number of dividing cells. Finally, during the ________ phase the number of dividing cells is ________ the number of ________ cells.

Hint for part (b)

Use only the words in the box. Read each full sentence; a phrase like 'equal to' or 'less than' must make the comparison true.

Written-answer check

After your attempt, see the full worked explanation

Worked explanation

  1. The passage follows the growth curve in order, so I expect lag, exponential, stationary and death.
  2. Blank 1: the first phase, when bacteria are not dividing yet. That is the lag phase.
  3. Blank 2: bacteria multiplying rapidly. The box has 'exponential', which is the log phase.
  4. Blank 3: nutrients are running out and this is the third stage, the stationary phase. In this phase the population stays level, so dying cells are 'equal to' dividing cells.
  5. Blank 4: the final phase is the death phase. The word in the box is 'death', not 'dying'.
  6. Blanks 5 and 6: in the death phase numbers fall, so dividing cells must be 'less than' the number of 'dying' cells.
  7. Check: 'stationary' is not needed, because the third stage is described, not named. Each blank uses a different word.
Model answer · written by olevels.com, not an official Pearson mark scheme

The first stage is the lag phase. The second stage is the exponential phase. In the third stage the number of dying cells is equal to the number of dividing cells. Finally, during the death phase the number of dividing cells is less than the number of dying cells.

How the marks are earned

One mark for each blank in order: lag for the first phase, exponential for rapid division, equal to for the level phase, death for the final phase, then less than and dying to show that numbers fall. A blank with 'more than' or 'stationary' in the wrong place gets no mark.
