Knowledge card

Explain bacterial growth conditions and how food preservation slows it

Written
  • A bacterial colony grows in four phases: lag, exponential (log), stationary and death.
  • Lag phase: the bacteria are adjusting and making enzymes, so they are not yet dividing. Exponential phase: numbers double again and again, so the population rises very fast.
  • Stationary phase: food runs short and waste builds up, so the number of cells dying equals the number of new cells made. Death phase: more cells die than are made, so numbers fall.
  • Bacteria multiply fastest when they have food, moisture and a warm temperature near their optimum. Different groups have different optimum temperatures: psychrophiles like cold, mesophiles like body temperature, thermophiles like about 50-70 °C.
  • A fridge (about 4 °C) slows bacterial enzymes, so bacteria multiply slowly; it does not kill them. Freezing stops growth for longer, but bacteria can grow again after thawing.
  • High temperature (boiling, pasteurisation, thorough cooking) denatures bacterial enzymes and kills most bacteria. Sealing or vacuum packing keeps new microorganisms and air out.
  • In yoghurt making, milk is boiled to kill unwanted microbes, then cooled so that the added useful bacteria are not killed.
  • Fluid trapped in a warm, damp place in the body (such as the middle ear in glue ear) gives bacteria ideal conditions, and bacteria are more likely to get stuck there, so infections are more common.

Before you begin

  • Enzymes have an optimum temperature; they work slowly when cold and are denatured when too hot.
  • Bacteria reproduce by dividing into two (binary fission).
  • Bacteria need nutrients and water to grow.
1

The method

  1. Name the condition in the question: temperature, moisture, food supply, or exposure to air or microbes.

    Why: The first mark usually goes to the correct condition or phase name.

  2. Say what the condition does to the bacteria's enzymes or to how fast they divide.

    Why: Examiners credit the link to enzyme activity or reproduction, not just the condition.

  3. State the result: bacteria multiply faster or slower, or are killed, so food spoils faster or slower.

    Why: The final mark is for the effect on spoilage or infection.

  4. For a graph, read the optimum temperature of the named group and compare it with the temperature given.

    Why: An answer that matches the data, not general belief, earns the mark.

  5. For a word box, read the whole sentence before choosing, and check that 'dying' and 'dividing' are in the right places.

    Why: Each blank is one mark, and the order matters.

Do not lose marks here

  • ✗
    Writing that a fridge kills bacteria. Cold only slows their growth.
  • ✗
    Assuming every bacterium is killed at 65 °C, when the graph shows thermophiles grow best near that temperature.
  • ✗
    Mixing up the phases, for example calling the phase where cells are not dividing the 'stationary' phase instead of the lag phase.
  • ✗
    Putting 'more than' for the death phase in the wrong place: in the death phase dividing cells are fewer than dying cells.
  • ✗
    Counting freezing or refrigeration as heat treatment.

Common mistakes: Inferred, not measured. Inferred from the official schemes' credit and rejection patterns; not measured student-error statistics.

Worked explanation

  1. The passage follows the growth curve in order, so I expect lag, exponential, stationary and death.
  2. Blank 1: the first phase, when bacteria are not dividing yet. That is the lag phase.
  3. Blank 2: bacteria multiplying rapidly. The box has 'exponential', which is the log phase.
  4. Blank 3: nutrients are running out and this is the third stage, the stationary phase. In this phase the population stays level, so dying cells are 'equal to' dividing cells.
  5. Blank 4: the final phase is the death phase. The word in the box is 'death', not 'dying'.
  6. Blanks 5 and 6: in the death phase numbers fall, so dividing cells must be 'less than' the number of 'dying' cells.
  7. Check: 'stationary' is not needed, because the third stage is described, not named. Each blank uses a different word.
Model answer · written by olevels.com, not an official Pearson mark scheme

The first stage is the lag phase. The second stage is the exponential phase. In the third stage the number of dying cells is equal to the number of dividing cells. Finally, during the death phase the number of dividing cells is less than the number of dying cells.

How the marks are earned

One mark for each blank in order: lag for the first phase, exponential for rapid division, equal to for the level phase, death for the final phase, then less than and dying to show that numbers fall. A blank with 'more than' or 'stationary' in the wrong place gets no mark.
