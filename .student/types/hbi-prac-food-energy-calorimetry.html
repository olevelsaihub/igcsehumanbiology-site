Measure food energy by burning and explain why the value is too low

Burning a food sample under a tube or can of water, describing the method, explaining heat losses and how a calorimeter or apparatus changes improve accuracy.

Type 9 Investigations HBI-PRAC-FOOD-ENERGY-CALORIMETRY

★★☆☆☆ · 4 of 36 eligible examination papers, 2019–2025. Difficulty: Medium.

The method is a standard sequence, but the student must also link losses to a low result. Energy transfer and heat loss are simple ideas, but students must see that only part of the energy reaches the water. Some members use the formula energy = mass x 4.2 x rise; the method parts need only a temperature change. The apparatus varies (test tube, copper can, calorimeter) but the practical is a named specification practical. Combines method writing, control of variables and evaluation of accuracy in one question.

4

The method

  1. Water

    Measure a set volume and its starting temperature.

  2. Food

    Weigh it, burn it under the tube until complete.

  3. Rise

    Stir, record the highest temperature, find the rise.

  4. Compare

    Repeat each food with the same mass and water.

  5. Losses

    Name each loss, then pair it with a fix.

Do not lose marks here

  • ✗
    One temperature

    Record the start and the end, then find the rise.

  • ✗
    "Amount" of food

    Write the same mass of food.

  • ✗
    Energy destroyed

    Energy goes to the air, glass or needle.

  • ✗
    Repeat as a fix

    Improve accuracy with insulation or a lid.

Worked explanation

  1. The command word is Describe and there are 6 marks, so I need about six separate steps in order.
  2. The diagram shows a boiling tube of water with a thermometer, a clamp stand and food on a needle. So the water is heated by the burning food.
  3. The question says compare different foods. So the foods must start with the same mass, and I must do the test for each food.
  4. Readings needed: water temperature before and after, so I can find the rise.
  5. Steps: weigh food, record start temperature, burn food under the tube, record highest temperature, repeat for each food, compare rises.
  6. Final check: six steps, each one a separate idea. I add 'completely burnt' and 'same volume of water' to be safe.
Model answer · written by olevels.com, not an official Pearson mark scheme

Put a measured volume of water, for example 20 cm³, into the boiling tube and clamp it to the stand. Record the starting temperature of the water with the thermometer. Weigh a piece of the first food and push it onto the needle. Set the food alight in a Bunsen flame and hold it under the tube until it has completely burnt. Record the highest temperature of the water and work out the rise. Repeat with the same mass of each of the other foods, using fresh water of the same volume each time. Compare the temperature rises: the food that gives the biggest rise contains the most energy.

How the marks are earned

One mark each for: weighing the food or using the same mass; taking the water temperature at the start; burning the food under the tube; taking the water temperature at the end; doing the test again for every food; and comparing the change in water temperature between foods. 'Amount' is a weak word; say mass.
