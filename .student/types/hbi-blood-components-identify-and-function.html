Identify blood components and match them to their features and jobs

Recognising red cells, white cells, platelets and plasma in smears or drawings, and tick grids or naming prompts pairing each with what it contains or does.

Type 1 Internal transport HBI-BLOOD-COMPONENTS-IDENTIFY-AND-FUNCTION

★★★★★ · 10 of 36 eligible examination papers, 2019–2025. Difficulty: Easy.

One step: match what you see to a name or a job. Needs recall of five components and their features, which is basic content. No calculation. Diagrams and tables change in style, and one item asks about frozen blood, but the content is standard. Uses only blood component knowledge.

Knowledge card

Identify blood components and match them to their features and jobs

Written

Blood has four parts: red cells (haemoglobin, no nucleus), white cells (a nucleus), platelets (cell fragments) and plasma (the liquid).

1

Name the component

Say it first, then tap the ? to check.

5 names to find

Name the component from its features.

  1. Red blood cell
  2. Phagocyte
  3. Lymphocyte
  4. Platelets
  5. Plasma

Red blood cell

Write it as
Small, full of haemoglobin, no nucleus.

Phagocyte

Write it as
A white blood cell with a lobed nucleus; it engulfs pathogens.

Lymphocyte

Write it as
A white blood cell with a large round nucleus; it makes antibodies.

Exam tipLymphocytes do not engulf.

Platelets

Write it as
Fragments of cells, not whole cells.

Exam tipPlatelets have no nucleus.

Plasma

Write it as
The liquid part, about 90% water.

3 names to find

Give the job, not the appearance.

  1. Carry oxygen
  2. Carries dissolved substances
  3. Help blood clot

Carry oxygen

Write it as
Haemoglobin carries oxygen.

Carries dissolved substances

Write it as
Glucose, urea, carbon dioxide and hormones.

Help blood clot

Write it as
Platelets help the blood to clot.

4

The method

  1. Look

    Size, shape and nucleus.

  2. Decide

    Disc, lobed, round, tiny bits, liquid.

  3. Row by row

    In a tick table, one feature at a time.

  4. Exact name

    Phagocyte, lymphocyte, platelets.

  5. Give the job

    The job, not the appearance.

Do not lose marks here

  • ✗
    Platelets are cells

    They are fragments.

  • ✗
    Liquid called water

    Say plasma.

  • ✗
    Extra ticks

    They can cancel a correct row.

  • ✗
    One cell, two jobs

    Lymphocytes make antibodies; phagocytes engulf.

Worked explanation

  1. I read the question: name X, Y and Z. That is three marks, one for each label.
  2. X points to the empty area between the cells. Nothing is drawn there, so it must be the liquid part of the blood. That is plasma.
  3. Y points to a small round cell with a pale dip in the middle and no nucleus. The dip shows a biconcave shape. This is a red blood cell.
  4. Z points to a larger cell with a dark nucleus that has lobes, like a curved sausage. A lobed nucleus means a phagocyte, which is a type of white blood cell.
  5. I also notice small irregular bits in the picture. They are platelets, but no label points to them, so I do not need them.
  6. I write one name on each line.
Model answer · written by olevels.com, not an official Pearson mark scheme

X: plasma

Y: red blood cell (erythrocyte)

Z: phagocyte (a white blood cell)

How the marks are earned

One mark for each correct name. X earns its mark only for plasma, the fluid. Y earns its mark for red blood cell. Z earns its mark for white blood cell or the more exact name phagocyte; the scheme also accepts a named type of phagocyte.
