Knowledge card

Identify bacterial cell structures and compare with human cells

Written
  • A bacterial cell has a cell wall, cell membrane, cytoplasm, ribosomes and a single loop of DNA that is not in a nucleus.
  • Many bacteria also have plasmids (small rings of DNA), a flagellum for movement, and sometimes pili and a slime capsule outside the wall. In a photo the outer layer can be named cell wall, capsule or slime coat.
  • A human cell (such as a skin cell or sperm) has a nucleus, mitochondria, cell membrane, cytoplasm and ribosomes, but no cell wall and no plasmids.
  • Similarities: both have a cell membrane, cytoplasm, ribosomes and DNA.
  • Differences: bacteria have a cell wall, no nucleus, plasmids, often a flagellum, and no mitochondria.
  • Functions: cell membrane controls what enters and leaves; ribosomes make proteins; DNA holds the genetic code; flagellum helps the cell move; cell wall supports and protects.

Before you begin

  • Structure of a typical animal cell: nucleus, cytoplasm, cell membrane, mitochondria, ribosomes.
  • DNA carries genetic information.
  • Cell membrane and cell wall are different structures.
1

The method

  1. Read whether the question wants similarities or differences, and how many.

    Why: A difference given as a similarity, or the reverse, gets no mark.

  2. For a difference, name a structure found in one cell but not the other, and say which cell has it.

    Why: The mark needs the feature and the direction, such as 'bacterium has a wall, skin cell does not'.

  3. For a label, follow the label line to the exact structure: the outer layer is the wall, the line inside it is the membrane, the tangled loop is DNA.

    Why: Labelling the wrong layer is the most common lost mark.

  4. For a matching task, draw exactly one line from each structure.

    Why: More than one line from a structure is rejected.

  5. Do not label more structures than asked.

    Why: Extra labels can lose marks for each wrong one.

Do not lose marks here

  • ✗
    Saying a bacterium has no DNA because it has no nucleus.
  • ✗
    Writing 'a skin cell has a cell membrane but a bacterium does not'. Both have one.
  • ✗
    Giving a difference with no direction, for example just 'cell wall'.
  • ✗
    Calling the flagellum 'cilia' or 'tail'.
  • ✗
    Labelling the cell wall as the cell membrane.

Common mistakes: Inferred, not measured. Inferred from the official schemes' credit and rejection patterns; not measured student-error statistics.

Worked explanation

  1. 'Give two differences', 2 marks: two different structures, each in only one cell.
  2. Look at the bacterium: thick outer wall, loose DNA, a long flagellum. Look at the skin cell: a clear nucleus, no wall.
  3. Difference 1: the bacterium has a cell wall; the skin cell does not.
  4. Difference 2: the bacterium has no nucleus; the skin cell does.
  5. Make each answer a full sentence that names both cells.
Model answer · written by olevels.com, not an official Pearson mark scheme

1 The bacterial cell has a cell wall, but the human skin cell does not.

2 The bacterial cell has no nucleus (its DNA is free in the cytoplasm), but the human skin cell has a nucleus.

How the marks are earned

One mark for each difference that names a structure present in one cell only: cell wall, nucleus, plasmid, flagellum, pili, mitochondria or slime capsule. A structure both cells have, such as the membrane, scores nothing.
