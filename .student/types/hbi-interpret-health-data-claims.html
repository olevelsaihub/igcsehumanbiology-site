Pearson Edexcel · International GCSE Human Biology · 4HB1

Judge whether data support a claim and spot their limits

Health and investigation data where the student decides if evidence supports a claim, why an estimate is uncertain, which result is anomalous, or why data are unreliable or incomplete.

Type 12 Homeostatic mechanisms HBI-INTERPRET-HEALTH-DATA-CLAIMS

★★★☆☆ · 6 of 36 eligible examination papers, 2019–2025. Difficulty: Difficult.

Must weigh evidence for and against a claim and reach a judgement. Ideas of correlation, sampling and anomaly are simple but must be applied. Reading and comparing values; at most a simple subtraction. Each question uses a new context and data set. Combines data handling with biology of the context, such as urea or kidney function.

4

The method

  1. Read the claim

    Underline what it says will happen.

  2. Quote figures

    Use one or two values with units.

  3. Name the limits

    Other factors, sample size, variation, anomaly.

  4. Anomaly

    Name the value; it does not fit the pattern.

  5. Judge

    Finish with a short judgement.

Do not lose marks here

  • ✗
    Link means cause

    Both rising is a correlation only.

  • ✗
    "Not reliable"

    Say why, for example a small sample.

  • ✗
    Describing the trend

    Judge the evidence instead.

  • ✗
    No judgement

    Always end with supports or not.

Written-answer check

After your attempt, see the full worked explanation

Worked explanation

  1. The claim is: muscle breakdown happens at a constant rate and diet does not change it.
  2. I look at the table. Urea is 14.7 g on the high protein diet but only 2.2 g on the low protein diet. Uric acid and ammonia also change a lot. So these come mostly from protein in the food.
  3. Now creatinine: 0.58 g on the high protein diet and 0.60 g on the low protein diet. The difference is only 0.02 g, so it is almost the same.
  4. Creatinine is the substance that could come from muscle breakdown. It stays almost the same whatever the diet.
  5. So the table supports the statement. I write the comparison with figures and then the conclusion.
Model answer · written by olevels.com, not an official Pearson mark scheme

The masses of urea, uric acid and ammonia are very different for the two diets. For example urea is 14.7 g on the high protein diet but only 2.2 g on the low protein diet, so these depend on what the person eats. Creatinine is different: its mass is almost the same, 0.58 g and 0.60 g, in both diets. Creatinine is likely to be produced when muscle tissue is broken down. Because creatinine output stays the same on both diets, the table supports the statement that muscle breakdown happens at a steady rate that diet does not change.

How the marks are earned

One mark for saying the urea, uric acid and ammonia values change between the diets. One mark for picking out creatinine. One mark for saying its mass is the same or nearly the same, and one for making clear this is true in both diets. Linking creatinine to muscle breakdown is another creditable point, so the answer has more than four points. An answer that talks only about urea scores little, because urea comes from dietary protein.

Calculation check

  • round(0.60-0.58,2) = 0.02
