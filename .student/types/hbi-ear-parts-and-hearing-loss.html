Identify ear parts and use the sound path to explain hearing and hearing loss

Naming ear structures, roles of pinna, eardrum, ossicles, cochlea, Eustachian tube and semicircular canals, and how glue ear, pressure, ageing or damage reduce hearing.

Type 8 Coordination HBI-EAR-PARTS-AND-HEARING-LOSS

★★☆☆☆ · 3 of 36 eligible examination papers, 2019–2025. Difficulty: Medium.

Some items need cause-and-effect reasoning about blocked steps. Mainly a path of named parts and their jobs. No arithmetic is needed. Glue ear and diabetes contexts are less familiar. Links ear structure with receptors and the brain.

The method

  1. Follow the path

    Pinna, canal, eardrum, ossicles, cochlea, nerve.

  2. Say what it does

    What each part does and where it passes it.

  3. Find the failed step

    Say what can no longer pass on.

  4. Balance chain

    Canals, fluid moves, impulses, brain.

Do not lose marks here

  • ✗
    Ossicles "hear"

    They pass on and amplify vibrations.

  • ✗
    Eardrum sends impulses

    It only vibrates.

  • ✗
    Cochlea or canals

    Cochlea hears; canals balance.

  • ✗
    "Cannot hear"

    Say which step is blocked.

Worked explanation

  1. P, Q and R are the three small bones between the eardrum and the inner ear. They are the ossicles. The question is worth 4 marks.
  2. First idea: they pass on (transmit) vibrations.
  3. Second idea: the vibrations come from the eardrum.
  4. Third idea: they carry them across the middle ear.
  5. Fourth idea: they pass them to the oval window and the cochlea.
  6. Fifth idea: they make the vibrations larger (amplify them). Five ideas for four marks is safe.
Model answer · written by olevels.com, not an official Pearson mark scheme

P, Q and R are the ossicles (hammer, anvil and stirrup). They pick up the vibrations of the eardrum and pass these vibrations across the middle ear. They pass them on to the oval window of the cochlea. As they do this they amplify the vibrations, making them bigger.

How the marks are earned

Marks are for: transmitting vibrations; from the eardrum; across the middle ear; to the oval window or cochlea; amplifying the vibrations. Any four give full marks. Saying the bones make impulses is wrong.
