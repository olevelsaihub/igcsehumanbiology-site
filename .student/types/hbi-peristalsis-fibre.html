Food is pushed along as a bolus by peristalsis: circular muscles contract behind it and relax in front. Fibre adds bulk to keep it moving.

Worked explanation

  1. Two parts: how food moves, and how fibre helps. Five marks.
  2. Movement: the process is peristalsis; the food is a bolus.
  3. Mechanism: circular muscles in the gut wall contract behind the bolus, squeezing it forward in a wave.
  4. Fibre: it cannot be digested, so it adds bulk to the food.
  5. Link: the bulk stimulates the muscles to contract, so food keeps moving and constipation is prevented.
Model answer · written by olevels.com, not an official Pearson mark scheme

Food is moved by peristalsis. The food is formed into a bolus. Circular muscles in the wall of the gut contract behind the bolus and relax in front of it, so a wave of contraction pushes the bolus along. Fibre cannot be digested, so it adds bulk to the food. This bulk stimulates the gut muscles to contract, so food keeps moving and constipation is prevented.

How the marks are earned

Marks are for: naming peristalsis, the bolus, muscles behind contracting to push it on, fibre not being digested, fibre adding bulk, and bulk stimulating the muscles. Five of these six give full marks.
