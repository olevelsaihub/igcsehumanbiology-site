Knowledge card

Improve reliability with repeats, means and larger samples

Written
  • Results are reliable if you get similar results when the investigation is repeated.
  • Repeat each test (at least three times) and calculate a mean for each condition.
  • Testing many people instead of one gives a mean that is less affected by one unusual person.
  • An anomalous result does not fit the pattern; leave it out when you calculate the mean, unless the question tells you to use all the values.
  • To show a result is true in general, test other groups too: other ages, sexes or other types of bacteria.
  • Results can also be checked against data from another group or another study.
  • Widen the data: test more people in each group, more ages with smaller gaps (such as every year), or measure over more years.

Before you begin

  • How to calculate a mean.
  • What an anomalous result is.
  • The difference between reliability, accuracy and validity.
1

The method

  1. Find what was done only once or with too few people or organisms.

    Why: This tells you what to repeat or enlarge.

  2. Say to repeat each test, or use more people in each group.

    Why: This is the first mark in almost every scheme.

  3. Add: calculate a mean, leaving out anomalies.

    Why: This is the second mark.

  4. If the question is about general conclusions, add testing other groups or organisms.

    Why: Some schemes credit widening the sample instead of averaging.

Do not lose marks here

  • ✗
    Saying 'repeat it' with no mention of calculating a mean in a 2-mark question.
  • ✗
    Saying 'use more accurate equipment', which is accuracy, not reliability.
  • ✗
    Including the anomaly in the mean.
  • ✗
    Saying 'change the temperature' and calling it a repeat; a repeat keeps the conditions the same.

Common mistakes: Inferred, not measured. Inferred from the official schemes' credit and rejection patterns; not measured student-error statistics.

What a complete response includes

Mark for repeating or enlarging the sample; second mark for averaging or excluding anomalies, or for widening to other groups.

Worked explanation

  1. The method tests only one person of each age. One person may have unusual hearing.
  2. Reliability means getting similar results again. So the test must be repeated.
  3. Two marks: the repeat, and what to do with the repeats, which is find a mean.
Model answer · written by olevels.com, not an official Pearson mark scheme

Test several people of each age, and repeat the hearing test for each person, then calculate a mean maximum frequency for each age.

How the marks are earned

One mark for repeating the investigation, one for working out a mean. The scheme also allows a different pair: check the results against another group's data.
