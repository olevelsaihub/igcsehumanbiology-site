Explain how the pupil responds to changes in light intensity

Iris muscle actions that widen or narrow the pupil in dim or bright light and why a quick response protects the retina.

Knowledge card

Explain how the pupil responds to changes in light intensity

Written

The iris muscles change the pupil size to control the light entering. Dim light: radial contract, pupil wider. Bright light: circular contract, pupil narrower.

4

The method

  1. Brighter or dimmer

    A large pupil means dim light.

  2. Say retina detects

    Receptors in the retina detect the change.

  3. Give both muscles

    Radial and circular, opposite actions.

  4. Pupil and purpose

    Wider or narrower, and what that does.

Do not lose marks here

  • ✗
    Pupil contracts

    The muscles of the iris contract.

  • ✗
    Radial and circular mixed

    Dim: radial contract.

  • ✗
    One muscle only

    Give both actions.

  • ✗
    Focusing

    Ciliary muscles are for focus.

Worked explanation

  1. The diagram shows the iris in light condition A and light condition B. In B the pupil is larger, so the light has become dimmer. The question is worth 5 marks.
  2. Step 1: the light becomes dimmer.
  3. Step 2: the retina detects the drop in light.
  4. Step 3: the radial muscles in the iris contract.
  5. Step 4: the circular muscles in the iris relax.
  6. Step 5: the pupil widens.
  7. Step 6: more light enters, which keeps the amount reaching the retina steady. Six ideas for five marks is safe.
Model answer · written by olevels.com, not an official Pearson mark scheme

The light gets dimmer when the condition changes from A to B. The lower light level is detected by receptors in the retina. Impulses cause the radial muscles of the iris to contract and the circular muscles to relax. This makes the pupil wider (its diameter increases), so more light enters the eye and the amount of light reaching the retina stays about the same.

How the marks are earned

Marks are for: the light becoming dimmer; the retina detecting the dim light; radial muscles contracting; circular muscles relaxing; the pupil widening; the idea of keeping light entering constant (letting more light in is allowed). Any five of the six give full marks.
