State or explain safety precautions for a practical

Burning food, hot or cold water, reagents, glass, mouthpieces, spirometers and handling cells or bacteria: hazards and how to reduce risk.

Knowledge card

State or explain safety precautions for a practical

Written
  • A hazard is something that could cause harm; a precaution is an action that reduces the risk.
  • Burning food: wear goggles (hot water or food may spit), hold the tube with a holder or clamp (avoid burns), take care with the sharp needle, never taste the food (allergies).
  • Hot or ice-cold water on skin: check the water temperature with a thermometer first.
  • Shared mouthpieces in spirometers or breath tests: sterilise the mouthpiece and do not share it; breathe gently so liquid is not sucked into the mouth.
  • Irritant reagents such as Biuret: wear goggles and wash hands. Broken glass: clear it up with a dustpan and brush.
  • Cells and bacteria: wear gloves and a mask, wash hands, sterilise equipment, keep dishes sealed, to reduce the risk of infection.
  • When burning food, keep your face well away from the flame (an arm's length), and place glassware where it cannot be knocked off the bench.

Before you begin

  • The apparatus for each practical (food burning, spirometer, breath test).
  • How microorganisms spread infection.
1

The method

  1. Read the method and find the real hazards: flame, hot liquid, sharp needle, glass, irritant, shared mouthpiece, microbes.

    Why: Marks are only for precautions that match a hazard in this practical.

  2. Give a specific action or item of equipment for each hazard, e.g. 'wear safety goggles'.

    Why: 'Be careful' alone does not earn a mark.

  3. For Explain or Describe, add what the precaution protects against.

    Why: The second mark is for the reason linked to the precaution.

  4. Give different precautions for different hazards when several are asked.

    Why: Two answers for the same hazard may earn only one mark.

Do not lose marks here

  • ✗
    Writing 'be careful' or 'wear safety equipment' without naming it.
  • ✗
    Writing 'wear gloves' for an irritant or broken glass where schemes ignore it.
  • ✗
    Giving a fair-test point, such as 'same volume of water', as a safety precaution.
  • ✗
    In an Explain question, naming the precaution but not the reason.

Common mistakes: Inferred, not measured. Inferred from the official schemes' credit and rejection patterns; not measured student-error statistics.

How each command word is answered

Command wordTimes askedWhat to write
State6Give the precaution or hazard in a few words.
Give2Give separate, specific precautions, one per line.
Explain1Give one precaution and the harm it prevents, e.g. goggles because hot water may spit into the eyes.
Describe1Say what to do and what it checks or prevents, e.g. check the water with a thermometer before putting a hand in, so the skin is not burnt.
Complete1Write a precaution that matches the hazard in the same row of the table.

Worked explanation

  1. Explain one safety precaution, 2 marks: precaution + reason.
  2. The method heats water with burning popcorn under a test tube, so hot water may spit out.
  3. Precaution: wear safety goggles. Reason: hot water may spit into the eyes.
Model answer · written by olevels.com, not an official Pearson mark scheme

Wear safety goggles, because the hot water may spit out of the test tube and could burn the eyes.

How the marks are earned

One mark for the precaution (goggles) and one for a reason that matches it (water may spit). The other accepted pair is using a test tube holder to avoid burning the hands.
