The method

  1. Factor

    State five or more values.

  2. Equal

    Same volume and concentration.

  3. Set

    Water bath; start the reaction.

  4. Measure

    Name the quantity and when.

  5. Repeat

    Repeat each value; compare.

Do not lose marks here

  • ✗
    'Some enzyme'

    Give a volume.

    Bunsen burner

    Use a water bath.

Worked explanation

  1. The graph shows enzyme activity against temperature, with a peak. So I change temperature and measure activity.
  2. I choose amylase and starch, because I can test for starch with iodine.
  3. Put starch in a tube in a water bath; add amylase.
  4. Measure the time until iodine no longer turns blue-black.
  5. Keep volume and concentration of both solutions the same; repeat at other temperatures.
Model answer · written by olevels.com, not an official Pearson mark scheme

Put 5 cm³ of 1% starch solution into a test tube and 1 cm³ of amylase solution into another tube. Stand both tubes in a water bath at 20 °C for 5 minutes. Add the amylase to the starch and start a stopwatch. Every 30 seconds, put one drop of the mixture into iodine solution on a spotting tile. Record the time when the iodine stays orange-brown, because the starch has been digested. Repeat at 30, 40, 50 and 60 °C, using the same volume and concentration of starch and amylase each time.

How the marks are earned

Marks for: substrate in a test tube; the tube in a water bath; enzyme added to substrate; timing how long the reaction takes; keeping the volume or concentration of enzyme and substrate the same; doing the test at other temperatures. 'Amount' is rejected.
