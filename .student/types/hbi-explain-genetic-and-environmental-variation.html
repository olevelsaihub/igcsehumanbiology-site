Knowledge card

Explain genetic and environmental causes of variation

Written
  • Variation is the differences between individuals of the same species.
  • Genetic variation comes from the different alleles each person inherits from their parents (for example blood group, eye colour, inherited conditions).
  • Environmental variation comes from surroundings and lifestyle (for example diet, exercise, disease, accidents, stress).
  • Many features, such as height and body mass, are affected by both genes and environment.
  • A condition that 'runs in families' is evidence of a faulty or mutated allele being passed from parents to children.
  • If an allele gives an advantage in a population's environment or diet, people with it survive and reproduce more, so the allele becomes more common over generations.
  • Passage clues: diet, stress, infection, the menopause or another illness (such as diabetes) point to an environmental cause; 'runs in families' or 'starts in childhood with no known cause' point to a genetic cause. Excess sweating can come from either.
  • Where people's ancestors drank a lot of milk, being able to digest lactose was an advantage, so people with the tolerance allele survived better and the allele became common.

Before you begin

  • Genes and alleles are sections of DNA on chromosomes.
  • Offspring get one allele of each gene from each parent.
  • A mutation is a change in DNA that can create a new allele.
1

The method

  1. Decide whether each factor is genetic (inherited alleles) or environmental (surroundings, diet, lifestyle).

    Why: Sorting factors stops you repeating the same type of cause.

  2. When listing factors, give different ones: genes, sex, age, nutrition.

    Why: Each distinct factor scores one mark; synonyms of one factor do not.

  3. For evidence from a passage, quote the clue (for example 'runs in families').

    Why: The quoted evidence is usually the first mark.

  4. Then explain what the clue means: a faulty allele or gene is passed from parent to offspring.

    Why: The link from evidence to genes earns the remaining marks.

  5. For 'evolved tolerance' questions, link the diet to an advantage for people with the allele, so the allele spreads.

    Why: Both the dietary cause and the survival advantage are credited.

Do not lose marks here

  • ✗
    Listing 'food', 'diet' and 'nutrition' as three different factors.
  • ✗
    Saying a condition is genetic just because 'it is common'.
  • ✗
    Writing 'it runs in families' with no link to genes or alleles being passed on.
  • ✗
    Saying people evolved tolerance because they decided to drink milk, instead of a selection advantage.
  • ✗
    Using 'gene' and 'allele' as if they meant 'chromosome'.

Common mistakes: Inferred, not measured. Inferred from the official schemes' credit and rejection patterns; not measured student-error statistics.

Worked explanation

  1. Scan the passage for the primary focal type: it begins in childhood and 'often runs in families'.
  2. Running in families is the evidence; I quote it first.
  3. What does that tell me? A faulty (mutated) allele or gene is involved.
  4. How does it run in families? The allele is passed from parents to their children in gametes.
  5. Three marks, so I give evidence, the faulty allele, and how it is passed on.
Model answer · written by olevels.com, not an official Pearson mark scheme

The passage says the condition often runs in families. This suggests it is caused by a faulty, mutated allele of a gene. The allele is inherited, because the genetic information is passed from parents to their offspring.

How the marks are earned

One mark for the evidence that it runs in families; one for a faulty or mutated gene, allele or chromosome; one for this information passing from parents to offspring.
