Explain genetic and environmental causes of variation

Whether a feature varies because of inherited alleles, surroundings or both.

Type 8 Reproduction and heredity HBI-EXPLAIN-GENETIC-AND-ENVIRONMENTAL-VARIATION

★★☆☆☆ · 3 of 36 eligible examination papers, 2019–2025. Difficulty: Medium.

A short chain from evidence to inheritance. Genes versus environment is a clear idea. None. Set in new passages such as hyperhidrosis or lactose intolerance. Links inheritance with evolution and lifestyle.

Knowledge card

Explain genetic and environmental causes of variation

Written

Genetic variation comes from alleles inherited from parents; environmental variation comes from surroundings and lifestyle. Many features, such as height, have both.

4

The method

  1. Decide the type

    Genetic (alleles) or environmental (surroundings, lifestyle).

  2. List different factors

    Genes, sex, age, nutrition.

  3. Quote the clue

    For example "runs in families".

  4. Explain the clue

    A faulty allele is passed from parent to offspring.

Do not lose marks here

  • ✗
    Food, diet, nutrition

    That is one factor, not three.

  • ✗
    Common means genetic

    Look for the inheritance clue.

  • ✗
    Chose to drink milk

    Say selection advantage.

  • ✗
    Gene, allele, chromosome

    They are not the same.

Worked explanation

  1. Scan the passage for the primary focal type: it begins in childhood and 'often runs in families'.
  2. Running in families is the evidence; I quote it first.
  3. What does that tell me? A faulty (mutated) allele or gene is involved.
  4. How does it run in families? The allele is passed from parents to their children in gametes.
  5. Three marks, so I give evidence, the faulty allele, and how it is passed on.
Model answer · written by olevels.com, not an official Pearson mark scheme

The passage says the condition often runs in families. This suggests it is caused by a faulty, mutated allele of a gene. The allele is inherited, because the genetic information is passed from parents to their offspring.

How the marks are earned

One mark for the evidence that it runs in families; one for a faulty or mutated gene, allele or chromosome; one for this information passing from parents to offspring.
