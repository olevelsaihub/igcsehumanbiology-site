Knowledge card

Describe how blood clots and explain why clotting matters

Written
  • When a blood vessel is damaged, platelets collect at the wound.
  • Damaged platelets and tissue release an enzyme (thrombokinase). With calcium ions, this changes prothrombin into thrombin.
  • Thrombin is an enzyme. It converts soluble fibrinogen into insoluble fibrin.
  • Fibrin forms a mesh of threads across the wound. The mesh traps red blood cells and platelets to form a clot, which dries to a scab.
  • A clot is important because it stops too much blood being lost and stops pathogens entering, so it prevents infection and lets the wound heal.
  • Anticoagulants stop clotting. In a dialysis machine they are added as blood enters, so clots do not form and block the machine. They are not added just before the blood returns, because the blood must be able to clot normally again inside the body.
  • If thrombin is not made, fibrinogen is not converted to fibrin, so no mesh forms and there is no clot.
  • A clot forms a plug over the cut and protects the skin while it heals.

Before you begin

  • Enzymes change one substance into another.
  • Platelets are cell fragments in the blood.
  • Pathogens cause infection when they enter the body.
1

The method

  1. Start with platelets at the damaged area.

    Why: Most schemes give a mark for a reference to platelets.

  2. Say an enzyme is released, and that prothrombin becomes thrombin.

    Why: These steps are credited even though the names are not required by the specification.

  3. State that fibrinogen is converted into fibrin, and that fibrin is insoluble.

    Why: This is the one step the specification names and nearly every scheme credits.

  4. Finish with the fibrin mesh trapping red blood cells to form a clot.

    Why: The mesh and the trapped cells are separate marks.

  5. For importance, give two different ideas: less blood loss and keeping pathogens out (so less infection).

    Why: These are separate marks; two ways of saying 'less bleeding' count once.

Do not lose marks here

  • ✗
    Writing 'fibrin turns into fibrinogen' (the wrong way round).
  • ✗
    Saying white blood cells form the clot.
  • ✗
    Saying platelets 'secrete' the enzyme; say they release it.
  • ✗
    Giving only one importance idea (blood loss) and repeating it.
  • ✗
    Writing that a scab forms without describing the fibrin mesh.

Common mistakes: Inferred, not measured. Inferred from the official schemes' credit and rejection patterns; not measured student-error statistics.

Worked explanation

  1. The question asks me to describe blood clotting. It is worth 5 marks, so I need at least five steps in order.
  2. Step 1: a vessel is damaged and the platelets there are damaged too. They release an enzyme.
  3. Step 2: this enzyme, with calcium ions, changes prothrombin into thrombin.
  4. Step 3: thrombin changes soluble fibrinogen into insoluble fibrin.
  5. Step 4: fibrin threads make a mesh.
  6. Step 5: the mesh traps red blood cells and this forms the clot.
  7. I use the word 'release', not 'secrete', for the enzyme.
Model answer · written by olevels.com, not an official Pearson mark scheme

When a blood vessel is cut, platelets collect at the wound and are damaged. The damaged platelets release an enzyme called thrombokinase. With calcium ions (Ca2+) present, this enzyme turns prothrombin into thrombin. Thrombin then changes soluble fibrinogen into insoluble fibrin. The fibrin threads form a mesh across the cut. Red blood cells are trapped in the mesh, and this forms a clot.

How the marks are earned

Marks are given for: an enzyme (thrombokinase or thromboplastin); its release from damaged platelets; prothrombin becoming thrombin; fibrinogen becoming fibrin; a mesh forming; red cells caught in it making the clot; and calcium ions. Any five earn full marks. The word 'secrete' is rejected for the enzyme.
