Knowledge card

Sequence and describe the stages of mitosis

Written
  • The four stages of mitosis, in order, are prophase, metaphase, anaphase and telophase (PMAT).
  • Before mitosis each chromosome is copied, so it is made of two identical chromatids.
  • Prophase: the chromosomes coil up, become shorter and thicker and become visible; the nuclear membrane breaks down.
  • Metaphase: the chromosomes line up across the middle (equator) of the cell, attached to spindle fibres.
  • Anaphase: the chromatids of each chromosome separate and are pulled to opposite ends (poles) of the cell.
  • Telophase: a new nuclear membrane forms around each set of chromosomes; then the cytoplasm divides.

Before you begin

  • Chromosomes are made of DNA and are found in the nucleus.
  • DNA is copied before division.
1

The method

  1. Write PMAT down first.

    Why: Getting the order right is often a mark on its own.

  2. For each stage, write one chromosome event using a position word: visible, middle, poles, new nucleus.

    Why: Each stage-event pair is one mark.

  3. For a set of pictures, find metaphase first (chromosomes in a line), then work outwards.

    Why: Metaphase is the easiest stage to recognise and fixes the others.

  4. For a metaphase drawing, draw the chromosomes in a straight line across the middle, with spindle fibres from the poles.

    Why: Marks are for chromosomes lined up and for showing the equator.

Do not lose marks here

  • ✗
    Putting anaphase before metaphase.
  • ✗
    Saying whole chromosomes, not chromatids, move to the poles in anaphase.
  • ✗
    Saying the chromosomes line up 'at the sides' or 'at the poles' in metaphase.
  • ✗
    Saying the cell membrane forms in telophase instead of the nuclear membrane.
  • ✗
    Describing what the cell does but not what the chromosomes do.

Common mistakes: Inferred, not measured. Inferred from the official schemes' credit and rejection patterns; not measured student-error statistics.

Worked explanation

  1. The question asks what happens to the chromosomes during the four stages. 5 marks: one for the order and one for each stage.
  2. I write the order first: prophase, metaphase, anaphase, telophase.
  3. Prophase: chromosomes condense and become thicker, so they can be seen.
  4. Metaphase: chromosomes line up on the equator of the cell.
  5. Anaphase: the chromatids separate and move to opposite poles.
  6. Telophase: a nuclear membrane forms again around each group of chromosomes.
Model answer · written by olevels.com, not an official Pearson mark scheme

The four stages happen in the order prophase, metaphase, anaphase, telophase.

Prophase: each chromosome coils up, becoming shorter and thicker, so it becomes visible.

Metaphase: the chromosomes are arranged in a line across the equator of the cell.

Anaphase: the two chromatids of each chromosome split apart and are pulled to opposite poles of the cell.

Telophase: a new nuclear membrane forms around each group of chromosomes, making two nuclei.

How the marks are earned

One mark for the correct order of the four names. Then one mark for each stage: chromosomes condensing or becoming visible; lining up at the equator; chromatids separating to the poles; the nucleus or nuclear membrane forming again.
