The method

  1. Write PMAT

    Do this first.

  2. One event each

    Use visible, middle, poles, new nucleus.

  3. Find metaphase

    Chromosomes in a line, then work outwards.

  4. Draw metaphase

    A straight line of chromosomes, spindle fibres from poles.

Do not lose marks here

    Anaphase first

    Metaphase comes before anaphase.

    Whole chromosomes move

    Chromatids move to the poles.

Worked explanation

  1. The question asks what happens to the chromosomes during the four stages. 5 marks: one for the order and one for each stage.
  2. I write the order first: prophase, metaphase, anaphase, telophase.
  3. Prophase: chromosomes condense and become thicker, so they can be seen.
  4. Metaphase: chromosomes line up on the equator of the cell.
  5. Anaphase: the chromatids separate and move to opposite poles.
  6. Telophase: a nuclear membrane forms again around each group of chromosomes.
Model answer · written by olevels.com, not an official Pearson mark scheme

The four stages happen in the order prophase, metaphase, anaphase, telophase.

Prophase: each chromosome coils up, becoming shorter and thicker, so it becomes visible.

Metaphase: the chromosomes are arranged in a line across the equator of the cell.

Anaphase: the two chromatids of each chromosome split apart and are pulled to opposite poles of the cell.

Telophase: a new nuclear membrane forms around each group of chromosomes, making two nuclei.

How the marks are earned

One mark for the correct order of the four names. Then one mark for each stage: chromosomes condensing or becoming visible; lining up at the equator; chromatids separating to the poles; the nucleus or nuclear membrane forming again.
