Explain how beta-blockers and ACE inhibitors lower heart rate or pressure

Written

Beta-blockers block receptors on heart muscle, so heart rate and pressure fall. ACE inhibitors stop the enzyme ACE, so vessels widen.

4

The method

  1. Name the target

    A receptor (beta-blocker) or an enzyme (ACE inhibitor).

  2. What is prevented

    Adrenaline cannot bind, or angiotensin II is not made.

  3. Body change

    Slower heart rate, or wider vessels.

  4. Final effect

    Say blood pressure falls, or the sport effect.

Do not lose marks here

  • ✗
    Stops release

    Beta-blockers block receptors.

  • ✗
    ACE and receptors mixed

    ACE inhibitors act on the enzyme.

  • ✗
    No final effect

    End with lower heart rate or pressure.

Worked explanation

  1. The diagram shows a nerve ending releasing neurotransmitter, and beta-blocker molecules sitting in receptors on the heart muscle.
  2. Step 1: the beta-blocker binds to the receptors on the heart muscle.
  3. Step 2: so the neurotransmitter cannot bind to those receptors.
  4. Step 3: so the heart muscle is stimulated less, the heart rate slows and blood pressure falls.
  5. Lower pressure and a slower heart reduce the risk of heart disease.
Model answer · written by olevels.com, not an official Pearson mark scheme

The beta-blocker molecules fit into and bind to the receptors on the heart muscle. This stops the neurotransmitter from binding to the receptors. As a result, the heart muscle is stimulated less, so the heart rate falls and blood pressure is lowered, which reduces the risk of heart disease.

How the marks are earned

One mark for the beta-blocker attaching to receptors on heart muscle. One mark for the neurotransmitter being unable to attach. One mark for a lower heart rate or lower blood pressure.
