Use aseptic technique to test antibacterial agents on agar

Petri-dish investigations of antibiotics, cleaners or disinfectants: aseptic transfer, contamination control, culture safety and judging effectiveness by clear zones or colonies.

Type 12 Investigations HBI-PRAC-ASEPTIC-AGAR-PLATE-TEST

★★★☆☆ · 5 of 36 eligible examination papers, 2019–2025. Difficulty: Difficult.

Students must plan a sequence and give a reason for each sterile step. Needs understanding of contamination, pathogens and how agents affect growth. No calculation. Contexts (pit latrines, MRSA, garlic) are unusual, but the technique is standard. Combines aseptic technique, safety, control variables and measurement.

Worked explanation

  1. Design, 7 marks, full sentences. I need at least seven separate details.
  2. The factor changed is the antibacterial solution (two types). Control: same volume and same concentration.
  3. How to test: wash the slab with one solution, wait a set time, then sample the slab.
  4. Sampling must be aseptic: sterile swab or loop, onto sterile nutrient agar.
  5. Incubate for a stated time. Then count colonies: fewer colonies means a better solution.
  6. Repeat with the second solution and compare. I end with repeats for reliability.
Model answer · written by olevels.com, not an official Pearson mark scheme

The scientist uses the same volume (50 cm³) of each antibacterial solution, at the same concentration. One area of the concrete slab is washed with solution A and is left for 30 minutes. A sterile swab, or an inoculating loop heated in a Bunsen flame and cooled, is rubbed over a fixed area of the washed slab. The sample is spread onto sterile nutrient agar in a Petri dish, and the lid is taped on. The dish is incubated at 25 °C for 3 days. The same steps are repeated with solution B on a similar area of the slab. The number of bacterial colonies on each dish is counted, and the solution whose dish has fewer colonies is the more effective one. The whole investigation is repeated and the mean numbers of colonies are compared.

How the marks are earned

Marks come from: equal volumes; equal concentrations; washing the slab with one solution; a stated waiting time; a sterile loop or swab; moving the sample onto agar; incubating for a stated time; counting colonies or measuring clear zones; doing the same with the second solution; comparing the results. Any seven score full marks.
