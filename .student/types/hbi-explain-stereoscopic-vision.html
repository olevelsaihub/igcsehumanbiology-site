Knowledge card

Explain stereoscopic vision and how it helps judge distance

Written
  • Stereoscopic vision means seeing with two eyes that face forward.
  • Each eye sees the object from a slightly different angle, so the two views are a little different.
  • The brain combines the two views into one three-dimensional (3D) image.
  • This lets us judge depth, distance and speed better, for example when catching a ball.
  • A person who can see with only one eye has no stereoscopic vision, so they find it harder to judge distance.
  • A fair test compares the same people catching with two eyes and then with one eye, with everything else kept the same and many repeats.
  • Stereoscopic vision is about judging depth and distance. Seeing in colour and focusing on near and far objects are done by other parts of the eye, so they are wrong answers in a multiple-choice question.

Before you begin

  • The eye forms an image on the retina and the brain interprets it.
  • Fair testing: independent, dependent and control variables.
  • Repeating an experiment makes results more reliable.
1

The method

  1. Say there are two eyes, each giving a slightly different view.

    Why: Two eyes and the different views are separate marks.

  2. Say the brain combines the views into a 3D image.

    Why: Stereoscopic or 3D vision is a credited idea.

  3. Link it to the task: better judgement of distance, depth or speed.

    Why: The benefit is the final mark.

  4. For an investigation, change one thing (two eyes or one eye), keep the person, ball, distance and light the same, repeat many times, and count the catches.

    Why: Each of these planning points earns credit, up to six.

Do not lose marks here

  • ✗
    Saying stereoscopic vision means 'seeing in colour' or 'seeing far and near'.
  • ✗
    Saying 'two eyes see more' without mentioning the slightly different views.
  • ✗
    In an investigation, using different people for the one-eye and two-eye tests.
  • ✗
    Not saying what is measured, such as the number of catches out of ten.
  • ✗
    Saying the person with one eye 'cannot see the ball'. They can see it but judge distance less well.

Common mistakes: Inferred, not measured. Inferred from the official schemes' credit and rejection patterns; not measured student-error statistics.

Worked explanation

  1. The question compares a person using both eyes with a person blind in one eye, catching a ball. It is worth 3 marks.
  2. The person with two eyes has stereoscopic (3D) vision. That is my first idea.
  3. Why? Each eye sees the ball from a slightly different angle. That is my second idea.
  4. So the brain can judge how far away the ball is and how fast it is coming. That is my third idea.
Model answer · written by olevels.com, not an official Pearson mark scheme

A person using both eyes has stereoscopic (3D) vision. Each eye sees the ball from a slightly different position, and the brain combines the two views. This allows the person to judge the distance and speed of the ball more accurately, so they can put their hands in the right place to catch it.

How the marks are earned

One mark for stereoscopic or 3D vision (better depth judgement is allowed here). One mark for each eye giving a slightly different view. One mark for better judgement of distance or speed.
