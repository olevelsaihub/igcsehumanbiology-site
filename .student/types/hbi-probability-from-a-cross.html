Work out the probability of a child's genotype or phenotype from a cross

Single outcomes read from a Punnett square and combined outcomes such as an affected boy or two affected children, for autosomal, sex-linked and blood-group crosses.

Knowledge card

Work out the probability of a child's genotype or phenotype from a cross

Written
  • A probability can be written as a fraction (1/4), a decimal (0.25), a percentage (25%) or '1 in 4'. Do not write it as a ratio like 1:3 unless you are sure the scheme allows it.
  • From a Punnett square of four boxes, probability = number of boxes with the outcome / 4.
  • Every child has a 1/2 (0.5) chance of being a boy and 1/2 of being a girl.
  • For an autosomal condition, the chance of 'a boy with the condition' = chance of a boy x chance of the condition (for example 1/2 x 1/4 = 1/8).
  • For a sex-linked cross, the sex is already in the square (XY boxes are boys), so read 'affected boy' straight from the four boxes; do not multiply by 1/2 again.
  • Each child is a separate event, so the chance that two children are both affected = the chance for one child x the chance for the other (1/4 x 1/4 = 1/16).
  • In blood groups, IO is recessive: IB IO is group B, and only IO IO is group O.
  • You can also split an X-linked question: chance of a boy x chance that a boy is affected. If the mother is XhXh, every son is XhY, so a boy with haemophilia = 1/2 x 1 = 1/2. Both ways give the same answer.
  • Y-linked traits: a girl is XX and has no Y chromosome, so she can never have the trait; the chance of a girl without it is just the chance of a girl, 1/2.
  • If neither parent has the allele that causes a dominant condition (both hh), every child is hh, so the chance of no condition is 1 (certain).

Before you begin

  • Drawing a genetic diagram and Punnett square.
  • Sex determination: half of sperm carry X, half carry Y.
  • Multiplying fractions and converting between fractions, decimals and percentages.
1

The method

  1. Write both parents' genotypes using the given symbols, deducing them from the pedigree or blood groups if needed.

    Why: Parent genotypes are often the first mark and errors here ruin the rest.

  2. Write the gametes and complete a labelled Punnett square.

    Why: The offspring genotypes are usually a marking point, and the square shows your working.

  3. Count the boxes that match the outcome and write it over 4.

    Why: This single-event probability is a mark in its own right.

  4. If two things must both happen (a boy AND affected, or two children), multiply the separate probabilities. In an X-linked square, either count the matching XY boxes out of four, or multiply 1/2 by the chance that a boy is affected; never both.

    Why: The multiplication step is credited separately in combined-event items, and doing it twice gives a wrong answer.

  5. Give the final answer clearly as a fraction, decimal or percentage, and check it is between 0 and 1.

    Why: The final value earns the last mark, and a clear answer avoids ambiguity.

Do not lose marks here

  • ✗
    Writing a ratio such as 3:1 when the probability is 3/4.
  • ✗
    Multiplying by 1/2 for sex after already counting the XY boxes out of all four boxes in an X-linked square (this counts sex twice).
  • ✗
    Adding probabilities for two children instead of multiplying them.
  • ✗
    Counting the carriers as affected in a recessive condition.
  • ✗
    Writing that IB IO is a mixed blood group instead of group B.

Common mistakes: Inferred, not measured. Inferred from the official schemes' credit and rejection patterns; not measured student-error statistics.

Worked explanation

  1. Both parents in the 2nd set are group B, so each is IB IB or IB IO.
  2. Child M in this family has group O, which means genotype IO IO. M got one IO from each parent, so both parents must be IB IO.
  3. Gametes: each parent makes IB or IO.
  4. Punnett square gives IB IB, IB IO, IB IO and IO IO.
  5. IB IB and IB IO are group B, so 3 of the 4 boxes are group B.
  6. Probability = 3/4 = 0.75 = 75%.
Model answer · written by olevels.com, not an official Pearson mark scheme

Parent genotypes: IB IO (mother) and IB IO (father)

Gametes: IB or IO from each parent

Punnett square:

| IB | IO

IB | IB IB | IB IO

IO | IB IO | IO IO

IB IB = group B; IB IO = group B (two boxes); IO IO = group O

Probability of blood group B = 3/4

probability = 0.75 (75%)

How the marks are earned

One mark for both parents as IB IO (gametes alone can also earn this); one mark for offspring IB IB, two IB IO and IO IO; one mark for the final 3 in 4 (0.75 or 75%). Using B and O letters for the parents still earns the offspring mark by error carried forward.

Calculation check

  • 3 ÷ 4 = 0.75
  • 3 ÷ 4 × 100 = 75.0
