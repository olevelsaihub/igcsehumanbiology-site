Pearson Edexcel · International GCSE Human Biology · 4HB1

State the variables to control in an investigation

Bench investigations and investigations on people where the student lists factors to keep the same.

Type 5 Investigations HBI-PRAC-NAME-CONTROL-VARIABLES

★★★★★ · 18 of 36 eligible examination papers, 2019–2025. Difficulty: Easy.

One step: separate changed and measured factors from the rest. Needs simple knowledge of what affects enzymes or people's responses. No calculation. Contexts change often (aspirin, chymosin, yoghurt, sweat), but the skill is the same. A single skill used on its own.

State the variables to control in an investigation

Written

A control variable is a factor kept the same in every test. Name it exactly, such as volume of enzyme. Never give the factor you change or measure.

The method

  1. Find

    Name what is changed and what is measured.

  2. List

    Note every other quantity in the method.

  3. Add

    Include temperature or pH if missing.

  4. Write

    One precise factor per line.

Do not lose marks here

  • ✗
    'Amount'

    Write mass or volume.

  • ✗
    The changed factor

    It is not a control.

  • ✗
    'Fair test'

    Name a factor instead.

After your attempt, see the full worked explanation

Worked explanation

  1. The independent variable is the concentration of phenolphthalein phosphate, because six different concentrations are made.
  2. The dependent variable is the colorimeter reading of pink colour.
  3. The volumes (5.0 cm³ and 1.0 cm³) are already fixed, so I look for other factors that change enzyme activity.
  4. Enzymes are affected by temperature and pH, and by how much enzyme is present.
  5. So I write three precise factors: temperature, pH, concentration of phosphatase.
Model answer · written by olevels.com, not an official Pearson mark scheme

1 temperature

2 pH

3 concentration of the enzyme phosphatase

How the marks are earned

Each correct factor that would change the colour if it varied earns one mark: enzyme concentration, inhibitor concentration, temperature, pH, or using the same colorimeter filter. Substrate concentration is the independent variable and earns nothing.
