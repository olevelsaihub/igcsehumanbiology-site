Knowledge card

Describe how heart disease is prevented and treated

Written
  • Lifestyle risk factors for heart disease include a diet high in saturated fat, salt and sugar, lack of exercise, smoking, too much alcohol, obesity and stress.
  • To prevent heart disease: eat less saturated fat and cholesterol, eat less salt, exercise more, stop smoking and keep a healthy mass.
  • Statins are drugs that lower blood cholesterol. Plant stanol esters in some foods also reduce cholesterol absorbed from the gut.
  • A stent is a mesh tube. A catheter carries it into the narrow artery, a balloon inflates to expand it, and it holds the artery open so more blood and oxygen reach the muscle.
  • A bypass uses a vessel to carry blood around the blockage to the heart muscle beyond it.
  • Heart transplants have problems: few donors, the tissue must match or the heart may be rejected, and the donor heart may be diseased, the wrong size or kept outside the body too long.
  • In a drug trial with a placebo, if the placebo group has nearly the same symptoms as the drug group, the drug probably does not cause those symptoms; age, fitness or lifestyle may be the cause.

Before you begin

  • How a blocked coronary artery damages heart muscle.
  • Cholesterol and saturated fat in the diet.
  • What a placebo is and why it is used in drug trials.
1

The method

  1. Decide whether the question is about prevention (lifestyle, statins) or treatment (stent, bypass, transplant).

    Why: The correct set of ideas depends on this.

  2. For prevention, give a specific change in a direction, such as 'eat less saturated fat' or 'do more exercise'.

    Why: A vague word like 'diet' alone may not score.

  3. For a treatment, describe what it does and then the effect: more blood flow, more oxygen to heart muscle, more aerobic respiration.

    Why: Schemes credit the method and each link of the effect.

  4. For a study, quote numbers from the table, compare the groups and draw a careful conclusion.

    Why: Discuss marks need data and a judgement.

  5. Give the number of separate points the question asks for.

    Why: Each distinct point is one mark.

Do not lose marks here

  • ✗
    Writing 'eat healthily' or 'diet' without saying what to eat less or more of.
  • ✗
    Saying a stent 'removes the fat' from the artery.
  • ✗
    Saying statins are used to lower blood pressure.
  • ✗
    In a study, concluding statins definitely cause the symptoms without noticing the placebo score is similar.
  • ✗
    Giving 'the donor is too old' when the question asks for other reasons.

Common mistakes: Inferred, not measured. Inferred from the official schemes' credit and rejection patterns; not measured student-error statistics.

Worked explanation

  1. The question asks for changes in diet and lifestyle that could lower cholesterol. It has 2 marks, so I need two separate changes.
  2. Diet change: eat less fat, especially saturated fat, and fewer foods with cholesterol.
  3. Lifestyle change: do more exercise.
  4. I make each change clear with a direction (less or more) and an example.
Model answer · written by olevels.com, not an official Pearson mark scheme

Eat less saturated fat and fewer foods high in cholesterol, such as fatty meat, butter and cream. Do more regular exercise, such as walking, running or swimming.

How the marks are earned

One mark for cutting fat or cholesterol in the diet. One mark for raising the amount of exercise. Each needs a clear direction of change.
