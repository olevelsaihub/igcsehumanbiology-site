Describe how heart disease is prevented and treated

Written

Prevent heart disease with specific lifestyle changes or statins. Treat a blocked artery with a stent or bypass that restores blood and oxygen to the muscle.

What does each treatment do?

  1. Holds the artery open
  2. Blood goes round the block
  3. Rejection or few donors

Holds the artery open

Write it as
A mesh tube is expanded by a balloon and holds the artery open, so more blood and oxygen reach the muscle.

Exam tipIt does not remove the fat.

Blood goes round the block

Write it as
A vessel carries blood around the blockage to the muscle beyond.

Rejection or few donors

Write it as
Few donors; the tissue must match or the heart is rejected.

4

The method

  1. Prevent or treat

    Decide which the question asks for.

  2. Prevention

    Name a specific change: less saturated fat, more exercise.

  3. Treatment

    Say what it does, then the effect: more blood and oxygen to the muscle.

  4. Study data

    Quote numbers and compare the groups.

  5. Count points

    Give as many separate points as marks.

Do not lose marks here

  • ✗
    "Eat healthily"

    Say less saturated fat or salt.

  • ✗
    Stent "removes fat"

    It holds the artery open.

  • ✗
    Statins for pressure

    Statins lower cholesterol.

After your attempt, see the full worked explanation

Worked explanation

  1. The question asks for changes in diet and lifestyle that could lower cholesterol. It has 2 marks, so I need two separate changes.
  2. Diet change: eat less fat, especially saturated fat, and fewer foods with cholesterol.
  3. Lifestyle change: do more exercise.
  4. I make each change clear with a direction (less or more) and an example.
Model answer · written by olevels.com, not an official Pearson mark scheme

Eat less saturated fat and fewer foods high in cholesterol, such as fatty meat, butter and cream. Do more regular exercise, such as walking, running or swimming.

How the marks are earned

One mark for cutting fat or cholesterol in the diet. One mark for raising the amount of exercise. Each needs a clear direction of change.
