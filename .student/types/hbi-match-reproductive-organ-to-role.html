Human Biology / Identify reproductive organs from their roles or positions

Pearson Edexcel · International GCSE Human Biology · 4HB1

Identify reproductive organs from their roles or positions

Male and female reproductive system organs located on a diagram or matched to a stated job.

Type 2 Reproduction and heredity HBI-MATCH-REPRODUCTIVE-ORGAN-TO-ROLE

★★★★☆ · 7 of 36 eligible examination papers, 2019–2025. Difficulty: Easy.

Direct recall of one organ per job; no chain of reasoning. Needs a clear picture of both reproductive systems and the similar-sounding tubes. None. The same jobs and organs appear in almost every paper. Some items also need diagram reading to follow label lines.

The method

  1. One job at a time

    Say which organ does it.

  2. Follow the label line

    Go to its end before choosing a letter.

  3. Copy the word

    Use the box word with correct spelling.

  4. Check every row

    A word can be used more than once if the question allows.

Do not lose marks here

  • ✗
    Ureter or urethra

    Urethra carries urine and semen out.

  • ✗
    Fertilisation in uterus

    It happens in the oviduct.

  • ✗
    Stored in the testis

    Storage is in the epididymis.

  • ✗
    Organ in a hormone row

    Oxytocin, not uterus, causes birth contractions.

Worked explanation

  1. Row 1, 'duct that carries urine and sperm': the shared tube through the penis is the urethra, not the ureter.
  2. Row 2, 'produces sperm': sperm are made by meiosis in the testes.
  3. Row 3, 'where fertilised ovum is implanted': the embryo sinks into the thick lining of the uterus.
  4. Row 4, 'produces the fluid part of semen': the box gives seminal vesicle (the prostate is not in this box), so I choose seminal vesicle.
  5. Row 5, 'where sperm is deposited during intercourse': the penis places sperm in the vagina.
  6. Row 6, 'where fertilisation happens': sperm meet the ovum in the oviduct.
  7. Row 7, 'releases ova': ova are released from the ovary at ovulation.
  8. Check: epididymis and vulva were not needed, which is allowed.
Model answer · written by olevels.com, not an official Pearson mark scheme

duct that carries urine and sperm: urethra

produces sperm: testes

where fertilised ovum is implanted: uterus

produces the fluid part of semen: seminal vesicle

where sperm is deposited during intercourse: vagina

where fertilisation happens: oviduct

releases ova: ovary

How the marks are earned

Seven marks, one per row, each for the single correct organ name. Ureter for row 1 or uterus for row 6 would score nothing.
