Human Biology / Identify a measurement weakness and improve accuracy

Pearson Edexcel · International GCSE Human Biology · 4HB1

Identify a measurement weakness and improve accuracy

Methods with subjective scoring, uneven bubble counts, too few or widely spaced intervals, or a contaminated reading.

Type 8 Investigations HBI-PRAC-EVALUATE-MEASUREMENT-ACCURACY

★★★☆☆ · 5 of 36 eligible examination papers, 2019–2025. Difficulty: Medium.

Students must judge what is weak in the data and design a better measurement. Needs the meaning of accuracy and some enzyme knowledge. No calculation. Methods are unusual (bubble scores, cheese making) but the flaws are common. Combines accuracy, reliability and enzyme optimum ideas.

The method

  1. Spot

    Find the score, count or reading by eye.

  2. Replace

    Use an instrument number.

  3. Smaller steps

    For an optimum, test between the top two values.

  4. Repeats

    Add repeats if reliable is asked.

Worked explanation

  1. The student scores bubbles from 0 to 5 by eye. This is a judgement, so it is not accurate.
  2. Better: measure the volume of oxygen produced.
  3. Apparatus: a gas syringe connected to a sealed flask.
  4. The test was done only once, so for reliability I add repeats and a mean.
  5. Three marks: volume of oxygen, gas syringe, repeats.
Model answer · written by olevels.com, not an official Pearson mark scheme

Measure the volume of oxygen given off in a fixed time, for example 2 minutes, instead of scoring the bubbles by eye. Collect the oxygen in a gas syringe connected to a sealed flask. Repeat each condition three times and calculate a mean.

How the marks are earned

One mark for measuring the volume of oxygen, one for naming suitable apparatus such as a gas syringe, and one for doing repeats.
