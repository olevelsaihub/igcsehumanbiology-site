Knowledge card

Explain systolic, diastolic and changing pressure around the circulation

Written

Systolic pressure is the peak when the ventricles contract; diastolic is the low when they relax. Blood always flows from high to low pressure.

Rise

Arteries

Write it as

Capillaries

Why no swings
The walls have no muscle or elastic tissue.

Exam tipPressure falls; it does not "stop".

Veins have the lowest pressure.

4

The method

  1. Name the region

    Read the shape: big swings to zero is the ventricle.

  2. Rise

    The ventricle contracts and blood is forced into the aorta.

  3. Fall

    The ventricle relaxes, so pressure drops.

  4. Capillaries and flow

    No pulse, falling pressure; blood goes high to low.

Do not lose marks here

  • ✗
    Atria contract

    The ventricles make the high pressure.

  • ✗
    Systolic and diastolic swapped

    Systolic is the highest, diastolic the lowest.

  • ✗
    "Pressure stops"

    In capillaries it keeps falling.

After your attempt, see the full worked explanation

Worked explanation

  1. I look at region 1. Pressure rises to about 120 mmHg and falls almost to 0, again and again.
  2. Only a heart chamber can fall almost to zero, so region 1 is the heart, the left ventricle.
  3. Why does it rise? The left ventricle muscle contracts. This forces blood out of the heart into the aorta at high pressure.
  4. Why does it fall? The ventricle relaxes, so the pressure inside it drops.
  5. This repeats with every heartbeat, so the graph goes up and down.
  6. I write each idea as a separate sentence to collect five marks.
Model answer · written by olevels.com, not an official Pearson mark scheme

Region 1 is the heart (the left ventricle). When the muscle of the left ventricle contracts, it squeezes the blood and forces it out of the heart into the aorta. This makes the pressure very high. Then the ventricle relaxes, so the pressure in it falls to almost zero. This contracting and relaxing happens with every heartbeat, so the pressure keeps rising and falling.

How the marks are earned

Marks go for: saying it is the heart; the left ventricle contracting; blood being pushed out of the heart or into the aorta; the pressure being high; the ventricle relaxing; and the pressure dropping. Five of these six give full marks.
