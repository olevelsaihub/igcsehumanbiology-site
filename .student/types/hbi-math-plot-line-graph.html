Plot a line graph from a table of results

Continuous data in a table are plotted on a grid, for one or several series, joined with ruled lines or a smooth curve as instructed.

Type 11 Maths skills HBI-MATH-PLOT-LINE-GRAPH

★★★★★ · 13 of 36 eligible examination papers, 2019–2025. Difficulty: Medium.

Students must choose the axes and a scale. Needs the idea of independent and dependent variables. Choosing a scale and plotting accurately is careful numerical work. Graph plotting is very common. Links practical skills with data handling.

Plot a line graph from a table of results

Written

Put the thing you change (often time) on the x-axis. Label both axes with units, use an even scale over half the grid, plot carefully, then join as told.

Worked explanation

  1. Time was chosen by the scientist, so 'Time in minutes' goes on the x-axis. 'Blood glucose level in mg per 100 cm3' goes on the y-axis.
  2. The grid is 16 big squares wide and 12 big squares tall.
  3. Time runs from 0 to 180 minutes. 15 minutes per big square uses 12 big squares, which is more than half of 16.
  4. Glucose runs from 65 to 230. A scale from 0 to 240 at 20 per big square uses all 12 big squares.
  5. Plot the seven points for each person: without diabetes (0, 65), (30, 120), (60, 130), (90, 95), (120, 80), (150, 65), (180, 65); with diabetes (0, 110), (30, 215), (60, 230), (90, 200), (120, 190), (150, 180), (180, 170).
  6. Join each set with straight ruled lines and label them 'without diabetes' and 'with diabetes', or add a key.
Model answer · written by olevels.com, not an official Pearson mark scheme

x-axis: Time in minutes, 0 to 180, 15 minutes per big square. y-axis: Blood glucose level in mg per 100 cm3, 0 to 240, 20 per big square. All 14 points plotted accurately, each set joined point to point with straight ruled lines, and a key showing which line is the person without diabetes and which is the person with diabetes.

How the marks are earned

One mark for both axes labelled with quantity and unit. One mark for time on the x-axis with a sensible linear scale. Two marks for accurate points (one is lost for each wrong point). One mark for straight lines joining the points. One mark for identifying the two lines.

Calculation check

  • 180 ÷ 15 = 12.0
  • 240 ÷ 20 = 12.0
