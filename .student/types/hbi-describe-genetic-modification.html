Describe genetic modification and gene therapy for human health

Engineering bacteria or yeast to make human insulin, Golden Rice for vitamin A, and gene therapy that delivers a working allele into body cells with a viral vector.

Type 12 Cells and tissues HBI-DESCRIBE-GENETIC-MODIFICATION

A long ordered method with several named stages. Needs enzymes, vectors and recombinant DNA ideas. No calculation. Hosts change: bacteria, yeast, rice, patient cells. Links DNA, protein synthesis and disease.

Written

Genetic modification moves a gene, not the protein. A restriction enzyme cuts it out, ligase joins it into a plasmid, and the host makes the protein.

The method

  1. Find and cut

    Cut the wanted gene out with a restriction enzyme.

  2. Vector

    Cut a plasmid open with the same enzyme.

  3. Join

    Ligase makes recombinant DNA.

  4. Insert

    Plasmid into the host, which makes the protein.

  5. Gene therapy

    A virus carries a normal allele into cells.

Do not lose marks here

  • ✗
    Insulin put in

    Say the gene goes in.

  • ✗
    Enzymes swapped

    Restriction cuts; ligase joins.

  • ✗
    No plasmid

    Include the vector step.

  • ✗
    Gene or allele

    Gene therapy delivers a normal allele.

Worked explanation

  1. The question asks how bacteria are modified to make human insulin. 6 marks, so I plan at least six steps.
  2. Step 1: find the insulin gene in human DNA.
  3. Step 2: cut it out using a restriction enzyme.
  4. Step 3: take a plasmid out of a bacterium and cut it open with the same restriction enzyme.
  5. Step 4: put the insulin gene into the opened plasmid.
  6. Step 5: use DNA ligase to join the gene into the plasmid. This is now recombinant DNA.
  7. Step 6: put the plasmid back into a bacterium. The bacterium then makes human insulin.
Model answer · written by olevels.com, not an official Pearson mark scheme

The gene for human insulin is found in human DNA. It is cut out using a restriction enzyme. A plasmid is taken out of a bacterium and is also cut open with the same restriction enzyme. The insulin gene is placed into the cut plasmid, and the enzyme DNA ligase joins the gene and plasmid together. The plasmid now contains recombinant DNA. The plasmid is then put back into a bacterial cell. The bacterium reproduces, and all the bacteria make human insulin.

How the marks are earned

Up to six marks from: taking out the plasmid; finding the insulin gene; cutting out the gene; cutting the plasmid; naming restriction enzymes; putting the gene into the plasmid; naming ligase; recombinant DNA; returning the plasmid to a bacterium. The answer covers all nine points.
