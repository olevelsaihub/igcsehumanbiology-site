Explain how temperature or pH changes enzyme activity

Explaining rising, falling or zero enzyme activity as temperature or pH changes, why tests are kept at 37 °C or one pH, and why body temperature and pH must stay near normal.

Type 7 Biological molecules HBI-ENZYME-TEMP-PH-DENATURE

★★★★★ · 12 of 36 eligible examination papers, 2019–2025. Difficulty: Medium.

A four- or five-step causal chain, plus deciding which side of the optimum applies. Kinetic energy, collisions and denaturing by heat or pH at the molecular level. Graph and table reading only, no calculation. Contexts vary (hypothermia, biosensors, yoghurt, catalase, pepsin) but the same two chains apply. Links enzymes to homeostasis, digestion and practical work.

Knowledge card

Explain how temperature or pH changes enzyme activity

Written

Warming gives more kinetic energy, so more collisions and a faster rate. Too hot, or the wrong pH, denatures the enzyme by changing its active site.

4

The method

  1. Name the factor

    Temperature or pH, and which side of the optimum.

  2. Below the optimum

    Kinetic energy, collisions, complexes, rate.

  3. Too hot or wrong pH

    Denatured, active site changes, no binding.

  4. Use the context

    Say what it does to the result or the person.

Do not lose marks here

  • ✗
    Enzymes are killed

    They are denatured, not alive.

  • ✗
    Cold denatures

    Low temperature only slows the enzyme.

  • ✗
    Substrate changes

    It is the active site that changes shape.

  • ✗
    Everything is pH 7

    Pepsin works best at about pH 2.

Worked explanation

  1. Look at the graph: A is at about 43 °C and B at about 58 °C. Activity rises from A to B. So this is the rising side, before the optimum.
  2. The cause is the rise in temperature. I say that first.
  3. Higher temperature gives the enzyme and substrate molecules more kinetic energy, so they move faster.
  4. They collide more often, so more enzyme-substrate complexes form each second.
  5. So the rate of enzyme activity increases. That is five linked ideas for five marks. I will not mention denaturing, because that happens after B.
Model answer · written by olevels.com, not an official Pearson mark scheme

From A to B the temperature increases. The enzyme and substrate molecules gain more kinetic energy and move faster. This means they collide more often, so more enzyme-substrate complexes are formed every second. As a result, more product is made per second and the rate of enzyme activity rises.

How the marks are earned

One mark each for: the rise in temperature; more kinetic energy; more collisions; more enzyme-substrate complexes; a higher rate of activity. Five ideas for five marks, so every link must be written.
