Knowledge card

Explain how insulin and glucagon control blood glucose

Written

The pancreas detects blood glucose. Insulin makes the liver and muscles store it as glycogen; glucagon makes the liver turn glycogen back into glucose.

The method

  1. High or low

    Decide whether glucose is too high or too low.

  2. Organ

    The pancreas releases the hormone.

  3. Hormone

    Insulin for high; glucagon for low.

  4. Target and direction

    Liver and muscles: glucose to glycogen, or glycogen to glucose.

Do not lose marks here

  • ✗
    Glucagon or glycogen

    Hormone versus storage carbohydrate.

  • ✗
    Insulin from the liver

    The pancreas makes it.

  • ✗
    "Sugar"

    Write glucose.

After your attempt, see the full worked explanation

Worked explanation

  1. When does insulin act? When blood glucose rises above normal.
  2. Where is it from? The pancreas releases it into the blood.
  3. What does it do? It causes glucose to be converted into glycogen.
  4. Where? In the liver and muscles. So the blood glucose level falls back to normal.
Model answer · written by olevels.com, not an official Pearson mark scheme

When the blood glucose concentration rises, the pancreas releases insulin into the blood. Insulin causes glucose to be converted into glycogen in the liver and muscles, so the glucose concentration in the blood falls back to normal.

How the marks are earned

Marks for: released by the pancreas; released when glucose levels rise; glucose converted to glycogen; this happening in the liver or muscles. Any three earn full marks.
