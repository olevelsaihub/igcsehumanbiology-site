Knowledge card

Identify bones and bone tissues on a diagram

Written
  • The humerus is the single bone of the upper arm.
  • The forearm has two bones: the radius on the thumb side and the ulna on the little-finger side.
  • The finger bones are the phalanges.
  • The femur is the thigh bone; the tibia is the large shin bone; the patella is the kneecap.
  • The clavicle is the collarbone, the scapula is the shoulder blade, and the vertebral column is the backbone.
  • A long bone has hard compact bone in the shaft, spongy bone inside the ends, and cartilage covering the ends.
  • The fibula is the thin bone beside the tibia in the lower leg. Cartilage covers the bone ends inside the knee joint.
  • A bone with a long shaft and two wider ends, with compact bone outside and spongy bone inside, is a long bone.

Before you begin

  • Main body regions: arm, leg, chest, back.
  • The skeleton supports and protects the body.
1

The method

  1. Find a landmark you know: the elbow, knee, shoulder, thumb or wrist.

    Why: Position is the only clue on many diagrams.

  2. Work out each bone from its position: upper arm or thigh has one bone, forearm or lower leg has two.

    Why: This avoids swapping humerus and femur.

  3. For forearm bones, use the thumb: the radius is on the thumb side.

    Why: The radius and ulna are the most often swapped pair.

  4. For a bone section, compact bone is the dense outer layer, spongy bone has spaces, and cartilage caps the ends.

    Why: Bone tissue items give one mark per correct name.

  5. Spell bone names carefully.

    Why: A word that mixes two names, such as 'tibula', is rejected.

Do not lose marks here

  • ✗
    Swapping the radius and ulna.
  • ✗
    Calling the upper arm bone the femur.
  • ✗
    Writing made-up names such as 'tibula'.
  • ✗
    Mixing up compact bone and spongy bone in a section.
  • ✗
    Labelling the clavicle where the scapula is.

Worked explanation

  1. The model shows one long bone at the top, then two bones side by side, then the wrist, palm and fingers. Finger A is the thumb, on the left.
  2. Bone 1 is the single upper arm bone: the humerus.
  3. Bones 2 and 3 are the forearm bones. Bone 2 is on the same side as the thumb, so it is the radius. Bone 3 is the ulna.
  4. Label 4 points to the finger bones: the phalanges.
Model answer · written by olevels.com, not an official Pearson mark scheme

1 humerus; 2 radius; 3 ulna; 4 phalanges (finger bones)

How the marks are earned

One mark per bone: humerus, radius, ulna and phalanges (fingers is also accepted). The radius must be the bone on the thumb side.
