Knowledge card

Match human cell structures to their functions

Written
  • The nucleus holds the chromosomes, which carry the genetic information, and it controls what the cell does.
  • The cell membrane controls which substances move into and out of the cell.
  • Mitochondria are where aerobic respiration happens, so they release energy (as ATP) for the cell.
  • Ribosomes are the site of protein synthesis: amino acids are joined into proteins there.
  • The endoplasmic reticulum (ER) carries substances, such as new proteins, through the cell and helps to fold and modify them.
  • The cytoplasm is the jelly where most of the cell's chemical reactions take place.
  • The nuclear membrane keeps the DNA inside the nucleus and controls the movement of substances, such as mRNA, between nucleus and cytoplasm.
  • Cilia are tiny hairs on the cells that line the trachea. They beat together to sweep mucus up and out of the airways.
  • At a synapse, mitochondria in the neurone release energy (ATP). The neurone uses it to make neurotransmitter (acetylcholine) and to move vesicles to the membrane so they release it into the gap.
  • A new protein made on the ribosomes passes into the ER, which folds it into its correct shape and carries it to where it is needed. If a protein cannot bind to the ER, it may be the wrong shape and may not reach its destination, such as the cell membrane.

Before you begin

  • Aerobic respiration releases energy from glucose.
  • Proteins are made of chains of amino acids.
  • DNA is the genetic material and is found in chromosomes.

The method

  1. Read which column or box is missing: the name, the function, or both.

    Why: Tables often give marks for each cell separately, so you must fill every empty box.

  2. Name the structure from its position and shape if a diagram or letter is given.

    Why: The name mark needs the exact structure; a wrong name loses that mark, although the function can still score.

  3. Write one clear job using an action verb: controls, releases, makes, transports.

    Why: A verb plus what is acted on (for example 'releases energy') is what earns the function mark.

  4. If a context is given (a sperm, a synapse, a faulty protein), link the job to that context.

    Why: Applied questions give extra marks for what the job is used for, such as energy for making and releasing neurotransmitter.

  5. In matching or tick questions, draw exactly one line or tick per row.

    Why: Two lines or ticks from one item scores zero for that item.

Do not lose marks here

  • ✗
    Writing that mitochondria 'make energy' or 'produce energy' instead of release energy by respiration.
  • ✗
    Saying the cell membrane 'protects the cell' without saying it controls what enters and leaves.
  • ✗
    Mixing up ribosomes and the ER: ribosomes make the protein, the ER moves and processes it.
  • ✗
    Giving only 'controls the cell' for a 2-mark nucleus question and forgetting the genetic information it stores.
  • ✗
    Saying cilia move the whole cell, or 'filter' the air, instead of sweeping mucus along the airway.

Worked explanation

  1. The diagram shows two cheek cells. Label X points to the outer edge of a cell, Y points to the dark oval inside, and Z points to the area inside the cell around the nucleus.
  2. The outer edge of an animal cell is the cell membrane, because animal cells have no wall. So X is the cell membrane.
  3. The dark oval with a dot inside is the nucleus. So Y is the nucleus.
  4. The clear area that fills the cell around the nucleus is the cytoplasm. So Z is the cytoplasm.
  5. Now give one job for each. Membrane: it controls what goes in and out. Nucleus: it holds the genetic information and controls the cell. Cytoplasm: chemical reactions happen there.
  6. The table has six boxes and the question is worth 6 marks, so each box earns one mark. Check every box is filled.
Model answer · written by olevels.com, not an official Pearson mark scheme

X: cell membrane. Function: controls which substances enter and leave the cell.

Y: nucleus. Function: contains the genetic information (DNA) and controls the activities of the cell.

Z: cytoplasm. Function: the place where most chemical reactions in the cell happen.

How the marks are earned

Six marks: one for each correct name (membrane, nucleus, cytoplasm) and one for each correct job (controlling entry and exit; storing genetic material or controlling the cell; being where reactions happen). A wrong name does not cancel the function mark in the same row, so always write a function even if you are unsure of the name.
