Explain water movement by osmosis in body contexts

Applying water potential to blood, cells, gut and tissue fluid in diabetes, dehydration, lactose intolerance and oedema, plus finding cell concentration from osmosis data.

Type 3 Movement of substances into and out of cells HBI-EXPLAIN-OSMOSIS-IN-BODY

★★★☆☆ · 5 of 36 eligible examination papers, 2019–2025. Difficulty: Difficult.

Students must work out which side has the lower water potential and then predict an effect. Water potential is abstract and the direction is easy to reverse. The potato item asks for a method, not a calculation. Contexts are new: lactose intolerance, oedema, diabetes, alcohol and dehydration. Links osmosis to digestion, the kidney and blood.

Written-answer check

After your attempt, see the full worked explanation

Worked explanation

  1. Too much water enters body cells. Water moves into cells by osmosis, so I name osmosis first.
  2. For water to go in, the inside of the cell must have the lower water potential. So the tissue fluid has the higher water potential.
  3. Water moves from the higher water potential to the lower water potential, down the water potential gradient.
  4. It crosses the cell membrane, which is partially permeable. That completes the chain for 3 marks.
Model answer · written by olevels.com, not an official Pearson mark scheme

Water enters the body cells by osmosis. The cytoplasm of the cells has a lower water potential than the tissue fluid around them, so the tissue fluid has the higher water potential. Water therefore moves across the partially permeable cell membrane from the higher water potential in the tissue fluid to the lower water potential inside the cells, down a water potential gradient.

How the marks are earned

One mark for naming osmosis. One mark for comparing water potentials correctly (lower inside the cells, higher in the tissue fluid). One mark for the direction: water goes from high to low water potential. The reverse direction (low to high) earns nothing.
