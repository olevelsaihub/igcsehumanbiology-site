Explain how a mutation changes a protein and phenotype

Chaining a base change through codons and amino acids to a faulty or missing protein and a named condition or characteristic.

Type 16 Cells and tissues HBI-EXPLAIN-MUTATION-TO-PHENOTYPE

★★★★☆ · 7 of 36 eligible examination papers, 2019–2025. Difficulty: Medium.

A four-step causal chain that must be kept in order. Needs gene to protein to phenotype understanding. No calculation. Named disorders and traits change between papers. Links genetics, protein synthesis and enzymes.

Worked explanation

  1. The question asks how a gene mutation can affect the phenotype. 3 marks means at least three links.
  2. Link 1: the mutation changes the base sequence (the codons) in the gene's DNA.
  3. Link 2: so a different order of amino acids is coded.
  4. Link 3: so the protein has a different shape and may not work properly.
  5. Link 4: so a characteristic of the person changes, for example the kidneys develop cysts.
  6. Four links for three marks gives me a spare point.
Model answer · written by olevels.com, not an official Pearson mark scheme

The mutation alters the sequence of bases in the DNA of the gene, so some codons change. This means a different sequence of amino acids is coded for. The protein made has a different shape, so it cannot carry out its function properly. This changes a characteristic of the person, for example cysts form in the kidneys and the kidneys do not work well.

How the marks are earned

Any three from: the base or codon sequence changes; the amino acid order changes; the protein has a different shape or function; a characteristic or body function changes. The answer gives all four.
