Pearson Edexcel · International GCSE Human Biology · 4HB1

Draw, label and compare the structure of a neurone

Drawing a labelled motor neurone, naming a part such as the axon, giving the role of the myelin sheath, and spotting structural differences between two neurone drawings.

Type 6 Coordination HBI-DRAW-LABEL-NEURONE-PARTS

★★★☆☆ · 5 of 36 eligible examination papers, 2019–2025. Difficulty: Medium.

Recall the shape and place the labels; little reasoning. Needs the parts of a neurone and the job of the myelin sheath. No arithmetic is needed. Drawing from memory is less familiar than writing, and comparison drawings change between papers. Combines drawing skill with recall of structure.

4

The method

  1. Cell body

    A circle at one end, with a nucleus and dendrites.

  2. Axon

    One long fibre ending in small branches.

  3. Myelin

    Blocks along the axon.

  4. Labels

    Ruled lines that touch the exact part.

  5. Compare

    One feature at a time: fewer, shorter.

Do not lose marks here

  • ✗
    Nucleus outside

    It belongs inside the cell body.

  • ✗
    Short axon

    Make it much longer than the cell body.

  • ✗
    Lines in empty space

    Each line must touch its part.

  • ✗
    One-word comparison

    Name the feature: fewer branches.

Worked explanation

  1. The question is worth 4 marks. It asks for a drawing of a motor neurone with labels for nucleus, axon and myelin sheath.
  2. So I expect 1 mark for a correct drawing and 1 mark for each of the three labels.
  3. I draw a round cell body on the left with a dot inside for the nucleus. I add five or six short branches (dendrites) around it.
  4. From the cell body I draw a long fibre going right. It is many times longer than the cell body. This is the axon.
  5. I draw a row of small blocks along the axon for the myelin sheath, and I end the axon with a few small branches.
  6. I add three ruled label lines: one touching the dot (nucleus), one touching the long fibre (axon), and one touching a block on the fibre (myelin sheath).
Model answer · written by olevels.com, not an official Pearson mark scheme

[Drawing] A round cell body at one end containing a nucleus, with several short dendrites branching from it. A long axon (many times the length of the cell body) leaves the cell body and ends in small branched endings. The axon is covered by a myelin sheath shown as a series of blocks.

Labels with lines: nucleus (touching the dot inside the cell body); axon (touching the long fibre); myelin sheath (touching the covering on the axon).

How the marks are earned

One mark is for the drawing itself: a cell body at one end with dendrites, and a long axon with branched endings. Then one mark for each correct label: nucleus, axon and myelin sheath. A label line that points to the wrong part does not score.
