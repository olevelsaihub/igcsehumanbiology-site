Knowledge card

Identify alimentary canal organs and describe what each does

Written
  • Food passes in order through: mouth, oesophagus, stomach, small intestine (duodenum then ileum), large intestine (colon, rectum), anus.
  • The liver, gall bladder and pancreas join the gut by ducts; food does not pass through them.
  • Mouth: teeth chew food; saliva contains amylase, which starts digesting starch into maltose.
  • Stomach: hydrochloric acid gives pH about 2; protease (pepsin) starts protein digestion.
  • Pancreas: makes pancreatic juice that flows into the duodenum. Its protease digests protein to amino acids, its amylase digests starch to maltose, and its lipase digests lipids to fatty acids and glycerol.
  • Small intestine: finishes digestion and absorbs products through villi. Large intestine: absorbs most of the remaining water.
  • In the mouth, chewing breaks food into smaller pieces and mixes it with saliva; salivary amylase then digests starch to maltose.
  • The bile duct joins the small intestine (duodenum). If the large intestine is removed (for example for colon cancer), less water is absorbed, so the person easily becomes dehydrated.
  • The small intestine is warm, has a steady pH, plenty of digested food and no predators, so gut parasites such as pinworms grow well there.

Before you begin

  • Digestion means breaking large insoluble molecules into small soluble ones.
  • The main digestive enzymes.
  • Absorption into the blood.

The method

  1. Find the mouth and follow the tube downwards in order.

    Why: Tracing the route stops you mixing up organs that look alike.

  2. Follow each label line to its exact end point.

    Why: A line may pass near one organ but end on another.

  3. Use position: the liver is large, under the right ribs; the gall bladder is under the liver; the pancreas lies below the stomach.

    Why: Accessory organs are commonly confused.

  4. For a role question, give the process and the product where possible.

    Why: Describe answers earn marks for each step (chewing, saliva, starch digestion, maltose).

Do not lose marks here

  • ✗
    Calling the oesophagus the trachea or windpipe.
  • ✗
    Swapping the large and small intestine because of their size in the diagram.
  • ✗
    Labelling the liver as the stomach, or the gall bladder as the pancreas.
  • ✗
    Saying digestion of protein starts in the mouth.
  • ✗
    Ending a label line in empty space between two organs.

Common mistakes: Inferred, not measured. Inferred from the official schemes' credit and rejection patterns; not measured student-error statistics.

Worked explanation

  1. Start at the mouth. X is the narrow tube going down through the chest: the oesophagus.
  2. Y is the bag on the left side below the chest that the oesophagus enters: the stomach.
  3. Z points to the many tight coils in the centre of the abdomen: the small intestine.
  4. W points to the wider tube running round the outside of the coils: the large intestine.
  5. Check the order: oesophagus, stomach, small intestine, large intestine.
Model answer · written by olevels.com, not an official Pearson mark scheme

W: large intestine (colon)

X: oesophagus

Y: stomach

Z: small intestine (ileum)

How the marks are earned

One mark for each correct name. Colon is allowed for W and ileum for Z.
