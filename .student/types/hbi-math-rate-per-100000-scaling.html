Scale a rate per 100 000 people to a whole population

Disease cases or deaths are given, often read from a graph, per 100 000 people; the number in a stated population is required.

Type 16 Maths skills HBI-MATH-RATE-PER-100000-SCALING

★★☆☆☆ · 4 of 36 eligible examination papers, 2019–2025. Difficulty: Medium.

Students must pick the correct line and year. A rate per 100 000 is a new idea for many students. Large numbers and a two-step scale, sometimes adding groups. The 'per 100 000' base looks unusual. Combines graph reading with proportional scaling.

Knowledge card

Scale a rate per 100 000 people to a whole population

Calculation
  • A rate 'per 100 000' tells you how many cases there are in every 100 000 people.
  • Number of groups of 100 000 = population / 100 000.
  • Number of cases = rate x (population / 100 000).
  • Write the population as a full number first (for example 60 million = 60 000 000).
  • Read the rate for the correct group (male or female) and the correct year or age.
  • Some data use a different base, such as per million or per 1000. Divide the population by that base instead of 100 000.
  • If the question is about a whole age group, add the male and female rates (or numbers) together first.

Before you begin

  • Reading a value from a line graph with a key.
  • Working with millions and powers of ten.
  • Rounding to significant figures.
1

The method

  1. Read the rate for the right group and year. Use a ruler on graphs.

    Why: Some schemes give a mark for the correct rate reading.

  2. Write the population as a full number.

    Why: Mistakes with millions give answers 1000 times too big or small.

  3. Divide the population by 100 000 (or by the base the data uses).

    Why: This scaling factor is a marking point.

  4. Multiply the scaling factor by the rate. Add groups together if the question asks for more than one.

    Why: This gives the final number of people.

  5. Round as asked and check the answer is a sensible number of people.

    Why: The final mark needs the right figures.

Do not lose marks here

  • ✗
    Reading the female line when the question asks about males.
  • ✗
    Multiplying the rate by the population without dividing by 100 000.
  • ✗
    Writing 60 million as 6 000 000.
  • ✗
    Giving 22 250 when three significant figures (22 300) are asked for.

Common mistakes: Inferred, not measured. Inferred from the official schemes' credit and rejection patterns; not measured student-error statistics.

Worked explanation

  1. The question is about men in 2006, so I use the solid line with diamonds (Males).
  2. On the y-axis there are 5 small squares between 0 and 100, so each small square is 20. The 2006 male point sits just under the 640 line, between 620 and 640 and very close to 640, so I read 640 deaths per 100 000.
  3. The population is 60 million = 60 000 000.
  4. Groups of 100 000: 60 000 000 / 100 000 = 600.
  5. Deaths = 600 x 640 = 384 000.
Model answer · written by olevels.com, not an official Pearson mark scheme

Rate for males in 2006 = 640 per 100 000. 60 000 000 / 100 000 = 600. 600 x 640 = 384 000 men.

How the marks are earned

One mark for dividing the population by 100 000 to give 600. One mark for multiplying 600 by the male rate of 640. One mark for the answer 384 000.

Calculation check

  • 60000000 ÷ 100000 = 600.0
  • 60000000 ÷ 100000 × 640 = 384000.0
