Match hormones to glands and effects, and contrast them with nerves

Naming the gland or system producing insulin, glucagon, ADH, adrenaline or thyroxine, stating their effects, and comparing hormonal with nervous signalling.

Type 5 Coordination HBI-MATCH-HORMONE-SOURCE-AND-EFFECT

★★★★☆ · 8 of 36 eligible examination papers, 2019–2025. Difficulty: Easy.

Mostly matching, with a short reason for the speed difference. Needs several gland-hormone-effect facts. No arithmetic is needed. Familiar hormones; passages give the context. Links hormones with nerves and blood transport.

Knowledge card

Match hormones to glands and effects, and contrast them with nerves

Written

Hormones are chemical messages carried in the blood, so they are slower than nerve impulses. Learn each gland with its hormone and effect.

    Think of the hormone and its gland together.

  2. Use each word once

    In a word box, cross off words you use.

  3. Same feature

    Compare route, signal and speed in both columns.

  4. Speed reason

Do not lose marks here

  • ✗
    Liver makes insulin

    The pancreas makes it.

  • ✗
    Thyroxine or adrenaline

    Thyroxine is metabolic rate; adrenaline is fight or flight.

  • ✗
    Hormones are big

    Slow because they travel in the blood.

Worked explanation

  1. There are seven processes and ten words in the box. Each row is 1 mark. Three words will not be used.
  2. Regulation of water in the blood: this is ADH.
  3. Conscious thought: this happens in the cerebrum.
  4. Transport of oxygen: red blood cells carry it using haemoglobin.
  5. Urea formation: urea is made in the liver when amino acids are broken down.
  6. Control of metabolic rate: thyroxine.
  7. Detection of body position: the semicircular canals in the ear sense head movement and position.
  8. Less blood going to the gut: adrenaline sends blood to the muscles and away from the gut.
  9. Unused words: cerebellum, cochlea and stomach. That is fine.
Model answer · written by olevels.com, not an official Pearson mark scheme

Row 1 (water levels in the blood): ADH

Row 2 (conscious thought): cerebrum

Row 3 (carrying oxygen): haemoglobin

Row 4 (making urea): liver

Row 5 (metabolic rate): thyroxine

Row 6 (body position): semicircular canals

Row 7 (less blood to the gut): adrenaline

How the marks are earned

One mark for each correct row, seven in total. The common trap is the cerebellum for conscious thought or the cochlea for body position; both are wrong here.
