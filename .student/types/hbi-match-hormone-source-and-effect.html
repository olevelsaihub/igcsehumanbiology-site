Knowledge card

Match hormones to glands and effects, and contrast them with nerves

Written
  • Glands that release hormones into the blood make up the endocrine (hormonal) system. The pituitary gland is part of it.
  • The pancreas makes insulin (lowers blood glucose) and glucagon (raises blood glucose). These are not digestive enzymes.
  • The pituitary gland releases ADH, which controls the water content of the blood.
  • The adrenal glands release adrenaline. It raises heart rate and breathing rate, widens the pupils, speeds up the change of glycogen to glucose (so blood glucose rises), increases respiration in muscles, and sends more blood to the muscles and brain and less to the gut.
  • The thyroid gland releases thyroxine, which controls the metabolic rate.
  • Nervous system: electrical impulses along neurones, very fast, short-lived. Hormonal system: chemical messages carried in the blood, slower, often longer-lasting.
  • Hormones are slower because they can only travel as fast as the blood flows.
  • Mixed tables can include parts that are not glands: haemoglobin transports oxygen, the liver forms urea, the cerebrum controls conscious thought, the semicircular canals detect body position, and the cochlea detects sound.

Before you begin

  • Blood carries substances around the body.
  • Nerve impulses are electrical and travel along neurones.
  • Homeostasis keeps blood glucose and water levels constant.
1

The method

  1. Read the process or hormone and recall its gland or effect from your list.

    Why: Each correct gland, hormone or effect is one mark.

  2. If a box of words is given, use each word only for the process it really controls, and cross off words you use.

    Why: Using the same word twice usually means one row is wrong.

  3. For a nerve-hormone comparison, compare the same feature in both columns: route, type of signal, speed.

    Why: Each row of the comparison table is one mark.

  4. To explain the speed difference, say hormones travel in the blood and so move only as fast as blood flows.

    Why: The scheme credits transport in blood and the limit set by blood flow.

Do not lose marks here

  • ✗
    Saying the liver makes insulin. The liver is where insulin acts; the pancreas makes it.
  • ✗
    Giving digestive enzymes when asked for substances from the pancreas that are not used in digestion.
  • ✗
    Writing 'hormone' as the type of signal for the hormonal system. The answer is 'chemical'.
  • ✗
    Confusing thyroxine (metabolic rate) and adrenaline (fight or flight).
  • ✗
    Saying hormones are slow 'because they are big' instead of linking speed to the blood.

Common mistakes: Inferred, not measured. Inferred from the official schemes' credit and rejection patterns; not measured student-error statistics.

Worked explanation

  1. There are seven processes and ten words in the box. Each row is 1 mark. Three words will not be used.
  2. Regulation of water in the blood: this is ADH.
  3. Conscious thought: this happens in the cerebrum.
  4. Transport of oxygen: red blood cells carry it using haemoglobin.
  5. Urea formation: urea is made in the liver when amino acids are broken down.
  6. Control of metabolic rate: thyroxine.
  7. Detection of body position: the semicircular canals in the ear sense head movement and position.
  8. Less blood going to the gut: adrenaline sends blood to the muscles and away from the gut.
  9. Unused words: cerebellum, cochlea and stomach. That is fine.
Model answer · written by olevels.com, not an official Pearson mark scheme

Row 1 (water levels in the blood): ADH

Row 2 (conscious thought): cerebrum

Row 3 (carrying oxygen): haemoglobin

Row 4 (making urea): liver

Row 5 (metabolic rate): thyroxine

Row 6 (body position): semicircular canals

Row 7 (less blood to the gut): adrenaline

How the marks are earned

One mark for each correct row, seven in total. The common trap is the cerebellum for conscious thought or the cochlea for body position; both are wrong here.
