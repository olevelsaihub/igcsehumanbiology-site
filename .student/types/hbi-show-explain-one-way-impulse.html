Knowledge card

Show and explain why impulses travel one way across a synapse

Written
  • In a reflex arc, impulses always travel from the receptor, along the sensory neurone, through the relay neurone, to the motor neurone and the effector.
  • At a synapse the impulse goes from the neurone with the synaptic knob (the pre-synaptic neurone) to the next neurone (the post-synaptic neurone).
  • Vesicles holding neurotransmitter are found only in the synaptic knob of the first neurone, so neurotransmitter is released from that side only.
  • Neurotransmitter crosses the gap by diffusion, from a high concentration near the knob to a low concentration near the next neurone.
  • Receptor molecules for the neurotransmitter are found only on the post-synaptic membrane.
  • So the synapse is the structure that makes impulses travel in one direction only.
  • The receptor is joined to the outer end of the sensory neurone, the end farthest from the spinal cord (in the skin or sense organ).
  • If asked which structure stops impulses going backwards, the answer is the synapse (the synaptic cleft is also accepted).

Before you begin

  • The order of neurones in a reflex arc: sensory, relay, motor.
  • Diffusion is the net movement of particles from a high to a low concentration.
  • The structure of a synapse: synaptic knob, vesicles, gap (cleft), next neurone's membrane.
1

The method

  1. On a synapse diagram, find the side with the synaptic knob and vesicles. Draw the arrow from that side across the gap to the other neurone.

    Why: The arrow mark is only given for the correct direction.

  2. On a reflex arc, find the receptor end (skin) and the effector end (muscle). Draw the arrow from sensory to motor.

    Why: An arrow from motor to sensory, or arrows pointing both ways, loses the mark.

  3. To explain one-way travel, give the release side: neurotransmitter comes only from the first neurone.

    Why: This is the first key idea the scheme looks for.

  4. Then say it diffuses across the gap from high to low concentration.

    Why: The diffusion and concentration gradient ideas each earn credit.

  5. Finish with the receptors: they are only on the membrane of the second neurone.

    Why: Without this point the explanation is incomplete and loses a mark.

  6. To show where a receptor connects, draw your line to the free end of the sensory neurone, the neurone whose cell body sits outside the spinal cord.

    Why: The mark is for the correct position only, so the line must end on that neurone, not on the relay or motor neurone.

Do not lose marks here

  • ✗
    Drawing the arrow from the next neurone back towards the synaptic knob.
  • ✗
    Drawing two arrows that point opposite ways 'to be safe'. This scores zero.
  • ✗
    Saying 'the impulse jumps across the gap'. It is the neurotransmitter chemical that crosses.
  • ✗
    Explaining only the release side and forgetting that the receptors are only on the second neurone.
  • ✗
    Answering 'relay neurone' or 'spinal cord' when asked which structure stops impulses going the wrong way; the answer is the synapse.

Worked explanation

  1. The question asks why an impulse can only travel one way across a synapse. It is worth 4 marks, so I need four separate ideas.
  2. First idea: where the chemical comes from. The vesicles with neurotransmitter are only in the synaptic knob, so the chemical is released from that side only.
  3. Second idea: how it moves. It diffuses across the gap.
  4. Third idea: which way diffusion goes. It moves from high concentration near the knob to low concentration on the far side, so it only spreads one way.
  5. Fourth idea: where it acts. The receptors that the chemical binds to are only on the membrane of the next neurone.
  6. I write these in order as short sentences.
Model answer · written by olevels.com, not an official Pearson mark scheme

Neurotransmitter is stored in vesicles in the synaptic knob, so it can only be released from the first (pre-synaptic) neurone. It then diffuses across the gap in one direction, from where its concentration is high (next to the knob) to where it is low (next to the second neurone). The receptor molecules that the neurotransmitter binds to are found only on the post-synaptic membrane of the second neurone, so a new impulse can only start on that side.

How the marks are earned

One mark for saying the neurotransmitter comes out of one side only. One mark for saying it can only diffuse one way. One mark for the high-to-low concentration idea. One mark for saying receptors are only on the post-synaptic membrane. Saying 'the impulse jumps the gap' earns nothing.
