    Recessive allele on the X chromosome.

  2. Compare sets

    Males XY have one X; females XX have two.

  3. Males

    One faulty allele shows: affected, never carriers.

  4. Females

    Two needed; one makes a carrier.

After your attempt, see the full worked explanation

Worked explanation

  1. Four marks, so I need at least four linked ideas.
  2. Where is the allele? On the X chromosome.
  3. Is it dominant or recessive? Recessive, so it does not show in a heterozygote.
  4. Males are XY: one X only, so one faulty allele means the male has haemophilia. Males cannot be carriers.
  5. Females are XX: they can have one normal allele that masks the faulty one, so they are usually carriers, not affected.
  6. Therefore more males than females have the condition.
Model answer · written by olevels.com, not an official Pearson mark scheme

The haemophilia allele is found on the X chromosome and it is recessive, so it is hidden when a normal dominant allele is also present. Males are XY and have only one X chromosome, so a male with the faulty allele will have haemophilia; males cannot be carriers. Females are XX and have two X chromosomes, so a female with one faulty allele is a heterozygous carrier and does not have haemophilia. A female must inherit two faulty alleles to be affected, so males are affected more often.

How the marks are earned

Any four of: allele on the X chromosome; recessive, not expressed in heterozygotes; no male carriers; only females can be carriers; females have two X chromosomes; males are XY with one X.
