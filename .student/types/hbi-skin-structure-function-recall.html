Knowledge card

Identify skin structures and state what each does

Written
  • The skin has three main layers: the epidermis on the outside, the dermis below it, and a layer of fat (adipose tissue) under the dermis.
  • The dermis contains sweat glands, hair follicles, sebaceous glands, blood vessels and sense receptors.
  • The fat layer is an insulator: it reduces the loss of heat from the body.
  • Melanin is a pigment that absorbs ultraviolet light, which lowers the risk of skin cancer.
  • The skin protects the body from pathogens, senses touch, pain and temperature, and excretes some water and salts in sweat.
  • If the skin is burned or cut, pathogens can get through to the blood and cause infection.
  • Structures that help control body temperature include the brain (hypothalamus), the skin, sweat glands, muscles and the arterioles in the skin.
  • A first degree burn damages only the epidermis; a second degree burn reaches the dermis; a third degree burn also reaches the fat layer. Use the diagram to see which structures lie in each layer.

Before you begin

  • Pathogens cause disease
  • Heat moves from warm to cool places
  • UV light can damage DNA
1

The method

  1. Find the layer: outer thin layer is epidermis, thick middle is dermis, bottom is fat.

    Why: Knowing the layer narrows down which structure a letter can be.

  2. Name the structure exactly: sweat gland, arteriole, hair follicle, sebaceous gland.

    Why: Each correct name is one mark.

  3. Give a specific function, not a vague one: 'insulates, reducing heat loss', not 'keeps warm'.

    Why: Some schemes reject vague phrases.

  4. For infection, link broken skin to pathogens entering the body or blood.

    Why: Both the damage and the entry of pathogens earn marks.

  5. For a burns or layers question, check on the diagram which layer each structure is drawn in before you choose.

    Why: Some structures, such as sweat glands and hair follicles, reach down into the deeper layer, so the diagram decides the answer.

Do not lose marks here

  • ✗
    Writing 'fat keeps the body warm'; one scheme refuses this. Say it insulates and reduces heat loss.
  • ✗
    Calling the fat layer 'skin fat' or 'muscle'.
  • ✗
    Saying melanin 'blocks the sun' without mentioning UV light.
  • ✗
    Confusing sweat glands with sebaceous glands.
  • ✗
    Saying burns let 'germs in' without saying they enter the body or blood.

Common mistakes: Inferred, not measured. Inferred from the official schemes' credit and rejection patterns; not measured student-error statistics.

Worked explanation

  1. The question asks about the layer of fat and body temperature.
  2. Fat is a poor conductor of heat. So it is an insulator.
  3. An insulating layer reduces the heat energy lost from the body to the surroundings.
  4. I avoid writing only 'keeps the body warm' because that is too vague.
Model answer · written by olevels.com, not an official Pearson mark scheme

The layer of fat acts as an insulating layer. It reduces the loss of heat energy from the body to the surroundings.

How the marks are earned

One mark for saying the fat insulates. One mark for saying it traps heat or reduces heat loss. 'Keeps the body warm' is not accepted.
