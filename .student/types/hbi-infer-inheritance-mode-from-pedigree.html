Build or read a pedigree to decide how a condition is inherited

Drawing a family tree from a description, and using pedigree patterns to judge dominant versus recessive and autosomal versus sex-linked inheritance.

Type 17 Reproduction and heredity HBI-INFER-INHERITANCE-MODE-FROM-PEDIGREE

★★★☆☆ · 6 of 36 eligible examination papers, 2019–2025. Difficulty: Difficult.

Must test two hypotheses against the pedigree and argue from what each would predict. Combines dominance and sex linkage and the logic of carriers. Only counting affected people. Each pedigree, condition and description is new. Uses genotype deduction, sex linkage and pedigree drawing together.

4

The method

  1. Read the key

    Who is affected, males and females.

  2. Count

    Many more males points to X-linked.

  3. Find the pattern

    Unaffected parents with an affected child; affected daughter, unaffected father.

  4. Name people

    Use their numbers, then state the conclusion.

Do not lose marks here

  • ✗
    Male affected

    That alone does not mean sex-linked.

  • ✗
    No numbers

    Always name the people used.

  • ✗
    Swapped symbols

    Squares are males, circles females.

Written-answer check

After your attempt, see the full worked explanation

Worked explanation

  1. Mark the affected people: 6 (female), 8 (female) and 13 (male). Two females and one male are affected.
  2. If the condition were X-linked recessive, more males than females would be affected. Here it is the other way round.
  3. Look at 6: she is affected but her father, 1, is not. For an X-linked recessive condition she would need an X with the allele from her father, so he would also be affected.
  4. The same applies to 8 and her unaffected father 3.
  5. So males 1 and 3 must be carriers. Under sex linkage males cannot be carriers; only females can.
  6. Conclusion: the condition is not sex-linked.
Model answer · written by olevels.com, not an official Pearson mark scheme

Alkaptonuria is not sex-linked. In this pedigree more females (6 and 8) are affected than males (only 13); if it were sex-linked we would expect more males to be affected. If it were sex-linked, only females could be carriers. Females 6 and 8 have the condition, so if it were sex-linked their fathers, males 1 and 3, would also have to show the condition. But males 1 and 3 are unaffected, so they must be carriers of the allele, which is impossible for a sex-linked condition because males cannot be carriers.

How the marks are earned

Up to four from: the conclusion that it is not sex-linked; more affected females than males (sex linkage would give more affected males); under sex linkage, carriers could only be female; males 1, 3 or 11 would need to show the condition if it were sex-linked; yet these men are in fact carriers.
